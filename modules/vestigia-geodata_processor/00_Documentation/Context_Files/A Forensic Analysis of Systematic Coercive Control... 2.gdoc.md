## **Part IV: Strategic Recommendations for Pro Se Litigation**

This final section synthesizes the preceding analysis into a coherent, powerful narrative suitable for legal presentation. It provides a strategic framework and actionable recommendations for effectively arguing the case as a pro se litigant in a Michigan family court.

### **Defining the Core Narrative: A Campaign of Control, Not a Conflict of Equals**

To succeed in court, it is essential to frame the history correctly. This is not the story of a "toxic relationship" or a "high-conflict divorce" where both parties share equal blame. The evidence supports a much more specific and serious narrative. The core storyline to be presented is:  
**"This case documents a systematic and escalating eight-year campaign of coercive control, perpetrated by a skilled manipulator against a partner and child. This campaign involved sophisticated psychological warfare, professional and social sabotage, direct child endangerment, and the weaponization of the legal system itself. The evidence demonstrates that the respondent's actions were not reactive or situational, but were part of a calculated, ongoing strategy to maintain dominance and evade accountability, all to the detriment of the child's safety and well-being."**  
This framing is vital. It shifts the court's focus from a "he said/she said" dispute to an evidence-based analysis of a unilateral pattern of abuse, which is far more compelling in a custody determination.

### **Evidence Strategy: Collection, Authentication, and Presentation**

#### **Legality of Evidence Collection**

It is critical to understand that accessing a spouse's phone or private accounts without their explicit permission can have serious legal consequences under both federal and Michigan law. Evidence obtained this way may be ruled inadmissible, and the person who obtained it could face civil or criminal penalties. The argument that the device was a "hand-me-down" for your daughter or that she accessed your devices first does not provide a legal justification for accessing her private communications. Similarly, the "digital garbage" analogy does not apply; deleting a digital account is an assertion of privacy, not an abandonment of it.

#### **The Correct Legal Path: Subpoenas and Discovery**

The proper and legal way to obtain phone records, text messages, and social media data is through the formal discovery process once a court case is active.

* **Subpoenas:** You can file a motion to have the court issue a subpoena to the relevant phone or tech companies. This requires specifying the phone number and a reasonable time frame. This process can take several weeks or months and may involve fees.  
* **Other Discovery Tools:** You can also use interrogatories (written questions) and requests for admission to have the other party admit or deny key facts under oath.

#### **Authentication of Digital Evidence**

For any digital evidence to be admissible in a Michigan court, it must be authenticated, meaning you must prove it is what you claim it is.

* **MRE 901:** Under Michigan Rule of Evidence 901, you can authenticate evidence through witness testimony (someone with knowledge of the communication), or by its "distinctive characteristics". This includes the sender's known phone number, their unique writing style, or references to facts only they would know.  
* **Metadata:** The timestamps, GPS data, and sender/receiver information from official phone records obtained via subpoena are the strongest forms of authentication.  
* **Presentation:** Always present the full context of conversations, not just selected messages, to maintain credibility.

#### **Organizing Your Evidence**

Meticulous organization is crucial for a pro se litigant.

* **Create a "Narrative Binder":** This binder should be your master file, containing a chronological timeline, detailed incident logs, printed communications, and a list of potential witnesses with summaries of their knowledge.  
* **Label Everything:** Use tabs and clear labels for each piece of evidence (e.g., "Exhibit A: Texts regarding Jake photos, July 2024").

### **Securing a Psychological Evaluation**

#### **Filing the Motion in Genesee County**

In Michigan, a psychological evaluation must be ordered by the court. As a pro se litigant in Genesee County, you must file a motion with the 7th Judicial Circuit Court.

* **The Correct Form:** Use **Form FOC 87, "Motion Regarding Custody"**. There is no specific form for requesting a psychological evaluation; the request must be made within this custody motion.  
* **Justifying the Motion:** In your motion, you must explain why the evaluation is necessary for determining the "best interests of the child". Clearly and factually outline the patterns of behavior that raise concerns, such as substance abuse, child endangerment, and extreme manipulative behaviors that suggest a personality disorder.  
* **Filing and Fees:** File the completed motion with the Genesee County Clerk's office. A filing fee is required (typically $100 for a post-judgment custody motion). If you cannot afford this, you can file **Form MC 20, "Fee Waiver Request,"** at the same time.

#### **Requesting a *Forensic* Psychologist**

A standard Friend of the Court (FOC) investigation is often conducted by a social worker and is not equipped to uncover sophisticated psychological abuse. You must argue for a full **forensic psychological evaluation** conducted by a licensed, doctoral-level psychologist.

* **Justification:** Your motion should argue that the complexity of the case—involving allegations of coercive control, a potential personality disorder, and systematic manipulation—requires the specialized expertise of a forensic psychologist. A forensic psychologist can administer standardized psychological tests (like the MMPI-2 or PAI) designed to detect deception and underlying psychopathology, which an FOC investigator cannot do.  
* **Cost of Evaluation:** Private forensic evaluations are expensive, ranging from $5,000 to over $30,000. FOC evaluations are much cheaper (up to $300) and may be waived by the court. In your motion, you can ask the judge to allocate the cost of a private evaluation based on the parties' respective incomes or argue that the other party should bear the cost since her behavior necessitated the evaluation.

### **Interacting with a Forensic Psychologist**

* **Do Not Contact Preemptively:** Wait for the court to appoint an evaluator. Contacting one beforehand can damage your credibility.  
* **Presenting Sensitive Information:** A forensic evaluator's goal is to gather all relevant information. While a judge may deem illegally obtained evidence inadmissible in court, an evaluator may be permitted to review it to form a professional opinion. Be honest about how you obtained the information, but frame the discussion around the *behaviors* the evidence reveals and their impact on the child's welfare.  
* **Be Prepared and Child-Focused:** Organize your evidence binder for the evaluator. Be honest and accountable for your own past and your reactive responses. Frame every concern in terms of its impact on Kailah's safety and well-being, linking the behaviors to the "Best Interest" factors.

### **Anticipating and Countering Defenses**

The greatest vulnerability in this case is the history of past perpetration and the documented instances of reactive abuse (yelling, repeated calling). Katrina will certainly use these against you. The strategy is not to deny them, but to **contextualize them within the framework of reactive abuse**.

* **Her likely claim:** "He harassed me with constant phone calls."  
* **Your prepared response:** "Your Honor, those calls occurred during a two-month period when the respondent, in retaliation for a CPS report, was illegally withholding my daughter from me. Any parent in that situation would be desperate to make contact. The issue is not my calls, but the punitive and illegal child withholding that provoked them."

The greatest strength is the evidence of her premeditation, most notably the "throw Kristen in his face" comment. This comment must be a centerpiece of the argument. It proves her manipulation is not impulsive or a product of emotional distress, but is **calculated and strategic**.
