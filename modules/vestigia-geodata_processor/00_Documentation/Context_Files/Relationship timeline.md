Case Background and Summary

Parties & Child: Matthew Salem (father, petitioner) and Katrina Kinzel (mother, respondent) share one minor child, Kailah (DOB 1/31/2020). The parents never married. They cohabited for several years in a volatile relationship described by Father as 7+ years of manipulation, emotional abuse, and control by Mother. Father has documented incidents of Mother calling him derogatory slurs (e.g. homophobic and belittling epithets) and using the child to emotionally manipulate situations. Despite this toxicity, Father was the child’s primary caregiver from birth until mid-2022, while Mother worked. This gave the child a strong bond with Father and a stable routine under his care.

Relationship History: By late 2022, the relationship deteriorated irreparably. Father was effectively expelled from the household in Fall 2022 amid conflict. Since then, Mother has held primary physical custody de facto. Father’s involvement depended on Mother’s willingness; there was no court order initially. The ensuing years (2023–2024) saw cycles of attempted co-parenting and setbacks: brief reconciliations followed by new conflicts and separations. For example, in mid-2023 Mother cut off Father’s access for a month over a false claim (the “welfare check” incident). In late 2024 they tried to reconcile, but Mother’s infidelity and concealment of plans (e.g. considering a move out of state) reignited disputes. Each rupture was marked by Mother withholding the child as a means of punishment or control, rather than resolving issues through communication.

Custody Arrangements Attempted: Until mid-2025, the parties operated without a formal custody order. Father consistently sought a regular parenting schedule, but Mother’s cooperation was erratic. She allowed visits at times, then arbitrarily suspended contact after arguments (sometimes for days, other times weeks or more). Father attempted to reason with Mother and even offered conciliatory approaches – for instance, proposing use of a co-parenting app and joint decision-making to improve communication. These overtures were largely rebuffed. No mediated agreement or interim family court arrangement was reached, despite Father’s efforts. By early 2025, the situation worsened to a point where Father was entirely shut out of the child’s life, prompting him to seek court intervention.

Child’s Living Situation: Since the 2022 separation, Kailah has lived primarily with Mother, but in an unstable environment. Mother’s living arrangements changed multiple times (at one point residing with her mother; later cohabiting with a new boyfriend, etc.). These transitions, coupled with Mother’s pattern of bringing new partners into the child’s life quickly, introduced uncertainty. For example, Mother introduced a boyfriend (“Jake”) to Kailah and even allowed the child to sleep overnight on him within two weeks of dating. She had criticized Father for an innocuous incident of Kailah sleeping in Father’s bed (with Father and his then-partner present) yet engaged in a riskier version of the same behavior, revealing a concerning double standard and lapse in judgment. Father maintains a stable home environment (the longtime marital home or a consistent residence, possibly with extended family support) and asserts that continuity in this familiar environment is in the child’s best interest. However, Father is facing financial hardship – partly due to Mother’s actions (e.g. her refusal to share child-related tax benefits and interference with his work) – which has put his housing at risk. This raises the stakes: Father’s home is not only historically stable for the child, but losing it (through eviction) could undermine his custody case and further destabilize Kailah’s life.

Legal Context: Father (Matthew) filed an Emergency Ex Parte Motion in June 2025 as a pro se litigant when Mother’s complete cutoff of contact signaled a critical situation. In that motion he requested immediate parenting time, joint legal custody, and a prohibition on Mother relocating Kailah out-of-state. The ex parte relief was not granted; no immediate order was issued (likely because the court did not find imminent physical harm to the child). Consequently, the dispute remains unresolved pending a full hearing. Father’s case thus far frames Mother’s behavior as parental alienation, coercive control, and neglect – a “campaign” to marginalize him from Kailah’s life. The core legal theory is that Mother’s conduct is causing harm to the child’s well-being, warranting court intervention to protect the child’s best interests. Specifically, Father alleges that Mother’s actions detrimentally affect multiple Best Interest Factors under Michigan law (e.g., love/affection, guidance, health care, moral fitness, and willingness to facilitate parent-child relationship). His ultimate goal is to obtain a 50/50 joint custody arrangement (legal and physical), restoring balance and consistent access for both parents. He is not seeking to “win” sole custody outright, but rather to stop the unilateral deprivation of his role and ensure Kailah has the benefit of both parents in her life.

Current Issues in Focus: Several high-conflict issues define this case:

Mother’s Relocation Plans: Mother has threatened to relocate Kailah out of Michigan, having family bases in other states (AL and AR) and a history of covertly planning a move. This looms as a major concern if not legally restrained.

Lack of Transparency: Mother consistently fails to share crucial information about the child (medical visits, school updates, current address if she moves residences). She even removed Father from school contact lists to keep him in the dark. Father often learns of health issues or changes only after the fact, if at all.

Emotional Manipulation & Gatekeeping: The case is rife with evidence of Mother using emotional tactics and withholding the child as a weapon in disputes. Father describes a pattern of coercive control: Mother demands Father’s compliance or silence on issues, or she will refuse contact with the child. She has called police on him without justification, vilified him to friends/family with false narratives (projecting her own behavior onto him), and only allows communication on her terms.

New Partner/Cohabitation: Mother’s introduction of new partners into Kailah’s life without caution and her reliance on those partners for housing/support raise red flags. The child’s routine is disrupted by these changing dynamics, and one new partner effectively became a caregiver figure almost immediately (an image of Kailah sleeping on the boyfriend was taken by Mother, contradicting her claims about the child’s sleeping arrangements). Father worries about the character of these individuals and the lack of any vetting or adjustment period for the child.

Potential Endangerment: A combination of factors suggests Kailah’s welfare may be at risk: (1) Medical Neglect – Mother’s lapses in managing Kailah’s health (asthma episodes not properly followed up, delayed vaccinations, incomplete autism evaluation). (2) Unsafe Situations – e.g. the June 22, 2023 car incident and instances of Mother or her associates possibly driving intoxicated with the child present (Father has expressed concern about Mother’s stepfather’s alcoholism and Mother’s own drinking episodes). (3) Emotional Harm – The atmosphere of conflict, with Mother allegedly badmouthing Father to the child and others, could be psychologically damaging. Father points out that Michigan’s definition of domestic violence includes causing a family member to feel terrorized or threatened, and he believes Mother’s behavior falls into this category by creating fear and confusion for the child.

In sum, the case background reflects a father fighting to remain in his daughter’s life against mounting barriers, and a mother whose resentment or personal issues are spilling into parenting decisions. The legal narrative to be presented will emphasize that Mother’s conduct is inimical to Kailah’s best interests and that swift court intervention is needed to prevent long-term harm to the parent-child relationship.
