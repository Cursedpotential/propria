# Michigan CPS / MDHHS Resources

Children's Protective Services investigation process, forms, Central Registry, mandated reporting, and how CPS intersects with custody proceedings.

## CPS Investigation Process

### How to Report
- **Hotline**: 855-444-3911 (24/7)
- **Online (mandated reporters)**: MORS (Mandated Online Reporting System)
- **Form**: DHS-3200 (Complaint of Child Abuse/Neglect)
- **Emergency**: Call 911 first, then CPS hotline

### Investigation Stages

**Stage 1: Intake & Screening**
- Report received via hotline or form
- Screened for: Does it meet statutory definition? Is it CPS jurisdiction?
- Decision: Screen-in (investigate) or screen-out (no investigation)
- If screened out, may refer to community resources

**Stage 2: Investigation (begins within 24 hours)**
- CPS investigator assigned
- In-person contact with child (usually within 24 hours)
- Interviews: alleged victim, alleged perpetrator, other household members
- Home visit and safety assessment
- Collateral contacts: school, medical, law enforcement
- May involve law enforcement/prosecutor collaboration

**Stage 3: Determination (within 30 days, may extend)**
- Investigator determines: Was abuse/neglect substantiated?
- Preponderance of evidence standard
- Possible dispositions (see below)

**Stage 4: Post-Investigative Services**
- If substantiated: Safety plan, services, possible court involvement
- If unsubstantiated: Case closed (may offer voluntary services)
- Central Registry placement if criteria met

### CPS Dispositions

| Category | Criteria | Central Registry? |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| Category I | Severe abuse (serious physical harm, sexual abuse) | Yes |
| Category II | Abuse/neglect confirmed, not Category I | Yes |
| Category III | Abuse/neglect confirmed, low/moderate severity | Yes (some) |
| Category IV | Services needed but insufficient evidence | No |
| Category V | Unsubstantiated / No evidence | No |

**Central Registry placement** occurs for confirmed cases involving:
- Methamphetamine production
- Serious abuse or neglect
- Sexual abuse or exploitation
- Select child-related convictions

## Definitions (MCL 722.622)

### Child Abuse
Harm or threatened harm to a child's health or welfare by a parent, legal guardian, teacher, teacher's aide, clergy, or any other person responsible for the child's welfare that occurs through nonaccidental physical or mental injury, sexual abuse, sexual exploitation, or maltreatment.

### Child Neglect
Harm or threatened harm to a child's health or welfare by a parent, legal guardian, or any other person responsible for the child's health or welfare that occurs through negligent treatment, including failure to provide adequate food, clothing, shelter, or medical care. Also includes placing a child at an unreasonable risk of harm.

### Who is Covered
- Victims: Children under 18
- Perpetrators: Parents, guardians, teachers, clergy, youth program adults (18+), anyone responsible for child's welfare

## Mandated Reporters (MCL 722.621 et seq.)

### Who Must Report
- Physicians, dentists, nurses, other medical professionals
- School administrators, teachers, school counselors
- Social workers, psychologists, therapists
- Law enforcement officers
- Clergy (with exceptions for privileged communications)
- Child care providers
- Regulated child care organization employees
- Court-appointed special advocates (CASA)

### Reporting Requirements
- **Trigger**: Reasonable cause to suspect child abuse or neglect
- **Method**: Immediate oral report to CPS (855-444-3911), followed by written report within 72 hours
- **Form**: DHS-154 (Mandated Reporter form, via MORS online)
- **Immunity**: Good-faith reporters are immune from civil/criminal liability
- **Penalty for failure**: Criminal misdemeanor

### In Custody Context
- Either parent can file a CPS report (be cautious — false reports can backfire)
- Mandated reporters (therapists, teachers) may file based on child's statements
- CPS reports can be used as evidence in custody proceedings
- Pattern of unfounded CPS reports by one parent may indicate alienation/harassment

## Central Registry

### What It Is
A statewide database maintained by MDHHS of individuals with substantiated (confirmed) CPS findings for serious abuse or neglect.

### How You Get On It
- CPS investigation confirms Category I, II, or certain Category III findings
- Certain child-related criminal convictions

### Implications for Custody
- Registry status affects parental fitness evaluations under MCL 722.23(f) and (g)
- Courts and FOC can access registry information
- Can affect employment (childcare, teaching, healthcare)
- Significant evidence against the listed parent in custody proceedings

### How to Get Off (Expungement)
- Request administrative hearing to challenge the finding
- File petition for expungement after statutory waiting period
- Reversal of finding removes registry entry
- Consult attorney — process is complex

## CPS Records Access

### Who Can Access
- **Parents/guardians**: Can request their own CPS file from local MDHHS office
- **Adults investigated**: Can request records related to their investigation
- **Courts/FOC**: Access via legal process (subpoena, court order)
- **Attorneys**: Via client authorization or court order

### How to Request
- In person at local MDHHS office
- Bring photo ID
- Request form available at office or via Michigan Legal Help

### What's Redacted
- Complainant identity (who made the report) — ALWAYS redacted
- Ongoing investigation details
- DV safety information
- Mental health treatment records (some)

### Michigan Legal Help Guide
- **URL**: https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/family/cps-and-your-family
- Explains CPS process, rights, record access in plain language

## CPS and Custody Intersection

### How CPS Findings Affect Custody

1. **Factor (f) Moral Fitness**: Substantiated abuse/neglect goes to moral fitness
2. **Factor (g) Mental/Physical Health**: May indicate untreated conditions
3. **Factor (k) Domestic Violence**: If abuse involved DV
4. **Factor (l) Other Relevant Factors**: Any CPS history is relevant

### Using CPS Records in Court
- Subpoena CPS records via discovery (MCR 2.310)
- Authenticate via CPS worker testimony or business records exception (MRE 803(6))
- CPS findings are NOT binding on family court — court makes independent determination
- But substantiated findings carry significant weight

### Defending Against False CPS Reports
- Document pattern of reports (dates, allegations, dispositions)
- Show all investigations resulted in unsubstantiated findings
- Argue: Pattern of false reporting = alienation tactic = Factor (j) violation
- Request court admonish reporting parent if pattern is clear

### CPS Safety Plans
- CPS may impose safety plans during investigation
- Can restrict contact, require supervision, mandate services
- Not a court order but failure to comply can lead to court involvement
- In custody context: Voluntary compliance shows good faith

## Agent Protocol

When CPS issues arise in custody context:

1. **Immediate safety**: If child in danger, report to 855-444-3911 and 911
2. **Record access**: Guide parent to request CPS file from local MDHHS with photo ID
3. **Court use**: Subpoena records via MCR 2.310; authenticate via MRE 803(6)
4. **False reports**: Document pattern, connect to Factor (j) alienation analysis
5. **Central Registry**: Check if opposing parent is listed — significant custody evidence
6. **Defend findings**: If parent has CPS finding, show rehabilitation, treatment compliance
7. **Never advise filing false CPS report** — this is a crime and backfires in custody
