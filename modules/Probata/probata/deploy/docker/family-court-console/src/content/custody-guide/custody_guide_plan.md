# Project Plan: Pro Se Custody Guide for Genesee County, Michigan

**Companion files:** This plan governs drafting of the full treatise. Research foundation is in `custody_guide_research.md`. Full section-by-section skeleton is in `custody_guide_outline.md`. The drafting model (Claude Opus) must treat all three as a single package and re-verify anything flagged `[UNVERIFIED]` before publishing final text.

---

## 1. Purpose and Positioning

This guide is a self-help substitute for ICLE's "Effectively Tackle Custody Issues Through Judgment," a continuing-legal-education course restricted to licensed attorneys. It cannot replicate the ICLE course's copyrighted content (which was never accessed) — instead, it independently mirrors and expands the same *functional lesson architecture* ICLE uses for practicing attorneys (case strategy → pretrial → final judgment), rebuilding each lesson from primary Michigan law and Genesee County practice, and adding entirely new material ICLE does not need to cover because it targets attorneys, not the litigants navigating the system without one.

The guide exists because a pro se parent cannot buy what a lawyer buys: procedural fluency, insider knowledge of local practice, and a playbook for high-conflict opposing parties. This product supplies all three, in plain language, calibrated to one specific forum: the 7th Circuit Court, Family Division, Genesee County, Michigan.

## 2. Audience

**Primary reader:** A pro se (self-represented) parent in a contested Genesee County custody, parenting-time, or child-support matter, where the opposing party is alleged to be high-conflict, narcissistic, or abusive, and where parental alienation is a live concern. The reader:

- Has no legal training and may never have set foot in a courtroom.
- Is under emotional strain (the underlying dispute is adversarial and personal, not a commercial transaction).
- Needs to perform, without an attorney, tasks a lawyer would normally do: draft motions, prepare exhibits, examine witnesses, respond to a referee recommendation, negotiate or resist mediation, and present proofs at trial.
- May be a domestic violence survivor, a target of false allegations, or both, and may have safety needs that override ordinary procedural advice (see Section 6, Disclaimers, for the safety-resources carve-out).
- Is comfortable with structured, sequential material (the reader profile documented for this project skews technical/analytical), so a workbook/checklist format will land better than narrative prose alone.

**Secondary audience:** The drafting model itself (Claude Opus), which needs unambiguous, non-narrative instructions per section — this is why the outline exists as a separate, highly structured document rather than folding drafting notes into prose.

## 3. Goals

1. **Functional parity with ICLE's structure**, adapted so every attorney task in the original 3-lesson architecture has a pro se-executable analog (e.g., "prepare a trial brief" becomes "trial brief skeleton + fill-in template + worked example").
2. **Legally accurate, source-traceable content.** Every statute, rule, and case citation in the final treatise must trace back to a URL in `custody_guide_research.md` or a verification the drafting model performs itself.
3. **Genesee County specificity**, not generic Michigan boilerplate — local judges, FOC contacts, motion praecipe mechanics, filing fees, and mediation resources must be named and sourced, with explicit flags where local practice varies by assigned judge or may have changed since research was performed.
4. **Actionable pedagogy.** Mirror ICLE's reported use of 20+ hands-on exercises: the reader should finish each module having produced a real work-product draft (a filled-in template, a completed evidence log entry, a drafted objection), not just having read text.
5. **High-conflict/abuse-aware framing throughout**, not siloed into one chapter. Documentation discipline, communication hygiene (e.g., OurFamilyWizard), and safety planning should be referenced wherever they are procedurally relevant (e.g., when discussing discovery, when discussing best-interest factor (k), when discussing witness prep).
6. **Unambiguous non-lawyer positioning.** The guide must never cross from legal information into legal advice, and must say so repeatedly and specifically, not once in a boilerplate footer.

## 4. Scope

**In scope:**
- Michigan substantive family law (Child Custody Act, FOC Act, MCSF) as applied in a Genesee County circuit court proceeding.
- Michigan and Genesee County procedure: motion practice, referee hearings, mediation, discovery, trial, judgment entry, and post-judgment enforcement/modification.
- Pro se-specific survival skills: e-filing/paper-filing status, fee waivers, courtroom decorum, limited-scope attorney help, self-help centers.
- High-conflict-litigant and alienation-specific strategy: documentation systems, best-interest factor (j)/(k) evidence-building, LGAL and psychological evaluation processes, PPO interplay.
- Templates, checklists, and exercises tied to each module.
- A glossary of terms used throughout.

**Out of scope (explicitly, and the drafting model should say so where relevant):**
- Divorce property division, spousal support, and other non-custody divorce issues (only touched where they intersect with custody/support, e.g., a divorce judgment that also sets custody).
- Adoption, paternity establishment procedure (only referenced where custody depends on an existing paternity determination), termination of parental rights, and child protective proceedings (referenced only to distinguish LGAL rules, per the research file's Section 9 caution).
- Criminal defense for the reader (PPO defense strategy if the reader is a *respondent* is out of scope; the guide is written from the perspective of a parent pursuing or defending custody/parenting time, with DV content framed primarily as protective and evidentiary, not as criminal-defense counsel).
- Appeals to the Michigan Court of Appeals (the guide ends at judgment entry and post-judgment motion practice at the circuit court level; an appendix pointer to appellate resources is sufficient, not a full appellate module).
- States other than Michigan, and Michigan counties other than Genesee for anything county-specific (statewide statutory/case-law content applies everywhere in Michigan and should be flagged as such).

## 5. Pedagogical Design

ICLE's course reportedly uses 20+ hands-on exercises for attorneys working through real scenarios. The pro se guide reproduces this exercise density but recalibrates exercise *type* for a non-lawyer, first-time user:

| Exercise type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Fill-in-the-blank template** | Produce an actual filing-ready (or filing-adjacent) document | Blank motion for parenting time with bracketed prompts |
| **Worked example** | Show a completed, realistic (fictionalized) version of the same template | A filled sample motion for parenting time with realistic facts |
| **Checklist** | Sequence a multi-step process so nothing is missed | Pre-hearing checklist: praecipe filed, service completed, exhibits copied, witness list ready |
| **Scenario exercise** | Force the reader to apply a legal standard to facts | "Which of these five fact patterns meet the Vodvarka change-of-circumstances threshold?" |
| **Self-assessment / diagnostic** | Help the reader determine which path applies to them | "Do you have an established custodial environment? Answer these six questions." |
| **Evidence log / journal template** | Build ongoing documentation habits from day one | Custody journal with date/time/witness/category columns |
| **Practice script** | Prepare the reader for live oral interaction | Referee hearing opening statement script; direct-examination question bank |
| **Red-flag spotting exercise** | Train the reader to recognize high-conflict/alienation tactics and evidentiary opportunities | "Match these ex-partner behaviors to the best-interest factor they may support" |

Every lesson/module in the outline specifies which exercise type(s) apply. The drafting model should not invent new exercise types without noting the addition, so the guide's pedagogy stays consistent end to end.

**Sequencing principle:** exercises should build cumulatively — e.g., the custody journal template introduced in Lesson 4 (high-conflict module) should be referenced again as an evidentiary source in Lesson 3.3 (trial proofs) and Lesson 2.5 (referee hearing), not re-taught from scratch. The outline flags these cross-references explicitly so Opus maintains continuity across modules drafted at different times.

## 6. Disclaimers Plan

Disclaimers must be prominent, specific, and repeated at natural decision points — not confined to a single front-matter page. Required disclaimer types and placement:

1. **Master disclaimer (front matter, before Lesson 1):** This is legal information, not legal advice; it does not create an attorney-client relationship; Michigan statutes, court rules, local administrative orders, forms, and fees change and may have changed since this guide was written; the reader must independently verify current law and local practice at legislature.mi.gov, courts.michigan.gov, and the Genesee County/7th Circuit Court's own sites before relying on anything here; outcomes in any individual case depend on facts, judge, and evidence that no guide can predict.
2. **Per-lesson recap disclaimer** (short, one or two sentences, at the start of each Lesson): reminds the reader this lesson may reference laws/forms/fees that change, and to verify before filing.
3. **High-stakes-action disclaimer** (attached specifically to: filing any motion, responding to a PPO, any step during an active safety threat, and any discussion of parental alienation strategy): explicit "consider consulting an attorney for this specific step, even if only via limited-scope representation" language, with a pointer to the limited-scope representation module and free/low-cost resources.
4. **Safety-first override notice** (front matter AND repeated at the start of the high-conflict/abuse lesson and the PPO section): if the reader or a child is in immediate danger, stop reading and call 911; Michigan's 24-hour domestic violence hotline and the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) take priority over any procedural step in this guide; a PPO or emergency motion may be time-sensitive in ways this guide cannot fully anticipate, and the reader should contact the Genesee County YWCA PPO-filing assistance (on-site at the courthouse, see research file Section 6.6) or a domestic violence advocate immediately rather than self-navigating from a book alone.
5. **"Not a substitute for an attorney" reminder** tied to every template: each fill-in template should carry a short caption noting that a completed template is a starting draft, not a guarantee of legal sufficiency, and that court clerks/referees cannot give legal advice on how to fill it out (a fact pro se litigants frequently misunderstand).
6. **Currency-of-law flags inline**: anywhere the research file marks a fact `[UNVERIFIED]` or flags a pending administrative order (e.g., Genesee's e-service pilot under AO 2026-3), the drafted text must carry that flag forward visibly, not silently resolve it.

The drafting model should treat disclaimers as content, not boilerplate — varying the specific wording by context (e.g., the PPO disclaimer should differ from the trial-brief disclaimer) while keeping the same core messages.

## 7. Style Guide

**Voice:** Direct, calm, non-alarmist, but not falsely reassuring. Avoid tone that could inflame an already high-conflict situation if the opposing party ever reads a reader's copy (i.e., do not use the words "narcissist," "abuser," or similar labels as the primary organizing vocabulary of the *legal* content — use them only in framing/motivational sections; the legal content itself should map behaviors to statutory factors and evidentiary categories, since that is what persuades a court, not clinical labels).

**Reading level:** Plain language, roughly 8th–10th grade reading level for connective prose; legal terms of art are permitted but must be defined on first use in each module (not just once, globally) and collected in the glossary. Sentences should be short enough to read aloud in a stressed state.

**Legal precision:** Every statute/rule/case citation must:
- Use full, correct Michigan citation format (e.g., *Vodvarka v Grasmeyer*, 259 Mich App 499; 675 NW2d 847 (2003)) on first reference in each module, and short form thereafter within that module.
- Quote verbatim statutory text in blockquotes when the research file provides verbatim text (do not paraphrase away precision on operative legal standards, e.g., the ECE clause or the best-interest factors).
- Immediately follow any legal claim with a plain-language explanation of what it means for the reader's situation — never leave a citation to stand alone without translation.
- Carry forward, visibly, any `[UNVERIFIED]` flag from the research file using a consistent visual marker, e.g., a blockquote or callout labeled **VERIFY BEFORE RELYING**.

**How to present law without giving "legal advice":** Present the law and procedure descriptively and give the reader decision *frameworks* (checklists, self-assessment questions, "questions to ask a lawyer if you consult one") rather than telling the reader what to do in their specific case. Use conditional/informational framing ("courts generally...", "the statute provides...", "one option available is...") rather than imperative framing ("you should..."). Where a judgment call is inherently strategic (e.g., whether to accept a mediation settlement), present the tradeoffs and point to where to get individualized advice, rather than recommending a choice.

**Citation format within the guide itself:** Inline citations to primary sources should carry the URL (matching the research file's URLs) either as a footnote-style reference or an inline parenthetical, consistently applied guide-wide. The drafting model should pull the exact URL from `custody_guide_research.md` rather than inventing or reformatting one.

**Templates:** Each template should be presented in three parts: (1) a short explainer of when/why to use it and what rule/form it corresponds to, (2) the blank fill-in version with bracketed placeholder text, (3) a realistic (fictionalized, non-identifying) worked example. Where the template corresponds to an actual SCAO or Genesee County form (e.g., FOC 65, FOC 87, FOC 68, MC 20, CC 375), the guide should point to the official form/link rather than recreating a competing version, and should frame the guide's own template as a *drafting aid* for the narrative/attachment portions (e.g., an attached brief, an affidavit, an exhibit list) that these official forms often require but do not themselves generate.

**Tone toward the opposing party:** Neutral, factual, court-facing language throughout — the guide should model the register the reader should use in their own filings (avoid editorializing labels; focus on documented conduct and its relevance to specific best-interest factors).

## 8. Drafting Instructions for Claude Opus (General, apply to every module)

1. **Work section by section from `custody_guide_outline.md`.** Each outline entry specifies learning objectives, planned templates/examples, planned exercises, and relevant glossary terms — treat these as a contract for that section's contents, not a mere suggestion.
2. **Pull every legal claim from `custody_guide_research.md` first.** If a fact needed for drafting is not in the research file, or is present but marked `[UNVERIFIED]`, do one of: (a) perform a fresh, targeted verification search/fetch against a primary source (legislature.mi.gov, courts.michigan.gov, geneseecountymi.gov, 7thcircuitcourt.com) before writing the claim as settled fact, or (b) if verification is not feasible in the drafting pass, keep the flag visible in the drafted text using the **VERIFY BEFORE RELYING** callout format from Section 7, rather than silently smoothing it into confident prose.
3. **Never state a fee, phone number, address, judge's name, or form number without a citation.** These are exactly the class of facts most likely to be stale by the time a reader uses the guide; treat currency-sensitive facts differently from stable legal-standard facts (e.g., the *Vodvarka* holding is stable; the FOC's phone number is not).
4. **Do not draft new case law summaries from memory.** Use only the case summaries and holdings already verified in the research file's Section 4, and if a new case is needed for a module not covered there (e.g., a specific alienation-adjacent case), perform a fresh search rather than relying on training-data recall, since Michigan family case law is exactly the kind of niche, jurisdiction-specific content prone to hallucination.
5. **Maintain the ICLE-mirroring numbering scheme** (Lesson 1/1.1–1.3, Lesson 2/2.1–2.5, Lesson 3/3.1–3.4) for the core three lessons, and use clearly distinct numbering (e.g., Lesson 4, Lesson 5, Appendix A/B/C) for the new pro se-specific material, so a reader (or a future editor) can always see which parts map onto the original ICLE architecture and which are original additions.
6. **Cross-reference, do not duplicate.** When a concept recurs across modules (e.g., the custody journal, the best-interest factors, the ECE standard), draft it fully once at its primary location (per the outline's designation) and reference it by section number elsewhere, to keep the treatise internally consistent and to avoid drift if the primary explanation is later revised.
7. **Insert disclaimers per the Section 6 plan at every specified trigger point** — front matter, lesson openers, high-stakes actions, safety contexts, and template captions — rather than treating disclaimers as a one-time task.
8. **Write every exercise with a clear completion criterion.** The reader should be able to tell when they are "done" with an exercise (e.g., "you have completed this checklist when every box is checked and you have made copies of every checked item").
9. **Populate the glossary definitions in a final consolidated pass** (the outline provides only the term list; the drafting model should write definitions once all modules using each term are drafted, ensuring the definition matches how the term was actually used and cited in context, then place definitions alphabetically in the glossary appendix).
10. **Flag scope boundaries explicitly.** When a reader's fact pattern would fall outside this guide's scope (e.g., they need to defend against a criminal PPO respondent case, or need appellate help), say so plainly and redirect to Section 4's out-of-scope list and Section 10's resources, rather than silently omitting guidance.
11. **Use the exercise-type taxonomy from Section 5** consistently; if a module seems to need a new exercise type, add it but note the addition so the overall pedagogy document stays synchronized.
12. **Preserve neutrality and safety framing** per Section 7's tone guidance — this is not a place for the drafting model to advocate a specific litigation outcome; it teaches the reader how the system works and how to use it competently and safely.

## 9. Per-Module Drafting Depth Guidance

To keep the eventual treatise usably long rather than sprawling, target rough depth per module type (guidance, not a hard cap):

- **Core procedural modules** (e.g., 2.1 Discovery, 2.3 Motions/temporary orders, 3.2 Trial prep): full explanatory text + at least one template + at least one checklist + one worked example. These are the backbone chapters and should be the most complete.
- **Conceptual/legal-standard modules** (e.g., ECE/burden of proof, Vodvarka standard): full explanatory text with verbatim quoted standards, a self-assessment/diagnostic exercise, and a scenario exercise; templates are less central here.
- **High-conflict/alienation modules**: full explanatory text, heavy emphasis on documentation templates (custody journal, evidence matrix) and red-flag-spotting exercises, with careful, non-diagnostic framing (the guide should not have the reader attempt to clinically diagnose the other parent; it should map behavior to admissible-evidence categories).
- **Resource/appendix modules** (SCAO forms directory, glossary, resource list): reference-style, lower narrative density, organized for lookup rather than linear reading.
