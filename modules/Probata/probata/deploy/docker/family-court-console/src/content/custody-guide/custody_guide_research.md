### 1.2 MCL 722.23 — Best Interest of the Child Factors (verbatim, a–l)
Full verbatim text confirmed via Michigan Legislature search-result snippet, cross-checked against [Michigan Legal Help's plain-language breakdown](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/family/best-interests-of-child-factors) and law-firm summaries. Source: [MCL 722.23, Michigan Legislature](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-23).

> As used in this act, "best interests of the child" means the sum total of the following factors to be considered, evaluated, and determined by the court:
> (a) The love, affection, and other emotional ties existing between the parties involved and the child.
> (b) The capacity and disposition of the parties involved to give the child love, affection, and guidance and to continue the education and raising of the child in his or her religion or creed, if any.
> (c) The capacity and disposition of the parties involved to provide the child with food, clothing, medical care or other remedial care recognized and permitted under the laws of this state in place of medical care, and other material needs.
> (d) The length of time the child has lived in a stable, satisfactory environment, and the desirability of maintaining continuity.
> (e) The permanence, as a family unit, of the existing or proposed custodial home or homes.
> (f) The moral fitness of the parties involved.
> (g) The mental and physical health of the parties involved.
> (h) The home, school, and community record of the child.
> (i) The reasonable preference of the child, if the court considers the child to be of sufficient age to express preference.
> (j) The willingness and ability of each of the parties to facilitate and encourage a close and continuing parent-child relationship between the child and the other parent or the child and the parents. A court may not consider negatively for the purposes of this factor any reasonable action taken by a parent to protect a child or that parent from sexual assault or domestic violence by the child's other parent.
> (k) Domestic violence, regardless of whether the violence was directed against or witnessed by the child.
> (l) Any other factor considered by the court to be relevant to a particular child custody dispute.

Practical note for pro se litigants (source: [Michigan Legal Help, "The Best Interests of the Child Factors"](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/family/best-interests-of-child-factors)): the judge must consider and make findings on **every** factor but need not weigh them equally; factor (j)'s carve-out (protective actions against DV/sexual assault cannot count against a parent) is directly relevant to abuse-related fact patterns.

### 1.3 MCL 722.24 — Lawyer-Guardian Ad Litem (LGAL)
Source: [MCL 722.24, Michigan Legislature](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-24). The court may appoint an LGAL for the child if it believes the child's best interests are not being adequately represented. Per [WomensLaw.org's Michigan custody summary](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/mi/custody/basic-info-and-definitions/what-lawyer-guardian-ad-litem) (citing MCL 722.24(2)–(4)): the LGAL represents the child (not either parent), may submit a written report/recommendation that is *not* automatically admitted into evidence (admission requires all-party agreement), and parents may be required to pay part or all of the LGAL's fees.

### 1.4 MCL 722.26a — Joint Custody

"Joint custody" can mean joint legal custody, joint physical custody, or both. **[VERIFY exact subsection text against current legislature.mi.gov before drafting — page was robots-blocked for direct fetch; only search snippet and secondary sources retrieved.]**

### 1.5 MCL 722.27 — Powers of the Circuit Court; Modification Standard
Verbatim excerpt confirmed via [WomensLaw.org's mirror of MCL 722.27](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/mi/statutes/72227-powers-circuit-court-child-custody-dispute-enforcement-judgment-or-order):
> (1)(a) Award custody ... and provide for payment of support ...
> (1)(b) Provide for reasonable parenting time ...
> (1)(c) Subject to subsection (3), modify or amend its previous judgments or orders for **proper cause shown or because of change of circumstances** until the child reaches 18 ...

The established-custodial-environment (ECE) clause, quoted directly from a Court of Appeals opinion citing the statute (source: [Ludema v Ludema opinion, michbar.org](https://www.michbar.org/file/opinions/appeals/2003/011403/17689.pdf)):
> "The court shall not modify or amend its previous judgments or orders or issue a new order so as to change the established custodial environment of a child unless there is presented clear and convincing evidence that it is in the best interest of the child. The custodial environment of a child is established if over an appreciable time the child naturally looks to the custodian in that environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort. The age of the child, the physical environment, and the inclination of the custodian and the child as to permanency of the relationship shall also be considered."

**Burden of proof summary (for the outline's ECE/burdens module):**
- No ECE exists, or ECE is with the party seeking the change: **preponderance of the evidence** standard applies to the best-interest analysis.
- ECE exists with the non-moving party (or is disputed) and the requested change would alter it: **clear and convincing evidence** standard applies.
- Threshold showing before any best-interest hearing occurs: **proper cause or change of circumstances** (see Vodvarka, below) — a preponderance standard applies to this threshold showing itself, per general Michigan case law discussion in secondary sources; **[CONFIRM threshold burden standard directly against Vodvarka text before drafting]**.

### 1.6 MCL 722.27a — Parenting Time
Full text of subsections (1)–(3) confirmed via [Justia mirror of MCL 722.27a](https://law.justia.com/codes/michigan/2006/mcl-chap722/mcl-722-27a.html):
> (1) Parenting time shall be granted in accordance with the best interests of the child. It is presumed to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents...
> (2) If the parents ... agree on parenting time terms, the court shall order the parenting time terms unless the court determines on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that the parenting time terms are not in the best interests of the child.
> (3) A child has a right to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health.

Note: MCL 722.27a(4) addresses parenting time limitations where a child was conceived through certain criminal sexual conduct — relevant to abuse fact patterns; confirm exact renumbering/current subsections at [Chapter 722, Michigan Legislature](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/Index?ObjectName=mcl-chap722) since the statute has been amended multiple times, most recently regarding the domestic-violence protective-action carve-out.

Michigan Bar Journal discusses whether there is a "formula" for parenting time (there is no rigid formula; it is best-interest-driven with FOC guidelines as advisory tools): [Michigan Bar Journal, "Is there a formula for parenting time?"](https://www.michbar.org/journal/Details/Is-there-a-formula-for-parenting-time?ArticleID=4566). The FOC Parenting Time Guideline (advisory, not mandatory) is published by SCAO: [2000 Parenting Time Guideline PDF](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a4a99/siteassets/court-administration/standardsguidelines/archived-standards-and-guidelines/focb/2000pt_gdlns.pdf) — **[flag: confirm whether a newer edition has superseded the 2000 version before citing in the drafted guide]**.

## 2. Friend of the Court Act (MCL 552.501 et seq.)

Source: [MCL Chapter 552, Act 294 of 1982, Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/michigan/2014/chapter-552/statute-act-294-of-1982/section-552.522/) and [MCL 552.501, Michigan Legislature listing](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-552-501). The Friend of the Court (FOC) is a statutorily created office within each circuit court's family division, supervised by the chief judge, that investigates and makes recommendations on custody/parenting time/support, helps parties settle disputes, and provides enforcement services. Confirmed function description: [Michigan Legal Help, "Genesee County Friend of Court"](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/courts-and-agencies/genesee-county-friend-of-court).

### 2.1 Opt-Out of FOC Services — MCL 552.505a
Michigan law requires the FOC to open/maintain a case for each domestic relations matter unless the parties jointly opt out (source: [Shiawassee County FOC, "Opting In/Opting Out"](https://shiawassee.net/friend-of-the-court/opting-in-opting-out/)). Parties who opt out assume full responsibility for administering and enforcing their own custody/parenting time/support arrangements. Genesee County's own opt-out packet confirms the process and required documents: [Genesee County FOC Opt-Out Packet](https://cms7files.revize.com/genessecountymi/Document_Center/Courts%20and%20Law%20Enforcement/Friend%20of%20the%20Court/Online%20Forms/Pro%20Per/Opt%20out%20packet.pdf) — filing fee **$20** if filed as a motion in an existing case; filed with Circuit Court Records, **not** at the FOC office. **[VERIFY exact MCL 552.505a subsection text directly from legislature.mi.gov before drafting — only secondary/local sources retrieved.]**

### 2.2 FOC Mediation Programs
Two distinct mediation pathways exist for Genesee County domestic relations cases per the [Genesee County FOC Handbook (2022)](https://cms7files.revize.com/genessecountymi/New%20Handbook%202022.pdf):
- **FOC Domestic Relations Mediation (MCR 3.224):** FOC-offered mediation for custody/parenting time disputes; confidential except violent/criminal-activity disclosures; barred if domestic violence is discovered unless the victim consents in writing and safety precautions are taken; the same mediator cannot later serve as referee/enforcer on that case.
- **Court Rule Mediation (MCR 3.216):** Broader domestic-relations mediation ordered by the court or agreed to by parties, not limited to custody/parenting time; confidentiality governed by MCR 2.412(D); mediator entitled to a reasonable fee typically split by the parties.
- Genesee County's FOC has "limited mediation services" but partners with the **Community Resolution Center** (the county's CDRP center) for free/low-cost mediation (same handbook source).

### 3.2 2025 Michigan Child Support Formula Manual — Key Concepts
Primary source: [2025 Michigan Child Support Formula Manual, courts.michigan.gov](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a7a53/siteassets/court-administration/standardsguidelines/foc/2025mcsf.pdf) (effective January 2025 per [ICLE community blog announcement](https://community.icle.org/blogs/lindsey-a-dicesare/2024/09/30/2025-michigan-child-support-formula-effective-janu)).

Core calculation steps (Chapter 2 "Determining Income" and Chapter 3 "Calculating Each Parent's Obligation"):
1. **Determine each parent's net income** — includes wages, self-employment, bonuses, and imputed income where applicable; excludes/limits certain benefit income; "Low Income Threshold" concept excludes below-threshold income from the family-income combination in some calculations.
2. **Combine parents' net incomes** into monthly family income; base support amount is derived from that combined figure and the number of children.
3. **Apply the Parental Time Offset (MCSF §3.03)** — support is adjusted based on the percentage of annual overnights each parent has with the child, reflecting that a parent who has the child more nights is presumed to already be covering more day-to-day costs. Source: [2025 MCSF Chapter 3](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/court-administration/standardsguidelines/foc/2025-child-support-formula-manual-responsive-html5.zip/2025_Child_Support_Formula_Manual/Ch_3/Ch_3.htm) (fetch blocked directly by robots.txt; content captured via search snippet — **confirm exact percentage brackets before drafting**).
4. **Medical/health-care obligation (MCSF §3.04)** — ordinary medical expense (OME) allocation; 2025 manual made changes here (see Major Changes list below).
5. **Deviation from the Formula (MCSF §1.04)** — the manual lists **20 deviation factors** a judge may cite to deviate from the formula amount if it would be "unjust or inappropriate"; the 2025 manual clarified that a deviation is *not mandatory* even where a factor exists, and the list of factors is *not exhaustive*. Source: [Michigan Legal Help, "Child Support in a Nutshell"](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/family/child-support-nutshell) and [WomensLaw.org, "Can a child support order ever be different..."](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/mi/child-support/can-child-support-order-ever-be-different-what-michigan-child-support-formula). **[Full enumerated list of the 20 factors was not captured verbatim in this research pass — pull the complete §1.04(E) list directly from the 2025 MCSF PDF before drafting the deviation-factors appendix.]**
6. **2025 Major Changes** (source: [2025 MCSF "Major Changes" page](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/court-administration/standardsguidelines/foc/2025-child-support-formula-manual-responsive-html5.zip/2025_Child_Support_Formula_Manual/Major_Changes/Major_Changes.htm)): clarified §1.04(E) deviation is discretionary and the factor list is non-exhaustive; removed at least two prior deviation factors (e.g., alternative health care — confirm exact list); changes to Ordinary Medical Expense (OME) treatment when both parents have qualifying assistance.

## 4. Established Custodial Environment, Burdens of Proof, and Proper Cause / Change of Circumstances

### 4.1 Vodvarka v Grasmeyer — the controlling threshold case
**Citation:** *Vodvarka v Grasmeyer*, 259 Mich App 499; 675 NW2d 847 (2003), confirmed via [vLex case record](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/vodvarka-v-grasmeyer-docket-893147210) and cross-referenced against multiple Court of Appeals opinions quoting it, e.g. [Miller v Johnson opinion, courts.michigan.gov](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49d7c6/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20170808_c336083_47_336083.opn.pdf) and a Grand Traverse County FOC memo: [gtcountymi.gov PDF](https://www.gtcountymi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/230).

**Holding/rule (quoted from a Court of Appeals opinion citing Vodvarka, 259 Mich App at 507–508):**
> "[A] movant must prove that, since the entry of the last custody order, the conditions surrounding custody of the child, which have or could have a significant effect on the child's well-being, have materially changed" to establish "change of circumstances," and normal life changes (age, income fluctuation, etc.) generally do not suffice. "Proper cause" requires "one or more appropriate grounds that have or could have a significant effect on the child's life to the extent that a reevaluation of the child's custodial situation should be undertaken." [Full text source: [Paul Scott Associates memo, "From Vodvarka to Shade and Everything In Between"](https://www.paulscottassociates.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Rifkin-Shade-v.-Vodvarka.pdf)]

Vodvarka is the threshold gatekeeper: **before** a court will even hold a best-interest hearing to modify a custody order, the moving party must first show proper cause or a change of circumstances by a preponderance of the evidence. This applies a **higher bar to custody modifications** than to parenting-time modifications.

### 4.2 Shade v Wright — lower threshold for parenting-time-only modifications
**Citation:** *Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17; 805 NW2d 1 (2010), confirmed via [vLex case record](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/shade-v-wright-docket-892098710) and quoted in [Sutariya v Sutariya, Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/court-of-appeals-unpublished/2020/345115.html): "Generally, a trial court may only modify previous orders regarding parenting time and custody 'for proper cause shown or because of [a] change in circumstances...' Shade v Wright, 291 Mich App 17..." Shade held that the Vodvarka "change of circumstances" standard is appropriately applied to **custody** modification requests but that a **lesser showing** suffices for parenting-time-only modification requests that do not alter the established custodial environment. **[Pull the exact Shade holding language directly from the full published opinion — [Shade v Wright opinion PDF, courts.michigan.gov](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49d995/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20101202_c296318_33_178o-296318-final.pdf) — before drafting, since this research pass relied on secondary quotations.]**

### 4.3 Fletcher v Fletcher — standards of review; best-interest factor weighting
**Citation:** *Fletcher v Fletcher*, 447 Mich 871; 526 NW2d 889 (1994), full text confirmed via [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1994/97232-6.html). The Michigan Supreme Court clarified the three-tiered standard of appellate review in custody cases: (1) findings of fact reviewed against the great weight of the evidence; (2) discretionary rulings (e.g., custody decision itself) reviewed for abuse of discretion; (3) questions of law reviewed for clear legal error. The Court also confirmed that a trial judge need not give equal weight to each MCL 722.23 best-interest factor.

### 4.4 Baker v Baker — best-interest factors as sum total, not simple majority
**Citation:** *Baker v Baker*, 411 Mich 567; 309 NW2d 532 (1981), confirmed via [Justia full opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/1981/63660-2.html) and cited repeatedly in later briefs, e.g., [Michigan Supreme Court case brief, 165279](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a824d/siteassets/case-documents/briefs/msc/2022-2023/165279/165279_57_01_ae_supp_brf.pdf) ("Baker v Baker, 411 Mich 567, 577; 309 NW2d 532 (1981)"). Baker holds that custody should not be changed except in the most compelling cases, reinforcing the stability policy behind the ECE doctrine and the proper-cause/change-of-circumstances threshold.

### 4.5 Pierron v Pierron — ECE and "important decisions" (school changes, relocation-adjacent)
**Citation:** *Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81; 782 NW2d 480 (2010), confirmed via [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/pierron-v-pierron-docket-890869213) and summarized in [Casemine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/pierron-v.-pierron:-defining-the-modification-of-established-custodial-environments-in-child-custody-cases/view). Holding: a proposed school change 60 miles away did **not**, on these facts, modify the established custodial environment, and the trial court need not walk through every MCL 722.23 factor when a factor is not relevant to the specific decision at issue (e.g., a school-change dispute). The Michigan Supreme Court affirmed application of the standard the Court of Appeals used (preponderance where ECE not modified).

### 4.6 Foskett v Foskett — joint custody / cooperation requirement
**Citation:** *Foskett v Foskett*, 247 Mich App 1; 634 NW2d 363 (2001), confirmed via [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/foskett-v-foskett-docket-891725829). Relevant to joint-legal-custody disputes: courts examine whether parents can cooperate and generally agree on important child-related decisions; inability to cooperate can defeat joint legal custody even where each parent is individually fit. Referenced alongside a parallel case ("Appeals Court Vacates Sole Legal Custody To Father" — [Speaker Law Firm blog](https://www.speakerlaw.com/blog/appeals-court-vacates-sole-legal-custody-to-father)) discussing the flip side (erroneously granting sole legal custody despite evidence of cooperative capacity).

### 4.8 McIntosh v McIntosh — psychological evaluations and trial court's independent best-interest determination
**Citation:** *McIntosh v McIntosh*, 282 Mich App 471; 768 NW2d 325 (2009) (year/citation confirmed via [Quimbee case brief](https://www.quimbee.com/cases/mcintosh-v-mcintosh); full reporter citation "768 N.W.2d 325 (2009)" — **confirm the Mich App volume number, e.g., 282 Mich App, directly against a primary reporter before final drafting**). Facts: a court-ordered neutral psychological evaluation recommended joint custody; the trial court weighed evidence of alcohol use, unjustified withholding of the child, and a violent incident, and **declined to follow the evaluator's recommendation**, instead awarding sole legal and physical custody to the mother. Significance for the guide: confirms Michigan trial courts are **not bound** by a psychological evaluator's custody recommendation and must make their own findings on the MCL 722.23 factors — highly relevant to the parental-alienation/expert-witness module.

### 4.9 Bofysil v Bofysil — a working parent and the established custodial environment
**Citation:** *Bofysil v Bofysil*, 332 Mich App 232; 956 NW2d 544 (2020), confirmed via [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/bofysil-v-bofysil-no-893507823) and [Referees Association of Michigan case-law update](https://www.referees-association.org/new-blog-style-publication/domestic-caselaw-update5467837). Holding, per secondary source [Legal News](https://legalnews.com/macomb/1488957): the fact that a parent works outside the home to support the family should **not** be held against that parent when determining the established custodial environment. Directly useful for pro se parents facing an "absentee working parent" narrative from an opposing party.

### 4.13 De Novo Review of Referee Recommendations — MCL 552.507 and MCR 3.215
- **MCL 552.507** governs the right to a judicial (de novo) hearing after an FOC referee's recommended order; referenced directly on SCAO Form FOC 68 instructions: "Pursuant to MCL 552.507(4) and MCR 3.215, if you object to the Referee Recommendation and Order, you have a right to a judicial hearing. To request a judicial hearing, you must file objections within 21 days..." Source: [Justia-hosted county form mirror](https://formfiles.justia.com/pdf/michigan/0375/77.pdf) and confirmed independently via [Michigan Legal Help, "Friend of Court Overview"](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/family/friend-of-court-overview) ("If you disagree with the recommendation, you can file an objection within 21 days from the date of service...").

### 6.3 Referee Hearing Process (Genesee County specifics)
Per the county's own Custody Motion Packet instructions: after a hearing, the case is "heard by a Referee," who issues a "Recommendation for an Order," mailed to each party. **The recommendation becomes a court order automatically unless a party files an objection within 21 days after the recommendation was mailed.** A party may request a judicial (de novo) hearing by completing an "Objection to Referee's Recommended Order" form (Genesee-specific blank forms available at the clerk's office for **50 cents**) within the 21-day window; the objecting party must then serve the objection and Notice of Hearing per MCR 2.119(C). Source: [Genesee County Custody Motion Packet](https://cms7files.revize.com/genessecountymi/Document_Center/Courts%20and%20Law%20Enforcement/Friend%20of%20the%20Court/Online%20Forms/Pro%20Per/fillable%20Custody%20motion.pdf).

## 8. Domestic Violence, PPOs, and MCL 722.23(k)

- **MCL 722.23(k)** makes "domestic violence, regardless of whether the violence was directed against or witnessed by the child," an explicit, mandatory best-interest factor (see Section 1.2 above).
- **MCL 722.23(j)**'s protective-action carve-out (a parent cannot be penalized in the best-interest analysis for reasonable protective action against sexual assault or domestic violence) is directly relevant to false-allegation defense and to protecting a parent who limits contact for safety reasons.
- **Personal Protection Orders (PPOs)**: A **Domestic Relationship PPO** can be sought against someone with whom the petitioner has a qualifying relationship (spouse, former spouse, co-parent, dating partner, etc.), using **Form CC 375**. Source: [Michigan Legal Help, "Domestic Relationship Personal Protection Orders"](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/personal-safety/domestic-relationship-personal-protection-orders).
- **Interaction between PPOs and existing custody/parenting-time orders**: "A PPO takes precedence over any existing custody or parenting time order until the PPO has expired, or the court having jurisdiction over the custody or parenting time order modifies the custody or parenting time order to accommodate [the PPO]." Source: [Michigan Judicial Institute Domestic Violence Benchbook, Ch. 5](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/dvbb/dvbbresponsivehtml5.zip/DVBB/Ch_5_Special_Orders/Issuing_a_PPO_in_Domestic_Relations_Proceedings.htm). Additionally, MCL 600.2950(1)(l) authorizes a PPO to affect custody/parenting time **without** a separate MCL 722.23 best-interest analysis at the PPO-issuance stage — source: same Benchbook, [Ch. 8, Effect of Domestic Abuse on Child Custody Determinations](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/dvbb/dvbbresponsivehtml5.zip/DVBB/Ch_8_Effects/Effect_of_Domestic_Abuse_on_Child_Custody_Determinations.htm).
- **Genesee-specific PPO filing assistance**: the YWCA Greater Flint assists with PPO filings on-site at the courthouse (see Section 6.6).

## 9. Lawyer-Guardian Ad Litem, Psychological Evaluations, and Parenting-Communication Tools

- **LGAL appointment and role** — see Section 1.3 above (MCL 722.24). Additional detail: an LGAL's duty runs to the child's best interests, not to either parent or to the court, and the LGAL may be paid by the parties. Sources: [MCL 722.24, Michigan Legislature](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-24); [WomensLaw.org](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/mi/custody/basic-info-and-definitions/what-lawyer-guardian-ad-litem). (Note: LGAL appointment in **child-protective proceedings** under MCL 712A is mandatory and distinct from the **discretionary** custody-dispute LGAL under MCL 722.24 — do not conflate the two frameworks; the drafting model should clearly separate "custody-case LGAL" from "child-protective-proceeding LGAL." See [MJI Child Protective Proceedings Benchbook, LGAL chapter](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a26a2/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/cpp/cppresponsivehtml5.zip/CPP/Ch_7_Preliminary_Steps/Lawyer-Guardian_Ad_Litem_L-GAL-.htm) for the CPS-context rule, which should NOT be presented as identical to the custody-case rule.)
- **Court-ordered psychological evaluations**: governed procedurally in part by **MCR 3.219** (dissemination of professional reports) — see Section 6, MCR table. *McIntosh v McIntosh* (Section 4.8) confirms the trial court is not bound by an evaluator's recommendation. **[Pull the full current text of MCR 3.219 and any MCR provision specifically authorizing court-ordered evaluations before drafting — this pass located only secondary discussion, e.g., a family-law practice blog: [labrelaw.com, "What to Expect from a Court-Ordered Psychological Evaluation..."](https://labrelaw.com/what-to-expect-from-a-court-ordered-psychological-evaluation-for-a-child-custody-case-in-michigan/), which is not a primary source and must be corroborated against the actual rule text.]**
- **OurFamilyWizard / co-parenting communication platforms**: not created or mandated by Michigan statute; used as a **court-ordered or agreed-upon tool** in high-conflict cases, typically memorialized via a model order. Sample model order language is published by the vendor: [OurFamilyWizard Model Order Language for Courts](https://www.ourfamilywizard.com/practitioners/model-order-language) and [full model order template](https://www.ourfamilywizard.com/sites/default/files/inline-files/us-modelordertemplate-ofw.pdf). Local Michigan county examples of adoption include Manistee County: [manisteecountymi.gov/420/Our-Family-Wizard](https://manisteecountymi.gov/420/Our-Family-Wizard). **[No Genesee-County-specific OurFamilyWizard order or policy was located in this pass — confirm whether Genesee 7th Circuit judges have a standard local order/preference before drafting a Genesee-specific template; absent that, present the vendor model order as a generic template the reader can propose.]**

## 10. Limited-Scope Representation and Free/Low-Cost Help

- **MCR 2.117(B)** (not MCR 6.101, and not a probate-only rule) is the correct current citation for limited-scope/limited appearance by an attorney in a civil action, alongside companion rule **MCR 2.107** and Michigan Rules of Professional Conduct 1.0, 1.2, 4.2, 4.3. Source: [State Bar of Michigan, "New Limited Scope Representation Rules Effective January 1"](https://www.michbar.org/news/newsdetail/New-Limited-Scope-Representation-Rules-Effective-January-1?nid=5507); implementing SCAO benchbook excerpt: [Stages of Limited Scope Representation, courts.michigan.gov](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4ae0a5/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/qrms/civil/pretrial-issues/stages-of-limited-scope-representation.pdf). This lets a pro se parent hire an attorney for a **discrete task** (e.g., drafting a motion, appearing at one hearing, "ghostwriting" a brief) without full-scope representation — directly relevant to a "how to afford some help" module. Note: **MCR 5.117** is the separate, analogous rule for **probate court** appearances — [Casetext text of MCR 5.117](https://casetext.com/rule/michigan-court-rules/michigan-court-rules/chapter-5-probate-court/subchapter-5100-general-rules-of-pleading-and-practice/rule-5117-appearance-by-attorneys) — and should not be cited for a circuit-court family-division case.
- **Statewide and Genesee-specific free/low-cost resources** are compiled in Section 6.6 above (Michigan Legal Help, Genesee County Legal Resource Center, Legal Services of Eastern Michigan, State Bar Lawyer Referral Service).
