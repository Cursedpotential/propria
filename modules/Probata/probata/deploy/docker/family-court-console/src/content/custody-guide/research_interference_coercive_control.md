# Research Addendum — Interference, Coercive Control, and Weaponized Process

**Date:** August 9, 2026
**Purpose:** Michigan recognizes no freestanding "parental alienation" cause of action (verified — see `verification_ledger.md` §4). This addendum maps the legal channels Michigan *does* recognize that reach the same conduct, so the guide can plead behavior a court can actually act on.
**Status of statutory text below:** fetched verbatim from `legislature.mi.gov` this pass. The site banner reads "Michigan Compiled Laws Complete Through PA 2 of 2025."

> **Framing discipline.** "Coercive control" is **not** a Michigan cause of action either. It is a descriptive frame. Everything below is a *recognized statutory hook* — this addendum exists so the guide never asks a court to adopt a theory Michigan law does not have. The reader pleads **conduct and its statutory consequence**, never a syndrome or a label.

---

## 1. Why the reframe is necessary, not cosmetic

A published-opinion search returns only four Michigan appellate opinions using the phrase "parental alienation," none establishing it as a claim. A reader who files a motion titled "Motion re: Parental Alienation" is asking for relief under a theory the court cannot grant, and — per every council report — is heard as the high-conflict party.

The same facts, routed through the channels below, ask for relief the court grants routinely.

---

## 2. Channel A — Best-interest factor (j), and its built-in limit

**MCL 722.23(j), verbatim:**

> "(j) The willingness and ability of each of the parties to facilitate and encourage a close and continuing parent-child relationship between the child and the other parent or the child and the parents. **A court may not consider negatively for the purposes of this factor any reasonable action taken by a parent to protect a child or that parent from sexual assault or domestic violence by the child's other parent.**"

Two things the guide must teach together:

1. **The first sentence is the primary hook.** Interference with the other parent's relationship is not a side issue — it is a named statutory best-interest factor.
2. **The second sentence is a hard limit** (added by 2016 PA 95, eff. Aug 1, 2016) and it **cuts against a reader who is wrong about the facts.** If the conduct the reader calls "alienation" was in fact reasonable protective action against sexual assault or domestic violence, factor (j) **cannot** be scored against that parent. A reader building a factor (j) case must first honestly audit whether the other parent has a protective-action explanation — which is precisely the outline's **Acceptance Test 9 (adversarial-fairness)**, and the single most likely way a pro se factor (j) argument collapses.

**MCL 722.23(k)** is the companion: "Domestic violence, regardless of whether the violence was directed against or witnessed by the child."

## 3. Channel B — The separate parenting-time factor list

The outline correctly notes MCL 722.23 is not the only list. **MCL 722.27a(7)** governs *frequency, duration, and type* of parenting time. Verified verbatim; the operative subsections for interference:

| Sub | Text | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **(c)** | "The reasonable likelihood of abuse or neglect of the child during parenting time." | Direct child-safety hook |
| **(d)** | "The reasonable likelihood of **abuse of a parent** resulting from the exercise of parenting time." | Reaches parent-directed abuse occurring at exchanges — the classic coercive-control pressure point |
| **(f)** | "Whether a parent **can reasonably be expected to exercise parenting time in accordance with the court order**." | The compliance hook |
| **(g)** | "Whether a parent has **frequently failed to exercise** reasonable parenting time." | The failure-to-exercise hook |
| **(h)** | "The **threatened or actual detention of the child with the intent to retain or conceal the child** from the other parent or from a third person who has legal custody. A custodial parent's temporary residence with the child in a domestic violence shelter shall not be construed as evidence of the custodial parent's intent to retain or conceal." | The concealment hook — **and it carries its own protective carve-out**, same two-way logic as factor (j) |

The council identified (f) and (g). **(d) and (h) are additional and more on-point for the conduct this guide addresses**, and (h) is the one that most directly describes withholding a child.

Two framing provisions worth quoting to a court:

- **MCL 722.27a(1):** "It is **presumed** to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents."
- **MCL 722.27a(3):** "A child **has a right** to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health."

That is a presumption plus a clear-and-convincing burden placed on the party seeking to curtail contact — the strongest statutory language in the guide for a parent being cut out.

## 4. Channel C — Enforcement under the Support and Parenting Time Enforcement Act

**MCL 552.641** (verified verbatim) — what the FOC must and may do:

- **(1)** On a complaint alleging a custody or parenting-time order violation, the FOC **shall** do one or more of: (a) apply the makeup parenting time policy; (b) **commence civil contempt proceedings**; (c) **file a motion to modify parenting time to ensure parenting time**, unless contrary to the child's best interests; (d) schedule mediation; (e) schedule a joint meeting.
- **(2)** The FOC **may decline** to respond if:
  - (a) the complainant previously submitted **2 or more** complaints found unwarranted, costs were assessed, and **those costs are unpaid**;
  - (b) the violation occurred **more than 56 days** before the complaint is submitted;
  - (c) **the order "does not include an enforceable provision that is relevant to the … violation alleged."**

Three corrections/additions to how the package currently treats this:

1. **The 56-day rule is discretionary, not jurisdictional.** The statute says the FOC "may decline." The outline and council treat it as a hard bar. Teach it as: *assume it is fatal, because the FOC may treat it that way* — but do not tell the reader their right is extinguished.
2. **(2)(c) is the most important sentence in the enforcement module**, and it points backward at order drafting. A vague order ("reasonable parenting time as the parties agree") gives the FOC a lawful basis to decline to enforce anything. This is the strongest argument in the entire guide for the outline's **operational-order test (Acceptance Test 11)**: an order that cannot be placed on a calendar cannot be enforced.
3. **(2)(a) cuts against the reader.** A parent who files repeated unwarranted complaints can lose FOC access entirely. Adversarial-fairness content, not a footnote.

**MCL 552.642** (verified verbatim) — makeup parenting time:

- Same **type and duration** as what was denied — weekend for weekend, holiday for holiday, weekday for weekday, summer for summer.
- Must be taken **within 1 year** of the denied time.
- **The wrongfully denied parent chooses when.**
- Notice: **≥1 week** ahead for makeup weekend/weekday time; **≥28 days** ahead for makeup holiday/summer time.
- **A 21-day deemed-agreement rule.** The FOC notice must carry, in boldface 12-point type: *"FAILURE TO RESPOND IN WRITING TO THE OFFICE OF THE FRIEND OF THE COURT WITHIN 21 DAYS AFTER THIS NOTICE WAS SENT SHALL BE CONSIDERED AS AN AGREEMENT THAT PARENTING TIME WAS WRONGFULLY DENIED AND THAT THE MAKEUP PARENTING TIME POLICY … WILL BE APPLIED."*

**Correction to the first draft of this addendum.** The outline **does** carry these four periods — Module 26.4 lists all of them and notes "all four periods belong in Appendix A." The gap is narrower than first stated: the clock is absent from the **FM.0 triage router**, and Appendix A is still a stub. The genuine addition here is the **trigger**: the 21 days run from the date the FOC notice **was sent**, not from receipt — a distinction the outline's own **Acceptance Test 3** requires be stated explicitly, and which Module 26.4 does not yet state. It is simultaneously the denied parent's most efficient remedy and a severe trap for a parent who ignores FOC mail.

The same applies to §10 below: Module 26.5 already carries the MCL 552.644 remedy list, the escalating bad-faith sanctions, and the MCL 552.645 license-suspension window. What §10 adds is the subsection **(3)** requirement that the court state on the record why it is *not* imposing a listed sanction, and the statutory definition of "good cause" as including party and child safety.

## 5. Channel D — Build the order so interference is self-proving

**MCL 722.27a(9)** is a statutory menu of conditions "that facilitate the orderly and meaningful exercise of parenting time." Verified verbatim, the ones that matter for a high-conflict case:

| Sub | Condition |
|---|---|
| (a)/(b) | Division of responsibility, and of cost, for transporting the child |
| (c) | **Restrictions on the presence of third persons** during parenting time |
| (d) | Child **ready at a specific time** |
| (e) | Parent **arrives and returns the child at specific times** |
| (f) | Parenting time **in the presence of a third person or agency** (supervised) |
| (g) | **A party posts a bond to assure compliance** |
| (h) | **Reasonable notice when parenting time will not occur** |
| (i) | Any other reasonable condition |

(g) — a compliance bond — is a real, underused tool a pro se reader would never guess exists. (d), (e), and (h) convert "she was late again" from an unprovable grievance into a documented breach of a specific term, which is exactly what MCL 552.641(2)(c) requires before the FOC will act.

**The pedagogy this implies:** the interference module and the order-drafting module are one system. Ask for MCL 722.27a(9) terms *at the order stage* so that enforcement is mechanical later.

## 6. Channel E — Where Michigan law actually reaches coercive control

Michigan has no "coercive control" offence. It has three instruments whose text reaches non-physical, pattern-based control.

### 6.1 The domestic violence definition — non-physical by its terms

**MCL 400.1501(d)** (Domestic Violence Prevention and Treatment Act; amended by **2023 PA 182, eff. Feb 13, 2024**), verbatim — "domestic violence" means any of the following acts **that is not an act of self-defense**:

> "(i) Causing or attempting to cause physical or mental harm to a family or household member.
> (ii) Placing a family or household member in fear of physical or mental harm.
> (iii) Causing or attempting to cause a family or household member to engage in involuntary sexual activity by force, threat of force, or duress.
> **(iv) Engaging in activity toward a family or household member that would cause a reasonable individual to feel terrorized, frightened, intimidated, threatened, harassed, or molested.**"

**(d)(iv) is the coercive-control hook** — no physical contact required, judged by a reasonable-individual standard. And **"family or household member" expressly includes "an individual with whom the person has a child in common"** (MCL 400.1501(e)(vi)) — so it covers co-parents who never married or cohabited.

This matters because **MCL 722.23(k)** makes domestic violence a best-interest factor without defining it narrowly.

### 6.2 Stalking — the "course of conduct" architecture

**MCL 750.411h** (amended by **2023 PA 199, eff. Feb 13, 2024** — the same effective date as the DV act amendment; these were a coordinated package). Verified verbatim:

- **"Course of conduct"** = "a pattern of conduct composed of a series of **2 or more separate noncontinuous acts** evidencing a continuity of purpose."
- **"Stalking"** = a willful course of conduct involving repeated or continuing harassment "that would cause a reasonable person to feel **terrorized, frightened, intimidated, threatened, harassed, or molested** and that actually causes the victim to feel" so.
- **"Harassment" excludes "constitutionally protected activity or conduct that serves a legitimate purpose."** ← the limiter. Ordinary co-parenting communication, however unwelcome, serves a legitimate purpose.
- **(2)(c):** where the parties are spouses/former spouses, have or had a dating relationship, **have a child in common**, or share(d) a household — misdemeanor, up to 1 year.
- **(4) A rebuttable presumption:** continuing a course of unconsented contact *after the victim has asked it to stop* raises a presumption that the continuation caused the victim to feel terrorized, frightened, intimidated, threatened, harassed, or molested.

**Note the drafting link:** MCL 400.1501(d)(iv) and MCL 750.411h(1)(e) use the *identical* six-word standard — "terrorized, frightened, intimidated, threatened, harassed, or molested." The civil DV definition tracks the criminal stalking standard. That is a genuine doctrinal bridge, and it means documentation built for one is usable for the other.

### 6.3 The PPO conduct list — a coercive-control menu

**MCL 600.2950(1)** lets the family division restrain a spouse, former spouse, **person with a child in common**, dating partner, or (former) household member from, among other things:

| Sub | Conduct | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **(d)** | "**Removing minor children from the individual having legal custody** … except as otherwise authorized by a custody or parenting time order" | Direct interference hook |
| **(f)** | Interfering with efforts to remove children or property from the respondent's premises | |
| **(g)** | Interfering at the petitioner's workplace or school, or "engaging in conduct that **impairs petitioner's employment or educational relationship or environment**" | Economic/institutional coercion |
| **(i)** | Accessing records about the parties' child that would reveal the petitioner's or child's **address, phone number, or the petitioner's work address** | Information-based control; pairs with confidential-address planning |
| **(j)** | Conduct prohibited under **MCL 750.411h / 411i** (stalking, aggravated stalking) | Imports the course-of-conduct architecture above |
| **(k)** | Harming or taking an animal "with the intent to cause the petitioner mental distress or **to exert control over the petitioner**" | The statute uses the words "exert control" |
| **(l)** | "**Any other specific act or conduct that imposes upon or interferes with personal liberty** or that causes a reasonable apprehension of violence" | The broadest hook — "imposes upon or interferes with personal liberty" is where non-enumerated coercive control lands |

Three structural provisions the guide must carry:

- **MCL 600.2950(6): a court "shall not refuse to issue" a PPO solely because of the absence of a police report, a medical report, an administrative finding, or "physical signs of abuse or violence."** This is the provision that makes a coercive-control petition viable at all.
- **MCL 600.2950(8): "A court shall not issue a mutual personal protection order."** Correlative separate orders require both parties to have properly petitioned. Counters a common retaliatory move.
- **MCL 600.2950(12)–(14):** an ex parte PPO issues on a showing of immediate and irreparable injury; it is **valid not less than 182 days**; the restrained party must move to modify or rescind **within 14 days** of service or actual notice absent good cause; and the court schedules that hearing **within 14 days** of filing (**5 days** where a firearm prohibition affects a respondent who must carry as a condition of employment).

> **Scope boundary.** Per `custody_guide_plan.md` §4, PPO *respondent* defense is out of scope. This section supports a reader **seeking** protection or explaining an existing PPO's interaction with custody orders. A reader who is a respondent needs counsel — outline §0.4 hard stop.

## 7. Channel F — Weaponized process

Litigation itself as the instrument of control. Michigan's hooks:

- **MCL 600.2591** (verified verbatim). On motion, if a court finds a civil action or defense **frivolous**, it **shall** award costs and fees to the prevailing party, assessed against the nonprevailing party **and their attorney**. "Frivolous" means at least one of:
  > "(i) **The party's primary purpose in initiating the action or asserting the defense was to harass, embarrass, or injure the prevailing party.**
  > (ii) The party had no reasonable basis to believe that the facts underlying that party's legal position were in fact true.
  > (iii) The party's legal position was devoid of arguable legal merit."

  **(3)(a)(i) is the statutory name for weaponized filing.** Note "prevailing party" is defined narrowly — "a party who wins on the entire record."
- **MCR 3.215(F)(3)** — a frivolous referee objection, or one interposed for delay, may draw costs and attorney fees.
- **MCR 3.206(D)(2)(b)** — fees where the other party **refused to comply with a previous court order despite having the ability to comply**, or engaged in discovery practices violating the rules. (Note the corrected subrule number — see `verification_ledger.md` C-4.) This is the provision that can fund limited-scope counsel for the next stage.
- **MCL 552.641(2)(a)** — the mirror image: a parent who files repeated unwarranted FOC complaints and leaves assessed costs unpaid can lose FOC responsiveness.

**Teach these as a matched pair.** Every one of them can be aimed at the reader.

## 8. The constraint this domain imposes on the guide

Every hook above is symmetrical. Factor (j) has a protective-action limit. MCL 722.27a(7)(h) has a DV-shelter carve-out. Stalking excludes "conduct that serves a legitimate purpose." MCL 600.2591 and MCL 552.641(2)(a) punish over-filing. A parent who reads this material as a menu of attacks will lose, and the guide will have caused it.

The drafting rule that follows: **each channel in the treatise must be drafted with its own limiter in the same section, not in a distant caveat** — satisfying Acceptance Test 9 structurally rather than by disclaimer.

Banned from templates regardless: "narcissist," "alienator," "PAS," "coercive control" as a pleaded label. The reader pleads **dates, denials, specific order terms breached, and the statutory factor**.

## 10. Channel G — Contempt: the teeth behind "intentional violation"

**MCL 552.644** (verified verbatim) is the enforcement engine, and it is far stronger than the outline suggests.

**The finding is mandatory.** "(2) If the court finds that either parent has violated a parenting time order **without good cause**, the court **shall** find that parent in contempt" — and may then order one or more of:

| Sub | Sanction |
|---|---|
| (a) | Additional terms and conditions consistent with the parenting time order |
| (b) | After notice and hearing if requested, **modify the parenting time order to meet the child's best interests** |
| (c) | **Makeup parenting time** for the wrongfully denied parent |
| (d) | Fine of not more than $100 |
| (e)/(f) | **Commit the parent to jail** or an alternative — with or without work-release privileges |
| (g) | **Condition suspension of an occupational, driver's, or recreational/sporting license** on noncompliance with an order for makeup and ongoing parenting time |
| (h) | Community corrections program |
| (i) | **FOC supervision** with conditions — parenting program, drug/alcohol counseling, work program, seeking employment, other counseling, continued compliance, arrearage plan, **facilitating makeup parenting time** |

Four provisions a pro se reader would never find:

1. **MCL 552.644(3): "The court shall state on the record the reason the court is not ordering a sanction listed in subsection (2)."** A court that finds contempt and does nothing must say why, on the record. That is a preserved appellate issue handed to the reader by statute.
2. **"Good cause" is defined inclusively and safety-first:** "'good cause' includes, but is not limited to, consideration of the safety of a child or party who is governed by the parenting time order." The other parent's safety defense is built into the statute — another symmetry the reader must anticipate.
3. **Commitment caps:** not more than **45 days** for a first finding, **90 days** for each subsequent finding; and the parent must be released "if the court has reasonable cause to believe that the parent will comply."
4. **Bad-faith sanctions run both ways** — **(6)** escalating sanctions of not more than **$250 / $500 / $1,000** for first, second, and third-or-subsequent bad-faith findings, and **(8)** "If the court finds that a party to a parenting time dispute has acted in bad faith, the court **shall** order the party to pay the other party's costs."

Also: **MCL 552.644(1)(b)** — the contempt notice must state the parent's right to a hearing on a proposed **modification of parenting time if requested within 21 days after the date of the notice**. Another 21-day clock, triggered by *notice date*, missing from the outline's Appendix A.

## 11. Channel H — Agreed parenting arrangements, and the consent trap

The user-facing question — *what happens when the parties agree?* — has a two-sided answer, and the second side is where cases are lost.

### 11.1 Agreement is powerful

| Provision | Effect of agreement |
|---|---|
| **MCL 722.26a(2)** | "If the parents **agree on joint custody**, the court **shall** award joint custody unless the court determines on the record, based upon **clear and convincing evidence**, that joint custody is not in the best interests of the child." |
| **MCL 722.27a(2)** | "If the parents of a child **agree on parenting time terms**, the court **shall** order the parenting time terms unless the court determines on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that the terms are not in the best interests of the child." |

An agreed parenting plan therefore arrives with a presumption and shifts a clear-and-convincing burden onto anyone — including the court — who would depart from it. This is the strongest argument for negotiating hard and reducing everything to specific written terms.

### 11.2 Agreement is also how rights are lost

Michigan domestic-relations practice is built on consent, and each consent point is a place a pro se litigant signs away something they did not know they had. Verified consent gates:

| Gate | What consent does |
|---|---|
| **MCL 722.27(1)(c)** — the ECE clause | "The custodial environment of a child is **established if over an appreciable time the child naturally looks to the custodian** in that environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort." **The clause says nothing about court orders.** An ECE is a *factual* condition. Agreeing to a "just for now" arrangement in which the child primarily lives with the other parent can, over an appreciable time, create an ECE **with that parent** — which the agreeing parent must later overcome by **clear and convincing evidence** to change. This is the trap Opus put on page one, and the statutory text confirms it: *Hayes v Hayes*, 209 Mich App 385; 532 NW2d 190 (1995). |
| **MCR 3.207(C)(2)** | "A temporary order may not be issued without a hearing, **unless the parties agree otherwise** or fail to file a written objection or motion." Consent replaces the hearing. |
| **MCR 3.215(E)(8)** | "The parties may **waive their right to object** to the referee's recommendation for an order by consenting in writing to the immediate entry of the recommended order." Signing the recommended order forfeits the de novo hearing. |
| **MRE 1101(b)(9)** + *Duperon* | The FOC report is exempt from the evidence rules for the court's consideration, but is inadmissible absent both parties' agreement. **Stipulating it in** converts a document that is not evidence into evidence — for or against. |
| Evaluation appointment orders | Opus's finding: appellate authority has found hearsay objections waived where the stipulated appointment order itself waived them. *Still unverified — see §12.* |
| **MCL 552.505a(2)** | Moving, with initial pleadings, for no FOC case to be opened forfeits FOC enforcement services — including the parenting-time enforcement machinery in §§10 and 4 above. |

**The drafting rule this implies:** every template in the treatise that a reader might *sign* — not just those they file — needs a "what you are giving up by signing this" block. That is a new template component the outline's 12-point contract does not currently require, and it should be added as item 9(b).

## 12. Channel I — Behavior patterns → statutory factor → admissible proof

The user's shorthand is understood, and the translation discipline is exactly the point: **a court is not persuaded by a personality description; it is persuaded by dated conduct tied to a factor and proved by an admissible exhibit.** Clinical vocabulary in a filing shifts the court's attention to the filer.

This mapping table is the reframe the guide needs, replacing any diagnostic framing. Each row: observable conduct → the statutory hook verified above → the admissibility route.

| Observable conduct | Statutory hook | Proof route |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule changed unilaterally and repeatedly; exchanges missed or moved | MCL 722.27a(7)(f); MCL 552.641; MCL 552.644(2) | The order's own specific terms + a dated denial log + the FOC complaint (within 56 days) |
| Child not produced; whereabouts withheld | MCL 722.27a(7)(**h**) (detention with intent to retain or conceal) | Dated log; exchange-location witness; police "civil standby" report if one exists |
| Reader's calls/messages to the child blocked or monitored | MCL 722.23(**j**) (failure to facilitate) | OFW/message-platform export, authenticated by MRE 901 live testimony — **no Michigan self-authentication route exists for these** (MRE 902 stops at (11)) |
| Reader cut out of school, medical, activity information | MCL 722.23(j); joint **legal** custody decision-making under MCL 722.26a(7)(b) | Records obtained **directly from the school/provider** — third-party business records, which *can* ride MRE 902(11) certification with pretrial notice |
| Disparagement to the child; child repeating adult content | MCL 722.23(j) | **Not** via the child's statements — MRE 803A is criminal/delinquency only. Legitimate routes: MRE 803(3) state of mind, the **in camera** interview (MRE 1101(b)(6)), the LGAL, and **MRE 801(d)(2)** admissions by the other parent |
| Messages designed to provoke, intimidate, or exhaust | MCL 400.1501(d)(iv); MCL 750.411h course of conduct; MCL 600.2950(1)(l) | The messages themselves — volume and pattern matter more than any single one; 2+ noncontinuous acts establish "course of conduct" |
| Filings/complaints used to drain and harass | **MCL 600.2591(3)(a)(i)**; MCR 3.215(F)(3); MCL 552.641(2)(a) | The docket itself; prior unwarranted-complaint findings and unpaid cost assessments |
| Repeated unfounded CPS/police referrals | MCL 722.23(j) and (l); CPS **Category V** (false or erroneous information) | CPS disposition letters — *Category V and the three-unfounded-reports FOC notification provision remain unverified; see §12* |
| Money used as leverage; support withheld | MCL 722.23(c); **MCR 3.206(D)(2)(b)** | Payment records; the prior order allegedly violated |

**Three rules govern every row.** (1) The conduct must breach a **specific term** of an order, or the FOC may lawfully decline to act — MCL 552.641(2)(c). (2) Every digital exhibit needs a **sponsoring witness** under MRE 901. (3) Each row's limiter travels with it: protective action defeats the (j) inference; safety is "good cause" under MCL 552.644(3); "legitimate purpose" defeats harassment under MCL 750.411h(1)(d).
