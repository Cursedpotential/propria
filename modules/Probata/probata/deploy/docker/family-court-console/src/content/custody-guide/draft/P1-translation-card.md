## The governing rule — pattern first, and even then: effect, not mind

Words like the ones in the left column below are **claims about the other parent's mind.** A mind
cannot be entered into evidence. A calendar can.

The discipline has three steps, and most people stop one step too late:

1. **Plead facts.** Dates, requests, responses, non-responses. No adjectives.
2. **Prove the pattern.** The log, the letters, the green cards, laid end to end. The pattern is
   established by exhibits, not asserted by you.
3. **Argue the *effect*.** Once the pattern is in evidence, you may characterize **what the conduct
   has done** — never **why the person did it.** "Since [date], the provision has produced no
   parenting time" is effect. "She is doing this to erase me" is motive. The first is proven by
   your exhibits; the second cannot be proven by anything, and saying it invites the fight to move
   from her calendar (where you win) to her mind (where nobody wins).

**Proving the pattern does not unlock motive language. It replaces the need for it.** A judge or
referee who is handed twenty-two dated requests and zero overnights will draw the inference — that
is their job, and an inference the factfinder draws is worth more than a conclusion the movant
declaims.

**The one place "willingness" legitimately enters:** best-interest factor (j) is, verbatim, "[t]he
willingness and ability of each of the parties to facilitate and encourage a close and continuing
parent-child relationship…" (MCL 722.23(j), VERIFIED). The statute's own word does the work. You
prove conduct; the word "willingness" is already in the factor the court applies. You never have to
say it about her — you say the dates, and cite the factor. And factor (j)'s limiter travels with
it: reasonable protective action against sexual assault or domestic violence cannot be counted
under this factor. Do not build the case on (j); build it on the request record and let (j) be
supporting material.

**Where intent actually is an element, it is not your burden.** Criminal custodial interference
requires intent — and it is a *prosecutor's* charge, never a line in your motion. Civil contempt
under the SPTEA runs on violation of a **specific order term**, not on motive (and safety is "good
cause" against sanctions — MCL 552.644(3)). You carry no burden to prove why. Do not volunteer one.

**Formatting habits that keep you on the right side of the line:**

- **One dated act per sentence.** A sentence with two accusations and no date is argument; a
  sentence with one act and one date is evidence waiting for its exhibit.
- **Quote, don't paraphrase, the other side's words.** Their words are the one category of paper
  that is not hearsay when you offer it (MRE 801(d)(2)).
- **Numbers over adverbs.** "Repeatedly" is an adverb; "[n] times: [dates]" is a count. Every
  "always/never/constantly" in a draft should be replaced by a number or deleted.
- **Read it as her lawyer.** If a sentence would let opposing counsel say *"the pleadings speak to
  my client's character rather than to any provision of the order,"* rewrite it until it speaks to
  a date, a term, or an exhibit.

## Effect-language bank — permitted characterizations

These characterize **conduct and consequence**, never mind or motive, and each must sit on top of
exhibits already described:

- "The provision has produced no parenting time since [date]."
- "The practical effect of routing scheduling through counsel has been [n] requests and [n]
  parenting-time sessions in [n] months."
- "The pattern spans [n] months and [n] written requests."
- "No alternative date has been proposed in any response."
- "The minor child has not seen [parent] since [date]."
- "Each request proposed specific dates, times, and a public exchange location; none occurred."
