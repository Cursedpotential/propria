# MCL 722.23 Best Interest Factors: Reference & Structured Mapping

Michigan courts determine custody based on these 12 factors. When invoked to analyze a case, systematically map provided facts to ALL 12 factors. Never skip a factor — if facts are insufficient, flag it as a gap.

---

## PART 1: FACTOR REFERENCE & DOCUMENTATION GUIDES

### Factor (a): Love, Affection, and Emotional Ties

**What Courts Look For:**
- Quality of parent-child bond
- Emotional attunement
- Affection and warmth
- Understanding of child's needs

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Daily routines that show connection (bedtime, meals, activities)
- Special traditions or bonding activities
- How you comfort children when upset
- Knowledge of children's friends, interests, fears, hopes
- Photos/videos of positive interactions
- Children's artwork, cards, expressions of love

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Emotional unavailability
- Lack of knowledge about children's lives
- Using children for emotional support (parentification)
- Conditional love based on children's compliance
- Inability to attune to children's emotional needs
- Children's fear or anxiety around them

---

### Factor (b): Capacity and Disposition to Provide Love, Affection, and Guidance

**What Courts Look For:**
- Ability to meet emotional needs
- Providing appropriate discipline and structure
- Teaching values and life skills
- Emotional stability to guide children

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- How you handle discipline (consistent, age-appropriate)
- Teaching moments (helping with homework, life skills)
- Emotional regulation (staying calm under stress)
- Encouraging children's growth and independence
- Supporting children's interests and education

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Harsh, inconsistent, or absent discipline
- Inability to regulate own emotions
- Explosive anger or emotional volatility
- Lack of guidance or structure
- Undermining children's confidence
- Parentification or role reversal

---

### Factor (c): Capacity to Provide Material Needs

**What Courts Look For:**
- Food, clothing, shelter
- Medical and dental care
- Educational needs
- Extracurricular activities
- Stable housing

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Stable housing appropriate for children
- Consistent provision of meals, clothing
- Attendance at medical/dental appointments
- Support for education (supplies, involvement)
- Financial stability and planning

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Unstable housing (frequent moves, unsafe conditions)
- Failure to provide basic needs during parenting time
- Missing medical appointments
- Not paying child support
- Spending money on self while children lack necessities

---

### Factor (d): Length of Time in Stable, Satisfactory Environment

**What Courts Look For:**
- Continuity and stability
- Established routines
- Community connections
- Avoiding unnecessary disruption

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- How long children have lived with you
- Stability of home, school, community
- Established routines and consistency
- Children's comfort and security in environment

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Frequent moves
- Unstable living situations
- Disruption of children's routines
- Lack of established home for children

**Note:** Courts favor maintaining stability. If children have been primarily with you, this factor weighs in your favor.

---

### Factor (e): Permanence of Proposed Custodial Home

**What Courts Look For:**
- Likelihood of stability going forward
- Commitment to remaining in area
- Secure housing situation
- Long-term planning for children

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Commitment to staying in area
- Secure housing (own or long-term lease)
- Established community ties
- Long-term planning for children's needs

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Plans to move
- Unstable housing
- Transient lifestyle
- Lack of long-term planning

---

### Factor (f): Moral Fitness of the Parties

**What Courts Look For:**
- Character and values
- Honesty and integrity
- Appropriate behavior
- Modeling good citizenship

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Community involvement
- Volunteer work, church involvement
- Honest and ethical behavior
- Positive role modeling

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Criminal history
- Dishonesty (lying to court, children)
- Inappropriate behavior (affairs, substance abuse)
- Exposing children to inappropriate situations
- Violating court orders

**Note:** This is NOT about judging lifestyle choices. It's about behavior that impacts children.

---

### Factor (g): Mental and Physical Health of the Parties

**What Courts Look For:**
- Ability to care for children
- Management of health conditions
- Impact of health on parenting
- Substance abuse issues

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Good physical health or well-managed conditions
- Mental health treatment if needed (shows responsibility)
- Ability to meet children's physical demands
- No substance abuse

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Untreated mental health conditions
- Substance abuse (alcohol, drugs)
- Physical health issues that impair parenting
- Refusal to get treatment
- Impact on children (fear, having to care for parent)

**Important:**
- Being in therapy is a STRENGTH, not a weakness
- Courts care about UNTREATED conditions, not seeking help
- Document specific impact on children

---

### Factor (h): Child's Home, School, and Community Record

**What Courts Look For:**
- School performance and attendance
- Extracurricular involvement
- Community connections
- Social development

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Children's school attendance and performance under your care
- Your involvement in school (conferences, volunteering)
- Support for extracurriculars
- Children's friendships and community ties
- Stability in these areas

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Children missing school during their parenting time
- Lack of involvement in education
- Disruption of activities
- Isolation from friends/community
- Decline in performance correlated with their involvement

---

### Factor (i): Child's Reasonable Preference

**What Courts Look For:**
- Child's stated preference (if old enough)
- Reasons for preference
- Whether preference is based on manipulation
- Child's maturity and reasoning

**Important Considerations:**
- Courts consider this more heavily for older children (typically 12+)
- Preference must be "reasonable" (based on legitimate factors)
- Courts look for coaching or manipulation
- Children should NEVER be put in the middle

**Document Appropriately:**
- If child expresses preference, note it factually
- Note the reasons child gives (their words)
- NEVER coach or pressure children
- Let therapist or GAL elicit preferences
- Focus on children's comfort and security, not loyalty

**If Other Parent Is Coaching:**
- Document sudden changes in child's statements
- Note use of adult language or concepts
- Show pattern of alienation
- Let professionals identify manipulation

---

### Factor (j): Willingness to Facilitate Relationship with Other Parent

**What Courts Look For:**
- Encouraging relationship with other parent
- Following parenting time orders
- Sharing information
- Not badmouthing other parent
- Supporting children's love for both parents

**Document YOUR Strengths:**
- Following parenting time schedule
- Encouraging children to call/contact other parent
- Sharing school, medical information
- Being flexible when reasonable
- Not speaking negatively about other parent to children
- Supporting children's relationship even when difficult

**Document OTHER PARENT'S Deficits:**
- Interfering with parenting time
- Badmouthing you to children
- Withholding information
- Refusing to communicate
- Alienating behaviors
- Using children as messengers or spies

**CRITICAL:** This is often used against protective parents. You must show:
- You support the relationship when SAFE
- You follow court orders
- Your concerns are about SAFETY, not control
- Other parent's behavior is the barrier, not you

---

### Factor (k): Domestic Violence

**What Courts Look For:**
- History of domestic violence
- Impact on children (witnessing or experiencing)
- Pattern of abuse
- Current safety concerns

**Types of Domestic Violence:**
- Physical abuse
- Sexual abuse
- Emotional/psychological abuse
- Coercive control
- Stalking
- Economic abuse

**Document:**
- Police reports
- Personal Protection Orders (PPOs)
- Medical records from injuries
- Photos of injuries
- Witness statements
- Pattern of escalation
- Children witnessing violence
- Impact on children (fear, trauma symptoms)

**Important:**
- Emotional abuse and coercive control ARE domestic violence
- Courts are increasingly recognizing psychological abuse
- Post-separation abuse counts (stalking, harassment, litigation abuse)
- Children witnessing abuse is harmful even if not directed at them

---

### Factor (l): Any Other Relevant Factor

**This is the catch-all factor. Use it for:**
- Substance abuse (detailed documentation)
- Parental alienation
- Mental health concerns not covered in (g)
- Special needs of children
- Cultural or religious considerations
- Sibling relationships
- Extended family support
- Work schedules and availability
- History of caregiving (who did what before separation)
- New partners and their impact on children
- Relocation concerns
- Any pattern of behavior affecting children's best interests

## PART 2: STRUCTURED MAPPING WORKFLOW

When invoked for case analysis, systematically map the provided case facts to ALL 12 best interest factors. Never skip a factor — if facts are insufficient, flag it as a gap.

### Mapping Template

For each factor, provide:

1. **Factor text** (verbatim from statute)
2. **Parent A evidence** (strengths + weaknesses)
3. **Parent B evidence** (strengths + weaknesses)
4. **Rating**: Strong A | Lean A | Neutral | Lean B | Strong B
5. **Key case law** supporting the analysis
6. **Strategic note** on how to strengthen or defend

```
## Factor (a): Love, Affection, Emotional Ties
**Statute**: "The love, affection, and other emotional ties existing between the parties involved and the child."

**Parent A**:
- Strengths: [evidence]
- Weaknesses: [evidence]
- Defense for weaknesses: [strategy]

**Parent B**:
- Strengths: [evidence]
- Weaknesses: [evidence]
- Attack vectors: [strategy]

**Rating**: [rating]
**Key Authority**: [case cite]
**Strategic Note**: [recommendation]
```

### All 12 Factors (Statutory Text)

(a) Love, affection, emotional ties
(b) Capacity to give love, affection, guidance, continuation of education/religion
(c) Capacity to provide food, clothing, medical care, material needs
(d) Length of time in stable, satisfactory environment; desirability of maintaining continuity
(e) Permanence as a family unit of existing or proposed custodial home
(f) Moral fitness of the parties
(g) Mental and physical health of the parties
(h) Home, school, and community record of the child
(i) Reasonable preference of the child (if old enough)
(j) Willingness and ability to facilitate a close and continuing parent-child relationship with the other parent (except DV)
(k) Domestic violence regardless of directed at child
(l) Any other factor considered by the court to be relevant

### After Mapping: Summary Matrix

Provide a **Summary Matrix** showing all 12 factors at a glance:

```
| Factor | Parent A | Parent B | Rating | Key Evidence |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|-------------|
| (a)    |          |          |        |              |
| (b)    |          |          |        |              |
| ...    |          |          |        |              |
| (l)    |          |          |        |              |
```

Then identify:
- **Strongest factors** for Parent A (top 3)
- **Most vulnerable factors** for Parent A (top 3)
- **Factors needing more evidence**
- **Recommended motion strategy** based on factor balance
