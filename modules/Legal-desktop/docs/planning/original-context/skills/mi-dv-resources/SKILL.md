# Michigan Domestic Violence & Psychological Abuse Resources

DV statutes, PPO procedures, coercive control, psychological abuse recognition, DV-custody intersection under MCL 722.23(k), and Genesee County DV resources.

## DV Statutes

### Criminal DV Offenses

**MCL 750.81 — Domestic Assault**
- Assault or battery against person in domestic relationship
- **1st offense**: Misdemeanor, up to 93 days jail and/or $500 fine
- **2nd offense**: Misdemeanor, up to 1 year jail and/or $1,000 fine
- **3rd+ offense**: Felony, up to 5 years prison
- Assault = placing someone in imminent fear of battery (no injury required)

**MCL 750.81a — Aggravated Domestic Assault**
- Domestic assault causing serious injury or involving a weapon
- **1st offense**: Up to 1 year jail / $1,000 fine (misdemeanor) OR up to 5 years / $5,000 (felony)
- **2nd+ offense**: Automatic felony, up to 5 years prison
- Any object used as weapon (gun, household item) elevates charge

**MCL 750.411h — Stalking**
- Willful course of conduct involving repeated unconsented contact
- Causes victim to feel terrorized, frightened, intimidated, threatened, harassed, or molested
- Misdemeanor: Up to 1 year jail and/or $1,000 fine

**MCL 750.411i — Aggravated Stalking**
- Stalking plus: credible threat, violation of PPO/bond, prior stalking conviction, or stalking a minor
- Felony: Up to 5 years prison and/or $10,000 fine

### Domestic Relationships Defined (MCL 750.81)
- Spouse or former spouse
- Dating partner or former dating partner
- Person with child in common
- Resident or former resident of same household

### What Constitutes DV Under Michigan Law
Michigan recognizes these forms of domestic violence:

**Physical abuse**: Pushing, shoving, kicking, slapping, biting, strangling, hitting, punching, using weapons, throwing objects, physical force preventing departure, abandonment in dangerous situation

**Sexual abuse**: Forced sexual contact or acts, threats of sexual violence, non-consensual sexual activity

**Emotional/Psychological abuse**: Threats to harm victim/family/pets, ridicule, manipulation with lies and contradictions, convincing victim they are to blame, stalking, isolation, degradation

**Financial abuse**: Controlling finances, preventing access to money, sabotaging employment

## Personal Protection Orders (PPO)

### Types

| Type | Statute | Relationship Required | Covers |
|------|---------|----------------------|--------|
| Domestic PPO | MCL 600.2950 | Domestic relationship | Assault, threats, stalking, harassment by domestic partner |
| Stalking PPO | MCL 600.2950a | No relationship required | Stalking, harassment by anyone |

### Filing Process

**Ex Parte (Emergency)**:
1. File petition with circuit court (SCAO forms CC 375 or CC 376)
2. Judge reviews petition without hearing
3. If granted: Immediate protection, served on respondent
4. Respondent can request hearing to contest within 14 days
5. If not contested: PPO remains in effect

**After Hearing**:
1. If respondent contests, hearing scheduled
2. Petitioner must present evidence of abuse/stalking
3. Judge decides whether to continue, modify, or dismiss PPO
4. If continued: PPO remains in effect for duration set by judge

### Key SCAO Forms

| Form | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| CC 375 | Petition for PPO (Domestic Relationship) |
| CC 376 | Petition for PPO (Non-Domestic Stalking) |
| CC 377 | Petition for PPO (Sexual Assault) |
| CC 380 | Personal Protection Order (Domestic) |
| CC 381 | Personal Protection Order (Non-Domestic Stalking) |
| CC 382 | Personal Protection Order (Sexual Assault) |
| CC 386 | Motion to Extend, Modify, or Terminate PPO |

### PPO Violations
- Violation of a PPO is a criminal offense
- **1st violation**: Misdemeanor, up to 93 days jail and/or $500 fine
- **2nd+ violation**: Misdemeanor, up to 1 year jail and/or $500 fine
- **Violation involving assault/threat**: Can be charged as separate criminal offense
- Law enforcement SHALL arrest if PPO violation observed

### PPO and Custody Impact
- PPO is relevant evidence under MCL 722.23(k) — domestic violence factor
- PPO does not automatically change custody, but courts consider it
- PPO can restrict respondent's contact with children if specified
- PPO can affect parenting time (supervised exchanges, no-contact provisions)
- Filing for PPO is a "reasonable action to protect" under Factor (j) — cannot be held against filer

## DV and Custody Intersection

### MCL 722.23(k) — Domestic Violence Factor
- Courts MUST consider domestic violence in best interest analysis
- DV includes violence directed at OR witnessed by the child
- Applies regardless of whether DV resulted in criminal charges
- Emotional abuse, coercive control, and stalking qualify as DV

### MCL 722.27a — DV Considerations in Custody/Parenting Time
- If court finds DV history, must consider safety of child and victim
- Court may order:
  - Supervised parenting time
  - Safe exchange locations
  - No overnight parenting time
  - Completion of batterer intervention program
  - Drug/alcohol testing
- Court SHALL NOT order joint custody if DV is established (rebuttable)

### Factor (j) Protection
MCL 722.23(j) explicitly provides: *"A court may not consider negatively for the purposes of this factor any reasonable action taken by a parent to protect a child or that parent from sexual assault or domestic violence by the child's other parent."*

This means:
- Filing a PPO cannot be used against you
- Refusing to send children to an abusive parent is protected
- Reporting DV to police/CPS is protected
- Setting safety boundaries is protected

### How Courts Weigh DV in Custody

**Strong indicators (favor protective parent):**
- Criminal DV convictions
- Active or prior PPO
- Police reports documenting DV incidents
- Medical records of injuries
- Witness testimony
- Children's statements about witnessing DV
- Pattern of escalating behavior

**What courts look for:**
- Pattern vs. isolated incident
- Severity and frequency
- Impact on children (witnessing, fear, trauma symptoms)
- Whether perpetrator has sought treatment
- Current safety risk

## Psychological Abuse & Coercive Control

### Michigan Recognition
Michigan law recognizes emotional and psychological abuse as components of domestic violence. While Michigan does not have a standalone coercive control statute (as of 2026), courts increasingly recognize these patterns:

**Recognized forms of psychological abuse:**
- Threats to harm victim, family members, or pets
- Ridicule of beliefs, identity, heritage, religion
- Manipulation with lies and contradictions (gaslighting)
- Convincing victim they are to blame
- Isolation from support systems
- Monitoring and surveillance
- Economic control
- Using children as tools of control

### Presenting Psychological Abuse in Court

**Challenges:**
- No physical evidence (bruises, medical records)
- Abuser presents as calm, credible, reasonable
- Victim appears "emotional" or "unstable"
- Courts may dismiss as "he said/she said"

**Strategies:**
1. **Document the pattern** — Individual incidents seem minor; the pattern shows control
2. **Use FACT method** — Facts, Action, Context, Time (see documentation-methods skill)
3. **Preserve communications** — Texts, emails, voicemails showing manipulation
4. **Expert testimony** — DV expert can testify to coercive control patterns (MRE 702)
5. **Behavioral evidence** — Children's regression, fear, anxiety as observable impact
6. **Reframe for court** — Use court language, not clinical terms (see documentation-methods skill)

### Expert Testimony (MRE 702)
- Michigan uses the Daubert/Gilbert standard for expert testimony
- DV experts can testify about:
  - Coercive control dynamics
  - Why victims stay / trauma bonding
  - Impact on children of witnessing psychological abuse
  - Common abuser tactics (DARVO, gaslighting)
  - Behavioral patterns consistent with abuse
- Expert must be qualified (education, training, experience)
- Testimony must be based on reliable principles and methods

## Agent Protocol for DV in Custody Cases

1. **Safety first**: If immediate danger, direct to 911 and National DV Hotline (1-800-799-7233)
2. **Document the pattern**: Use FACT method, incident logs, pattern logs (documentation-methods skill)
3. **Map to best interest factors**: Factor (k) primarily, but DV affects (a), (b), (f), (g), (j), (l) too
4. **Cite statutes**: MCL 722.23(k), MCL 722.27a, MCL 600.2950
5. **Protect Factor (j)**: Remind user that protective actions cannot be held against them
6. **PPO if needed**: Guide through CC 375/376 filing process
7. **Reframe language**: Use court-appropriate language (documentation-methods skill reframing table)
8. **Expert testimony**: Consider DV expert for coercive control patterns (MRE 702)
9. **Genesee-specific**: File at 900 S. Saginaw St., use MiFILE for e-filing
10. **Never minimize**: Psychological abuse IS domestic violence under Michigan law
