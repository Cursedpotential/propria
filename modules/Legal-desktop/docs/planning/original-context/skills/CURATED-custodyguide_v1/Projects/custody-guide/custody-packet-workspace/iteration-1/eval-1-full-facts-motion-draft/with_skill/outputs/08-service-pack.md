# Part A — Who must be served

## The answer that is correct under every reading of the rules

**Serve the party. Also serve the attorney. Same papers, same day, both by first-class mail. Prove both on one proof of service.**

Here is why that is the safe answer, from the rules themselves:

- **After a final judgment or order, and after the appeal-of-right period has passed, documents "must be served on the party"** unless a postjudgment rule specifically allows attorney service — **MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c)** (VERIFIED verbatim). Your order is from March 15, 2023; the 21-day appeal-of-right period is long past.
- Postjudgment domestic-relations motions are governed by **MCR 2.119** (via **MCR 3.213**), which does **not** specifically allow attorney-only service. So the party must be served.
- **But copies of party notices must also go to the attorneys of record** — **MCR 3.203(I)(1)** — and that obligation runs "until postjudgment matters are concluded" (I)(2).
- Reading it the other way — attorney service under MCR 2.107(B)(1)'s opening clause — is **also** satisfied if you serve the attorney.

**Doing both satisfies every reading and violates none. The only cost is a second stamp.**

## Where to mail each copy

| Who | Address | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The party (the other parent)** | **PO Box 100, Flint, MI 48501** — the address of record with the Friend of the Court | The party's "last known mailing address" **is** the FOC address of record by rule — MCR 3.203(A)(2), (B). Mail service to it is valid **regardless of where the party actually lives.** |
| **The attorney of record** | **J. Marsh, 200 Main St, Flint, MI 48502** | MCR 2.107(B)(1); MCR 3.203(I)(1) |
| **The Friend of the Court** | One copy marked **"FRIEND OF THE COURT,"** handed to the **clerk** at filing (not mailed by you) | MCR 3.203(G) |

> **Register note.** If the other parent's true residence differs from the PO box, that is **not** something you argue or investigate. The rule makes the FOC address of record the service address by definition. In any paper, the neutral sentence is: *"Service was made to the address of record with the Friend of the Court (MCR 3.203)."* Never "she is hiding her address." If the address of record is ever formally confidential, ask the FOC for the service address — do not try to find it yourself.

## The communication restriction and service are two different things

Serving court papers is a step the rules require; it is not "contacting" the other parent in the sense the January 8, 2025 letter is aimed at. But **do not create the argument:**

- **Have a legally competent adult who is not you do the mailing,** and have that person sign the proof of service (MCR 2.107(D) requires the proof to be by the person who served — it need not be a party). This also keeps you at arm's length from any contact question.
- **Mail. Do not hand anything to the other parent yourself** — not at a door, not at an exchange, not through a relative.
- **The envelope contains the court papers and nothing else** — no note, no message.

# Part B — How to serve

- **First-class postage, fully prepaid, sealed envelope, U.S. mail.** That is the definition of service by mail — MCR 2.107(C)(3). **Certified mail is first-class mail with tracking added;** use certified with return receipt so you have proof, but the receipt is for you, not for the rule.
- **"Service by mail is complete at the time of mailing"** — MCR 2.107(C)(3), VERIFIED verbatim. Your service date is the day you drop it in the mail, not the day it arrives. That is what makes the 9-day mail deadline workable.
- There is a standing electronic-service directive in the rules (MCR 2.107(G)) that does not sit cleanly with the rest of the rule. **Do not rely on it.** Serve by mail and use the 9-day deadline; if you have an email for counsel, you may also email the same documents the same day as a courtesy and say so, but do not email the other parent directly. `⊗ CONFLICTED — resolved for you by serving the unambiguous way.`

# Part C — The two deadlines (meet both)

- **SERVICE — MCR 2.119(C)(1):** the motion, notice of hearing, brief, and supporting papers must be **served at least 9 days before the hearing if by first-class mail** (7 days if by personal delivery).
- **FILING — MCR 2.119(C)(4):** the motion must be **filed at least 7 days before the hearing.**

These are **two different obligations.** The county's older posted notice states only the filing rule; a parent who mails 7 days out is **two days late on service.** **File early, then serve the same day.**

## Worked backward calendar — example only

> The dates below are an **illustration** with a made-up hearing date, to show the method. Use your real hearing date once the court gives you one.

**Example hearing: Friday, ______ (a date the court assigns).** Count backward, not counting the hearing day:

| Count back | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 7 days | last day to **FILE** — MCR 2.119(C)(4) |
| 9 days | last day to **SERVE BY MAIL** — MCR 2.119(C)(1)(a) |

**Both are minimums, not targets.** Because the filing minimum (7) is shorter than the mail-service minimum (9), filing at the last legal moment makes on-time service impossible. **Pick a filing-and-serving day at least twice the minimum ahead** — roughly two-plus weeks before the hearing — and watch for weekends, holidays, and court closures near your deadlines (MCR 1.108 governs how days are counted; a deadline landing on a weekend/holiday runs to the next open day, but do not cut it that close).

# Part D — The proof of service (fill, then file)

> Drafting aid, not filing-ready. Use the court's own proof-of-service form if the clerk provides one; MCR 2.107(D) also allows the proof to be included at the end of a document. **Do not use it if** you did not personally do the mailing described — the person who did signs it.

     6.  Notice of Hearing

upon each of the following, by enclosing them in a sealed

shown, and depositing the envelope and its contents in the
United States mail at ______________, Michigan:

     [Other parent's name]
     PO Box 100
     Flint, MI 48501

     J. Marsh
     200 Main St
     Flint, MI 48502

     I declare under the penalties of perjury that this proof of
service has been examined by me and that its contents are true
to the best of my information, knowledge, and belief.

                               [Printed name of the person who
                                mailed the documents]

**Rules for completing it:**

- **List every document by name.** If the proposed order is not named, there is no paper record that you complied with Genesee LCR 2.119(B).
- **The date is the mailing date** (service by mail is complete at mailing).
- **The signer is the person who put it in the mail.** If a friend mailed it, the friend signs and gives their own address. The certified-mail receipts stay in **your** file — do not attach them to the proof.

# Part E — Filing, the FOC copy, fees, and the counter

- **File at the Circuit Court Records counter — not at the Friend of the Court office.** They are different offices. The FOC gets its copy from the clerk. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm the filing counter, location, and hours.`
- **Give the clerk one extra copy marked "FRIEND OF THE COURT"** at filing — MCR 3.203(G).
- **Formatting that gets a filing rejected:** 8½ × 11 paper, legible, English; body text 12 or 13 point; at least one-inch margins; combined motion + brief no more than 20 pages double-spaced, exclusive of exhibits (MCR 1.109(D)(1)(a); MCR 2.119(A)(2)). A clerk may reject nonconforming documents (MCR 1.109(D)(6)).
- **Motion fee:** one fee covers everything filed at the same time (MCR 2.119(G)(2)), and asking for a fee waiver is itself free (MCR 2.119(G)(3)(c)). The dollar amount is a local fact. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask the counter.`
- **If you cannot pay — the Fee Waiver Request (commonly SCAO form MC 20):** file it **with** the motion (MCR 2.002(B)). Means-tested benefits or a 125%-of-poverty income trigger a mandatory waiver (MCR 2.002(C), (F)). **The trap:** if a waiver is denied, you must pay within **14 days from the date the clerk sends notice, or the filing is rejected** and your hearing date goes with it (MCR 2.002(G)(1)). Put that 14-day date on a calendar the day you file. ⚠ TIME-CRITICAL. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — form number/revision; confirm with the clerk.`
- **Praecipe:** Genesee's local rules contain **no** praecipe requirement (VERIFIED — the only Genesee local rule is LCR 2.119, with subrules (A), (B), (C)). But local scheduling practice is not always written in the rules. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask: "Do I need a praecipe, and if so where is that written?"`

# Part F — Keep a service file

For the mailing, keep: a complete copy of the exact set mailed; the certified-mail receipt with tracking number; the return-receipt card or electronic delivery record; and a copy of the filed, date-stamped proof of service. **If service is ever challenged, this file is the answer — and it is the file that prevents a repeat of last time.**
