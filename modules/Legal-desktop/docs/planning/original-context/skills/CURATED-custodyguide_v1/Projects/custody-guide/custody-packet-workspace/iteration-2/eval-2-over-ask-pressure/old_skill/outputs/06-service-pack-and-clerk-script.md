## Part A — Who must be served

**The intuitive answer — "the other side has a lawyer, so serve the lawyer" — is not the
complete answer in a postjudgment case.**

> **MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c) (VERIFIED verbatim):** "After a final judgment or final order has
> been entered and the time for an appeal of right has passed, **documents must be served
> on the party** unless the rule governing the particular postjudgment procedure
> specifically allows service on the attorney."

The March 15, 2023 order is years old; the 21-day appeal-of-right period (MCR 7.204(A),
jurisdictional) has long passed. Postjudgment domestic-relations motions are governed by
MCR 2.119 (MCR 3.213), which does **not** specifically allow attorney-only service. So the
rule points to the **party**. Separately, MCR 3.203(I)(1) requires copies of party notices
to also go to the attorney of record.

> ### ✅ The instruction that survives every reading
> **Serve the party. Also serve the attorney. Same papers, same day, both by first-class
> mail. Prove both on one proof of service.** No rule is violated by an extra copy.

Service by mail goes to the **last known mailing address**, which by rule is the address of
record with the Friend of the Court — MCR 3.203(A)–(B) (VERIFIED). A stale or PO-box
address is the *other* party's compliance concern, not yours: serve the address of record
and mail service is valid on mailing.

| Who | What they get | Address | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The other party** | Full set (motion, brief, verified statement, proposed order, LCR 2.119(A) certificate, notice of hearing, Exhibit A) | **PO Box 100, Flint, MI 48501** (FOC address of record) | MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c); MCR 3.203(A)–(B); LCR 2.119(B) |
| **Attorney of record** | The same full set | **J. Marsh, 200 Main St, Flint, MI 48502** | MCR 2.107(B)(1); MCR 3.203(I)(1) |
| **Friend of the Court** | One copy marked "FRIEND OF THE COURT" | Handed to the **clerk** at filing | MCR 3.203(G) |

> **A note on the communication provision.** Your order says "the parties shall communicate
> regarding parenting time" — direct party contact. Serving the *motion* by mail on the
> party (and counsel) is a litigation step governed by the rules above, not
> parenting-time communication. If you prefer, have a friend do the physical mailing so you
> have no direct contact at all; the proof of service is signed by whoever mails it.

## Part B — The two clocks, worked

Two **separate** deadlines, both minimums:

> **MCR 2.119(C)(1) — SERVICE (VERIFIED):** a motion, notice of hearing, and supporting
> brief must be **served** "(a) at least **9 days** before the time set for the hearing, if
> served by first-class mail, or (b) at least **7 days** before, if served by delivery."
>
> **MCR 2.119(C)(4) — FILING (VERIFIED):** "a motion must be **filed** at least **7 days**
> before the hearing."

> ### ⚠ The county's own 2019 notice states only the 7-day FILING rule and is silent on
> the 9-day SERVICE rule. A parent who mails 7 days out, relying on that notice, is **two
> days late on service.** Both apply.

**Because the mail-service minimum (9 days) is longer than the filing minimum (7 days), you
cannot file at the last legal moment and still serve on time. File first, serve the same
day, well ahead.**

Worked example (fill in your real hearing date and check the weekdays against a calendar):

- Hearing date: **[H]**.
- Serve by first-class mail **at least 9 days** before **[H]** → serve on or before
  **[H − 9]**.
- File **at least 7 days** before **[H]** → file on or before **[H − 7]**.
- **Do both on the same early day.** If you file and serve, say, three weeks before **[H]**,
  both minimums are cleared with room to absorb a holiday or a rejected filing.

## Part C — Filing steps, in order

1. **Get a hearing date** from the court first (clerk-call item 2). You cannot notice a
   date you invented.
2. **Assemble the stack** (Part D). Confirm the proposed order is physically attached.
3. **File** at the Circuit Court Records counter `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm the filing
   location; it is not the FOC office`. Pay the motion fee, or file an **MC 20 Fee Waiver
   Request** with it.
4. **Serve** the same day — both envelopes, first-class mail, to the addresses in Part A.
5. **File the proof of service** promptly (MCR 2.107(D) permits it at the end of the
   document as filed).
6. **Calendar every downstream deadline** the day you file.

**Fee waiver timing, if used** (VERIFIED): a judge must grant or deny an MC 20 within **3
business days** (MCR 2.002(G)); **if denied, you must pay within 14 days from the date the
clerk sends notice, or the filing is rejected** (MCR 2.002(G)(1)) — and a rejected filing
takes your hearing date with it. Put that 14-day date on a calendar the day you file.

### After the hearing — the clocks that then start (calendar these the day they trigger)

- **7 days** to object to the accuracy/completeness of a *proposed recommended order* — MCR
  3.215(E)(3)(d) (VERIFIED). Its own fast track; do not confuse it with the merits
  objection.
- **21 days** to file a written objection **plus notice of hearing** to a referee
  *recommendation*, or it becomes final — MCR 3.215(E)(4) (VERIFIED). Count from the
  earliest date the recommendation could be in your hands and act well before it.
- On a contested postjudgment motion, the referee **must** find facts specially and state
  the law applied (MCR 3.215(E)(1)(a)), and findings are **required** at the judicial level
  too — MCR 3.210(D)(1) (VERIFIED). A recommendation with no findings and no stated
  standard is objectionable on that ground alone.
- **Do not stipulate the Friend of the Court report into evidence** — *Duperon v Duperon*,
  175 Mich App 77 (1989) (PROVISIONAL): it is not evidence unless both parties agree, and
  findings must rest on competent hearing evidence. Keeping it out preserves an objection
  if the referee leans on it.
