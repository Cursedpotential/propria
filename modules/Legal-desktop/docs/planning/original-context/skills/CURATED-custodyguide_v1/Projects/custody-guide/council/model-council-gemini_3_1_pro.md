# Council Review: Pro Se Custody Guide Outline (Genesee County, MI)

## 1. VERDICT: Executability and Practical Risks
The current outline provides an exceptionally rigorous, procedurally accurate scaffold that effectively translates the ICLE attorney-course structure into a pro se format. The integration of Genesee County specificities (color-coded praecipes, strict 9-day motion service, Monday motion days, and local forms like FOC 87 and FOC 68) demonstrates a deep understanding of local practice. 

However, **this outline is not yet executable by a drafting model without significant risk of failure in practice.** While it correctly maps the procedural "happy path," it critically underestimates the specific hurdles that routinely derail pro se litigants—specifically, overcoming hearsay and authentication hurdles at trial, effectively utilizing third-party discovery (subpoenas), and managing the post-judgment enforcement phase (makeup parenting time). Furthermore, without explicit instructions to the drafting model to avoid clinical "alienation" language in drafted templates, the guide risks leading a pro se litigant to submit pleadings that inflame the court.

## 2. SUBSTANTIVE GAPS
The following critical dimensions are missing and must be added to ensure the guide provides complete, legally sound, and safe advice:

*   **Evidentiary Foundation and Admissibility (The "Pro Se Trap"):** Lesson 3.3 mentions "laying foundation," but pro se litigants overwhelmingly fail because they do not understand hearsay (MRE 801/802) or authentication (MRE 901). The guide must explicitly explain how to get text messages, emails, and OurFamilyWizard logs admitted into evidence (e.g., party admissions under MRE 801(d)(2)), and how to properly authenticate them (printouts with dates/times/numbers visible).
*   **Michigan's Recording Law (MCL 750.539c & Vicarious Consent):** While Lesson 4.1 correctly flags the one-party consent rule, it needs deeper nuance. Based on *Sullivan v. Gray* (117 Mich App 476) and affirmed by the 6th Circuit in *Fisher v. Perron* (30 F.4th 289, 6th Cir. 2022) ([Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/united-states-recording-laws/one-party-consent-states/michigan-recording-laws/)), Michigan operates as a one-party consent state *only for participants* in the conversation. Crucially, the outline misses the **vicarious consent prohibition**: a parent *cannot* consent on behalf of a minor child to record the child's conversations with the other parent. Instructing a pro se parent to record without this strict caveat risks exposing them to felony eavesdropping charges.
*   **Subpoena Practice (MCR 2.506):** Pro se litigants often need records held by third parties (school attendance records, police reports, medical files). The discovery section (2.1) covers interrogatories and document requests directed at the opposing party, but omits the crucial process for issuing subpoenas (Form MC 11).
*   **Contempt and Make-Up Parenting Time (MCL 552.641-644):** Lesson 3.4 briefly mentions contempt, but high-conflict cases are defined by chronic parenting time interference. The guide must detail the statutory remedy of requesting "make-up parenting time" under MCL 552.641 and the specific process for filing a show-cause motion for contempt.
*   **Change of Domicile / 100-Mile Rule (MCL 722.31):** A frequent issue in high-conflict cases is a parent attempting to move away. The outline omits the 100-mile rule and the specific five-factor test the court must apply under MCL 722.31(4) before granting a change of domicile.
*   **Emergency Ex Parte Motions (MCR 3.207):** While temporary orders are covered, true emergencies (e.g., immediate risk of kidnapping or physical harm) require ex parte motions. The standard for obtaining these and the requirement for an immediate post-order hearing must be detailed.
*   **Appeals, Stays, and Reconsideration:** A brief overview of Motions for Reconsideration (MCR 2.119(F)) and the strict 21-day timeline for filing a Claim of Appeal with the Court of Appeals (MCR 7.204) is necessary so the reader understands their limited window for challenging a final judgment.

## 3. STRUCTURAL CRITIQUE
*   **Referee Hearings (Lesson 2.5) & De Novo Review (MCR 3.215):** The outline needs to accurately reflect the strict constraints of MCR 3.215(F)(2) for de novo hearings. While the court *must* allow live evidence, MCR 3.215(F)(2)(c) allows the judge, in their discretion, to "prohibit a party from introducing new evidence or calling new witnesses unless there is an adequate showing that the evidence was not available at the referee hearing" ([Michigan Courts](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20100427_c293956_28_293956.opn.pdf)). Pro se litigants often "save their best evidence for the judge"—the outline must explicitly warn against this fatal strategic error.
*   **Child Support Deviation Factors (Lesson 1.2):** The outline instructs the model to pull the deviation factors. The model must be instructed to use the 18 specific factors listed in Section 1.04(E) of the **2025** Michigan Child Support Formula Manual (the 2025 update reduced the list from 20 to 18) ([2025 MCSF Manual](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a7a53/siteassets/court-administration/standardsguidelines/foc/2025mcsf.pdf)).
*   **Sequencing and Module Granularity:** Mirroring the ICLE lesson numbering works well for mapping, but a stressed pro se litigant needs a consolidated timeline. A "Master Pretrial Deadline Tracker" (combining praecipe, service, and objection deadlines) should be elevated to a standalone visual checklist at the start of Lesson 2.

## 4. RISK REVIEW
*   **"Alienation" Terminology Trap:** The outline wisely avoids treating "parental alienation" as a standalone legal tort, focusing instead on statutory Factor (j). However, there is a high risk that the drafting model, when writing the "worked examples," might include clinical terms ("narcissist," "alienator," "gaslighting"). The drafting instructions must explicitly forbid the model from using these terms in any drafted pleading templates. Courts penalize litigants who attempt to clinically diagnose the opposing party.
*   **Safety Issues (DV and PPOs):** Lesson 4.4 handles PPOs well, but the guide must emphasize that the *act of filing* (either for custody or a PPO) often escalates the risk of violence. Safety planning (e.g., changing locks, alerting schools) must be integrated *before* filing the initial motion, not just discussed retroactively.
*   **Unauthorized Practice / "Ghostwriting":** The guide correctly references MCR 2.117(B) for limited-scope representation. However, relying too heavily on complex templates (like the Trial Brief) without attorney review carries a high risk of failure if the litigant misunderstands the applicable legal standard. The "VERIFY BEFORE RELYING" flags are sufficient for changing fees/addresses, but not for complex legal strategy.

## 5. REVISED OUTLINE (Additions and Modifications)
*(The following modifications must be integrated into the existing skeleton by the drafting model)*

**LESSON 1: Case Strategy**
*   *Modify 1.2 (Child Support):* Add objective: Understand the 18 specific MCSF deviation factors (2025 version) and how courts allocate the tax dependency exemption.
*   *Add New Module 1.4: Managing the Psychological and Practical Toll.*
    *   **Learning objectives:** Recognize the stress of high-conflict litigation; establish boundaries for reviewing case materials; identify support resources independent of the legal process.

**LESSON 2: Pretrial Matters**
*   *Add to 2.1 (Discovery):* Module on Subpoenas (Form MC 11) for obtaining third-party records (schools, medical, police).
    *   **Planned templates/examples:** Blank MC 11 Subpoena template with a worked example for requesting school attendance records.
*   *Modify 2.3 (Motions):* Add specific instructions on Emergency Ex Parte Motions (MCR 3.207) and the high threshold required to obtain them without notice.
*   *Modify 2.5 (Referee Hearings):* **CRITICAL ADDITION:** Explicitly warn about MCR 3.215(F)(2)(c)—the judge's discretion to bar *new* evidence at a de novo hearing. Exercise: Checklist for ensuring *all* evidence is presented at the referee stage.

**LESSON 3: Reaching a Final Judgment**
*   *Add to 3.3 (Trial):* Dedicated section on Evidentiary Basics for Pro Se Litigants.
    *   **Learning objectives:** Understand basic hearsay rules (MRE 801/802); learn how to authenticate digital evidence (texts, emails, OurFamilyWizard logs).
    *   **Planned templates/examples:** Script for admitting a text message into evidence over a hearsay objection (e.g., party admission).
*   *Modify 3.4 (Judgment/Enforcement):* Add detailed steps for enforcing parenting time, including filing for Contempt (show cause) and requesting Make-Up Parenting Time (MCL 552.641).
*   *Add New Module 3.5: Post-Judgment Relief.*
    *   **Learning objectives:** Understand Motions for Reconsideration (MCR 2.119(F)) and the strict 21-day appellate timeline (MCR 7.204).

**LESSON 4: High-Conflict Cases**
*   *Modify 4.1 (Documentation):* Clarify the Michigan recording law (MCL 750.539c). Emphasize the *Fisher v. Perron* (6th Cir. 2022) affirmation of the participant exception, but add a **strong warning against vicarious consent** (recording children) and the risk of judges excluding legal recordings if deemed prejudicial.
*   *Add New Module 4.5: The 100-Mile Rule and Change of Domicile.*
    *   **Learning objectives:** Understand the requirements of MCL 722.31 for moving more than 100 miles or out of state; analyze the five statutory factors the court must consider.

**Appendices:**
*   Add Form MC 11 (Subpoena) to Appendix A (SCAO Form Directory).
