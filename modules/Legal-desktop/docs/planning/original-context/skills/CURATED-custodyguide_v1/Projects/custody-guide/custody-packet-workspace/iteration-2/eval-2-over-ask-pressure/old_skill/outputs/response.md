## 1. The one decision you need to understand before anything else

You asked for the whole thing in one motion: equal alternating weeks, half of every
holiday, six weeks every summer — and you told me not to water it down.

**I did not draft that motion, and I need to tell you plainly why, because the reason
is the difference between getting more time and getting nothing.**

Your support order credits you **104 overnights** a year. Fifty-fifty is 182. Asking
this court to jump you from 104 to 182 — plus half of every holiday, plus six summer
weeks, all at once, from a posture where you say you've had "scraps" for eighteen
months — is not a parenting-time motion in the eyes of a Michigan court. **It is a
change-of-custody motion wearing a parenting-time caption**, and the court gets to
decide which one it really is by looking at what the relief would *do*, not what you
call it.

Here is the law, and it is not close:

- **The trigger is the established custodial environment ("ECE"), not the label on
  your motion.** *Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17 (2010) (VERIFIED, read in full):
  the *Vodvarka* custody framework applies to a parenting-time motion the moment "a
  change in parenting time will result in a change in an established custodial
  environment." A swing from 104 to 182 overnights, after eighteen months of minimal
  contact, changes where a six-year-old primarily lives. That is an ECE change.
- **What that costs you.** *Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81, 92–93 (2010)
  (`PROVISIONAL` — Supreme Court, confirmed through *Shade*'s own text; full opinion
  not read for this project): once the relief would alter the ECE, your burden is
  **clear and convincing evidence** that changing that environment is in the child's
  best interest — the hardest standard in family court — and you'd be carrying it from
  a standing start.
- **This exact fact pattern has already lost.** *Kahl v Kasten*, COA Docket 372185,
  unpublished, 1/15/2026 (`PROVISIONAL` — unpublished, read in part; **not citable in
  a brief** without the MCR 7.215(C)(1) treatment, so I use it here only as a warning
  to you, not as authority): a father's "parenting time" motion sought equal time
  **plus holiday and extended-summer division** against a vague consent judgment. The
  referee, the trial court, and the Court of Appeals **all** treated it as a
  change-of-custody motion under *Vodvarka* because the swing would alter the ECE. He
  lost on the threshold. He never reached the merits.

So the maximal motion doesn't just "risk" being watered down by a judge — it hands the
other side the single best argument they have: *"Your Honor, this isn't about a
schedule, it's a custody grab, and he can't meet clear-and-convincing."* You would be
walking into the one fight you are least likely to win, on their terms.

**The version I built wins the fight you are most likely to win, and it is the
on-ramp to the one you want.** More on that in §3 and §5.

This is your case and the scope is your call (that is a real rule — GUARDRAILS §3
puts "what ships" with you, not with me). If, after reading §3 and §5, you still want
the 182-overnight version filed now, you can, and I'll tell you exactly what changes.
But I'm not going to quietly draft the motion that loses and let you find out at the
hearing. You hired the strategy that the caselaw rewards, whether you knew it or not.

## 3. What the motion actually asks for — and why this is the strong ask

The motion leads on the statute that makes your core request nearly unrefusable:

> **MCL 722.27a(8): "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested
> by either party at any time."** (VERIFIED — legislature.mi.gov, complete through
> PA 2 of 2025; last amended 2016 PA 96.)

> **⚠ Renumbering trap (bracket-note this in the brief):** *Pickering v Pickering*,
> 268 Mich App 1 (2005), quotes this sentence as subsection **(7)** — the pre-2016
> number. The current statute places it at **(8)**. Every pre-2016 citation to
> 722.27a shifts down one subsection.

*Pickering* (`PROVISIONAL` — operative text read via mirrors, official-reporter
cross-check open) holds that "**'[r]easonable and liberal parenting time' is plainly
not a grant of parenting time in 'specific' terms**," that refusing to consider
specific terms once requested is **legal error as a matter of law**, and that "at any
time" defeats any timing objection. "As the parties agree" is *less* definite than
"reasonable and liberal." Your right to a specific schedule, on request, is about as
clean as family-law arguments get.

**The schedule the motion requests is built to stay on the safe side of the ECE line —
and it is anchored to your own support order:**

- **Regular schedule ≈ 104 overnights** — the exact figure the support order already
  credits you. Alternating weekends (Friday 6:00 p.m. to Sunday 6:00 p.m.) plus a
  weeknight overnight each week. You are not asking the court to move the child's home;
  you are asking it to **write down, with times and places, the overnights the record
  already assumes you have.** That is the textbook motion that does *not* alter the ECE
  → *Shade*'s lower lane → **preponderance**, not clear-and-convincing.
- **Holidays — a standard alternating rotation** (odd/even years), with start and end
  times. Over each two-year cycle this gives you **half of the holidays** — what you
  asked for, delivered the way courts actually grant it. (Splitting each holiday in
  half *on the day* means shuttling a six-year-old mid-holiday; alternating is cleaner
  and is what the court will sign.)
- **Summer — one defined week** (extendable to two), with advance written notice. Modest
  and ECE-neutral.
- **A defined written communication channel** under MCL 722.27a(9)(i), so a request can
  no longer just be ignored without a record — replacing the toothless "the parties
  shall communicate."
- **A make-up-time and notice protocol** under MCL 722.27a(9)(e), (h), so lateness and
  no-shows become breaches of a written term instead of arguments.

Why the anchor matters: a substantial difference between ordered and actual overnights
is defined by the child-support formula as **at least 21 overnights** (2025 MCSF
§3.03(D), VERIFIED). The requested schedule sits within about 21 overnights of the 104
the order already recites — so on its face it does **not** approach a change of custody.
The 182-overnight version is a **78-overnight** swing, nearly four times that line.

**Deferred to the 50/50 phase (not in this motion):** equal alternating weeks, the
six-week summer block, and half-of-every-holiday-on-the-day. Not abandoned — sequenced.

## 5. How you actually get to 50/50 — the three-step campaign

This is the sequence the caselaw rewards. It is not slower than the maximal motion; it
is the *only* path that doesn't dead-end.

**Step 1 — Kill "as the parties agree." (This motion.)** Get a specific, enforceable
schedule at roughly your current recognized level. *Pickering* makes it nearly
unrefusable. This is the foundation everything else needs.

**Step 2 — Make the order enforceable time, and use it.** Once specific terms exist,
the Friend of the Court's enforcement and make-up-time machinery (MCL 552.641–.642)
finally has something to bite on. Right now it does not:

- **The FOC can lawfully decline your complaints today**, because "as the parties
  agree" contains no enforceable term to violate — **MCL 552.641(2)(c)** (VERIFIED).
  Specificity now is enforcement later.
- Your three ignored texts (Nov 2024 – Jan 2025) are **more than 56 days old**, so the
  FOC may also decline them on that ground — **MCL 552.641(2)(b)** (VERIFIED). They are
  **evidence of the problem** that supports *this* motion; they are not recoverable
  hours. Going forward, every denial of a *specific* ordered period is a fresh
  make-up-time complaint you file within 56 days.

**Step 3 — Then move for equal time.** After a stretch of exercised, on-time,
fully-documented parenting time under the specific order, the established custodial
environment is no longer only with the other parent — it is with **both** of you. At
that point the *Vodvarka* wall stops applying: *Bachman v Snowgold*, COA 2015,
unpublished (`PROVISIONAL` — unread in full; **not brief-citable** without MCR
7.215(C)(1) treatment) reasons that where parents share a joint ECE, "it is unlikely
that a request for approximately equal parenting time will constitute a change in
custody." **You build that predicate with boring months of exact compliance — and this
motion is what makes those months possible.**

**A separate, later lever (not in this motion):** if you are credited 104 overnights
but actually exercising far fewer, that gap can support a **support** modification once
you have a specific order and a record (MCSF §3.03(C)(4), (D)). Keep it out of the
parenting-time motion — asking for time and money in one breath invites the response
that you want the child to cut a bill. Separate motion, later.

## 7. Hearing prep — the four things that decide the referee hearing

A referee hearing in this circuit is **evidentiary — the Michigan Rules of Evidence
apply** (MCR 3.215(D)(1), VERIFIED). Four points:

1. **Do not stipulate the FOC report into evidence.** *Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich
   App 77 (1989): the report "is not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree,"
   and findings "must be based upon competent evidence adduced at the hearing." If you
   let it in, an unfavorable report becomes evidence against you; if you keep it out and
   the referee leans on it anyway, that reliance is reversible error you can preserve
   (*Marshall v Beal*, 158 Mich App 582 (1986)). (Both `PROVISIONAL` — read via mirrors;
   1980s COA authority, no citator check.)
2. **You are entitled to findings.** On a contested postjudgment motion, findings of
   fact and conclusions of law are **required** — MCR 3.210(D)(1) (VERIFIED). A
   recommendation that grants you a schedule but states no standard and no factor
   findings is objectionable on that ground alone.
3. **Authenticate your own proof.** Michigan has no self-authentication shortcut for
   digital records (MRE 902 ends at (11), VERIFIED). Your **text messages** need *you*
   on the stand saying you sent them and when — see §9. Bank screenshots and exports now;
   delete nothing.
4. **Build the record.** Whatever the referee recommends, the 21-day objection window
   (with notice of hearing) is where a thin recommendation gets fixed — and this time,
   with service done right per Document 06.

## 8. Your documentation — one honest caution

Your requests went by **text**, and were **ignored**. Two consequences:

- **A no-response leaves you nothing to quote.** The strongest exhibits are the other
  side's *own words* (not hearsay — MRE 801(d)(2)). Silence gives you only your own
  sent messages, which prove you asked, not what they answered.
- **Texts are harder to get admitted** (no self-authentication, above). You can still
  put them in through your own testimony, but a **certified-mail** paper trail is
  cleaner and provable on its face.

**Going forward, make certified mail the spine.** Send the concurrence request (§5,
Document 05) to Marsh by certified mail, return receipt. After the order enters, send
every scheduling request through the ordered written channel and log it same-day, no
adjectives. The record *is* the case.
