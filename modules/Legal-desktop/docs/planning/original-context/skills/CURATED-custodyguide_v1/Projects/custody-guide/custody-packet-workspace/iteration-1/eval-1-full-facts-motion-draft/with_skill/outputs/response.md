## What I built, and why it is shaped this way

You asked for a motion for specific parenting time. A specific-terms motion in Genesee needs a small stack of documents that lock together, so I drafted the whole set, tailored to your facts. The legal spine is:

- **Lead:** **MCL 722.27a(8)** — "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time." A generally worded provision like "as the parties agree" is exactly what this subsection lets either parent convert into a calendar. **_Pickering v Pickering_, 268 Mich App 1 (2005)** held that a vague award ("reasonable and liberal") is "plainly not" specific terms, and that a request for specific terms must be considered "regardless of the timing."
- **In the alternative:** if the court wants a threshold, your requested order does **not** change where your daughter primarily lives, so under **_Shade v Wright_** the parenting-time standard governs (not the harder custody standard), and under **_Pierron_** the burden is a **preponderance**. **_Kaeb_** supplies the flexible standard for any condition (like the communication method).
- **The clincher on "this is really a custody grab":** your requested schedule — alternating weekends + one weeknight dinner — is **about 52 overnights a year, half the 104 the support order already credits you.** You are asking for *less* than the arrangement the court already recognizes. That is the cleanest possible answer to a recharacterization attack.
- **Why specificity matters:** under **MCL 552.641(2)(c)** the Friend of the Court can decline to enforce an order that has no enforceable term. "As the parties agree" is that order. **Specific terms now are enforcement later.**

Your facts give this motion three real strengths, and the packet is built to lean on all three:

1. **The mail-only restriction is not in your order.** Your order says "the parties shall communicate regarding parenting time." The mail-only narrowing is only in **J. Marsh's January 8, 2025 letter** — attached as Exhibit B, stated neutrally, and answered by asking the court to set a written, date-stamped channel (proposed order ¶8).
2. **Your conduct audit is clean.** Dated contact log, no order violation, current on support, no missed exchanges. That is your **MCL 722.27a(7)(f)** evidence, and it is worth more than any adjective about the other parent.
3. **The 104 overnights.** The support order's own number caps the "he wants more" argument before it starts.

## Before you file — the campaign, so you don't over-reach

This motion is **Step 1: replace "as the parties agree" with a specific, modest, enforceable schedule.** Two later steps are deliberately **not** in it:

- **Make-up time** for denied parenting time is a separate Friend-of-the-Court track (MCL 552.642) that needs a *specific* term to enforce — which is why Step 1 comes first. Do not fold it into this motion.
- **50/50 / expanded time** comes later, after a clean record of exercised time. Asking for it now is what gets a parenting-time motion recharacterized as a custody motion.
- **Support** stays out entirely. Parenting time and support are separate; asking for both invites the argument that you want time to move money. (Your near-zero actual overnights vs. the ordered 104 is a real support-modification hook for *later* — noted, not filed now.)

## An honest limit you should know

No court has squarely decided **how MCL 722.27a(8) interacts with the "proper cause / change of circumstances" threshold** for modifying an existing order. The other side's strongest argument is that (8) governs the *grant* of parenting time, not a *modification* — and it is a real argument. **That is not a weakness in your motion; it is the reason the motion pleads the alternative.** You lead with (8), and you are also ready to meet the threshold under _Shade_/_Pierron_. Do not let anyone (including a future edit of these documents) claim (8) is settled law that removes the threshold — it isn't, and saying so would hand the other side an easy point.
