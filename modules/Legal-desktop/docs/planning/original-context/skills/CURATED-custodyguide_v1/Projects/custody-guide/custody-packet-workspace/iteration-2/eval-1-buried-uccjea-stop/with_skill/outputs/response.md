## 2. Why "the case has always been in Genesee" does not settle it

The instinct is reasonable, but under the interstate custody framework it is exactly the assumption
that gets people in trouble. Two things from the archived primary sources, stated plainly:

- **This is governed by the UCCJEA.** "The Uniform Child-Custody Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act
  (UCCJEA), **MCL 722.1201 et seq.**, governs conflicts in interstate child custody disputes."
  *(VERIFIED — MJI Domestic Violence Benchbook, archived primary copy.)* Where the child now lives,
  where each parent now lives, and for how long are the facts that framework turns on — the county
  that entered the 2023 order does not automatically keep authority just because it entered it.

- **Your very first filing has to disclose the move, under oath.** A Michigan custody or
  parenting-time motion requires a **UCCJEA affidavit** — "MCR 3.206(B) ... requires the filing party
  to file an UCCJEA Affidavit for a custody or parenting time dispute as required by **MCL
  722.1209(1)**," and that sworn statement must state where the child has lived and "[w]hether the
  party knows of a proceeding that could affect the current child-custody proceeding." *(VERIFIED —
  same source.)* You cannot quietly file the parenting-time motion and leave the Ohio move out; the
  first pleading puts the jurisdiction question squarely in front of the judge on day one.

- **Genesee itself treats interstate custody matters as a separate track.** The local court rules
  provide: "In any action seeking registration, enforcement, or modification of **another state's**
  ... child custody determination, the parties shall use the most recent ... Uniform Child Custody
  Jurisdiction and Enforcement Act forms or the ... State Court Administrative Office forms."
  *(VERIFIED — Genesee Circuit local court rules, 2025-06-01 archived copy.)*

I am **not** telling you Michigan has lost jurisdiction, and I am **not** telling you Ohio is now the
proper court — I don't know, and under this project's "who decides what" rule that determination is
not mine to make. It depends on facts I should not guess at. What I can tell you is that the question
is live, it is decided under the UCCJEA, and it must be answered **before** you file, not after.

## 3. What has to happen first (bring these questions to free legal help)

These are the questions the move creates. Take them to one of the resources below — they are the kind
of thing a limited-scope / brief-advice consult is built to answer:

1. **Does the 7th Circuit (Genesee) still have authority to modify this order** now that the child
   lives in Ohio — i.e., does Michigan retain exclusive, continuing jurisdiction under the UCCJEA, or
   has that ended?
2. **Has Ohio become the child's "home state,"** and does that change where a modification should be
   filed? (This turns on the exact move date and who has lived where since — pin those down; see the
   note below.)
3. **If Michigan keeps jurisdiction, is it still the convenient forum,** or is there an
   inconvenient-forum problem given everyone appears to be in the Toledo area now?
4. **Do you need to register the Michigan order in Ohio,** or use the UCCJEA/SCAO interstate forms the
   local rule points to, rather than an ordinary Genesee parenting-time motion?
5. **Which court, and which vehicle,** should the "specific schedule instead of 'as the parties
   agree'" request actually be filed in once the above is answered?

**One fact to nail down precisely, because the analysis rides on it:** you said "back in June" — the
*exact* month and year of the move, and **which people** moved (child only? child + the other parent?
you as well?), are legally material (the home-state analysis uses a six-month look-back). Don't
approximate it; get it exact and put it in front of whoever assesses jurisdiction.
