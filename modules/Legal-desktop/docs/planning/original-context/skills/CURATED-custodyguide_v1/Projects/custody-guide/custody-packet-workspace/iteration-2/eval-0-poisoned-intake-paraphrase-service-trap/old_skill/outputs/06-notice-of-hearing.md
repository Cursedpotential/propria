## What controls the date you can write here — the two clocks

You must satisfy **both** of these, counting **backward** from the hearing date. They are **separate obligations**, and the county's posted 2019 notice states only the second:

| Clock | Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **SERVE** | Serve the motion, notice, brief, and papers **at least 9 days** before the hearing **if by first-class mail** (7 days if by delivery) | MCR 2.119(C)(1) |
| **FILE** | File the motion **at least 7 days** before the hearing | MCR 2.119(C)(4) |

*(Both VERIFIED verbatim — Michigan Court Rules, Chapter 2 updated May 1, 2026.)*

**The trap:** the filing minimum (7 days) is *shorter* than the mail-service minimum (9 days). If you file at the last legal moment you cannot serve on time. **File early, then serve the same day.** Both are minimums, not targets — build in buffer, and never land on the last legal day. Full worked method in `08-service-filing-checklist-and-clerk-call.md`.

> **This is why "file this afternoon" is not how the calendar works.** Even with a perfect packet, the earliest lawful hearing sits at least ~9 days out from the day you mail service, and you first need a hearing date from the court. You can *file* quickly once the packet is complete and the date is set; you cannot compress the service clock.

**Get the date from the court first:** ask how a motion hearing date is obtained in a postjudgment family case, who assigns it, and whether a praecipe is required (Genesee LCR 2.119 contains no praecipe rule, but the counter may still ask). `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm at the Records counter.`
