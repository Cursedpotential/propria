## ⚠⚠ Read this before you serve — you asked me to "just serve her at her house." That is not the safe instruction.

You said: *"Just serve her at her house, 512 Elm St, Flint MI 48503 — that's where she is."* Three problems, each of which can sink the filing the way the last one sank:

1. **Serve the party at the FOC ADDRESS OF RECORD — not at an address you happen to know.** In a case where the court retains jurisdiction, service by mail goes to the party's **"last known mailing address,"** which **MCR 3.203(B)** defines as the address the party gave the Friend of the Court **in writing**, or the address in the most recent order, or an address the FOC established. **512 Elm St may or may not be that address.** If it is not, mailing there is not valid service even if she lives there. **Confirm the address of record with the Friend of the Court; do not guess and do not use an address just because "that's where she is."** *(MCR 3.203(A)–(B), VERIFIED verbatim.)*

2. **You must also serve her ATTORNEY.** She has counsel of record — D. Blake. **MCR 3.203(I)(1):** copies of notices to the parties **also must be sent to the attorneys of record**. Serving only the party (or only at her house) leaves out counsel. **Serve both — the party AND the attorney — the same papers, the same day, on one proof of service.** This satisfies every reading of the rules and violates none. *(MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c), 3.203(I)(1), VERIFIED verbatim.)*

3. **Do not hand-deliver anything to her yourself.** Mail it. Have a **legally competent adult who is not you** drop the envelopes in the mail and sign this proof of service. Put nothing in the envelope except the court papers. (If any communication restriction exists in your case, this keeps service — a step the rules require — from being framed as "contact.")

> **The instruction that survives every reading of the rules:** *Serve the party at the FOC address of record, AND serve the attorney at the business address, same papers, same day, both by first-class mail, proven on one proof of service.*

## ⛔ Do not use this proof if

- You are serving a summons and complaint (that is service of *process* — different rules, MCR 2.105/2.103).
- You did not personally do the mailing described. **The person who mailed it signs it.**
- Any date in it is not exactly true.

## Rules for completing it

- **List every document by name.** "Motion and attachments" is not a list. If the **proposed order** is not named, there is no paper record that you complied with Genesee LCR 2.119(B) — which requires it served with the motion.
- **The date is the mailing date.** Service by mail is complete at mailing — MCR 2.107(C)(3).
- **The signer is the person who put it in the mail**, with their own address.
- **Copy the declaration sentence exactly** from MCR 1.109(D)(3)(b). No notary needed.
- **Attach nothing to the proof of service.** Keep the certified-mail receipts and return-receipt cards in your own service file as backup; they are not part of the proof.
- **Also drop a courtesy email copy to counsel the same day** if you have an email address for D. Blake, noting the papers are also being served by first-class mail. (Costs nothing; addresses the MCR 2.107(G) electronic-service directive, which this project could not reconcile with the agreement-based rule — `⊗ CONFLICTED`, so mail is your safe spine.)

### Also required at filing — the Friend of the Court copy

Separate from serving the party and counsel: hand the **clerk** one extra copy marked **"FRIEND OF THE COURT"** at the time of filing — **MCR 3.203(G)** *(VERIFIED verbatim)*. The clerk sends it to the FOC. Handing your motion to the FOC office is **not** filing it. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask whether Genesee's e-filing (MiFILE) changes this.`

**Disposition:** MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c), 2.107(C)(3), 2.107(D); MCR 3.203(A)–(B), (G), (I)(1); MCR 1.109(D)(3)(b) — all ✅ VERIFIED verbatim. The party's actual mailing address of record 🔲 must be confirmed with the FOC. MCR 2.107(G) treatment ⊗ CONFLICTED — mail is the safe method.
