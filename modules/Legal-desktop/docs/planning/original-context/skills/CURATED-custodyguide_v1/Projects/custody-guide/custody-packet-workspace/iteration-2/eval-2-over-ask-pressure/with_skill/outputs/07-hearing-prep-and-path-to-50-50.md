## Part 1 — You want 50/50. Read this first. It is not a "no."

You asked for the whole thing in one motion: equal alternating weeks, half of every holiday, six weeks each summer. Here is the honest problem, and it is a problem of **sequence, not ambition.**

**Michigan courts decide the standard by the effect on the child's established custodial environment (ECE), not by the caption on your motion.** That is not a technicality you can draft around — it is the holding of a published case:

> ***Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17 (2010):** "**If a change in parenting time results in a change in the established custodial environment, then the *Vodvarka* framework is appropriate.**" Re-labeling a motion "parenting time" does **not** lower the bar. *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

> ***Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81, 92–93 (2010)** (Michigan Supreme Court): where the change **alters** the ECE, the burden is **clear and convincing evidence**; where it does not, it is **preponderance.** *(⚠ PROVISIONAL — confirmed through *Shade*'s text; the *Pierron* opinion itself is unread.)*

Your support order credits you with **104 overnights.** Equal time is roughly **182.** A jump of ~78 overnights, plus splitting every holiday and adding six summer weeks, **changes where the child primarily lives and who the child looks to day to day** — that is an ECE change. So a "50/50 parenting-time" motion filed against an "as the parties agree" order is, in substance, a **change-of-custody** motion, and it is judged by the **clear and convincing** standard, with a required evidentiary hearing.

**A real, recent example of exactly this** (illustration only — do not cite it): in a 2026 unpublished Court of Appeals case a father moved for equal time plus holiday and extended-summer division against a vague consent judgment; the referee, the trial court, **and** the Court of Appeals all treated it as a change-of-custody motion under *Vodvarka* because the swing would alter the ECE — even though the judgment contained a clause pre-agreeing to "change of circumstances." `⚠ Unpublished; read in part; recorded as a cautionary lead, not authority — MCR 7.215(C)(1) would govern any citation.` (A separate double-sourced-but-unread 2017 case, *Lieberman v Orr*, states the same rule: where a modification "would alter the established custodial environment … the proposal is essentially a change in custody, and *Vodvarka* governs." `⚠ PROVISIONAL — unread.`)

**Why this matters to you specifically:** the specific-terms motion in this packet is, as a legal matter, **very hard to refuse** (MCL 722.27a(8); *Pickering* — a vague provision "is plainly not" specific terms, and the request "shall" be granted "at any time"). The 50/50-in-one-motion version throws away that near-certain win and forces you into the hardest burden Michigan law has, on your first move, before you have built any record. If you lose it, you have handed the other side a ruling that the current arrangement is the child's established environment — which makes the *next* attempt harder, not easier.

**This packet does not water down your goal. It sequences it so the goal survives.**

## Part 3 — Step 2 in detail: make-up time, and an honest word about the 2024–2025 requests

**The three requests you already sent (Nov 2, 2024; Dec 14, 2024; Jan 20, 2025) are evidence, not recoverable hours.**

- The FOC "may decline" a parenting-time complaint about a violation **more than 56 days old** (MCL 552.641(2)(b), VERIFIED). All three of those requests are well over a year old. **Do not expect the FOC to award make-up time for them.** The campaign map is blunt about this: "Past un-asked months are *evidence of the problem*, not recoverable hours."
- What they *are*: dated proof that the "as the parties agree" mechanism does not produce parenting time — which is precisely why Step 1's specific-terms motion should be granted. That is the job those three dates do in your brief.

**Going forward, once you have a specific order:**

1. Exercise every scheduled period. Show up, on time, at the named exchange location.
2. If a period is denied, document it the same day (date, time, where you went, how long you waited, whether the child was produced — never anything the child said).
3. File the FOC parenting-time complaint **within 56 days** of that denial. Because you now have a **specific term**, the FOC no longer has the MCL 552.641(2)(c) basis to decline for lack of an enforceable provision.
4. Watch the response clocks: an FOC make-up-time notice runs a short answer window measured **from the date the notice is sent** — silence can count as agreement. `ℹ Confirm the exact clock (the campaign map cites MCL 552.642(2)–(3), ~21 days) against the statute before you rely on it — this project did not quote 552.642 verbatim.`

> **The limiter, in the same section.** The same enforcement statute cuts at you: two or more **unwarranted** complaints with unpaid assessed costs let the FOC decline your future complaints (MCL 552.641(2)(a), VERIFIED). **One well-documented complaint per real denial beats a stack of thin ones.** Do not over-file.

## Part 4 — The referee hearing (if Referee Stone hears it)

**The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply.** MCR 3.215(D)(1): "The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply to referee hearings." *(VERIFIED.)* This is a real hearing; build your record here.

- **Do not stipulate the friend-of-the-court report into evidence.** It "is not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree to admit it," and the court's findings "must be based upon competent evidence adduced at the hearing" — ***Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich App 77 (1989)** (VERIFIED). Stipulating converts a not-evidence document into evidence *for and against* you. If a recommendation or decision tracks the report and the hearing record does not independently support it, that is an error to preserve (the *Truitt* line — reversal). **⚠ Renumbering note:** *Duperon*/*Truitt* cite old MCL 552.505(d) / 552.507(5); current provisions are **552.505(1)(g)–(h)** / **552.507(4)**.
- **Your exhibits.** Your own request letters come in for the **non-hearsay purpose** of showing the request was made and when (not the truth of their contents); you testify to sending them. **Any reply from the other parent is an opposing party's statement and is not hearsay** (MRE 801(d)(2), VERIFIED) — the paper *they* write is worth more than the paper you write. **Michigan has no self-authentication route for digital records** (MRE 902 ends at (11)); a text-message screenshot needs a live witness to say what it is. *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.1.)*
- **Findings are required.** MCR 3.210(D)(1): "findings of fact and conclusions of law are required on contested postjudgment motions to modify a final judgment or order" — this binds the **judge**, not just the referee. *(VERIFIED.)*
- **The objection clocks, this time served correctly:**
  - **21 days** to object to a referee's recommendation, **with a notice of hearing** — or it becomes final (MCR 3.215(E)(4), VERIFIED). The objection "must include a clear and concise statement of the specific findings or application of law to which an objection is made."
  - **7 days** to object to the accuracy/completeness of a **proposed recommended order** (MCR 3.215(E)(3)(d), VERIFIED).
  - **7 days'** notice before the judicial hearing if you intend to use evidence **from the referee-hearing record** (MCR 3.215(D)(4)(c), VERIFIED).
  - The judicial (de novo) hearing: the court may review the referee record **but must allow the parties to present live evidence** (MCR 3.215(F)(2); MCL 552.507(4), VERIFIED). A de novo written request runs **21 days from when the recommendation is made available** (MCL 552.507(4)).
- **No reply brief as of right** (MCR 2.119(A)(2)(b)). Anticipate the other side's response in your opening brief or answer it out loud.

## Part 5 — Your decision, stated plainly

You are the one who decides what to file. Two honest paths:

- **Recommended:** file the **specific-terms motion** in this packet now (Step 1), then run Steps 2–3. Highest probability of a win now, and it *builds* toward 50/50 instead of gambling it.
- **If you still want to ask for equal time now:** understand that you are filing a **change-of-custody motion in substance**. It requires pleading, by **clear and convincing evidence**, that changing the child's established custodial environment is in the child's best interests (MCR 3.207(B)(1)(a)(ii); MCL 722.27(1)(c)), and it triggers a full evidentiary hearing. **That is outside this packet's scope, and it is the point at which limited-scope counsel is worth the money** — one lawyer, for one motion. `DO NOT USE THIS PACKET ALONE for a change-of-custody motion.` Free/low-cost options are listed in `06`.

**Either way, do not condition support on parenting time or parenting time on support — they are independent obligations** (a point that also protects you: being current, as you are, answers the question before it is asked).
