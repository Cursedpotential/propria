### Drafting rules for the numbered paragraphs (read before you edit)

- **One fact per paragraph, each with a date.** No paragraph should need two dates to be
  true.
- **Quote the order; never characterize it.** Paragraph 2 is seven words in quotation
  marks. Everything after it is measured against those words.
- **No adjectives about a person. Not one.** If a sentence would change meaning read
  aloud in a flat voice by a stranger, rewrite it.
- **Nothing about the other parent's character, motives, mental state, or
  relationships.** The banned words appear nowhere: *narcissist, alienator, abuser,
  gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, vindictive.* A
  filing that contains them becomes a filing about the person who wrote it.
- **The history gets no paragraph.** Nothing about the March 15, 2023 hearing beyond the
  fact that an order entered. Every window to revisit it closed years ago; the motion
  litigates forward.
- **Paragraph 6 concedes the established custodial environment openly.** That is not a
  loss. It is exactly what puts you in the lower-burden lane (see Document 02, Part IV).
  State it truthfully.

### What paragraph 5 is doing, and its honest limit

Paragraph 5 is your compliance-and-request record. It shows, without argument, that you
used the ordered channel and asked. **Its limit:** each request drew *no response*, so
there is no reply to quote against the other side, and text messages carry no
self-authentication under MRE 902 — they come in through your own testimony (Document 03,
¶5). This is why the packet routes your **future** requests through certified mail
(Document 06 and response §8). Do not overstate what silence proves: it proves you
asked, not what was refused.
