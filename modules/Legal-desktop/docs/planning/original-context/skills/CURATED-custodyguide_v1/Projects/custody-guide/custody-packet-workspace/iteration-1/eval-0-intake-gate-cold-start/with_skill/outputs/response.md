## Why I'm pausing instead of drafting

Your message gives me your **goal**, but a real packet is tailored to your **case facts** — the exact
words in your order, the dates, the addresses on file with the court. I don't have any of those yet.

The rule I work under is simple and it protects you: **a missing fact is something I ask you for, never
something I guess.** A guessed case number, an invented order date, or a paraphrased order clause
doesn't save you time — it produces a defective filing. A judge reading a motion that quotes your order
slightly wrong, or names the wrong referee, or serves the wrong address, is a judge looking for a reason
to deny you. Your prior objection already died once on a defective-service technicality; the whole point
of doing this carefully is to not hand the other side a second free win. So I'd rather pause for one
message than fabricate anything to look productive.

**I have not drafted or assumed any of your facts.** The list below is everything I need. Once you send

## What I need from you (the intake checklist)

Fill in what you can. If an item genuinely doesn't exist (for example, there is no assigned referee, or
there was never a written denial), just say so — "none" is itself an answer. Don't strain to reconstruct
anything from memory; where you're unsure, say "unsure" and we'll mark it to confirm with the clerk.

### A. The court and the case
1. **Case number** (exactly as printed on your order).
2. **Court and county** — I expect the 7th Judicial Circuit Court, Family Division, Genesee County, but
   confirm it from your paperwork rather than letting me assume.
3. **Assigned judge**, and the **assigned referee** if a referee handles your matter (name and title).

### B. The order you're fixing — I need the *exact* words, not a paraphrase
4. **The parenting-time provision, quoted word-for-word** as it appears in the order. Copy it exactly,
   including "as the parties agree" or whatever the precise phrase is. The specific wording is the hinge
   of the whole motion, so a paraphrase won't do.
5. **The order's entry date** — the exact date it was signed/entered (not "about ten months ago").

### C. The communication provision
6. **The order's communication provision, quoted word-for-word** (the part that routes contact through
   opposing counsel, if that's in the order).
7. **The "mail-only" narrowing** — did it come from the **order itself**, or from **counsel's letter**?
   For whichever it is, give me the **date** and **who signed it**. (If it's in a letter, this matters a
   lot: an instruction from counsel is not the same as an order of the court.)

### D. The child — age only
8. **The child's age.** Please do **not** send the child's name or birthdate — age alone is what drives
   the schedule design, and I keep names and birthdates out of every document on purpose.

### E. The support order
9. **The number of overnights the child-support order recites** for you. (Michigan's support formula
   requires the order to state an overnight figure; it was likely computed on minimal time, and it's
   worth knowing what it says.)

### F. The request/denial record
10. **Your dated log of parenting-time requests and the responses.** For each: the date you asked, what
    specifically you asked for, and what came back (granted, denied, ignored, "mail only," etc.). Dates
    and exact wording beat summaries here.

### G. Who gets served (this is where the last filing failed)
11. **The other parent's address of record with the Friend of the Court.**
12. **Opposing counsel's name and mailing address.**

### H. Your own conduct audit — answer honestly
13. Since the order entered: Have you used **only** the permitted communication channel, every time? Any
    direct contact outside it? Anything sent or asked **through the child**? Any social-media posts about
    the case or the other parent? Any exchanges you missed? Answer these straight. Every rule that helps
    you here can also be aimed at you, so I'd rather know the weak spots now than have them surface at a
    hearing.

### I. Your proposed schedule ask
14. **The specific schedule you want** — days of the week, times, and exchange location. One important
    piece of strategy up front: keep this **modest** for the first motion. A request for a defined,
    workable schedule (for example, specified weekday and alternating-weekend time with set exchange
    times and place) is the ask the law rewards. An opening bid for **equal time plus all the holidays
    plus summers** tends to get recharacterized as a full custody fight — a much higher legal bar — and
    can sink the motion. We can build toward more time later, in sequence; the first move is turning
    "as agreed" into something real and enforceable.

## How to write items 6, 7, 10, and 13 so they're usable

When you describe conduct — yours or the other parent's — **lead with dated facts, not motives.** The
strongest version of your record looks like:

- *"Between [date] and [date], agreed time was cancelled on [n] occasions: [list the dates]."*
- *"Requests dated [d1]–[d3] received no response; [d4]–[d5] were declined without an alternative offered."*
- *"The provision has produced no parenting time since [date]."*

Skip words like *manipulation, alienation, narcissist, gaslighting, stonewalling.* They don't help a
judge and they can hurt you. One dated act per line, numbers over adjectives. Proving the pattern is
what does the work — it *replaces* the motive language, it doesn't unlock it. (When we draft, I'll
translate everything into court-safe wording for you; giving it to me in this form just makes that
cleaner.)

And again: **no child's name or birthdate anywhere** — not in your notes to me, not in anything we file.

## What I'll build the moment you send those facts back

As soon as the checklist is filled in, I'll produce your tailored set, in this order:

1. **Motion for specific parenting time** — leading on Michigan's specific-terms mandate, **MCL
   722.27a(8)**: *"Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any
   time,"* anchored by *Pickering v Pickering*, 268 Mich App 1 (2005) (a vague, generous-sounding
   provision does **not** satisfy the statute once specific terms are requested, and timing is no
   defense), with the *Shade*/*Kaeb* threshold pleaded in the alternative. It will add a defined written
   communication channel, exchange logistics, and a make-up-time protocol.
2. **Proposed order** with a fully specific schedule, **concurrence certificate** (Genesee's local rule
   requires it even for self-represented filers), and **notice of hearing**.
3. **Service pack** — served on the right people at the right addresses, with both filing and service
   deadlines computed, built specifically to prevent a repeat of the service failure.
4. **Hearing prep** for a referee hearing.
5. **A hand-back list**, including a single short clerk-phone-call script for the few local details
   (like local praecipe practice) that only the court can confirm.

Every document will carry its authority, its assumptions, and its "do not use if" list. None will be
labeled filing-ready — that judgment stays yours.

## Before you go further — a few things that would change the plan

This packet is built for one specific situation: a postjudgment fight to make a vague parenting-time
term specific. Tell me right away if **any** of these are in the picture, because they mean a different
path and, for several of them, I'd stop and point you to help rather than draft:

- Immediate danger or domestic violence
- Any CPS or police involvement
- A PPO in which **you** are the respondent
- The child living in or moving to **another state**, or any interstate/UCCJEA angle
- Any question about **recording** a call or conversation
- Any criminal exposure
- Native American heritage / tribal (ICWA/MIFPA) involvement
- Any appeal deadline you're trying to catch

None of those are things to draft around — they're things to get the right help for.
