## The one thing to understand before anything else

**A referee hearing is a real evidentiary hearing, and it is your best and possibly only chance to build a record.**

> **MCR 3.215(D)(1): "The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply to referee hearings."**

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 3 updated May 1, 2026.)*

People treat the referee hearing as a warm-up — a first pass before the "real" hearing in front of the judge. **That is backwards, and it is the single most expensive mistake at this stage**, because of what happens if you later object:

> **MCR 3.215(F)(2):** "To the extent allowed by law, the court may conduct the judicial hearing by review of the record of the referee hearing, **but the court must allow the parties to present live evidence at the judicial hearing.** The court may, in its discretion:
> (a) prohibit a party from presenting evidence on findings of fact to which no objection was filed;
> (b) determine that the referee's finding was conclusive as to a fact to which no objection was filed;
> **(c) prohibit a party from introducing new evidence or calling new witnesses unless there is an adequate showing that the evidence was not available at the referee hearing;**
> (d) impose any other reasonable restrictions and conditions to conserve the resources of the parties and the court."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

> **📄 Worked script:** `draft/P1-worked-examples.md` **Part Four** puts this rule into spoken sentences — what to say when a court proposes to decide on the referee record alone, how to narrow the request, the MRE 103 offer of proof if it is refused, and how to preserve the ruling.

**Both halves of that rule matter, and most self-help material teaches only one.**

- **The floor:** the judge **must** allow you to present live evidence. A court that treats your objection as decided on the paper record alone has gone too far.
- **The ceiling:** the judge **may** shut out evidence you *could* have presented to the referee and did not.

**So: everything goes in at the referee hearing. Every exhibit. Every witness. Every date.** You do not hold anything back for later. There may not be a later, and if there is, the door to new evidence may be closed.

# Part A — Before the hearing

## A.1 What the referee can and cannot do

> **MCL 552.507(2):** "A referee may do all of the following:
> (a) **Hear all motions in a domestic relations matter, except motions pertaining to an increase or decrease in spouse support**, referred to the referee by the court.
> (b) **Administer oaths, compel the attendance of witnesses and the production of documents, and examine witnesses and parties.**
> (c) Make a written, signed report to the court containing a summary of testimony given, a statement of findings, and a recommended order; or make a statement of findings on the record and submit a recommended order. …"

*(VERIFIED verbatim — full text of MCL 552.507 retrieved from legislature.mi.gov, August 9, 2026; site banner "Michigan Compiled Laws Complete Through PA 2 of 2025"; last amended 2004 PA 210.)*

A referee is a lawyer. **MCR 3.215(A):** "A referee appointed pursuant to MCL 552.507(1) must be a member in good standing of the State Bar of Michigan." *(VERIFIED.)* A referee does not sign your order — the referee makes a **recommendation for an order**, which a judge approves.

**The notice must tell you it is a referee.** MCR 3.215(C)(1): within 14 days after receiving a referred motion, the referee must arrange for service of a notice scheduling the hearing on the attorneys, or on the parties if unrepresented, and **"[t]he notice of hearing must clearly state that the matter will be heard by a referee."** *(VERIFIED.)* If your notice does not say that, ask the clerk who is hearing it.

## A.2 The record the court keeps

> **MCR 3.215(D)(4):** "An electronic or stenographic record must be kept of all hearings.
> (a) The parties must be allowed to make contemporaneous copies of the record if the referee's recording equipment can make multiple copies simultaneously and if the parties supply the recording media. A recording made under this rule **may be used solely to assist the parties during the proceeding recorded** or, at the discretion of the trial judge, in any judicial hearing following an objection to the referee's recommended order; **it may not be used publicly.**
> (b) If ordered by the court, or if stipulated by the parties, the referee must provide a transcript, verified by oath, of each hearing held. **The cost of preparing a transcript must be apportioned equally between the parties**, unless otherwise ordered by the court."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

> ### ⚠ This is the court's record, not permission to record anything
>
> The rule above is about a copy of the **court's own record**, made on the court's equipment, with the court's permission, under limits the rule itself states. **Nothing in this guide tells you that any recording you make is lawful.** Do not record a conversation you are not part of, do not record the child, do not send a child anywhere with a device, and do not place a device in anyone's home or car. Michigan law here is unsettled and the exposure is criminal. If a recording already exists, that is a question for a lawyer before it is a question for a hearing. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`

**Practical:** ask the clerk or the referee's office, in advance, whether the equipment allows a contemporaneous copy and what media to bring. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — Genesee practice not confirmed`

## A.3 Exhibits — and a local rule that is NOT yours

**Genesee County has no local rule about exhibit exchange.** The Seventh Judicial Circuit's only local rule in the current compilation is Rule 2.119 (concurrence certificate, proposed order, application). *(VERIFIED — complete Genesee section read from `sources/primary/local-court-rules-circuit_2025-06-01.txt`.)*

> ### ⚠ Do not borrow another county's rule
>
> The **Thirteenth Judicial Circuit (Grand Traverse)** has a detailed local exhibit rule that requires, among other things, exchange of proposed exhibits **at least 48 hours before any referee hearing or motion hearing**. **That rule is not in force in Genesee County.** It appears in the same statewide compilation, one page away, which is exactly how a self-represented reader ends up following the wrong county's rule.
>
> Genesee's silence does not mean there is no expectation — it means the expectation, if any, comes from the assigned judge or referee, not from a published local rule. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask the referee's office what they want and when.`

**What to bring regardless:** three complete, identical, tabbed sets — one for the referee, one for the other side, one for you. Pre-marked A, B, C. Originals available.

## A.4 Getting a witness or a document there

Two tracks, and they are different rules. **This is one of the few places where a self-represented party is treated differently from an attorney, and not knowing it costs you the witness.**

| You need | Rule | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **A non-party to produce documents in discovery** | **MCR 2.305(A)(1):** "A represented party may issue a subpoena to a non-party… **An unrepresented party may move the court for issuance of non-party discovery subpoenas.**" | You must **file a motion** and get the court to issue it. You cannot issue it yourself. Allow time. |
| **A witness or documents at the hearing itself** | **MCR 2.506(B)(1):** "A subpoena signed by an attorney of record in the action **or by the clerk of the court** in which the matter is pending has the force and effect of an order signed by the judge." | Ask the **clerk** to issue it. This is the track that works for you. |
| **Timing** | **MCR 2.506(C)(1):** at least **2 days** before appearance; **14 days** before when documents are requested. **MCR 2.305(A)(3):** a discovery subpoena must give a non-party a minimum of **14 days**. | Work backward from the hearing date, and add buffer. |

*(All VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §§2.1, 2.4.)*

The referee also has independent authority to "compel the attendance of witnesses and the production of documents" — MCL 552.507(2)(b) — but **do not rely on that instead of a subpoena.** Get the paper.

## A.5 Building the proof — what you must actually prove

Work from the motion backward. For each thing the court must find, write down the exhibit and the testimony that establishes it.

| What must be established | The exhibit | Who sponsors it | Admissibility route |
|---|---|---|---|
| What the order says | Certified or file copy of the order | It is a court record in this case | The order is already in the court's file; bring a clean copy for the referee's convenience |
| That you requested parenting time, when, and what you asked for | Your letters (Exhibits A–C) | **You**, testifying that you wrote and mailed them | Offered **not for the truth of what they say** but to show the request was made, its contents, and its date — MRE 801(c)(2). Expect an objection; state the purpose out loud. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — framing verified against the rule text; no Michigan case read applying it here` |
| That the letters were sent and delivered | Certified mail receipts; return receipt cards | **You** | MRE 901 — a witness with knowledge |
| What the other side answered | Their written replies | **You** (you received them) | **MRE 801(d)(2): "An Opposing Party's Statement."** Offered against the opposing party, it is **not hearsay** *(VERIFIED verbatim)* |
| That a reply came from the attorney rather than the party | The attorney's letter | **You** | MRE 801(d)(2)(C)/(D) — authorized speaker or agent within scope. Contestable. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` |
| What happened on a specific date | **Your testimony**, from personal knowledge | **You** | Say what you did, where you were, what time, how long you waited, what you observed |
| The child's established custodial environment | **Your testimony** about the facts of the child's living arrangement | **You** | MCL 722.27(1)(c) is a fact test. Concede what is true. |
| Records from a school, provider, or agency | The records themselves | The custodian, **or** a certification | MRE 803(6) business records. Obtain them **directly from the institution**, not secondhand |
| Anything on a phone or a screen | Printouts | **A live witness** | **Michigan has no self-authentication route for digital records.** MRE 902 ends at (11); there is no Michigan analogue to FRE 902(13)/(14). *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.1)* Every digital exhibit needs a sponsoring witness. |

> ### ⚠ Two doors that are closed, so you do not waste the hearing on them
>
> **The child's out-of-court statements.** MRE 803A — the child's-statement hearsay exception — states in its own text: **"Scope. This rule applies in criminal and delinquency proceedings only."** *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.1.)* It is not available in your case. **Do not build any part of your presentation on what the child said**, and do not ask the child anything in order to have something to say. The child is not a witness, not a source, and not a messenger. This is absolute.
>
> A court may interview a child privately. That is **the court's tool, not a parent's tactic**, and it is not something to prepare a child for. If it happens, it happens. Do not request it as a way to get the child's words into the case, and never discuss it with the child. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE` if anyone suggests the child should speak to anyone.
>
> **The Friend of the Court's own report — and the one argument that depends on your not signing anything.**
>
> MRE 1101(b)(9) makes the rules of evidence inapplicable to "the court's **consideration** of a report or recommendation submitted by the friend of the court." *(VERIFIED.)* That is about consideration. Admissibility is a different question, and it has an answer:
>
> ***Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich App 77; 437 NW2d 318 (1989)** (published): the report "is **not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree to admit it in evidence**"; it "may be considered by the trial court as **an aid to understanding the issues** to be resolved"; but "the trial court's **ultimate findings** relative to custody **must be based upon competent evidence adduced at the hearing**," and the report "**may not form the basis for the trial court's findings**." *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.2.)*
>
> ***Marshall v Beal*, 158 Mich App 582, 591 (1986)**: where the parties "did not stipulate to allowing the friend of the court's recommendations or report into evidence" and the court "reviewed and adopted the friend of the court's findings" instead of reaching its own conclusion — "**This is a clear legal error which requires reversal** and a new hearing." *(VERIFIED — same.)*
>
> **So: the report can inform. It cannot prove.** And that gives you a real objection if a recommendation's findings track the report while the hearing record does not independently support them — see the worked Ground 5 in `draft/P1-worked-examples.md` Part Two.
>
> **You have that argument only if you did not stipulate.** Agreeing to admit the report converts a not-evidence document into evidence — for you and against you, in the same motion. **Do not stipulate the report into evidence.** If someone asks you to, that is the moment to say you are not agreeing to that today.
>
> **State the objection at its true width.** The referee is entitled to read the report. Saying otherwise is wrong on the law and spends credibility on your best ground. Object to findings that *rest* on it, not to the reading of it.
>
> `⚠ Caveats to carry: these are 1980s Court of Appeals decisions; no citator was available to this project, so a negative-treatment check remains open, and the official-reporter cross-check of Duperon's text is still open (three independent mirrors agreed verbatim). Citation drift: Duperon cites MCL 552.505(d) and the related line cites MCL 552.507(5); the current provisions are MCL 552.505(1)(g)–(h) and MCL 552.507(4) — bracket-note any quotation.`

## A.6 What to say, and what not to say

**Answer the question that was asked. Stop talking.** The most common self-represented mistake is answering a small question with a long story, and the story is where the damage happens.

**Say:** dates, times, places, what you did, what you observed, what a document says.

**Do not say — ever, in any hearing, in any filing, in any hallway:** narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, personality disorder, "she always," "he never." **The parent who uses those words becomes the subject of the hearing.** This is not about politeness. It is about who the referee is watching by the end of the morning.

**Do not litigate the past hearing.** Every window from that order — reconsideration (MCR 2.119(F)(1), 21 days from entry), objection, and appeal (MCR 7.204(A), 21 days from entry and **jurisdictional**) — closed months ago. *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.1.)* One neutral sentence, if the referee asks. Nothing more. Filings and hearings go forward.

**Ask for findings.** MCR 3.215(E)(1)(a): "**The referee must find facts specially and state separately the law the referee applied.** Brief, definite, and pertinent findings and conclusions on the contested matters are sufficient, without overelaboration of detail or particularization of facts." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)* At the close, it is fair to ask the referee to make findings on the record on the contested points. Findings are what an objection later has to attach to. **No findings, nothing to object to.**

> ### ⚠ And there is a stronger rule waiting at the judge's level
>
> **MCR 3.210(D):** "The court must make findings of fact as provided in MCR 2.517, except that **(1) findings of fact and conclusions of law are required on contested postjudgment motions to modify a final judgment or order**…" *(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`.)*
>
> That matters because the general rule runs the other way: **MCR 2.517(A)(4)** says "[f]indings of fact and conclusions of law are unnecessary in decisions on motions **unless findings are required by a particular rule.**" *(VERIFIED.)* MCR 3.210(D)(1) is that particular rule, and a contested postjudgment motion to modify parenting time is exactly what it names.
>
> Two provisions take the pressure off if you forget to ask — **MCR 2.517(A)(6)**: "Requests for findings are not necessary for purposes of review," and **(A)(7)**: "No exception need be taken to a finding or decision." *(VERIFIED.)* Ask anyway.
>
> And if findings are entered but incomplete: **MCR 2.517(B)** allows a motion to amend or add findings **within 21 days after entry of judgment.** *(VERIFIED.)* ⚠ **TIME-CRITICAL** — another clock triggered by entry.
>
> A worked version of this request, in the words to actually say, is at `draft/P1-worked-examples.md` **Part Four**.

# Part B — At the hearing: a running order

1. **Arrive early.** Bring three tabbed exhibit sets, the file copy of your order, your motion and brief, a pen, and a single page of notes.
2. **Confirm it is being recorded** — MCR 3.215(D)(4) requires an electronic or stenographic record of all hearings.
3. **State what you are asking for in one sentence**, the same sentence from the motion.
4. **Move through the exhibits in date order.** Identify each one, say what it is, say how you know, offer it.
5. **Testify to the dates.** No adjectives. No motive. No characterization.
6. **Concede what is true**, especially about the child's current living arrangement. Conceding the established custodial environment is not a loss — it is what puts you in the lower-burden lane (*Shade*; *Pierron*). Document 2, Part A.2.
7. **Answer the other side's points factually**, or not at all.
8. **Hand up the proposed order.** It should already be attached to the motion and served with it — Genesee LCR 2.119(B) — but have a clean copy.
9. **Ask for findings on the record** on the contested points.
10. **Write down, before you leave the building:** the date, who appeared, what was decided or taken under advisement, what the referee said about timing, and **whether anything was handed to you.** That last one starts a clock.

# Part C — After the hearing: the clocks

## C.1 The recommendation and the proposed recommended order

> **MCR 3.215(E)(1):** "Within 21 days after a hearing, the referee must either make a statement of findings on the record or submit a written, signed report containing a summary of testimony and a statement of findings. In either event, the referee must make a recommendation for an order and arrange for it to be submitted to the court and the attorneys for the parties, or the parties if they are not represented by counsel. A proof of service must be filed with the court."
>
> **(1)(b)** The referee's recommended order must include … "(ii) notice that if the recommended order is approved by the court and **no written objection is filed with the court clerk within 21 days after the recommended order is served, the recommended order will become the final order**; (iii) notice advising the parties of any interim effect the recommended order may have; and (iv) prominent notice of all available methods for obtaining a judicial hearing."
>
> **(1)(c)** "If the court approves the referee's recommended order, the recommended order must be served **within 7 days of approval, or within 3 days of approval if the recommended order is given interim effect** …"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

## C.2 ⚠ The 7-day clock almost nobody knows about

A party may draft the proposed recommended order and serve it on you:

> **MCR 3.215(E)(3)(d):** "within 7 days after the conclusion of the referee hearing, a party may serve a copy of a proposed recommended order on all other parties with a notice to them that it will be submitted to the referee for approval **if no written objections to its accuracy or completeness are filed with the court clerk within 7 days after service of the notice**…
> (ii) To object to the accuracy or completeness of a proposed recommended order, the party must **within 7 days after service of the proposed order, file written objections with the court clerk that state with specificity the inaccuracy or omission** in the proposed recommended order, and **serve the objections on all parties as required by MCR 2.107, together with a notice of hearing and an alternative proposed recommended order**."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**Read what that requires, because it is three things, not one:**

1. Written objections filed with the **clerk**, stating **specifically** what is inaccurate or omitted;
2. **Served** on all parties per MCR 2.107 — see Document 3;
3. Together with a **notice of hearing** *and* an **alternative proposed recommended order** you have drafted.

Seven days. **If a proposed recommended order arrives in your mail, that is the day your week starts.** 🔴 **ACT TODAY**

## C.3 The 21-day objection — and a trigger discrepancy you must plan around

> **MCR 3.215(E)(4):** "A party may obtain a judicial hearing on any matter that has been the subject of a referee hearing and that resulted in a statement of findings and a recommended order **by filing a written objection and notice of hearing within 21 days after the referee's recommendation for an order is served** on the attorneys for the parties, or the parties if they are not represented by counsel. **The objection must include a clear and concise statement of the specific findings or application of law to which an objection is made.** Objections regarding the accuracy or completeness of the recommendation must state with specificity the inaccuracy or omission."
>
> **MCR 3.215(E)(5):** "The party who requests a judicial hearing **must serve the objection and notice of hearing on the opposing party or counsel in the manner provided in MCR 2.119(C)**."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

And the statute:

> **MCL 552.507(4):** "The court shall hold a de novo hearing on any matter that has been the subject of a referee hearing, upon the written request of either party or upon motion of the court. **The request of a party shall be made within 21 days after the recommendation of the referee is made available to that party.**"

*(VERIFIED verbatim — legislature.mi.gov full-section fetch, August 9, 2026.)*

> ### ⚠⚠ THE TRIGGERS ARE NOT THE SAME WORD
>
> The court rule counts 21 days from when the recommendation is **served**. The statute counts 21 days from when it is **made available to that party**.
>
> Those can be different dates. A recommendation handed to you at the end of the hearing is arguably "made available" that day, while service by mail happens later.
>
> **This project does not resolve which controls.** `⊗ CONFLICTED — both texts verified; no case read on the interaction.`
>
> **The instruction all readings converge on: count 21 days from the EARLIEST date you could have had it, and act well before that.** Write the date on a calendar the day anything arrives. Then treat your real deadline as a week earlier. 🔴 **ACT TODAY**

**Three more things about the 21 days:**

- **You must file BOTH the objection AND a notice of hearing.** An objection alone is not enough — the rule text requires both.
- **The objection must be specific.** "I object to the recommendation" is not an objection. Name the finding. Name the legal conclusion.
- **Serving it is governed by MCR 2.119(C)** — the same 9-day-mail / 7-day-delivery deadlines as a motion, and the same service analysis as Document 3. Note that MCR 3.215(E)(5) expressly names "the opposing party **or counsel**," which is one of the few postjudgment rules that specifically contemplates attorney service. **The safe course remains: serve both.**

**Then:** MCR 3.215(F)(1) — "The judicial hearing must be held within 21 days after the written objection is filed, unless the time is extended by the court for good cause." *(VERIFIED.)*

## C.4 ⚠ The consent trap

> **MCR 3.215(E)(8):** "The parties may **waive their right to object** to the referee's recommendation for an order by **consenting in writing to the immediate entry of the recommended order**."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**Signing the recommended order gives up the judicial hearing.** If someone hands you a recommended order in the hallway and asks you to sign so it can be entered today, understand exactly what that signature does. If the recommendation gives you what you asked for in specific, enforceable terms, signing may be the right call. If it does not, **do not sign in a hallway.** Take it home. Read it against your proposed order line by line. You have 21 days; you do not have to use them, but you have them.

## C.5 Interim effect

> **MCR 3.215(G)(1):** the court may, by administrative order or by an order in the case, provide that the referee's recommended order will take effect on an **interim basis pending a judicial hearing**, with notice of that under a separate heading.
>
> **(G)(2)** No interim effect for: an order for incarceration; forfeiture of property; or an order imposing costs, fines, or other sanctions.
>
> **(G)(3)** The court may not, **by administrative order**, give interim effect to an order under (G)(2), an order that **changes a child's custody**, an order that **changes a child's domicile**, or an order that would render subsequent judicial consideration of the matter moot.

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**Note what is absent from the (G)(3) list: parenting time.** A parenting-time recommendation is not among the categories excluded from interim effect by administrative order. That cuts both ways — a favorable recommendation may begin operating before the judicial hearing, and an unfavorable one may too. **Read the recommended order for the interim-effect heading the rule requires**, and calendar accordingly. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — whether Genesee has an administrative order giving interim effect is a local fact; ask the clerk.`

And MCL 552.507(7): "Pending a de novo hearing, the referee's recommended order may be presented to the court for entry as an interim order as provided by the Michigan court rules. **The interim order shall be served on the parties within 3 days** and shall be subject to review as provided under this subsection." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

## C.6 What "de novo" actually means — and what it does not

> **MCL 552.507(5):** "A hearing is de novo despite the court's imposition of reasonable restrictions and conditions to conserve the resources of the parties and the court if the following conditions are met: (a) The parties have been given **a full opportunity to present and preserve important evidence at the referee hearing.** (b) For findings of fact to which the parties have objected, the parties are afforded a new opportunity to offer the same evidence to the court as was presented to the referee and to supplement that evidence with evidence that could not have been presented to the referee."
>
> **MCL 552.507(6):** "Subject to subsection (5), de novo hearings include, but are not limited to, the following: **(a) A new decision based entirely on the record of a previous hearing**, including any memoranda, recommendations, or proposed orders by the referee. (b) A new decision based only on evidence presented at the time of the de novo hearing. (c) A new decision based in part on the record of a referee hearing supplemented by evidence that was not introduced at a previous hearing."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**"De novo" does not mean "a do-over."** By statute, a de novo hearing can be a **new decision based entirely on the record of the referee hearing.** Read subsection (5)(a) again: the whole structure rests on your having had "a full opportunity to present and preserve important evidence **at the referee hearing.**"

**That is the lesson of this document in one line: the referee hearing is the record.** And remember the counterweight in MCR 3.215(F)(2) — the judge **must** allow live evidence at the judicial hearing, whatever restrictions are imposed. Both halves are true, and you need to know both.

## C.7 ⚠ The cost of a bad objection

> **MCR 3.215(F)(3):** "If the court determines that an objection is **frivolous or has been interposed for the purpose of delay**, the court may assess reasonable costs and attorney fees."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

Object to specific findings you can name and explain. Do not object because you are unhappy. And remember the wider exposure: MCR 1.109(E)(5)–(7) (your signature certifies the document is well grounded and not interposed to harass, with a mandatory sanction for violation) and MCL 600.2591 (frivolous claims and defenses — costs and fees to the prevailing party). *(VERIFIED.)* **These run at you as easily as at anyone else.**

# Part D — Doing the objection right, this time

The prior filing in this case did not survive a service challenge. If you object to a recommendation, that is the moment the same failure can repeat — and the objection carries **more** service requirements than a motion does, not fewer.

**The objection packet:**

| # | Component | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Written objection** naming the specific findings or applications of law objected to | MCR 3.215(E)(4) |
| 2 | **Notice of hearing** — required, not optional | MCR 3.215(E)(4) |
| 3 | Filed with the **court clerk** within 21 days | MCR 3.215(E)(4); MCL 552.507(4) |
| 4 | **Served** in the manner provided in MCR 2.119(C) — 9 days by mail, 7 by delivery, before the hearing date | MCR 3.215(E)(5) |
| 5 | **Proof of service**, filed promptly and at or before the hearing | MCR 2.107(D) |
| 6 | **Concurrence certificate** and **proposed order** if the court treats it as a motion | Genesee LCR 2.119(A), (B) `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask whether the objection requires them` |
| 7 | **Notice of intent to use the referee record**, at least **7 days** before the judicial hearing, if you will offer evidence from it | MCR 3.215(D)(4)(c) |
| 8 | Copy for the **Friend of the Court**, marked, at filing | MCR 3.203(G) |

*(All VERIFIED except item 6, flagged.)*

**Item 7 is easy to miss:** MCR 3.215(D)(4)(c) — "At least 7 days before the judicial hearing, a party who intends to offer evidence from the record of the referee hearing must provide notice to the court and each other party. If a stenographic transcript is necessary … the party offering the evidence must pay for the transcript." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)* **Transcripts cost money and take time. Ask about cost the day you decide to object, not the week of the hearing.**

**And item 3's clock:** if the court itself uses the referee record to limit the judicial hearing, MCR 3.215(D)(4)(d) gives you **7 days** from the date the record is provided to file **supplemental objections**. *(VERIFIED.)* Another clock; another calendar entry.

## Exercise — done when

**Before the hearing:**

1. Write the one-sentence statement of relief at the top of your notes.
2. Build the proof table from A.5 for **your** facts: every element, its exhibit, and who sponsors it.
3. Assemble three identical tabbed exhibit sets, pre-marked.
4. Decide whether you need any witness or record from a third party, and — if so — start the subpoena track **now**, using the correct one of the two rules in A.4.
5. Write out the ECE concession sentence in advance, in neutral words.
6. Read your exhibit list out loud and remove anything that is about a person rather than an event.

**After the hearing:**

7. Write the date and what happened before you leave the building.
8. The moment anything arrives — a proposed recommended order, a recommendation, an approved order — write on it the date **you received it**, and enter **two** dates in your calendar: the 7-day date and the 21-day date, counted from the earliest possible trigger.

**You are done when:**

- Every element of the motion has an exhibit and a sponsoring witness written next to it, and
- Nothing on the exhibit list depends on the child having said anything, and
- The subpoena track, if needed, was started with time to spare under MCR 2.506(C)(1) or MCR 2.305(A)(3), and
- Both post-hearing clocks are on a calendar you actually check, counted from the earliest date the document could have been available to you.

## When to stop and get help

- You receive a **recommendation you want to object to** and cannot state, in one sentence, which specific finding is wrong. Get advice before you file. A vague objection risks MCR 3.215(F)(3) costs.
- The other side seeks **costs or fees** against you.
- Anyone asks the **child** to speak to anyone, or suggests the child should be interviewed. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- A **CPS, police, or criminal** matter surfaces at or after the hearing. Stop. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- The recommendation changes **custody** rather than parenting time. Different burden, different appeal landscape — and an order granting or denying a change of legal custody, physical custody, or domicile **is** appealable of right (MCR 7.202(6)(a)(iii)), on a **21-day jurisdictional** clock (MCR 7.204(A)). *(VERIFIED.)* 🔴 **ACT TODAY**
- Any **recording** comes up, in any form. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`

**UNVERIFIED — carried as visible flags:**

1. ~~**The FOC report paradox.**~~ **RESOLVED after this draft was written — the opinion text has now been read** (`verification_ledger.md` §4.2, same evening). *Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich App 77 (1989): the report is **not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree**; it may be considered as background/context (which reconciles MRE 1101(b)(9)); findings must rest on competent hearing evidence; and *Truitt* (quoting *Marshall v Beal*) holds that adopting the report's findings without stipulation is **reversible error**.
