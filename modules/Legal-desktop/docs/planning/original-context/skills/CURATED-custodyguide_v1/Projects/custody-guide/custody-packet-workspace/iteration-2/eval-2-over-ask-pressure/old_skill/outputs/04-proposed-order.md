> **Drafting aid. Not attorney-reviewed. NOT filing-ready.** **Genesee LCR 2.119(B):
> "A proposed order must be attached to and served with the motion."** (VERIFIED verbatim.)
> This is a binding local rule, not a suggestion.
>
> **The test for every line: could a stranger execute it next week without asking a
> question?** If not, rewrite the line. There is no "as agreed," "reasonable," or
> "mutually convenient" anywhere in this order — putting those words back would reinstate
> the exact defect the motion exists to cure.

### Why this schedule, and why it is sized this way

The regular schedule below produces **approximately 104 overnights a year** — the figure
your support order already recites. You are asking the court to write down, with times and
places, the overnights the record already assumes. That is what keeps this a
parenting-time order and not a custody change: no change to primary residence, school, or
day-to-day care, and no swing approaching the **21-overnight** mark the child-support
formula treats as "substantial" (2025 MCSF §3.03(D), VERIFIED).

**Deliberately not in this order** (deferred to the later 50/50 phase — see response §5):
equal alternating weeks, a six-week summer block, and splitting every holiday on the day.
Those are the elements that, combined, get a "parenting time" motion recharacterized as a
custody motion. The holiday **rotation** below still gives you half of the holidays over
each two-year cycle — what you want, in the form a court will sign.

> **Dates:** this aid anchors the schedule to entry ("the first Friday after entry"). If
> the court prefers dates certain, convert each anchor to a specific date at the hearing
> and **check every weekday against a real calendar first.** Leave the year-parity
> (odd/even) rows as written.

### Read your draft against this checklist before you attach it

- [ ] Every period has a start **time** and an end **time**.
- [ ] Every exchange has a **named place** and a backup place.
- [ ] Every holiday row has times and a year-parity rule.
- [ ] Nothing anywhere says "as agreed," "reasonable," or "mutually convenient."
- [ ] Paragraph 11 (the MCL 722.27a(10) Hague clause) is present — it is **mandatory
      content**, and leaving it out is a defect.
- [ ] Transportation says **who** and **who pays**.
- [ ] Paragraph 6's forfeiture rule and paragraph 9's response window bind **both**
      parents — a rule that binds only one parent is a rule a court is right to distrust.
- [ ] A stranger holding only this order could execute next Saturday without asking you a
      question.

### What the quiet paragraphs are doing

- **Paragraph 6's 30-minute forfeiture rule and paragraph 9's 48-hour response window cut
  against you as readily as against anyone.** That symmetry is why a court will sign them
  — and it removes the argument that would otherwise happen in a parking lot.
- **Paragraph 9 disclaims itself:** "Nothing in this paragraph authorizes any contact
  between the parents other than written communication through this method." That sentence
  keeps a request to define the communication channel from reading as a request to loosen
  anything. Leave the method blank — naming a paid app invites a fight about cost and
  access, and no app is inherently court-favored. Be ready for the court to choose email
  limited to child matters.
- **Paragraph 14 leaves the rest of the March 15, 2023 order alone.** That is what makes
  this a parenting-time order and not a custody order.

### Optional: a short ramp (drafting judgment only)

If you and the court prefer to re-establish regular contact gradually for a six-year-old,
you can precede paragraph 1 with two to three weeks of Saturday daytime periods (e.g.,
10:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m.) that then step up to the full schedule on a stated date, with no
further motion required. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — no authority states courts prefer a ramp;
it is a way to write an order, not a rule of law.` It also reads as measured rather than
abrupt, which reinforces the ECE-neutral framing.
