## Why this document exists

Service is the most common way a self-represented parent loses without losing: the papers are fine, the service fails, the merits are never reached. Service is mechanical — a checklist and a calendar, to the right people, by the right dates, proved on paper. Nothing about it requires persuasion or luck, only doing it in the right order.

## Route tag

**Postjudgment, domestic relations, Genesee County, opposing party represented by an attorney (J. Marsh).** Some of what follows changes if any of those four facts changes.

# Part A — Who must be served

## A.1 The rule everyone gets wrong — service when the other side has a lawyer

The intuitive rule — "the other side has a lawyer, so I serve the lawyer" — is only the opening clause of MCR 2.107(B)(1). The exception that fits a postjudgment case is **(B)(1)(c)**:

> **MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c):** "After a final judgment or final order has been entered and the time for an appeal of right has passed, **documents must be served on the party** unless the rule governing the particular postjudgment procedure specifically allows service on the attorney."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

Postjudgment domestic-relations motions are governed by MCR 2.119 — MCR 3.213. MCR 2.119 does **not** specifically allow attorney service. (Contrast MCR 3.215(E)(5), which for a referee objection expressly says serve "the opposing party **or counsel**" — that is what a rule specifically allowing attorney service looks like.) So the postjudgment default here runs to the **party**. Separately, MCR 3.203(I)(1) requires copies of party notices to also go to the attorney of record.

## A.2 The instruction that survives every reading

> **Serve the party. Also serve the attorney. Same papers, same day, both by first-class mail. Prove both on one proof of service.**

Read one way, the rules require party service (MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c)) plus attorney copies (MCR 3.203(I)(1)). Read the other way, attorney service (the opening clause). **Doing both satisfies either reading and violates none.** There is no downside except a second stamp.

| Who | Where | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **The other parent (the party)** | **PO Box 100, Flint, MI 48501** — the Friend of the Court **address of record** | MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c); MCR 3.203(A)(2), (B) |
| **J. Marsh (attorney of record)** | **200 Main St, Flint, MI 48502** — last known business address | MCR 2.107(B)(1); MCR 3.203(I)(1) |
| **The Friend of the Court** | One copy marked "FRIEND OF THE COURT," handed to the **clerk** at filing | MCR 3.203(G) |

**On the party's address.** MCR 3.203(B) defines the last known mailing address as the one the party provided in writing to the FOC or set in the most recent order. **The address of record is the service address by rule** — you serve PO Box 100 even if you believe the other parent lives elsewhere. A stale or PO-box address is the other party's own compliance matter, not yours; you serve the address of record and prove it. Confirm with the FOC that PO Box 100 is still the address of record before you mail.

## A.3 The communication instruction and service are two different things

Serving court papers is a step the rules require; it is not the "contact" a mail-only instruction is aimed at. But **do not create the argument**:

- **Have a competent adult who is not you do the mailing**, and have that person sign the proof of service. MCR 2.107(D) requires the proof to be a verified statement by the person who served. (Service of *process* — a summons and complaint — must be by a non-party under MCR 2.103(A); you are long past that stage, but using a non-party mailer here costs nothing and removes any argument.)
- **Mail. Do not hand anything to the other parent yourself** — not at a door, not at an exchange, not through a relative.
- **The envelope contains the court papers and nothing else.** No note, no message.

## A.4 The Friend of the Court copy — the step people forget

> **MCR 3.203(G):** where a child of the parties is under 18 or support is involved, "the parties must provide the friend of the court with a copy of all pleadings and other documents filed in the action. The copy must be marked 'friend of the court' and submitted to the court clerk at the time of filing." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

One extra copy, "FRIEND OF THE COURT" on the front, handed to the clerk with everything else. If Genesee is on e-filing, the transfer works differently — ask.

# Part B — How to serve

> **MCR 2.107(C)(3):** "Mailing a copy under this rule means enclosing it in a sealed envelope with first class postage fully prepaid, addressed to the person to be served, and depositing the envelope and its contents in the United States mail. **Service by mail is complete at the time of mailing.**" *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

- **The service date is the date you drop it in the mail,** not the date it arrives. That is what makes the 9-day mail deadline workable.
- **Certified mail is first-class mail with tracking added.** It satisfies the rule and gives you a receipt. Use certified mail with return receipt for your own file, but understand the receipt is for you, not for the rule.
- **There is a conflicting electronic-service directive in the rule** (MCR 2.107(G)) that this project does **not** resolve. `⊗ CONFLICTED`. The convergent safe course: **serve by first-class mail and use the 9-day deadline.** If you have an email for J. Marsh, you may also email the same documents the same day as a courtesy and say in the email that they are also served by mail. Do not rely on email alone, and do not email the other parent directly.

# Part C — The calendar: two deadlines, and why they are two

> **MCR 2.119(C)(1):** a motion, notice of hearing, and supporting papers must be **served** "(a) at least **9 days** before the time set for the hearing, if served by first-class mail, or (b) at least **7 days** before … if served by delivery."
> **MCR 2.119(C)(4):** "Unless the court sets a different time, a motion must be **filed** at least **7 days** before the hearing …"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**These are two separate obligations.** (C)(1) is about **service**; (C)(4) is about **filing**. Genesee's posted 2019 motion-practice notice states only the filing rule — a parent who reads only that notice and mails 7 days before the hearing is **two days late on service.** Meet **both**.

## Worked backward calendar — ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY

> These dates are an **example**, not your hearing. Fill in your real hearing date once the court assigns it, then count backward the same way. Every weekday label below is checked against a real calendar.

**Illustrative hearing: Friday, October 16, 2026.**

| Count back | Date | Day | |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Oct 16 | Friday | **HEARING** |
| 7 | Oct 9 | Friday | **last day to FILE** (MCR 2.119(C)(4)) |
| 9 | Oct 7 | Wednesday | **last day to SERVE BY MAIL** (MCR 2.119(C)(1)(a)) |

Look at what sits inside that window: **Monday, October 12, 2026 is Columbus Day / Indigenous Peoples' Day** — a day the courthouse may be closed. A closed court does not extend a backward-counted deadline; it just means you cannot file that day. Two weekend days sit inside the last nine as well. **This is why you never land on the last legal day.**

**The buffered plan:** file **and** mail on the same day, at least **twice the minimum** ahead — here, by roughly the **first week of October**. File first, then mail the same day. (The filing minimum, 7 days, is shorter than the mail-service minimum, 9 days, so filing at the last legal moment makes on-time service impossible. Both numbers are minimums, not targets.)

**Counting note.** MCR 1.108 is written for periods running forward; motion deadlines run backward from the hearing. The working method — count backward from the hearing day, not counting that day — is stated as practice, not as a verified rule of construction. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` — which is another reason to build in buffer.

## No reply brief

MCR 2.119(A)(2)(b): absent the court's permission, **no reply briefs may be filed.** The other side's response is served at least 5 days before (mail) and filed 3 days before. **Plan to answer a response out loud at the hearing, not on paper** — so anticipate their arguments in your opening brief.

# Part D — Proving it, filing it, paying (or not)

## D.1 The proof of service

Use `07-proof-of-service.md`. Rules for completing it: **list every document by name** (if the proposed order is not named, there is no record you complied with LCR 2.119(B)); **the date is the mailing date**; **the signer is the person who mailed it**; **copy the MCR 1.109(D)(3)(b) declaration exactly**; **attach nothing** — keep certified receipts in your own file.

**Keep a service file** for each mailing: a complete copy of the exact set mailed, the certified-mail receipt, the return-receipt card or electronic delivery record, and the filed date-stamped proof of service. This is the file that answers a service challenge — the file that did not exist last time.

## D.2 Where the papers go

**File at the Circuit Court Records counter — not at the Friend of the Court office.** They are different offices. The FOC gets its copy from the clerk. Handing your motion to the FOC is not filing it. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — Genesee filing counter location and hours not confirmed; call first.`

**Formatting that gets a filing rejected** (MCR 1.109(D)(1)(a), MCR 2.119(A)(2)(c)): 8½ × 11 paper, legible, English; body text **12 or 13 point**; at least **one-inch margins**; combined motion + brief no more than **20 pages double spaced**, exclusive of attachments. A clerk **may reject nonconforming documents** (MCR 1.109(D)(6)). The **caption** must carry the court, the parties, the full case number with its suffix, the document title, and — for a self-represented party — your name, address, and telephone (MCR 1.109(D)(1)(b)).

## D.3 The fee, and fee waiver

A motion fee is due on filing; **all motions filed at the same time draw a single fee** (MCR 2.119(G)(2)), and **asking for a fee waiver is itself free** (MCR 2.119(G)(3)(c)). The dollar amount is a Genesee local fact. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask at the counter.`

If you cannot pay, file a **Fee Waiver Request** (SCAO form, commonly **MC 20** `ℹ PROVISIONAL`) **with** the motion — MCR 2.002(B). If you receive any **means-tested public assistance**, the **clerk must waive** (MCR 2.002(C)); otherwise the court must waive at or below **125% of the federal poverty guidelines**, or on a hardship showing above it (MCR 2.002(F)). A judge decides within **3 business days** (MCR 2.002(G)). **⚠ TIME-CRITICAL:** if denied, you must pay **within 14 days from the date the clerk sends notice, or the filing is rejected** and your hearing date goes with it (MCR 2.002(G)(1)). Put that 14-day date on a calendar the day you file.

## D.4 The praecipe question

**Genesee's local rules contain no praecipe requirement** — Rule 2.119 has only (A) concurrence, (B) proposed order, (C) application, and no local motion-timing rule. *(VERIFIED — complete Genesee section read.)* But local scheduling practice is not always written in the rules. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask: "Do I need a praecipe, and if so where is that requirement written?"`

## When to stop and get help

- The other side files anything arguing your **service was defective**. Read it the day it arrives, note every date it asserts, get advice immediately. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- The FOC address of record turns out to be confidential by court order, or you cannot confirm it. Ask the FOC; do not investigate.
- J. Marsh withdraws, or a new attorney appears. Who must be served changes.
- Anyone says a deadline was extended. Get it endorsed in writing on the notice of hearing or in a separate order — MCR 2.119(C)(3). A verbal assurance is not that.
- Your filing is rejected for any reason. Fix and refile the same week; do not wait for the hearing.

**Free / low-cost help in Genesee County** `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm before relying`: Genesee County Legal Resource Center (courthouse); Legal Services of Eastern Michigan (Flint); YWCA Greater Flint; Michigan Legal Help (michiganlegalhelp.org); State Bar of Michigan lawyer referral — ask specifically about **limited-scope representation**, a lawyer for one hearing or one document.
