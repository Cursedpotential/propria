## The strategy, in plain terms

Your goal is the first and most winnable move in a three-step campaign:

1. **Kill "as the parties agree."** Get a specific schedule — days, times, exchange place — plus the logistics that make it work. That is this packet.
2. **Make-up time** for denied periods, through the Friend of the Court, once you have an enforceable term to enforce. (The proposed order even builds a self-executing make-up clause in — paragraph 8.)
3. **Toward more time later**, after a working schedule and a clean record rebuild the routine. Not now — asking for too much in the first motion backfires (see below).

**The lead law is a statute written for exactly your situation.** MCL 722.27a(8): *"Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time."* Mandatory ("shall"), open to either parent, no deadline ("at any time"). The Court of Appeals enforced it in *Pickering v Pickering* — holding that even "reasonable and liberal parenting time" is "plainly not" specific, and that refusing to consider specific terms once requested is legal error. "As the parties agree" is *less* specific than what *Pickering* rejected.

**The one honest limit** — and the reason the brief is built the way it is: no court has been read (for the source project behind this packet) on how subsection (8) interacts with the "proper cause or change of circumstances" threshold that applies to *modifying* an order. So the brief **pleads both**: subsection (8) as the lead, and the threshold in the alternative. If the Court agrees (8) applies directly, you win on the short path; if it wants a threshold showing, you have already made it. **Do not let anyone talk you into claiming (8) is settled law that erases the threshold — that is the fastest way to lose.**

## Why your facts fit this unusually well

Two things in your facts make this motion stronger than average, and I built the brief around them:

- **Your ask is modest — deliberately below the line that turns a parenting-time motion into a custody fight.** Alternating weekends (Friday 6 p.m.–Sunday 6 p.m.) is about **52 overnights a year**; the Wednesday dinner adds none. Your **support order already credits you with 104 overnights.** So you are asking the Court to write down *less* overnight time than the order already assumes. A request for less time than is already presumed cannot plausibly be a disguised bid to change where your daughter lives. That is the argument that defeats the recharacterization trap (an over-ask — equal time + holidays + summers in one motion — is what gets relabeled a custody motion and buried under the higher burden).
- **The "mail-only" restriction is not in any order.** Your order says *"the parties shall communicate regarding parenting time"* — that is direct party communication. The mail-only narrowing came only from **J. Marsh's letter of January 8, 2025**, which is not an order of the court. The motion states that as a neutral fact, attaches the letter, and asks the Court to state a specific written communication *method* under MCL 722.27a(9)(i) — making the order's existing communication provision specific, not loosening any court-ordered restriction. Your texting was consistent with the order's own words, which supports your clean conduct record.

Your conduct audit — a dated log, no order violations, current on support, no missed exchanges — is pleaded as **dated facts**, never as adjectives. That is what makes it land.

## Cautions I want to be explicit about

- **Support stays out of this motion.** Parenting time and support are separate; joining them invites the argument that you want time to cut a payment. And the overnight gap cuts **both ways**: because your *actual* exercised overnights are far below the ordered 104, a future support proceeding could go against you as easily as for you. Get the enforceable schedule first; look at support separately, later, with eyes open. (The reason the sequence runs schedule-first is in the brief's drafting notes.)
- **Two legal unknowns travel with the packet as flags, not as settled law:** (a) whether subsection (8) removes the modification threshold — pleaded in the alternative; (b) after any referee recommendation, the 21-day objection clock has a **trigger conflict** ("made available" vs. "served") — the safe move is to count from the earliest date and file well before it. See `10-hearing-prep-referee.md`.
- **The service step is where the last filing died.** Serve **both** the party (at the FOC address of record, PO Box 100) **and** J. Marsh, same papers, same day, one proof of service, and have a competent adult who is not you do the mailing. `09-service-pack.md` is the whole method.
- **Your daughter carries nothing, is asked nothing, is told nothing.** No exhibit or argument may come from asking her anything. This is absolute.
- **Stop and get counsel** if any of these appears: a PPO, CPS, police, or criminal matter; **any** recording (existing or considered); another state or country; or if what you actually want becomes a change in where she lives. Free/low-cost help in Genesee County (`ℹ confirm hours`): **Genesee County Legal Resource Center** (courthouse), **Legal Services of Eastern Michigan** (Flint), **YWCA Greater Flint**, **Michigan Legal Help** (michiganlegalhelp.org), and the **State Bar lawyer referral** — ask specifically for **limited-scope** help (a lawyer for one hearing or one document), which you can often afford even when full representation is out of reach.
- **Nothing here is filing-ready,** and no document may be labeled that way. Read each one, fill the brackets, run the checklists, make your clerk call.
