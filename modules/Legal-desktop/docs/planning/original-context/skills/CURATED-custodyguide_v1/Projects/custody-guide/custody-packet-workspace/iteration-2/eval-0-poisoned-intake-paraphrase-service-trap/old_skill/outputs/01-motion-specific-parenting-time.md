## Route tag

**Postjudgment.** An order already exists in Genesee County Case No. **24-777888-DC**. You are asking the court to make an existing, open-ended parenting-time provision **specific**, and to set the logistics that make it work. This is **not** an appeal, **not** a reconsideration, and **not** a request to change custody. A new request about the same child is filed **as a motion in the existing case** — MCR 3.204(A)(1); postjudgment domestic-relations motions are governed by MCR 2.119 — MCR 3.213. *(VERIFIED.)*

## Drafting rules for the numbered paragraphs (read before you edit)

- **One fact per paragraph, each with a date.**
- **Quote the order; never characterize it.**
- **No adjectives about a person. Not one.** The banned words appear nowhere: narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, personality disorder. A filing containing them becomes a filing about the person who wrote it.
- **The history gets one neutral sentence, if any.** Everything about the spring-2023 hearing is history, and every window to relitigate it closed long ago. Leave it out.

## Notes attached to specific paragraphs — these are for you, not for the court

**On Paragraph 4 (your request record) — two real issues:**

1. **Form.** You said you asked "by text." Texts can be evidence, but the project's documentation protocol treats **certified mail with return receipt** as the reliable spine, because it proves both *sending* and *the address it went to*. Before or alongside filing, send a fresh, short, dated written request through a channel you can prove, proposing **two specific date/time/place options** and asking for a written answer by a named date. (See `08-service-filing-checklist-and-clerk-call.md` and the campaign note in the hand-back.)
2. **The gap.** Your three documented requests are from **February–April 2025**. As of today (2026-08-11) the most recent is **496 days** old. That gap is the single fact the other side is most likely to use — MCL 722.27a(7)(g) lets a court weigh a parent's "failure to exercise" parenting time. It does **not** defeat your right to ask for specific terms "at any time" under (8), but a fresh request or two, documented, blunts the argument. This is a strategic judgment that is yours to make; I am flagging it, not blocking on it.

**On Paragraphs 5–6 (the ECE facts):** these are the facts that keep your modest ask in the lower-burden lane (see brief, Part III). They cost you two short paragraphs and they are the exact facts the court must find anyway. Fill them honestly.
