## Three things to understand before you sign

1. **You must actually do it before you can certify it.** Because the other party has counsel of record (D. Blake, 45 Court St, Flint MI 48502), that is who you contact for concurrence. The practical move: mail a short, separate concurrence-request letter (its own letter, not mixed with a parenting-time request) to counsel by **certified mail with return receipt**, enclosing the proposed order, asking whether the other side will concur, and stating a date for an answer. Then this certificate writes itself and the mailing receipt proves it.

2. **Sign honestly.** Your signature carries **MCR 1.109(E)(5)**: it certifies you read the document, that to the best of your knowledge after reasonable inquiry it is well grounded in fact and warranted by law or a good-faith argument, and **that it is not interposed for any improper purpose, such as to harass or cause unnecessary delay.** MCR 1.109(E)(6) requires the court to sanction a violation. *(VERIFIED.)*

3. **Pick the branch that is true.** If you contacted counsel and got no answer, that is the "reasonable and diligent attempts" branch. If concurrence was expressly denied, that is the first branch. Do not check the "denied" box unless it was actually denied.

**Disposition:** Genesee LCR 2.119(A) ✅ VERIFIED verbatim; MCR 1.109(E)(5)–(6) ✅ VERIFIED. Whether a Genesee judge expects the certificate on its own page or incorporated in the motion — `ℹ PROVISIONAL, ask the Records counter`.
