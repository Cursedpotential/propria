## Read your draft order against this checklist before you attach it

- [ ] Every period has a start **time** and an end **time**.
- [ ] Every exchange has a **named place**, and a backup place.
- [ ] Nothing anywhere says "as agreed," "reasonable," "mutually convenient," or "as the parties arrange."
- [ ] The MCL 722.27a(10) Hague paragraph is present (paragraph 11).
- [ ] Transportation says **who** provides it and **who pays** (paragraph 4).
- [ ] The communication paragraph names a method or leaves a blank to be filled — it does not say "as agreed."
- [ ] A stranger holding only this order could execute the next scheduled period without asking a question.

## Notes on the drafting choices

- **The start dates are anchored to entry, not to a calendar date.** "The first Friday that is at least 7 days after the date this order is entered" is fully specific once the entry date is known, and it does not go stale if your hearing moves. The alternative — a fixed calendar date — is also fine, but then you must bring a **corrected proposed order** to the hearing if the date has passed, and say so on the record. Anchoring to entry avoids that.
- **Paragraph 6's forfeiture rule and paragraph 8's make-up rule cut both ways.** A 30-minute forfeiture and an automatic make-up remedy bind you exactly as they bind the other parent. That symmetry is deliberate — a rule that binds only one parent is a rule a court is right to distrust, and these rules remove the arguments that otherwise happen in a parking lot.
- **Paragraph 8 (make-up time) is drafting judgment, flagged.** Building the make-up remedy into the order means you do not have to file a fresh FOC complaint for every missed period. If you prefer to keep the order minimal, you can strike paragraph 8 and rely on the FOC make-up process (MCL 552.642) instead. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — no authority states courts prefer a self-executing make-up clause; it is a way to draft, not a rule of law.`
- **Paragraph 9 names no commercial app.** Treating any application as inherently court-favored is unsupported, and cost, access, and disability all matter. Leave the method as a blank, and be ready for the Court to choose something simple — including "email limited to matters concerning the child." The order already directs the parties to communicate; you are asking the Court to state the method, not to create a new channel.
- **Paragraph 9's last sentence disclaims itself.** "Nothing in this paragraph authorizes any contact between the parents other than written communication through this method." That sentence keeps the communication request from reading as an attempt to open a channel it is not.
- **Paragraph 12 (holidays) is optional and strikeable.** You asked for alternating weekends and one weekday dinner; holidays were not in your request. A modest, symmetric rotation is included so that holidays are specified rather than left to "as agreed." It deliberately does **not** add extended summer or school-break blocks — adding those would push the request toward the equal-time territory that gets recharacterized as a custody motion. Mother's Day and Father's Day are the same in both columns on purpose; alternating them is a common drafting error.
- **Paragraph 13 leaves the rest of the existing order alone.** That is what keeps this a parenting-time motion and not a custody motion.

> ### ⚠ If the hearing date moves
> This order's start dates depend on the entry date, so an adjournment does not break them. But confirm at the hearing that the entry-anchored language is acceptable to the Court; some judges and referees prefer dates certain. Bring a clean copy either way.
