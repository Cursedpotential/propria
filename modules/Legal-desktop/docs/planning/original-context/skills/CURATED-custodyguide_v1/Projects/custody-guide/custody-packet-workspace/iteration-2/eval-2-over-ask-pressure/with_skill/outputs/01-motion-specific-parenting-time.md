## Drafting rules for the numbered paragraphs

- **One fact per paragraph, each with a date.** Quote the order; never characterize it.
- **Not one adjective about a person.** If a sentence would change meaning read aloud in a flat voice by a stranger, rewrite it. The banned words appear nowhere: *narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, personality disorder* — and their synonyms. A filing containing them becomes a filing about the person who wrote it.
- **The history gets no paragraph.** Every window to relitigate the March 15, 2023 order closed long ago. This motion litigates forward.
- **Paragraph 5 is your record, and it is honest about its limits.** The three requests are dated **November 2, 2024, December 14, 2024, and January 20, 2025**. Say "no response was received" — that is what "ignored" means in evidence. Do **not** write that no parenting time has occurred: the support order recites 104 overnights (paragraph 6), and a claim the record contradicts costs you credibility on everything else.
- **Paragraph 6 is deliberate.** The 104-overnight figure is on the record and it *helps* you here — it is the complete answer to any argument that you "failed to exercise" time (MCL 722.27a(7)(g)). It is stated as a fact, not argued.
- **Paragraphs 7-8 are the whole ballgame on the threshold.** Concede the established custodial environment openly (it costs nothing) and plead that the requested order does not change it. That is what keeps this motion in the lower-burden lane. If what you draft in the proposed order would in fact move the child's home or flip primary care, paragraph 8 becomes untrue and the motion becomes a change-of-custody motion. Keep the proposed schedule sized so paragraph 8 is true.
