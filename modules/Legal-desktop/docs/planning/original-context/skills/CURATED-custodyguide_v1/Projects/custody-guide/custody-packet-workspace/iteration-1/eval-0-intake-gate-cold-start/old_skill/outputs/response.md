## The plan your packet will execute

Your instinct to attack the vague language head-on is correct, and Michigan law is unusually strong here:

- **Lead argument — the statute.** MCL 722.27a(8): *"Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time."* Mandatory ("shall"), no threshold stated, no time limit ("at any time"), available to either parent.
- **Lead case.** *Pickering v Pickering*, 268 Mich App 1 (2005), a published decision: language like "reasonable and liberal" is *plainly not* a grant of parenting time in "specific" terms, and once a parent asks for specific terms the court's refusal to consider them is legal error. "As the parties agree" is even less specific than the language rejected in *Pickering*. Timing is not a defense.
- **Backup argument, pleaded in the alternative.** Because your order already exists, we also plead the modification threshold under *Shade v Wright* and *Kaeb v Kaeb* — belt and suspenders, so the motion stands even if the court treats it as a modification.
- **The trap we design around.** We keep the *first* ask modest — a specific, ordinary weekly or every-other-week schedule with exchange logistics and a workable communication channel. We do **not** pile equal time + all holidays + summers into this motion. An over-ask gets recharacterized as a change-of-*custody* motion, which triggers a much higher legal standard (this is exactly what sank the father in *Kahl v Kasten*, a January 2026 Court of Appeals case). Modest and specific now; expansion comes later, after a clean record.

That is the packet: a **motion for specific parenting time**, a **proposed order with an actual schedule**, the **concurrence certificate** and **notice of hearing** Genesee requires, a **service pack** built so it cannot die on service the way your last filing did, and **hearing prep**.

**C. The child**
5. The **child's age** only — never the name or birthdate, and I won't record them. Age drives the schedule design (and a special rule applies for children under a year who are nursing).

**D. The support order**
6. The **number of overnights the child-support order recites.** Michigan's support formula requires the order to state one, and it tells us what the record currently assumes your parenting time to be.

**E. The record of requests and denials**
7. Your **dated log of requests**: each time you asked for parenting time, the date, how you asked (mail / email / through counsel), and the response or non-response. If you *haven't* been sending written requests, tell me that plainly — it doesn't sink the motion, but it changes what we file first (we'd start the request record before, or alongside, the motion).

8. I need both addresses to build that pack.

**G. Your proposed ask**
9. The **specific schedule you want to request** — days, times, and where exchanges happen. Keep it modest and ordinary for this first motion (e.g., alternating weekends plus a weekday, or similar). Tell me what you want and I'll help you size it so it stays *below* the line that would turn it into a custody fight.

**H. Your own honest self-audit — do this on paper before we file**
Every tool in this packet points both ways; the same statutes that score the other parent's missed time also score yours. Answer these for yourself so nothing is a surprise at the hearing:
- Have you contacted the other parent outside the permitted channel since the restriction started? If so, when?
- Is there anything on your social media about the other parent, the case, or the child's living situation?
- Have you asked the child anything about the other parent's household, or said anything about the other parent in front of the child?
- Have you missed any parenting time that was offered? When, and why?
- Are you current on support? (You may never withhold support or parenting time because of the other — they're independent. Being behind won't defeat this motion, but it will come up.)
- Is there a safety or protective-action explanation a court might accept for the other parent's conduct? If so, tell me the dated facts.
