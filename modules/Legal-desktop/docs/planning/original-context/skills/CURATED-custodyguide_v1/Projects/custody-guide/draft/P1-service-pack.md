## Why this document exists

A filing in this case did not survive a service challenge. The papers were prepared; the service failed; the merits were never reached.

**That is the most common way a self-represented parent loses without losing.** It is also the most preventable. Service is mechanical. It is a checklist and a calendar. Nothing about it requires judgment, persuasion, or luck — only doing it in the right order, to the right people, by the right dates, and proving it on paper.

Work this document with the motion in one hand and a calendar in the other.

# Part A — Who must be served

## A.1 The general rule

> **MCR 2.107(A)(1):** "Unless otherwise stated in this rule, every party who has filed a pleading, an appearance, or a motion must be served with a copy of every document later filed in the action. A nonparty who has filed a motion or appeared in response to a motion need only be served with documents that relate to that motion."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 2 updated May 1, 2026.)*

## A.2 The rule everyone gets wrong — service when the other side has a lawyer

Here is the subrule, in full, because the exceptions are the whole point:

> **MCR 2.107(B) Service on Attorney or Party.**
>
> **(1)** Service required or permitted to be made on a party for whom an attorney has appeared in the action must be made on the attorney except as follows:
>
> **(a)** The original service of the summons and complaint must be made on the party as provided by MCR 2.105;
>
> **(b)** When a contempt proceeding for disobeying a court order is initiated, the notice or order must be personally delivered to the party, unless the court orders otherwise;
>
> **(c) After a final judgment or final order has been entered and the time for an appeal of right has passed, documents must be served on the party unless the rule governing the particular postjudgment procedure specifically allows service on the attorney;**
>
> **(d)** The court may order service on the party;
>
> **(e)** If an attorney files a notice of limited appearance under MCR 2.117 on behalf of a self-represented party, service of every document later filed in the action must continue to be made on the party, and must also be made on the limited scope attorney for the duration of the limited appearance. At the request of the limited scope attorney, and if circumstances warrant, the court may order service to be made only on the party.
>
> **(2)** If two or more attorneys represent the same party, service of documents on one of the attorneys is sufficient. An attorney who represents more than one party is entitled to service of only one copy of a document.
>
> **(3)** If a party prosecutes or defends the action on his or her own behalf, service of documents must be made on the party in the manner provided by subrule (C).

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`.)*

> ### ⚠⚠ READ (B)(1)(c) AGAIN
>
> The intuitive rule — "the other side has a lawyer, so I serve the lawyer" — is **subrule (B)(1)'s opening clause**. Subrule **(B)(1)(c)** is an exception to it, and it is the exception that fits a postjudgment case:
>
> **After a final judgment or final order has been entered, and the time for an appeal of right has passed, documents must be served on *the party*** — unless the rule governing that particular postjudgment procedure specifically allows service on the attorney.
>
> **Does it apply here?** That turns on two questions of fact about your own case:
>
> 1. Is the order you are moving to change a **"final judgment or final order"**? In a case that resolved custody, parenting time, and support, it may well be. Read your order: does it state that it resolves the last pending claim and closes the case? MCR 2.602(A)(3) requires a judgment to say so immediately above the judge's signature. *(VERIFIED.)*
> 2. **Has the time for an appeal of right passed?** The appeal-of-right period is 21 days from entry, and it is jurisdictional — MCR 7.204(A). If the order is ten months old, it has passed.
>
> **Does a postjudgment rule "specifically allow" attorney service here?** Postjudgment motions in domestic relations actions are governed by MCR 2.119 — **MCR 3.213**. MCR 2.119 sets deadlines for serving a motion; it does not say to serve the attorney. *(VERIFIED.)* Contrast **MCR 3.215(E)(5)**, which for a referee objection expressly says to serve "the opposing party **or counsel**" — that is what a rule specifically allowing attorney service looks like. *(VERIFIED.)*
>
> `ℹ PROVISIONAL — the rule text is verified; whether your particular order is a "final judgment or final order" for this purpose is a fact question about your file, and no case has been read on the point.`

## A.3 The domestic relations overlay — and the answer that cannot be wrong

Chapter 3 adds rules on top of MCR 2.107 for family cases:

> **MCR 3.203(A):** "Unless otherwise required by court rule or statute, the summons and complaint must be served pursuant to MCR 2.105. **In cases in which the court retains jurisdiction**
> (1) notice must be provided as set forth in the statute requiring the notice. Unless otherwise required by court rule or statute, **service by mail shall be to a party's last known mailing address**, and
> (2) court documents and notice for which the statute or court rule does not specify the manner of service **must be served as provided in MCR 2.107, except that service by mail shall be to a party's last known mailing address**."
>
> **MCR 3.203(I) Notice to Attorneys.**
> "(1) **Copies of notices required to be given to the parties also must be sent to the attorneys of record.**
> (2) The notice requirement of this subrule remains in effect until 21 days after judgment is entered or **until postjudgment matters are concluded, whichever is later**."
>
> **MCR 3.203(B) Place of Service; After Entry of Judgment or Order.** "When a domestic relations judgment or order requires the parties to inform the friend of the court office of any changes in their mailing address, a party's last known mailing address means the most recent address (1) that the party provided in writing to the friend of the court office, or (2) set forth in the most recent judgment or order entered in the case, or (3) the address established by the friend of the court office pursuant to subrule (D)."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 3 updated May 1, 2026.)*

> ### ✅ THE INSTRUCTION THAT SURVIVES EVERY READING
>
> **Serve the party. Also serve the attorney. Same papers, same day, both by first-class mail. Prove both on one proof of service.**
>
> Read one way, the rules require party service (MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c)) plus copies to the attorney of record (MCR 3.203(I)(1)). Read the other way, they require attorney service (MCR 2.107(B)(1) opening clause). **Doing both satisfies either reading.** No rule in this chapter is violated by an extra copy. There is no downside except a second stamp.
>
> **Where to mail the party's copy:** the party's **last known mailing address** as defined by MCR 3.203(B) — the address most recently provided in writing to the Friend of the Court, or the one set out in the most recent order. **Not** an address you happen to remember. If the party's address is confidential by court order, MCR 3.203(F) requires them to provide an alternative address for service — ask the Friend of the Court office; do not guess and do not try to find it yourself.

> ### ⚠ The communication restriction and service are two different things
>
> Serving court papers is a step the court rules require. It is not "contacting" the other parent in the sense a restriction is aimed at. **But do not create the argument.** Practical protections:
>
> - **Have a legally competent adult who is not you do the mailing**, and have that person sign the proof of service. MCR 2.107(D) requires the proof to be a written statement **by the individual who served the documents**, verified. It does not require that person to be a party or not a party. (Compare MCR 2.103(A), which requires a non-party for service of *process* — the summons and complaint — a stage you are long past. *(VERIFIED.)*)
> - **Mail. Do not hand-deliver anything to the other parent yourself.** Not to the door, not at an exchange, not through a family member.
> - Do not include a note, a message, or anything else in the envelope. **The envelope contains the court papers and nothing else.**

## A.4 The Friend of the Court copy — the step people forget

> **MCR 3.203(G) Notice to Friend of the Court.** "Except where electronic filing is implemented, if a child of the parties or a child born during the marriage is under the age of 18, or if a party is pregnant, or if child support or spousal support is requested, **the parties must provide the friend of the court with a copy of all pleadings and other documents filed in the action. The copy must be marked "friend of the court" and submitted to the court clerk at the time of filing.** The court clerk must send the copy to the friend of the court."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

So: **one extra copy, written "FRIEND OF THE COURT" on the front, handed to the clerk with everything else.** Where electronic filing is implemented, the court and the FOC work out the transfer instead — which makes "is Genesee on e-filing?" a question worth asking. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — Genesee's MiFILE status not confirmed. Ask the clerk.`

## A.5 Who gets what — the checklist

| Who | What they get | Rule | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The other parent (the party)** | Motion, brief, verified statement/affidavit, **proposed order**, concurrence certificate, notice of hearing, exhibits | MCR 2.107(B)(1)(c); MCR 3.203(A)(2), (B); Genesee LCR 2.119(B) | First-class mail to last known mailing address |
| **The attorney of record** | The same complete set | MCR 2.107(B)(1); MCR 3.203(I)(1) | First-class mail to last known business address |
| **The Friend of the Court** | One copy marked "FRIEND OF THE COURT" | MCR 3.203(G) | Handed to the **clerk** with the filing |
| **The court (clerk)** | Original + proof of service + fee or waiver request | MCR 1.109(C); MCR 2.119(G)(1) | Filed at the Records counter `ℹ PROVISIONAL` |
| **The judge's office** | A copy marked **JUDGE'S COPY** on the cover sheet | MCR 2.119(A)(2)(d) | Only where e-filing is not implemented `ℹ PROVISIONAL` |

**Note the proposed order.** Genesee LCR 2.119(B): "A proposed order must be attached to **and served with** the motion." It is not enough to file it. It goes in every envelope. *(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/local-court-rules-circuit_2025-06-01.txt`.)*

# Part B — How to serve

## B.1 The permitted methods

> **MCR 2.107(C) Manner of Service.** "Except under MCR 1.109(G)(6)(a), service of a copy of a document on an attorney must be made by delivery or by mailing to the attorney at his or her last known business address or, if the attorney does not have a business address, then to his or her last known residence address. Except under MCR 1.109(G)(6)(a), service on a party must be made by delivery or by mailing to the party at the address stated in the party's pleadings.
>
> **(3) Mailing.** Mailing a copy under this rule means enclosing it in a sealed envelope with first class postage fully prepaid, addressed to the person to be served, and depositing the envelope and its contents in the United States mail. **Service by mail is complete at the time of mailing.**"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**Three things follow, and each one matters:**

1. **"Service by mail is complete at the time of mailing."** The service date is the **date you drop it in the mail**, not the date it arrives. This is what makes the 9-day mail deadline workable.
2. **First-class postage, fully prepaid, sealed envelope, US mail.** That is the definition. Certified mail is first-class mail with tracking added — it satisfies the rule and gives you a receipt. **Use certified mail with return receipt for the proof, but understand you are not required to.** The receipt is for you, not for the rule.
3. **"Delivery" is a defined term, not a synonym for handing something over.** MCR 2.107(C)(1) defines delivery to an attorney as handing it to the attorney personally, serving it electronically under MCR 1.109(G)(6)(a), e-mailing it if the parties agreed under MCR 2.107(C)(4), leaving it at the attorney's office with the person in charge, or leaving it in a conspicuous place if no one is there. **Do not use the delivery branch unless you can meet its definition** — and do not use it at all for the other parent, given the communication restriction.

## B.2 ⊗ A conflict inside the rule itself — recorded, not resolved

The last subrule of MCR 2.107 reads:

> **MCR 2.107(G):** "Notwithstanding any other provision of this rule, until further order of the Court, **all service of process except for case initiation must be performed using electronic means (e-Filing where available, email, or fax, where available) to the greatest extent possible. Email transmission does not require agreement by the other party(s)** but should otherwise comply as much as possible with the provisions of subsection (C)(4)."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — it is in the July 31, 2026 rules text.)*

**This does not sit comfortably with the rest of the rule.** MCR 2.107(C)(4) builds an entire structure around *agreement* to alternative electronic service — filed stipulations, e-mail addresses on file, withdrawal procedures — and subrule (G) says e-mail "does not require agreement." Subrule (G) also says "service of process except for case initiation," which is close to a contradiction in terms, since process *is* what initiates a case.

**This project does not resolve that.** `⊗ CONFLICTED — both provisions are in the current rule text; no case, administrative order, or clerk has been consulted to say how the 7th Circuit applies subrule (G).`

**The practical instruction all readings converge on:**

- **Serve by first-class mail and use the 9-day deadline.** Mail service is unambiguous under (C)(3), and 9 days is the longer, safer branch.
- **If you have an e-mail address for the attorney of record, also e-mail the same documents the same day**, as a courtesy copy, and say in the e-mail that the documents are also being served by first-class mail. This costs nothing and can only help.
- **Do not rely on e-mail alone**, and do not e-mail the other parent directly while a communication restriction is in place.
- **Ask the clerk** how the 7th Circuit treats MCR 2.107(G) in postjudgment domestic relations motions. Add it to the call list.

# Part C — The calendar

## C.1 The two deadlines, and why they are two

> **MCR 2.119(C) Time for Service and Filing of Motions and Responses.**
>
> **(1)** Unless a different period is set by these rules or by the court for good cause, a written motion (other than one that may be heard ex parte), notice of the hearing on the motion, and any supporting brief or affidavits must be **served** as follows:
> **(a) at least 9 days before the time set for the hearing, if served by first-class mail, or**
> **(b) at least 7 days before the time set for the hearing, if served by delivery** under MCR 2.107(C)(1) or (2) or MCR 1.109(G)(6)(a).
>
> **(2)** … any **response** to a motion … must be served **(a) at least 5 days before the hearing, if served by first-class mail, or (b) at least 3 days before the hearing, if served by delivery**…
>
> **(3)** If the court sets a different time for serving a motion or response its authorization must be endorsed in writing on the face of the notice of hearing or made by separate order.
>
> **(4)** Unless the court sets a different time, **a motion must be filed at least 7 days before the hearing**, and any response to a motion required or permitted by these rules must be **filed** at least 3 days before the hearing.

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 2 updated May 1, 2026.)*

> ### ⚠ CORRECTION — this project previously stated that MCR 2.119(C)(4) does not exist. It does.
>
> An earlier verification pass recorded that "the current rule has no such (C)(4)" and that Genesee County's posted 2019 motion-practice notice therefore quotes a superseded rule. **The first half of that is wrong.** MCR 2.119(C)(4) is in the current rules text and it says exactly what the county notice says about filing: a motion must be **filed** at least 7 days before the hearing, and a response filed at least 3 days before.
>
> **The practical warning was still right, for a different reason.** (C)(1) and (C)(4) are **two separate obligations**:
>
> - **(C)(1) is about SERVICE** — 9 days if you mail it, 7 days if you deliver it.
> - **(C)(4) is about FILING** — 7 days, regardless of how you serve.
>
> The county notice states the **filing** rule and is silent on the **service** rule. A parent who reads only that notice, mails their motion 7 days before the hearing, and thinks they are on time **is two days late on service**. That is the trap. It is a trap of omission, not of superseded text.
>
> **Both deadlines apply. Meet both.** The correction is recorded at the end of this document as C-7, and the earlier FM.0 draft page has been fixed.

## C.2 How to count

> **MCR 1.108 Computation of Time.** "In computing a period of time prescribed or allowed by these rules, by court order, or by statute, the following rules apply:
>
> **(1)** The day of the act, event, or default after which the designated period of time begins to run is not included. **The last day of the period is included, unless it is a Saturday, Sunday, legal holiday, or day on which the court is closed pursuant to court order; in that event the period runs until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, legal holiday, or day on which the court is closed** pursuant to court order.
>
> **(2)** If a period is measured by a number of weeks, the last day of the period is the same day of the week as the day on which the period began.
>
> **(3)** If a period is measured by months or years, the last day of the period is the same day of the month as the day on which the period began…"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**⚠ An honest note about counting backward.** MCR 1.108 is written for periods that run **forward** from an event. The motion deadlines run **backward** from a hearing date. The conventional method is to count backward from the hearing day, not counting the hearing day itself, until you reach the required number of days. **This draft states that method as the working method, not as a verified rule of construction.** `ℹ PROVISIONAL — no rule text or case read that addresses backward computation`

**Which is exactly why you build in buffer. Never land on the last legal day.**

## C.3 Worked backward calendar — fictional

> **FICTIONAL DATES.** Assumed: a Genesee County postjudgment motion; service by first-class mail; a hearing date obtained from the court.

**Hearing: Friday, December 4, 2026.**

| Count back | Date | Day |
|---|---|---|
| — | December 4 | Friday — **HEARING** |
| 1 | December 3 | Thursday |
| 2 | December 2 | Wednesday |
| 3 | December 1 | Tuesday |
| 4 | November 30 | Monday |
| 5 | November 29 | Sunday |
| 6 | November 28 | Saturday |
| **7** | **November 27** | **Friday — last day to FILE (MCR 2.119(C)(4))** |
| 8 | November 26 | Thursday — **Thanksgiving** |
| **9** | **November 25** | **Wednesday — last day to SERVE BY MAIL (MCR 2.119(C)(1)(a))** |

**Now look at what that calendar actually contains:**

- The filing deadline lands on the **day after Thanksgiving**, when the courthouse may be closed. A closed court does not extend a deadline that has already been counted backward — it just means you cannot file.
- The mail-service deadline lands the **day before Thanksgiving**, into the heaviest mail week of the year.
- Two weekend days sit inside the last seven.

**The buffered plan for this fictional hearing:**

| Do this | By this date |
|---|---|
| Obtain the hearing date | As early as the court allows |
| Finalize motion, brief, affidavit, proposed order, concurrence certificate, notice of hearing | Friday, November 13 |
| **File** at the Records counter (with the FOC copy and the fee or waiver request) | **Wednesday, November 18** — 16 days out, more than double the minimum |
| **Mail** complete sets to the party and to the attorney, same day | **Wednesday, November 18** — 16 days out |
| File the proof of service | **Wednesday, November 18** or immediately after |
| Judge's copy delivered, marked JUDGE'S COPY | Wednesday, November 18 `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm whether Genesee still requires one` |

**File first, then serve the same day.** This solves a real ordering problem: the filing minimum (7 days) is *shorter* than the mail-service minimum (9 days), so if you file at the last legal moment you cannot possibly serve on time. **Both numbers are minimums, not targets.** Filing early and serving immediately satisfies both, and gives you room to fix a rejected filing.

## C.4 Response and reply

- The other side's **response** must be served at least **5 days** before the hearing by mail, or **3 days** by delivery (MCR 2.119(C)(2)), and **filed** at least 3 days before (MCR 2.119(C)(4)).
- **There is normally no reply.** MCR 2.119(A)(2)(b): "Except as permitted by the court or as otherwise provided in these rules, **no reply briefs, additional briefs, or supplemental briefs may be filed.**" *(VERIFIED.)* Plan to answer a response orally at the hearing, not on paper.
- If the court sets different times, that authorization "must be endorsed in writing on the face of the notice of hearing or made by separate order" — MCR 2.119(C)(3). **A verbal assurance from anyone is not that.**

# Part D — Proving it

## D.1 What a proof of service must be

> **MCR 2.107(D) Proof of Service.** "Except as otherwise provided by MCR 2.104, 2.105, or 2.106, proof of service of documents required or permitted to be served must be by written acknowledgment of service, or **a written statement by the individual who served the documents verified under MCR 1.109(D)(3)**. **The proof of service may be included at the end of the document as filed. Proof of service must be filed promptly and at least at or before a hearing to which the document relates.**"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

And the verification method:

> **MCR 1.109(D)(3):** "…If a document is required or permitted to be verified, it may be verified by (a) oath or affirmation of the party or of someone having knowledge of the facts stated; or (b) except as to an affidavit, including the following signed and dated declaration: **"I declare under the penalties of perjury that this _________ has been examined by me and that its contents are true to the best of my information, knowledge, and belief."** … In addition to the sanctions provided by subrule (E), a person who knowingly makes a false declaration under this subrule **may be found in contempt of court**."

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**So a proof of service does not require a notary.** The declaration language above, signed and dated, is enough. Copy it exactly — do not paraphrase it.

Assumed posture: postjudgment Genesee County motion; service by first-class mail on the party and on the attorney of record.
>
> ### ⛔ Do not use this if:

> - You are initiating a contempt proceeding. MCR 2.107(B)(1)(b) requires personal delivery to the party unless the court orders otherwise.
> - You did not personally do the mailing described. **The person who did it signs it.**
> - Any date in it is not exactly true.

**Rules for completing it:**

- **List every document by name.** "Motion and attachments" is not a list. If the proposed order is not named on the proof of service, there is no paper record that you complied with Genesee LCR 2.119(B).
- **The date is the mailing date**, because service by mail is complete at mailing.
- **The signer is the person who put it in the mail.** If a friend mailed it, the friend signs and puts their own address.
- **Copy the declaration sentence exactly** from MCR 1.109(D)(3)(b).
- **Attach nothing to the proof of service.** Keep certified mail receipts and green cards in your own file. They are your backup if service is challenged; they are not part of the proof.

## D.3 Keep a service file

For each mailing, keep: a complete copy of the exact set mailed; the certified mail receipt with tracking number; the return receipt card or electronic delivery record; and a copy of the filed, date-stamped proof of service.

**If service is ever challenged, this file is the answer.** It is also the file that did not exist last time.

# Part E — Filing, fees, and the counter

## E.1 Where the papers go

**File at the Circuit Court Records counter — not at the Friend of the Court office.** They are different offices with different functions. The FOC gets a copy, from the clerk, because MCR 3.203(G) says the clerk sends it. Handing your motion to the FOC is not filing it. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — the Genesee filing counter, its location, and its hours are not confirmed by this project. Call before you go.`

**Formatting that gets a filing rejected** — MCR 1.109(D)(1)(a) and MCR 2.119(A)(2)(c): 8½ × 11 paper, legible, English; body text **12 or 13 point**, footnotes no smaller than 10 point; at least **one-inch margins**; combined motion and brief no more than **20 pages double spaced**, exclusive of attachments and exhibits. A clerk **may reject nonconforming documents** — MCR 1.109(D)(6). *(All VERIFIED.)*

**The caption** must contain the court name, the parties, the case number with its year prefix and two-letter case-type suffix, the identification of the document, and — for a party without a lawyer — your name, an address, and a telephone number. MCR 1.109(D)(1)(b). *(VERIFIED.)*

## E.2 The motion fee

> **MCR 2.119(G):** "(1) A motion fee must be paid on the filing of any request for an order in a pending action, whether the request is entitled 'motion,' 'petition,' 'application,' or otherwise. **(2) The clerk shall charge a single motion fee for all motions filed at the same time in an action regardless of the number of separately captioned documents filed or the number of distinct or alternative requests for relief included in the motions.** (3) A motion fee may not be charged: … (c) for a request for an order waiving fees under MCR 2.002…"

*(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

**Two consequences worth money:** everything filed at the same time draws **one** fee, no matter how many separate requests are inside it — so file the parenting-time relief and the communication-protocol relief together, which the motion in Document 2 already does. And **asking for a fee waiver is itself free.**

The dollar amount is a Genesee local fact. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — current Genesee motion fee not confirmed. Ask at the counter.`

## E.3 If you cannot pay — MCR 2.002

You do not have to be destitute, and you do not have to explain yourself in person.

> **MCR 2.002(B):** "A request to waive fees **must accompany the documents the individual is filing** with the court… The request must be on a form approved by the State Court Administrative Office entitled **'Fee Waiver Request.'** … The information contained on the form shall be **nonpublic**. The request must be verified in accordance with MCR 1.109(D)(3)(b)…"
>
> **MCR 2.002(C):** "If a party shows that he or she is **receiving any form of means-tested public assistance**, **the clerk of the court must waive** payment of fees as to that party…" — the rule lists food assistance, Medicaid, the Family Independence Program, WIC, SSI, "or any other federal, state, or locally administered means-tested income or benefit."
>
> **MCR 2.002(D):** representation by a legal services program that is a grantee of the federal Legal Services Corporation or the Michigan State Bar Foundation, or by a law school clinic serving on the basis of indigence → **the clerk must waive**.
>
> **MCR 2.002(F):** "If an individual shows that he or she is unable because of indigence to pay fees, **the court shall order those fees waived. The court must waive fees when the individual lives in a household with gross income under 125% of the federal poverty guidelines.** The court must also waive fees when gross household income is above 125% … **if the payment of fees would constitute a financial hardship** on the individual."
>
> **MCR 2.002(G):** "A judge shall enter an order either granting or denying a request … **within three business days** and such order shall be nonpublic… (1) … if the court denied the request, the clerk shall also send a notice that **to preserve the filing date the individual must pay the fees within 14 days** from the date the clerk sends notice of the order **or the filing will be rejected**."
>
> **MCR 2.002(A)(5):** "If fees are waived under this rule postjudgment, **the waiver continues through the date of adjudication of the postjudgment proceedings.**"

*(All VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 2 updated May 1, 2026.)*

**What to do with that:**

- The form is the SCAO **"Fee Waiver Request."** It is commonly numbered **MC 20**. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — form number and current revision not confirmed this pass; ask the clerk or check the SCAO forms page, and remember that a court-website 404 means the page moved, not that the form was withdrawn.`
- **File it with the motion, not after.** The rule says it must accompany the documents.
- If you receive **any** means-tested benefit, say so on the form — that branch is a clerk decision, not a judge decision.
- **The 14-day trap:** if the request is denied, you have 14 days from the date the clerk sends notice to pay, **or the filing is rejected.** A rejected filing is not a filed motion, and your hearing date goes with it. **Put that 14-day date on a calendar the day you file.** ⚠ TIME-CRITICAL
- The waiver information is **nonpublic**. It does not go in the public file.

## E.4 The praecipe question

Some Michigan courts require a **praecipe** — a separate scheduling request — to get a motion on the calendar.

**Genesee's local rules contain no praecipe requirement.** The Seventh Judicial Circuit's only local rule in the current compilation is Rule 2.119, and it has three subrules: (A) the concurrence certificate, (B) the proposed order, (C) application. There is no praecipe provision, and no local motion-timing provision either. *(VERIFIED — `sources/primary/local-court-rules-circuit_2025-06-01.txt`, complete Genesee section read.)*

**That does not mean the counter will not ask you for one.** Local scheduling practice is not always written in the local rules. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask directly: "Do I need a praecipe, and if so where is that requirement written down?"`

## Exercise — done when

1. Write the hearing date at the top of a page.
2. Count backward and write in **both** deadlines: the mail-service day (9) and the filing day (7).
3. Look at both dates. Are either near a weekend, a holiday, or a court closure? Move your plan earlier.
4. Choose your actual filing-and-serving day, **at least twice the minimum** ahead.
5. Build the envelopes: one for the party, one for the attorney. Identical, complete contents.
6. Build the clerk stack: original, FOC copy marked "FRIEND OF THE COURT," judge's copy marked "JUDGE'S COPY," fee or Fee Waiver Request, proof of service.
7. Write out the document list for the proof of service and check it against what is physically in each envelope.

**You are done when:**

- The mail-service date and the filing date are both on a calendar you actually check, with the buffer date marked, and
- Every envelope contains the **proposed order** (Genesee LCR 2.119(B)) and the **concurrence certificate** (LCR 2.119(A)), and
- The proof of service names every document by name, and its date is the mailing date, and it is signed by the person who did the mailing, and
- The Friend of the Court copy is in the clerk stack, marked, and
- If you filed a Fee Waiver Request, the 14-day pay-or-rejected date is on the calendar too.

## When to stop and get help

- The other side files a motion or response arguing your service was defective. **Read it the day it arrives**, note every date it asserts, and get advice immediately. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- The other parent's address is confidential by court order, or you do not know where they live. Ask the Friend of the Court; do not investigate. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- The attorney of record has withdrawn, or a new attorney has appeared. Who must be served changes.
- Anyone tells you a deadline was extended. Get it in writing endorsed on the notice of hearing or in a separate order — MCR 2.119(C)(3).
- Your filing is rejected by the clerk for any reason. Fix and refile the same week; do not wait for the hearing.

**Free and low-cost help in Genesee County** `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm before relying`: Genesee County Legal Resource Center (courthouse Room 203); Legal Services of Eastern Michigan (Flint); YWCA Greater Flint; Michigan Legal Help (michiganlegalhelp.org); State Bar of Michigan lawyer referral, including **limited-scope representation** — a lawyer for one document or one hearing.
