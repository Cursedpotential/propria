> **Genesee LCR 2.119(A)** requires this certificate "signed by the attorney of record **or by the party in propria persona**," attached to or incorporated in the motion and notice of hearing filed with the clerk. *(VERIFIED verbatim from the 6/1/2025 local-rules compilation.)* The heading of the rule reads "by Attorney," but the text covers a self-represented party. **If you leave it off, your motion is out of compliance with a binding local rule.**
> **You must actually make the concurrence request before you can certify it.** Do that first — mail a short request to counsel enclosing the proposed order and asking for a written answer by a stated date. Because opposing counsel's January 8, 2025 letter asked for mail contact, mailing the concurrence request to counsel is the clean way to do this. Keep the certified-mail receipt.

## What to notice

- **The rule gives two branches:** concurrence requested and **denied**, **or** reasonable and diligent **attempts** to contact counsel. Use the branch that is true. If you get no answer, the second branch ("reasonable and diligent attempts") is the true one; if concurrence is expressly refused, say so.
- **The certificate recites what you actually did, with dates.** The bare rule language would technically satisfy the rule, but the recital is what makes the certificate provable if it is ever questioned — and your certified-mail receipt backs it up.
- **Your signature carries MCR 1.109(E)(5):** it certifies you read the document, that after reasonable inquiry it is well grounded in fact and warranted by law or a good-faith argument, and that it is **not interposed for any improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay.** MCR 1.109(E)(6) requires the court to impose an appropriate sanction for a violation. *(VERIFIED.)* Do not certify an attempt you did not make.
