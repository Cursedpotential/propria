```
     BRIEF IN SUPPORT OF MOTION FOR ENTRY OF PARENTING
     TIME IN SPECIFIC TERMS

I.   INTRODUCTION

     The order entered March 15, 2023 provides that parenting
time is "as the parties agree." It states no date, no time,
and no location. The moving party asks the Court to state
parenting time in specific terms and to set the conditions
that allow those terms to function. The relief sought does not
change where the child lives or who provides the child's
day-to-day care.

II.  FACTS

     1. The order entered March 15, 2023 provides that
parenting time is "as the parties agree."

     2. The order directs that "the parties shall communicate
regarding parenting time." The moving party has used that
provision. On November 2, 2024, December 14, 2024, and
January 20, 2025 the moving party requested parenting time in
writing by text message. (Exhibits A-D.) No parenting time
occurred on any requested date.

     3. By letter dated January 8, 2025, counsel for [the
other parent] stated that communication should be by United
States mail only. (Exhibit E.) That instruction appears in no
order of this Court.

     4. The child support order in this case states the moving
party's overnights as 104. MCSF § 3.03(E). (Exhibit F.)

     5. In the period since the order entered, the provision
quoted in paragraph 1 has produced no scheduled parenting
time.

III. THE COURT SHOULD GRANT PARENTING TIME IN SPECIFIC TERMS
     UNDER MCL 722.27a(8)

     MCL 722.27a(8) provides:

          "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms
          if requested by either party at any time."

     The moving party is a party. The moving party requests
specific terms. The subsection is written in the mandatory
voice ("shall"), it states no threshold, and it contains no
time limitation: it applies "at any time."

     The Child Custody Act contemplates both forms of order.
MCL 722.27(1)(b) permits a court to "provide for reasonable
parenting time of the child by the parties involved . . . by
general or specific terms and conditions." MCL 722.27a(8) is
the provision by which either parent obtains the specific
form.

     The Court of Appeals has enforced this subsection. In
Pickering v Pickering, 268 Mich App 1; 706 NW2d 835 (2005),
the trial court had granted "reasonable and liberal parenting
time." The Court of Appeals held that "'[r]easonable and
liberal parenting time' is plainly not a grant of parenting
time in 'specific' terms," vacated the provision, and remanded
"with the direction to consider a specific parenting time
order." The Court held that under the statute "requests for
specific parenting time terms must be considered by the court
regardless of the timing of the request." [Pickering quotes
the specific-terms sentence as MCL 722.27a(7); the current
statute places it at (8).] A provision that parenting time is
"as the parties agree" is no more specific than "reasonable
and liberal," and is less.

     The moving party does not contend that MCL 722.27a(8)
displaces any threshold this Court finds applicable to
modification of an existing order. Section IV addresses that
question in the alternative.

IV.  IN THE ALTERNATIVE, THE THRESHOLD IS SATISFIED

     A. The requested order does not change the established
        custodial environment.

     MCL 722.27(1)(c) provides that "[t]he custodial
environment of a child is established if over an appreciable
time the child naturally looks to the custodian in that
environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life,
and parental comfort."

     The minor child has an established custodial environment
with [the other parent]. The child lives primarily with that
parent, attends school from that residence, and looks to that
parent for day-to-day care. The moving party does not ask the
Court to disturb any of that. The proposed order changes no
residence, no school, and no day-to-day care. It states times,
dates, and locations that the current order leaves to
agreement. The regular schedule requested provides fewer
overnights than the 104 the support order already attributes
to the moving party.

     B. Because the established custodial environment is not
        changed, the parenting-time standard governs and the
        burden is a preponderance of the evidence.

     In Shade v Wright, 291 Mich App 17; 805 NW2d 1 (2010),
the Court of Appeals held:

          "We discern nothing in the Vodvarka opinion that
          requires the standards used to determine the
          existence of proper cause or change of circumstances
          for custody determinations to apply to
          determinations regarding parenting time, absent a
          conclusion that a change in parenting time will
          result in a change in an established custodial
          environment. . . . If a change in parenting time
          results in a change in the established custodial
          environment, then the Vodvarka framework is
          appropriate."

     Shade limited its own holding. In footnote 4 it stated:
"With our holding today, we do not seek to precisely define
the proper cause or change of circumstances necessary to
change parenting time. Our holding is limited to our
conclusion that the normal life changes that occurred with the
minor child in this case are sufficient to modify parenting
time." The moving party cites Shade for the distinction it
drew between changes that alter the established custodial
environment and changes that do not — not for a general test.

     In Pierron v Pierron, 486 Mich 81, 92-93; 782 NW2d 480
(2010), the Supreme Court allocated the burden: where the
proposed change alters the established custodial environment,
the standard is clear and convincing evidence; where it does
not, the standard is a preponderance of the evidence, and the
burden rests on the party proposing the change. Because the
requested order does not alter the established custodial
environment, the moving party accepts the preponderance burden
here.

     C. Proper cause and change of circumstances, if required.

     Vodvarka v Grasmeyer, 259 Mich App 499, 511, 513-514;
675 NW2d 847 (2003), defines the showing. "[P]roper cause
means one or more appropriate grounds that have or could have
a significant effect on the child's life to the extent that a
reevaluation of the child's custodial situation should be
undertaken." A change of circumstances requires proof that,
"since the entry of the last custody order, the conditions
surrounding custody of the child, which have or could have a
significant effect on the child's well-being, have materially
changed," and "something more than the normal life changes."

     The facts, stated without characterization: a provision
that operates only on agreement has produced no scheduled
parenting time since the order entered; three written requests
for parenting time, on the dates in paragraph II.2, produced
no parenting time; and the minor child, now 6, has continued
to develop over that period. Whether those facts meet the
threshold is for the Court; they are offered as dated facts,
not as conclusions.

     D. A change to a condition on parenting time uses a
        lesser standard.

     To the extent the requested written communication method
(paragraph 9 of the proposed order) is treated as a change to
a condition of parenting time, Kaeb v Kaeb, 309 Mich App 556,
571-572; 873 NW2d 319 (2015), holds that neither Vodvarka nor
Shade controls; a "lesser, more flexible" standard applies,
under which "proper cause" carries its ordinary meaning — "an
appropriate ground for taking legal action" — and the movant
shows that the condition "in its current form no longer serves
the child's best interests." [Kaeb cites the terms-and-
conditions provision as MCL 722.27a(8); the current statute
places it at (9).]

V.   THE STATUTORY FRAMING SUPPORTS SPECIFIC TERMS

     MCL 722.27a(1) provides: "It is presumed to be in the
best interests of a child for the child to have a strong
relationship with both of his or her parents," and that
parenting time "shall be granted to a parent in a frequency,
duration, and type reasonably calculated to promote a strong
relationship between the child and the parent granted
parenting time."

     A provision that has produced no scheduled parenting time
is not, in operation, reasonably calculated to promote that
relationship.

     MCL 722.27a(3) provides: "A child has a right to
parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record
by clear and convincing evidence that it would endanger the
child's physical, mental, or emotional health." No such
showing has been made or attempted in this case.

VI.  THE PARENTING-TIME FACTORS

     MCL 722.27a(7) provides that "[t]he court may consider
the following factors when determining the frequency,
duration, and type of parenting time to be granted." Two are
directly engaged by this record.

     Factor (f) asks "[w]hether a parent can reasonably be
expected to exercise parenting time in accordance with the
court order." The moving party has communicated regarding
parenting time as the order directs, has proposed parenting
time in writing on three dated occasions, has committed no
violation of the order, is current on support, and has missed
no exchange.

     Factor (g) asks "[w]hether a parent has frequently failed
to exercise reasonable parenting time." Exhibits A through D
show the moving party's written requests and the outcome of
each. The support order's recital of 104 overnights is
independent record confirmation that the moving party's role
is not that of a parent who has declined parenting time.

     [Older opinions cite these factors as MCL 722.27a(6)(a)-
     (i). The current statute places them at (7)(a)-(i). No
     factor beyond (f) and (g) is argued, because no other is
     engaged by this record.]

VII. THE REQUESTED CONDITIONS ARE AUTHORIZED BY STATUTE

     MCL 722.27a(9) provides that "[a] parenting time order
may contain any reasonable terms or conditions that facilitate
the orderly and meaningful exercise of parenting time by a
parent," including division of the responsibility and cost of
transporting the child, subsections (9)(a) and (b);
requirements that the child be ready at a specific time,
subsection (9)(d); requirements that the parent arrive and
return the child at specific times, subsection (9)(e);
requirements of reasonable notice when parenting time will not
occur, subsection (9)(h); and "[a]ny other reasonable
condition determined to be appropriate in the particular
case," subsection (9)(i).

     The proposed order tracks those subsections paragraph by
paragraph. As to subsection (9)(i), the proposed order asks
the Court to state the written method by which the parents
communicate regarding the child. The existing order already
directs that "the parties shall communicate regarding
parenting time"; it does not state a method, and that silence
has itself become a matter of dispute. A schedule with start
times, end times, an exchange location, and a notice
requirement needs a channel through which notice can be given
and proved. The moving party does not request direct in-person
contact and does not ask the Court to authorize any contact
other than written communication through the stated method.

     MCL 722.27a(10) requires that a parenting time order
"shall contain a prohibition on exercising parenting time in a
country that is not a party to the Hague Convention on the
Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction," absent both
parents' written consent. The proposed order contains that
provision. MCL 722.27a(11) provides that during each parent's
parenting time, that parent decides routine matters; the
proposed order states that as well.

VIII. AN ENFORCEABLE ORDER IS THE OBJECT OF THIS MOTION

     MCL 552.641(2) permits the office of the friend of the
court to decline to respond to an alleged parenting time
violation where "[t]he custody or parenting time order does
not include an enforceable provision that is relevant to the
custody or parenting time order violation alleged in the
complaint." MCL 552.641(2)(c).

     An order providing that parenting time is "as the parties
agree" contains no term that can be violated. The relief
requested would give both parents terms that can be followed,
and, if not followed, enforced.

IX.  RELIEF REQUESTED

     The moving party requests entry of the attached proposed
order.


Dated: __________              ____________________________
                               [YOUR PRINTED NAME]
                               In propria persona
```

## What makes this brief work — read before you finalize

- **Section III leads with the statute and one enforcing case, then gives something up.** "The moving party does not contend that MCL 722.27a(8) displaces any threshold" is the most important sentence here. No court has been read for this project on how subsection (8) interacts with the modification threshold. Claiming (8) settles that question would be a claim no one can back. Conceding it openly and answering it in Section IV removes the easiest way to lose and costs nothing. **Do not delete that sentence to sound stronger.**
- **Section IV.A is the whole case in one paragraph:** the requested order does not change the established custodial environment. Your 104-overnight recital makes this concrete — you are asking the Court to write down *less* overnight time than the support order already assumes. A request for less time than is already presumed cannot be a disguised custody grab.
- **Section IV.B carries *Shade*'s footnote 4** and says exactly what *Shade* is cited for. That is how you use a narrow holding without overstating it.
- **Section VI walks two factors, not nine.** Walking all nine reads as padding. Your conduct audit lands here — as dated facts under (f) and (g), never as adjectives.
- **Section VII maps each statutory subsection to a numbered paragraph of the proposed order,** and disclaims what you are not asking for. A judge or referee can check the work in thirty seconds.
- **No unpublished opinion is cited.** MCR 7.215(C)(1) would require you to explain why, where published authority exists, and to give the docket number and date. The simplest safe course is not to cite one. If you ever do, that rule is mandatory.
- **No case is cited whose holding this project has not read.** *Hayes v Hayes* is a natural fit for the ECE point and is deliberately absent for that reason.

## The FOC 65 form note

SCAO publishes a **Motion Regarding Parenting Time (FOC 65)**. Use the official form if you file on it — this packet does not publish a competing version. **The trap:** in *Shade*, Judge Shapiro's concurrence observed that the SCAO parenting-time motion form does not ask the movant for proper cause or change of circumstances at all, while the custody motion form does. So you can fill the form out completely and never be prompted for the showing the Court may require. **Attach this brief regardless.** The form is the container; the brief is the case. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — FOC 65 current title/revision/URL not confirmed this pass; a court-site 404 means moved, not withdrawn.`

## The pleading-content rule

MCR 3.207(B)(1)(a) requires a verified pleading or affidavit that "requests a change of custody or parenting time" to state (i) the established-custodial-environment facts and (ii) either that the requested order will not change the ECE, or clear-and-convincing facts that changing it is in the child's best interest. Your motion paragraphs 8-9 and your verified statement supply exactly this. Whether the subrule (which sits under the "Ex Parte Orders" heading) binds an ordinary noticed motion is a fair question — **it does not matter, because including the facts costs a paragraph and they are the facts the Court must find under MCL 722.27(1)(c) anyway.** `ℹ PROVISIONAL — rule text VERIFIED; scope reading not confirmed.`

### Drafting notes — NOT reader-facing

**The support caution (symmetry rule).** The 104-overnight recital is used here only to show the requested schedule is modest and does not alter the ECE. **Do not put a support request in this motion.** Parenting time and support are separate obligations; joining them invites the argument that you seek time to reduce a payment. Two-way risk to disclose to the owner: MCSF § 3.03(C)(4) credits overnights "lawfully and actually" spent and says "[d]o not consider overnights exercised in violation of an order," and § 3.03(C)(4)(a) lets the FOC credit *actual* overnights on credible evidence without a modification motion. Because actual exercised overnights here are far below the ordered 104, a future support proceeding could cut against the moving party as readily as for. Sequence: obtain the enforceable schedule first; consider support separately and later. The § 3.03(D) ≥21-overnight modification hook is noted for sequencing only.
