> **Status: working draft for your own case. NOT filing-ready.** A proposed order **must** be attached to and served with the motion — Genesee LCR 2.119(B), VERIFIED verbatim. This is a binding local rule, not a suggestion; the proposed order goes in every envelope (Document 8).
> **The test for every line: could a stranger holding only this order execute it next weekend without asking a question?** If not, rewrite the line. Nothing in it may say "as agreed," "reasonable," or "mutually convenient."

---

## Before you finalize this order — two design notes

**1. Your ask is deliberately modest, and that is the strategy.** Alternating weekends plus one weekday evening is about 52 overnights a year — half the 104 the support order already credits you. Keep it there. **Do not add equal ("50/50") time, extended summer weeks, or a long holiday block to this motion.** A parenting-time motion that reaches for those gets recharacterized as a *custody* motion and thrown into the higher clear-and-convincing burden (the *Vodvarka* framework). Grow the schedule later, from a clean record of exercised time. The holiday table below is kept intentionally small for the same reason.

**2. Dates certain vs. "after entry."** The regular schedule needs a starting anchor. Naming a **date certain** is the most specific and is preferred — but if the hearing is adjourned past that date, the start date passes before the order is signed. The fix the model teaches: name a date certain, and **bring a corrected copy to the hearing** with the date moved if it has slipped, saying so on the record. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — a way to draft, not a rule of law.`

### Drafting notes — not part of the order

- **Paragraph 6's forfeiture rule cuts both ways.** A 30-minute forfeiture and a 15-minute late-notice rule bind you exactly as they bind the other parent. A rule that binds only one parent is one a court is right to distrust; symmetry is why this belongs in the order (GUARDRAILS §7, symmetry rule).
- **Paragraph 8 is the answer to the January 8, 2025 mail-only letter — reframed as logistics, not grievance.** The order says "the parties shall communicate regarding parenting time"; counsel narrowed that to mail. Mail cannot carry notice of a same-day problem (paragraph 7). You are asking for a *more* accountable channel — written, dated, retrievable — not direct contact. The disclaimer sentence is what keeps this from reading as a bid to loosen a restriction. **If there were any safety history behind the mail-only instruction (your facts show none), the same framing holds: written and recorded is more accountable than mail, not less.**
- **The blank in paragraph 8 is deliberate.** Naming a specific commercial app as court-favored is unsupported, and cost/access matter. Offer it as a generic option and let the Court choose — including plain "email limited to matters concerning the child." `ℹ PROVISIONAL — no verified information on what any Genesee judge or referee uses.`
