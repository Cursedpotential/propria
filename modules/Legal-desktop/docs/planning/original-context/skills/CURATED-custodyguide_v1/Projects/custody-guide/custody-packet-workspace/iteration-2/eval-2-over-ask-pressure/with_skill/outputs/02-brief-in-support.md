## I. Introduction

The order entered March 15, 2023 provides for parenting time "as the parties agree" and directs that "the parties shall communicate regarding parenting time." It states no dates, times, or locations, and defines no method or deadline for the communication on which it depends. The moving party asks the Court, under **MCL 722.27a(8)**, to state parenting time in specific terms and to set conditions under **MCL 722.27a(9)**. The moving party does not ask the Court to change the child's established custodial environment.

## III. The Court should grant parenting time in specific terms under MCL 722.27a(8)

> **MCL 722.27a(8): "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time."**
> *(VERIFIED — legislature.mi.gov full-section fetch; banner "Complete Through PA 2 of 2025"; last amended 2016 PA 96, eff. Aug. 1, 2016.)*

Each word carries weight: **"shall"** (mandatory), **"in specific terms"** (the relief the statute names), **"if requested"** (on request — the moving party requests it here), **"by either party"** (available to this parent), **"at any time"** (no window closes; the age of the 2023 order is no answer).

The companion provision confirms the point from the other side: the court may provide parenting time "by general or specific terms and conditions" — **MCL 722.27(1)(b)** (VERIFIED). Michigan law expressly allows an order written in general terms; subsection (8) is the mechanism by which either parent converts general terms into specific ones.

**A generously worded but vague provision does not satisfy the statute.**

> ***Pickering v Pickering*, 268 Mich App 1; 706 NW2d 835 (2005)** (published) held that "'[r]easonable and liberal parenting time' is plainly not a grant of parenting time in 'specific' terms," and that "requests for specific parenting time terms must be considered by the court regardless of the timing of the request." Refusing to consider specific terms once requested was legal error; the Court of Appeals **vacated** the parenting-time provision and remanded "with the direction to consider a specific parenting time order."
> *(Opinion text read for this project via secondary mirrors under the convergence standard; `verification_ledger.md` §4.3. **⚠ Disposition: the operative text is read; the official-reporter citation is convergence-pending — cross-check 268 Mich App 1 against the bound volume before filing.**)*

"As the parties agree" is **less** definite than the "reasonable and liberal" language *Pickering* held insufficient — it makes parenting time contingent on an agreement that, as the request record shows, does not occur. The moving party has requested specific terms; under subsection (8) that request "shall" be granted.

> ### ⚠⚠ The honest limit on this argument — do not overstate it
> The **statutory text of subsection (8) is verified. The case law on how (8) interacts with the "proper cause or change of circumstances" gate for *modifying* an order (MCL 722.27(1)(c)) has not been resolved for this project.** *Pickering* itself arose at judgment (the request preceded entry), not postjudgment. Do not write, say, or imply that subsection (8) is settled law that removes any threshold the Court finds applicable. Plead it as the lead, and plead the threshold **in the alternative** (Section IV). If the Court applies (8) directly, you win on the short path; if it requires the threshold, you have already met it. `UNVERIFIED — appellate interaction of (8) with the (1)(c) threshold not resolved`
>

Every pre-2016 citation to MCL 722.27a shifts down one subsection. A brief that quotes *Pickering*'s "(7)" without this note points the Court at the wrong provision of the current statute. *(`verification_ledger.md` §4.3.)*

## IV. In the alternative: the threshold is met

### A. The requested order does not change the established custodial environment

> **MCL 722.27(1)(c):** the court shall not modify an order "so as to change the established custodial environment of a child unless there is presented clear and convincing evidence that it is in the best interest of the child. The custodial environment of a child is established if over an appreciable time the child naturally looks to the custodian in that environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort." *(VERIFIED — legislature.mi.gov full-section fetch.)*

The established custodial environment is a question of fact about how the child actually lives, not about the caption on the motion. **The requested order specifies times within the arrangement already reflected in the support order's 104-overnight figure; it does not change the child's primary residence, school, childcare, or day-to-day care.** That is the moving party's central factual assertion, and it is the exact showing MCR 3.207(B)(1)(a)(ii) invites.

### B. Because the ECE is not changed, *Shade* governs and the burden is preponderance

> ***Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17; 805 NW2d 1 (2010)** (published; read in full for this project): "We discern nothing in the *Vodvarka* opinion that requires the standards used to determine the existence of proper cause or change of circumstances for custody determinations to apply to determinations regarding parenting time, **absent a conclusion that a change in parenting time will result in a change in an established custodial environment**. … **If a change in parenting time results in a change in the established custodial environment, then the *Vodvarka* framework is appropriate.**" *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

> ***Shade*, footnote 4** (carry it — it prevents overstatement): "With our holding today, we do not seek to precisely define the proper cause or change of circumstances necessary to change parenting time. Our holding is limited to our conclusion that the normal life changes that occurred with the minor child in this case are sufficient to modify parenting time." *(VERIFIED.)*

> ***Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81, 92–93; 782 NW2d 480 (2010)** (Michigan Supreme Court): where the change alters the ECE, the standard is **clear and convincing evidence**; where it does not, the standard is **preponderance**, and the burden is on the party proposing the change. *(⚠ PROVISIONAL — citation and proposition confirmed through *Shade*'s own text; the *Pierron* opinion has not been read for this project. `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

Because the requested order does not alter the ECE, *Shade*'s more expansive approach governs and the burden is preponderance.

### C. Proper cause / change of circumstances, if the Court requires it

> ***Vodvarka v Grasmeyer*, 259 Mich App 499, 511, 513–514; 675 NW2d 847 (2003)** (published): "proper cause means one or more appropriate grounds that have or could have a significant effect on the child's life…"; a change of circumstances requires proof that "since the entry of the last custody order, the conditions surrounding custody of the child … have materially changed," something more than "the normal life changes." *(VERIFIED.)*

The dated facts — an order provision that operates only on an agreement that has not occurred, a documented record of unanswered written requests, and the ordinary developmental change of a child now six years old since the 2023 order — are pleaded as facts. Whether they clear the bar is for the Court; the moving party does not characterize them.

## V. The statutory framing supports specific terms

> **MCL 722.27a(1):** "It is presumed to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents … parenting time shall be granted … in a frequency, duration, and type reasonably calculated to promote a strong relationship." **MCL 722.27a(3):** "A child has a right to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by clear and convincing evidence that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

An open-ended provision that depends on an agreement that does not occur is not, on its face, "reasonably calculated" to promote a strong relationship. Subsection (3) places the burden of curtailing contact on whoever seeks to curtail it, at the clear-and-convincing level.

> **The limiter, in the same section.** Subsection (3) is a burden allocation, not a shield. The court may also consider "(c) The reasonable likelihood of abuse or neglect of the child during parenting time" and "(d) The reasonable likelihood of abuse of a parent resulting from the exercise of parenting time" — MCL 722.27a(7)(c), (d) (VERIFIED). Know what the record contains before invoking (3).

## VI. The parenting-time factors — walk only the ones the facts reach

> **MCL 722.27a(7):** "The court **may** consider the following factors…" — permissive, not mandatory. *(VERIFIED.)* **⚠ Renumbering trap:** older opinions cite this list as MCL 722.27a**(6)**(a)–(i); the current statute places it at **(7)**(a)–(i). Bracket-note any case citation. *(`verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

- **(7)(b) — nursing child under 6 months / under 1 year:** **not applicable.** The child is six years old. State this so the Court sees the schedule is shaped for a school-age child.
- **(7)(f) — whether a parent can reasonably be expected to exercise parenting time in accordance with the court order:** the documented request record and the moving party's compliance answer this affirmatively.
- **(7)(g) — whether a parent has frequently failed to exercise reasonable parenting time:** the **104 overnights recited in the support order** and the request record answer this. This factor is aimed at a parent with a silent record; the record here is not silent.

Do not walk factors your facts do not reach.

## VII. The requested conditions are authorized by statute

> **MCL 722.27a(9)** authorizes "any reasonable terms or conditions that facilitate the orderly and meaningful exercise of parenting time," including transportation responsibility and cost (a)/(b), a requirement that the child be ready at a specific time (d), arrival/return at specific times (e), notice when parenting time will not occur (h), and "(i) Any other reasonable condition determined to be appropriate in the particular case." **MCL 722.27a(10)** requires the Hague-Convention prohibition ("shall contain"). **MCL 722.27a(11):** during each parent's time, that parent decides routine matters. *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

Tie each proposed-order paragraph to its subsection (see `04-proposed-order.md`). The **communication method** requested in paragraph 11 of the motion rests on **(9)(i)** together with **(9)(h)**: a notice requirement is unworkable without a defined, provable channel and a response time. This is child-centered logistics — the order already commands the parents to "communicate," and this asks the Court only to say *how* and *by when*.

> **The limiter, in the same section.** This is a request for **different** structure (a written, recorded, child-only method), not for **less** structure. If any safety concern exists in this case, say plainly that the moving party seeks a written and recorded method, not direct access. Do not present any commercial co-parenting application as court-favored; propose it generically — "a written communication platform that retains records, or another method the Court directs." `ℹ PROVISIONAL — no verified information on what any Genesee judge or referee prefers.`

## VIII. An enforceable order is the point

> **MCL 552.641(2):** the friend of the court "may decline to respond" to an alleged parenting-time-order violation where "(c) The … order does not include an enforceable provision that is relevant to the … violation alleged." *(VERIFIED verbatim.)*

An order that provides parenting time "as the parties agree" contains no term that can be violated. Until specific terms exist, enforcement — make-up time, an FOC complaint, contempt — has nothing to attach to. **Specific terms now are enforceability later.**

> **The limiter, in the same section.** The same statute cuts the other way: MCL 552.641(2)(a) lets the FOC decline where a complainant filed two or more unwarranted complaints with unpaid assessed costs, and (2)(b) lets it decline a violation more than **56 days** old. Over-complaining can close the door this motion is trying to open. *(VERIFIED.)*

## IX. Findings are required, and the FOC report is not evidence unless you agree

- **MCR 3.210(D)(1):** "findings of fact and conclusions of law are required on contested postjudgment motions to modify a final judgment or order." This binds the **judge**, not only the referee. *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.4.)*
- If a friend-of-the-court report issues, **do not stipulate it into evidence.** It "is not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree to admit it" and the court's findings "must be based upon competent evidence adduced at the hearing" — ***Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich App 77; 437 NW2d 318 (1989)** (VERIFIED; `verification_ledger.md` §4.2). **⚠ Renumbering note:** *Duperon* cites the FOC duty as "MCL 552.505(d)" and the de novo hearing as "MCL 552.507(5)" — old numbering; current provisions are **MCL 552.505(1)(g)–(h)** and **MCL 552.507(4)**. Bracket-note any quotation.
