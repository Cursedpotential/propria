## The one thing to understand before anything else

**A referee hearing is a real evidentiary hearing, and it is your best and possibly only chance to build the record.**

> **MCR 3.215(D)(1): "The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply to referee hearings."** (VERIFIED verbatim.)

It is a mistake to treat the referee hearing as a warm-up. If you later object and get a hearing before Judge Reyes, the judge **may** decide largely on the referee record and **may** bar evidence you *could* have presented to the referee but did not — while still being required to let you present live evidence:

> **MCR 3.215(F)(2):** "the court may conduct the judicial hearing by review of the record of the referee hearing, **but the court must allow the parties to present live evidence at the judicial hearing.** The court may, in its discretion: … (c) prohibit a party from introducing new evidence or calling new witnesses unless there is an adequate showing that the evidence was not available at the referee hearing…" (VERIFIED verbatim.)

**So: everything goes in at the referee hearing.** Every exhibit, every date, all of it. Do not hold anything back for "later." There may not be a later, and the door to new evidence may be shut.

## Part A — Before the hearing

### Bring three tabbed exhibit sets

One for Referee Stone, one for the other side, one for you. Pre-marked **A** (your texts) and **B** (the January 8, 2025 letter). Print complete, legible copies. Bring the file copy of your March 15, 2023 order too.

### Build your proof table — what you must actually prove

| What must be established | The exhibit / testimony | How it comes in |
|---|---|---|
| What the order says | File copy of the March 15, 2023 order | It is already in the court's file; bring a clean copy |
| That you requested parenting time, when, and what you asked | **Exhibit A** (your texts) + **your testimony** that you sent them | Your messages offered to show the request was made and its date, not for their truth (MRE 801(c)); you sponsor them (MRE 901) |
| What the other side answered (or that no reply came) | The replies within **Exhibit A** | An opposing party's statement — **not hearsay**, MRE 801(d)(2) |
| The mail-only direction and its date | **Exhibit B** | You received it; you sponsor it |
| Where your daughter primarily lives (the ECE) | **Your testimony** about the living arrangement | MCL 722.27(1)(c) is a fact test; **concede what is true** |
| Your clean compliance record | **Your testimony** and your contact log | You kept a dated log, did not violate the order, are current on support, missed no exchange |

**On the texts:** Michigan has no self-authentication route for digital records (MRE 902 ends at (11), VERIFIED). Each text needs you as a live sponsoring witness. Preserve the full threads with date/time stamps; delete nothing.

### Two doors that are closed — do not waste the hearing on them

- **Your daughter's out-of-court statements.** MRE 803A applies "in criminal and delinquency proceedings only" by its own text (VERIFIED). Not available here. Build no part of your case on what she said, and ask her nothing in order to have something to say. She is not a witness, source, or messenger. Absolute.
- **The Friend of the Court's own report — and the argument that depends on your NOT signing anything.** The evidence rules do not apply to the court's *consideration* of the FOC report (MRE 1101(b)(9)), but the report is a different question as to *admissibility*:

> ***Duperon v Duperon*, 175 Mich App 77; 437 NW2d 318 (1989)** (published): the FOC report "is **not admissible as evidence unless both parties agree to admit it in evidence,**" "may be considered … as an aid to understanding the issues," but "the trial court's **ultimate findings … must be based upon competent evidence adduced at the hearing,**" and the report "**may not form the basis for the trial court's findings.**"
>
> ***Marshall v Beal*, 158 Mich App 582, 591 (1986):** where the parties "did not stipulate to allowing the friend of the court's recommendations or report into evidence" and the court "reviewed and adopted the friend of the court's findings" instead of reaching its own conclusion — "**This is a clear legal error which requires reversal** and a new hearing."

> **Do not stipulate the FOC report into evidence.** Signing it in converts a not-evidence document into evidence — for you and against you. If a recommendation's findings track the report while the hearing record does not independently support them, that is an objection you can make — **but only if you did not stipulate.** State the objection at its true width: the referee is *entitled to read* the report; object to findings that *rest* on it, not to the reading of it.
>

## Part B — At the hearing: a running order

1. **Arrive early.** Three tabbed exhibit sets, the file copy of your order, your motion and brief, one page of notes.
2. **Confirm it is being recorded** — MCR 3.215(D)(4) requires a record of all hearings. (This is the court's record. It is **not** permission for you to record anything.)
3. **State what you want in one sentence** — the same sentence from the motion.
4. **Move through the exhibits in date order.** Identify each, say what it is, say how you know, offer it.
5. **Testify to the dates.** No adjectives, no motive, no characterization.
6. **Concede what is true**, especially where your daughter primarily lives. Conceding the established custodial environment is not a loss — it puts you in the lower-burden (preponderance) lane under *Shade* and *Pierron*.
7. **Answer the other side's points factually, or not at all.**
8. **Hand up the proposed order** (already attached to the motion; have a clean copy).
9. **Ask for findings on the record** on the contested points.
10. **Before you leave the building, write down:** the date, who appeared, what was decided or taken under advisement, and **whether anything was handed to you** — that last one can start a clock.

### What not to say — ever, in any room or hallway

Not "alienation," "narcissist," "gaslighting," "coercive control," "weaponized," not "she always" or "he never." **The parent who uses those words becomes the subject of the hearing.** Answer the question asked, then stop talking. Do not litigate the March 15, 2023 hearing — every window from it closed years ago; one neutral sentence if the referee asks, nothing more.

## Part C — Likely opposition, and your one-line answers

Prepare a one-page answer sheet for your hand at the table (not filed, not read aloud). Anticipation has to live in your opening brief — **there is normally no reply brief** (MCR 2.119(A)(2)(b)), and the response can arrive as few as 5 days before the hearing.

| Their likely argument | Your one-sentence answer |
|---|---|
| **"Subsection (8) is for the *grant* of parenting time; this is a *modification*, which needs proper cause / change of circumstances."** | "I'm not asking the Court to decide that — Section IV of my brief pleads the threshold in the alternative, and I'm prepared to meet it today." |
| **"No change of circumstances — he just started texting."** | "The change isn't the texts; it's what a term operating only on agreement has produced since March 2023. Under *Shade* and *Pierron*, because this doesn't alter where my daughter lives, the burden is a preponderance." |
| **"This is really a bid for more time / custody."** | "The regular schedule I ask for is about 52 overnights a year — fewer than the 104 the support order already credits me. It doesn't change her primary home or day-to-day care." |
| **"A flexible, agree-as-you-go term serves the child."** | "A specific order is a floor, not a ceiling — nothing in it stops the parents from agreeing to more. What a floor gives us is something that works on a week we don't agree." |
| **"He didn't request time for a long stretch / hasn't exercised parenting time" (MCL 722.27a(7)(g)).** | "I'm not asking anyone to overlook any gap. What I can show is what happened once I asked in writing — the dated requests in Exhibit A — and that I've kept a log, not violated the order, stayed current on support, and missed no exchange (factor (f))." |
| **A safety "reservation" (MCL 722.27a(7)(c)/(d) or (3)).** | "No showing of endangerment has been made or attempted, and MCL 722.27a(3) requires clear and convincing evidence on the record. My motion doesn't depend on scoring the other parent; it rests on the statute, the presumption, and my own record." |

> **The limiter, in the same breath (GUARDRAILS §7).** Best-interest factor (j) cannot be scored against a parent for reasonable protective action against sexual assault or domestic violence — MCL 722.23(j), second sentence. That is exactly why this packet is built on MCL 722.27a(8), the subsection (1) presumption, the subsection (3) right, and your documented request record — **not** on characterizing the other parent. A case built on the record survives that moment; a case built on accusation collapses.

**On the reasons the other side may give for the missed dates:** do not fight them. If they say a date was unavailable or your daughter was ill, take it — "That may well be true. That is the argument for a specific order: under a term that operates only on agreement, an ordinary reason ends the parenting time entirely, with nothing to fall back on." **If you find yourself preparing to prove your daughter was not really sick, stop.** That fight is unwinnable and makes the hearing about the other parent's honesty instead of the order's terms.

## Part D — After the hearing: the clocks

**The moment anything is handed to you or arrives in the mail, write the date on it and calendar the deadline. Count from the earliest date you could have had it, and act a week early.** 🔴

| Clock | Trigger | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **7 days** — object to the accuracy/completeness of a **proposed recommended order** (file specific written objections + notice of hearing + an alternative proposed order) | Service of the proposed recommended order | MCR 3.215(E)(3)(d) |
| **21 days** — object to the **recommendation** on the merits: file a **written objection AND a notice of hearing**, stating the specific findings or law objected to | Recommendation **served** (rule) / **made available** (statute) — **count from the earlier** | MCR 3.215(E)(4); MCL 552.507(4) `⊗ CONFLICTED triggers — act early` |
| **7 days** — notice of intent to use referee-record evidence at the judicial hearing | Before that hearing | MCR 3.215(D)(4)(c) |
| **21 days** — motion to amend/add findings | Entry | MCR 2.517(B) |

### Two traps in the hallway

- **The consent trap — MCR 3.215(E)(8):** you waive your right to object by "consenting in writing to the immediate entry of the recommended order." **If the recommendation gives you specific, enforceable terms, signing may be right. If it does not, do not sign in a hallway.** Take it home; read it against your proposed order line by line; you have 21 days.
- **The same-day-hearing trap — MCR 3.215(E)(7):** if offered a same-day judicial hearing before you have seen the written recommendation, you may **refuse** it — and usually should, because you cannot yet know what you would object to.

### Ask for findings — and there is a rule stronger than most people know

At the referee level, MCR 3.215(E)(1)(a) requires the referee to "find facts specially and state separately the law the referee applied." **And at the judge's level, findings are *required*:**

> **MCR 3.210(D):** "The court must make findings of fact as provided in MCR 2.517, except that **(1) findings of fact and conclusions of law are required on contested postjudgment motions to modify a final judgment or order**…" (VERIFIED verbatim.)

**Your motion is a contested postjudgment motion to modify.** Ask for findings on the record. If they are entered but incomplete, MCR 2.517(B) gives you 21 days from entry to move to amend them.

## When to stop and get help

- A **recommendation you want to object to** but cannot state, in one sentence, which specific finding is wrong (a vague objection risks MCR 3.215(F)(3) costs).
- The other side seeks **costs or fees** against you.
- Anyone asks your **daughter** to speak to anyone, or suggests she be interviewed. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- A **CPS, police, or criminal** matter surfaces, or any **recording** comes up, in any form. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- The recommendation changes **custody** rather than parenting time — different burden, and that kind of order **is** appealable of right on a 21-day jurisdictional clock (MCR 7.202(6)(a)(iii); MCR 7.204(A)). 🔴
