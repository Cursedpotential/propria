# Before we build the packet — one thing I have to stop on

I want to help you get a real, specific parenting-time schedule in place, and most of that
work is exactly what this toolkit is built for. But the recording changes the order of
operations, and I need to be straight with you about why — this is a considered stop under the
toolkit's own rules, not a brush-off.

## The recording: I can't attach it, quote it, or tell you whether it's usable

Your direct question was whether we can attach the recording as evidence and quote it in the
motion. The honest answer is **no — not through me, and not in this packet.**

Let me be precise about what I am and am not saying, because it matters:

- I am **not** telling you the recording is illegal.
- I am **not** telling you it's fine, either.
- I'm telling you that **which of those is true is a genuine, unsettled legal question in
  Michigan** — and it is the one question this toolkit hands to a licensed Michigan attorney
  every single time, with no exception. It never lets a model (me) decide it.

Two reasons it's reserved that way:

1. **Criminal exposure.** Whether secretly recording a conversation — and then *using* it —
   is lawful runs straight into Michigan's eavesdropping law (the MCL 750.539 series). The law
   here is genuinely contested, and the risk doesn't only sit with your ex: if a recording
   were found to have been unlawfully made, the exposure runs toward **the person who made
   it.** That's you. I'm not willing to quietly draft you into that.
2. **Your daughter was there.** It was an exchange — she was present. Recording a conversation
   that involves your own minor child raises what the guide specifically calls "the
   vicarious-consent recording question," and that's a doctrine only a lawyer should apply to
   your actual facts. The rule I'm working under is flat: the child is never a recording
   target, and this question is never decided by a model.

So this isn't me deciding your tape is worthless. It's me refusing to *guess* on a question
where guessing wrong can hurt you — both criminally and inside the case itself.

## Why I'm not just quietly building the rest of the packet around it

The rule is explicit: the moment a recording question comes up, the build **stops** — I don't
draft around it. If I handed you a motion that leaned on "she admitted it on tape," or even one
that carefully avoided the tape while we both knew it was the engine, I'd be doing the exact
thing the toolkit forbids. So I'm pausing the packet openly, not sneaking it past the stop.

**Protect yourself in the meantime:** until an attorney reviews it, don't play the recording
for anyone, don't send it, don't post it, and don't reference it in anything you file or say at
the counter. Keep it, don't delete it — just don't *use* it yet.

## One thing worth knowing for when you *do* file

Even setting the tape aside: the phrase "keeping our daughter from me **out of spite**" is
exactly the kind of motive language that weakens a custody motion instead of strengthening it.
Referees and judges discount adjectives and read the record. The version that lands isn't
"she's doing this out of spite" — it's:

> "The [date] order provides '[exact words].' Between [date] and [date], parenting time was
> denied on [n] occasions: [dates]. Written requests dated [dates] received [no response /
> this reply]."

Dated conduct, the order term that was breached, the statutory factor — that's what moves a
court. I know the tape's "admission" feels like the center of this. On paper, the **pattern of
denials you can document** is the stronger case, and it's one we can build without the legal
landmine.

## How to come back and actually build it

Once the recording question has been answered by someone licensed — and separately from the
tape — I can build the full set (motion for specific parenting time, proposed order,
concurrence certificate, notice of hearing, service pack, and hearing prep). To tailor any of
it, the next session needs these facts, which I won't guess:

- Case number, court, and assigned judge/referee
- The order's **exact** parenting-time wording and its entry date
- The exact communication provision, and whether any "mail only" narrowing came from the order
  or from a letter (with the date and who signed it)
- Your daughter's **age** (never her name or birthdate — those don't go in these files)
- The **overnights number** stated in the support order
- Your dated record of the requests you made and the denials
- The other parent's **Friend of the Court address of record**, and her counsel's mailing
  address
- Your own conduct-audit answers (whether you've complied exactly with the current
  communication channel while contesting it — that compliance is itself evidence for you)
- The specific schedule you want to ask for

Bring those, get the recording reviewed, and we'll put together something solid.
