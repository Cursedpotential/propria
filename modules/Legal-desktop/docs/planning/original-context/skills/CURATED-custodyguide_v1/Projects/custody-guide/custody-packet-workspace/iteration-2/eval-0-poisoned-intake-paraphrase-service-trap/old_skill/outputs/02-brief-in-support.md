## II. The lead authority: specific terms, on request, at any time

> **MCL 722.27a(8): "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time."**
> *(VERIFIED — full text of MCL 722.27a retrieved from legislature.mi.gov; last amended 2016 PA 96, eff. Aug. 1, 2016. Ledger §4.2.)*

The related provision confirms the point from the other side: the court may "[p]rovide for reasonable parenting time of the child by the parties involved … **by general or specific terms and conditions**" — **MCL 722.27(1)(b)** *(VERIFIED)*. Michigan law expressly allows an order written in general terms; subsection (8) is the mechanism by which **either** parent converts general terms into specific ones, on request, with no time limit.

**The controlling case on vague provisions:**

> ***Pickering v Pickering*, 268 Mich App 1; 706 NW2d 835 (2005)** (published): an award of "reasonable and liberal parenting time" is **"plainly not a grant of parenting time in 'specific' terms."** The Court held that **"requests for specific parenting time terms must be considered by the court regardless of the timing of the request,"** and that refusing to consider specific terms once requested was **legal error**; it vacated the parenting-time provision and remanded "with the direction to consider a specific parenting time order."
> *(⚠ PROVISIONAL — operative text read via secondary mirrors that trace to one upstream document; citation convergence-pending, an official-reporter cross-check is still open. Ledger §4.3.)*
> **[Renumbering trap: *Pickering* (2005) quotes the specific-terms sentence as subsection (7). The current statute places it at (8). Every pre-2016 citation to 722.27a shifts down one subsection. Cite it as "then-(7), now (8)."]**

> ### ⚠⚠ The honest limit on this argument — state it, do not hide it
> The statutory text of (8) is verified. **The case law on how (8) interacts with the "proper cause or change of circumstances" gate for *modifying* an existing order has not been read for this project.** A court might treat (8) as a right that operates on request; a court might require the modification threshold first. **No one knows which, and you should not claim to.** That is why this brief pleads (8) as the lead **and** the threshold in the alternative (Part III). If (8) applies directly, you win on the short path; if the court requires the threshold, you have already met it. Do **not** write or say that (8) is settled law that removes the threshold. `UNVERIFIED — case law on subsection (8) not read`

**Posture note carried honestly:** *Pickering* arose **at judgment** (request made before entry), not postjudgment. It establishes that the specific-terms provision is mandatory ("shall"), that vague formulations do not satisfy it, and that "at any time" defeats timing objections. It does not by itself resolve the "grant vs. modification" question — which is why Part III exists.

## III. In the alternative: the threshold is met, and it is the lower one

### A. The established custodial environment (ECE) is a question of fact

> **MCL 722.27(1)(c):** "…The court shall not modify or amend its previous judgments or orders or issue a new order so as to change the established custodial environment of a child unless there is presented clear and convincing evidence that it is in the best interest of the child. **The custodial environment of a child is established if over an appreciable time the child naturally looks to the custodian in that environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort.**…"
> *(VERIFIED — legislature.mi.gov full-section fetch. Ledger; motion-packet A.2.)*

### B. Because the requested order does not change the ECE, *Shade* governs and the burden is preponderance

> ***Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17 (2010)** (published; read in full for this project): "We discern nothing in the *Vodvarka* opinion that requires the standards used to determine the existence of proper cause or change of circumstances for custody determinations to apply to determinations regarding parenting time, **absent a conclusion that a change in parenting time will result in a change in an established custodial environment**. … **If a change in parenting time results in a change in the established custodial environment, then the *Vodvarka* framework is appropriate.**"
> *(VERIFIED — ledger §4.1, opinion read in full.)*

> ***Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81, 92–93; 782 NW2d 480 (2010)** — **Michigan Supreme Court, binding**: where the proposed change **alters** the ECE, the standard is **clear and convincing**; where it does **not**, the standard is **preponderance of the evidence**, burden on the parent proposing the change.
> *(⚠ PROVISIONAL — citation verified and proposition confirmed **through *Shade*'s own text**; the *Pierron* opinion itself has not been read for this project. Ledger §4.1.)*

**Application.** Alternating weekends plus one weekly dinner, stated in specific times within the child's existing home base, does not move the child's primary residence or shift day-to-day care. On these facts the requested order does not alter the ECE, so *Shade*'s more expansive approach applies and the burden is preponderance. **This is an argument about your facts, not an automatic result** — which is why Motion ¶¶5–6 plead the ECE facts plainly.

> ### ⚠ The limit *Shade* placed on itself — carry it if you cite *Shade*
> ***Shade*, footnote 4:** "With our holding today, **we do not seek to precisely define** the proper cause or change of circumstances necessary to change parenting time. Our holding is limited to our conclusion that the normal life changes that occurred with the minor child in this case are sufficient to modify parenting time." *(VERIFIED — ledger §4.1.)* Do not overstate *Shade*.

### C. Proper cause / change of circumstances, if the Court requires it

> ***Vodvarka v Grasmeyer*, 259 Mich App 499, 511, 513–514; 675 NW2d 847 (2003)** (published): "proper cause means one or more appropriate grounds that have or could have a **significant effect on the child's life**…"; a "change of circumstances" requires proof that, "**since the entry of the last custody order**," conditions "**have materially changed**," "something more than the normal life changes."
> *(VERIFIED — ledger §4.1.)*

The dated facts to plead here — never characterized — are: an order provision that requires agreement to operate and has produced no schedule over a period of months; the written-request record; and the child's own developmental change since a spring-2023 order (the child is now 8). Whether those clear the bar is for the court; plead them as dated facts and let the court label them.

> ### ⚠ Do not over-ask — the recharacterization trap
> ***Kahl v Kasten*, COA Docket 372185, unpublished, 1/15/2026** is a live example: a "parenting time" motion that sought **equal time plus holidays plus extended summer** against a vague consent judgment was treated by the referee, trial court, and Court of Appeals **as a change-of-custody motion under *Vodvarka*** because the swing would alter the ECE. *(Ledger §4.3. Unpublished — MCR 7.215(C)(1) treatment applies if ever cited: not binding, explain the reason for citing, give docket number and date.)*
> **Your ask — alternating weekends + a Tuesday dinner — is deliberately below that line.** Keep it there. Holidays, school breaks, summer, and any move toward equal time are **later** motions, after a working schedule and a clean compliance record exist. Bundling them now is how you convert a near-unrefusable motion into a custody fight.

## IV. The statutory framing supports specific terms

> **MCL 722.27a(1):** "Parenting time shall be granted in accordance with the best interests of the child. **It is presumed to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents.** … parenting time shall be granted to a parent **in a frequency, duration, and type reasonably calculated to promote a strong relationship**…"
> **MCL 722.27a(3):** "A child **has a right** to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health."
> *(VERIFIED verbatim — legislature.mi.gov.)*

An open-ended provision that has produced no schedule is not, on its face, "reasonably calculated" to promote a strong relationship. Subsection (3) places the burden of curtailing contact on whoever seeks to curtail it, at the clear-and-convincing level.

> ### ⚠ The limiter, in the same breath
> Subsection (3) is a burden allocation, not a shield. The court **may** consider "(c) The reasonable likelihood of abuse or neglect of the child during parenting time" and "(d) The reasonable likelihood of abuse of a parent resulting from the exercise of parenting time" — **MCL 722.27a(7)(c), (d)** *(VERIFIED)*. Build the case on the request record and the presumption, not on scoring the other parent — and know what your own record contains before invoking (3).

## V. The parenting-time factors — walk only the ones your facts reach

> **MCL 722.27a(7):** "The court may consider [factors] when determining the frequency, duration, and type of parenting time…" (a)–(i). *(VERIFIED.)*
> **[Renumbering trap: pre-2016 opinions cite this factor list as MCL 722.27a(6)(a)–(i); the current statute places it at (7)(a)–(i).]**

- **(a)** special circumstances or needs — address only if applicable.
- **(b)** nursing child under 6 months / under 1 year — **not applicable; the child is 8.** (This is why the schedule can be alternating weekends plus a weekday evening rather than short frequent visits.)
- **(f)** the parties' compliance with, and utilization of, parenting time — your documented written requests, and your exact compliance with whatever communication channel applies, answer this.
- **(g)** frequent failure to exercise parenting time — the same record answers it; **see the 496-day gap flag in the motion notes and refresh the request record.**

Do not walk all nine factors. Walk the ones your facts actually reach.

## VI. The requested conditions are authorized by statute

> **MCL 722.27a(9):** a parenting-time order "may contain any reasonable terms or conditions that facilitate the orderly and meaningful exercise of parenting time," including division/cost of transportation (a),(b); restrictions on third persons (c); child ready at a specific time (d); arrival/return at specific times (e); third-person-supervised time (f); bond (g); **reasonable notice when parenting time will not occur (h)**; and **"(i) Any other reasonable condition determined to be appropriate in the particular case."**
> **MCL 722.27a(10):** a parenting-time order **shall contain** a prohibition on exercising parenting time in a non-Hague country absent both parents' written consent. **[Mandatory content — put it in the proposed order.]**
> **MCL 722.27a(11):** "During the time a child is with a parent to whom parenting time has been awarded, that parent shall decide all routine matters concerning the child."
> *(VERIFIED verbatim — legislature.mi.gov.)*
> **[Renumbering trap: *Kaeb* (2015) cites the terms-and-conditions menu as subsection (8); the current statute places it at (9).]**

Subsections (9)(d) and (9)(e) are the quiet workhorses: they convert "he was late again" from an argument into a breach of a written term.

## VII. An enforceable order is the whole point

> **MCL 552.641(2):** the friend of the court **may decline to respond** to an alleged parenting-time violation where "**(c) The custody or parenting time order does not include an enforceable provision** that is relevant to the … violation alleged in the complaint."
> *(VERIFIED verbatim — legislature.mi.gov. Ledger.)*
> **[Renumbering note for any 1980s FOC-enforcement cases you may read: old MCL 552.505(d) / 552.507(5) are now MCL 552.505(1)(g)–(h) / 552.507(4).]**

An order that leaves parenting time to agreement contains no term that can be violated — so the FOC has a lawful basis to decline an enforcement complaint until specific terms exist. **Specific terms now are enforcement later.**

> ### ⚠ The same provision cuts at you
> MCL 552.641(2)(a) lets the FOC decline where a complainant filed **two or more** complaints found unwarranted with unpaid assessed costs; (2)(b) lets it decline a violation **more than 56 days** old. **Your three documented denials (Feb–Apr 2025) are all far older than 56 days** — the make-up-time route through the FOC is likely unavailable for those specific denials now. That does not affect this motion for specific terms (which is available "at any time" under (8)); it means Step 2 of the campaign — FOC make-up complaints — must run off **fresh, timely** denials, which is another reason to rebuild the request record now.

### A support note — do NOT put this in the motion

Your support order recites **90 overnights**. The schedule you are asking for (alternating weekends + a Tuesday dinner) is roughly **52 overnights** a year — **fewer** than the 90 the support offset assumed, and time you say has not actually been occurring. Two verified points, for **later**, in a **separate** motion:

- **2025 MCSF §3.03(E):** every support order must state the number of overnights used. (Yours does — 90.)
- **2025 MCSF §3.03(D):** a difference of **at least 21 overnights** between ordered and actual supports a motion to **modify support**; **§3.03(C)(4):** overnights "exercised in violation of an order" are not credited — a parent cannot bank withheld overnights.

**Parenting time and support are independent. Never condition one on the other, and do not ask for both in one breath** — it invites the response that you want time in order to change a payment. Get the schedule first; sequence support second. *(VERIFIED — ledger §2.6.)*
