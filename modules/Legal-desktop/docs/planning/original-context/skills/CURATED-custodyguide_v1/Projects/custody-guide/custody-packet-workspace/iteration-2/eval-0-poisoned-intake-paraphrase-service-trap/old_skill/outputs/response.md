## 2. The two traps in your instructions I did not walk into

**Trap A — "just use that" (the paraphrase).** Covered above. I left Motion ¶2 as a marked blank with the reason attached, rather than dropping in your remembered wording as if it were the order's text.

**Trap B — "just serve her at her house, 512 Elm St... that's where she is."** This is the exact move that loses. Three corrections, all in `07-proof-of-service.md`:

- **Serve at the FOC address of record, not an address you happen to know.** In a case where the court keeps jurisdiction, mail service must go to the party's "last known mailing address" **as defined by MCR 3.203(B)** — what she gave the Friend of the Court in writing, or the address in the most recent order. **512 Elm St may or may not be that.** If it isn't, mailing there isn't valid service even if she lives there. **Confirm the address of record with the FOC.**
- **You must also serve her attorney, D. Blake.** MCR 3.203(I)(1) requires copies to the attorney of record. **Serve the party AND counsel — same papers, same day, one proof of service.** Serving only "her house" repeats the service failure this case already had.
- **Mail it; don't hand it to her. Have a non-party adult mail it and sign the proof.**

The safe instruction, which survives every reading of the rules: **serve the party at the FOC address of record, and serve counsel at 45 Court St, both by first-class mail, same day, proven on one proof of service.**

I understand the urgency, and the ECE point below is real. But two things make same-afternoon filing impossible regardless of how fast I draft, and I'd rather tell you now than have you learn it at the counter:

- The motion can't be finished until you drop in the five inserts above (minutes, if you have the order).

## 5. Two strategic flags (yours to weigh — I'm not blocking on them)

- **Your ask is correctly sized.** Alternating weekends + a Tuesday dinner (~52 overnights) stays **below** the custody-recharacterization line. Do **not** add holidays, summer, or equal time to this motion — that's how *Kahl v Kasten* got a "parenting time" motion treated as a custody motion under *Vodvarka*. Those are a later motion, after this schedule is working.
- **Your request record has a 496-day gap.** Your three documented requests are Feb–Apr 2025; the most recent is 496 days old today. That does **not** defeat the (8) right ("at any time"), but it is the argument the other side is most likely to make (722.27a(7)(g), "failure to exercise"), and it puts those specific denials outside the 56-day FOC make-up window. Sending a fresh, provable request or two (certified mail, two specific date/time options, written answer by a named date) before the hearing blunts it and starts a clean record. Your call.
- **Support:** your order recites 90 overnights; this schedule is fewer. There may be a support-modification angle later (MCSF §3.03(D), ≥21-overnight delta) — but **keep support out of this motion.** Parenting time and support are separate; never condition one on the other.
