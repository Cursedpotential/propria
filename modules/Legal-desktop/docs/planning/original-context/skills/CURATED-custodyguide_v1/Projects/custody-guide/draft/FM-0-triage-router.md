## Four things about deadlines that catch people

**1. The clock usually starts when you were *served*, not when you read it.**
Not when you understood it. Not when you found a lawyer. If a document sat in your mailbox for six days, you have already used six days.

**2. "Entry" of an order is not the day you got it.**
For appeals, the 21 days run from **entry** — the day the judge signs the order, or the day it is entered in the court's register of actions. You may receive it days later. You do not get those days back.

**3. Doing nothing is a decision that the court will honor.**

- Don't object to an ex parte order in 14 days → **it becomes a temporary order.**
- Don't object to a referee recommendation in 21 days → **it becomes a final order.**
- Don't answer an FOC make-up parenting time notice in 21 days → **you have agreed that you wrongfully denied parenting time.**

**4. Filing something is not the same as serving it, and neither is the same as getting it heard.**
In Genesee County a motion generally needs four things the rules do not put in one place: it must be **served** far enough ahead (9 days if you mail it, 7 if you deliver it), it must be **filed** at least 7 days ahead — a separate deadline from service — it must have a **proposed order attached and served with it**, and it must carry a **concurrence certificate** saying you contacted the other side and asked them to agree. Miss any one and your motion may not be heard. See **M4.3**. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm current practice with the Circuit Court Records counter`

## ⚠ One warning about a document you may find on the court's website

Genesee County posts a **"Notice Regarding Motion Practice."** It is dated 2019 and it tells you to file your motion **7 days** before the hearing.

**That is a true statement of one rule, and it leaves out the other one.** There are two separate deadlines, and you must meet both:

- **Filing:** a motion must be **filed** at least **7 days** before the hearing — MCR 2.119(C)(4). That is the number the county notice gives you.
- **Serving:** the motion and notice of hearing must be **served** at least **9 days** before the hearing if you serve by first-class mail, or **7 days** if you serve by delivery — MCR 2.119(C)(1). The county notice does not mention this.

If you follow the county's notice and mail your motion 7 days out, **you are two days late on service.**

Serve early, file early, and ask the clerk what the assigned judge expects. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — whether the 2019 notice is still in force, and current motion-day practice`

## What this book is, in one paragraph

This is **legal information, not legal advice.** No lawyer-client relationship is created by reading it. It cannot tell you what to do in your case, because outcomes turn on facts, evidence, and the judge — none of which a book can see. Michigan statutes, court rules, local orders, forms, and fees change, and the Genesee County details in this book have a **shorter shelf life than the rest of it**. Verify anything you are about to rely on. Where this book says a lawyer is needed, that is not a formality — it marks a place where getting it wrong is hard or impossible to undo. See **FM.1** and **Appendix K**.
