## Route tag

**Postjudgment.** An order already exists in your Genesee County case (entered March 15, 2023). You are asking the Court to state an existing parenting-time provision in specific terms and to set the logistics that make it work. This is **not** an appeal, **not** a reconsideration, and **not** a request to change custody. A new request about the same child in a case where the court has continuing jurisdiction is filed as a **motion in the existing case** — MCR 3.204(A)(1); postjudgment domestic-relations motions are governed by MCR 2.119 — MCR 3.213. *(VERIFIED.)*

## What you are asking for, in one sentence

> **You are asking the Court to state your parenting time in specific dates, times, and places, and to set the conditions that make those terms work, so the order can be followed and enforced without anyone having to agree to anything.**

## Drafting rules for the numbered paragraphs

- **One fact per paragraph, each with a date.**
- **Quote the order; never characterize it.** Paragraph 2 quotes it. Everything after is measured against those words.
- **No adjectives about a person.** Not one. If a sentence would change meaning read aloud in a flat voice by a stranger, rewrite it.
- **Nothing about the other parent's character, motives, mental state, or new relationships.** The banned words appear nowhere: *narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, personality disorder, manipulation, vindictive.* A filing containing them becomes a filing about the person who wrote it.
- **The history gets no more than one neutral sentence.** Every window to revisit the March 15, 2023 hearing closed long ago. The motion litigates forward.
- **Paragraph 4 is a compliance paragraph disguised as background.** It tells the Court, without argument, that you used the communication provision the order gives you. Your conduct audit — a dated contact log, no order violations, current on support, no missed exchanges — is proven by the exhibits, not asserted by adjective.
- **Paragraph 8 concedes the established custodial environment openly.** Conceding it is not a loss. It is what puts the motion in the lower-burden lane (see the brief, Part IV).

## Why paragraphs 5, 6, and 7 are written the way they are

- **Paragraph 5 (the text requests).** Text messages are writings. Your own sent texts, plus your dated log, establish that each request was made and when. Any *reply* from the other parent is an opposing party's statement and is **not hearsay** — MRE 801(d)(2). Where a request drew no reply, say "no response was received" and let the silence sit next to the date. Do not guess why. **Note:** Michigan has no self-authentication shortcut for text messages (MRE 902 ends at (11)); you sponsor them yourself through your verified statement. See `08-exhibit-list.md`.
- **Paragraph 6 (counsel's January 8, 2025 letter).** State it as a fact, attach it, and stop. The one load-bearing point — *"That instruction does not appear in any order of this Court"* — is neutral and true. The order itself directs the parties to communicate; a letter from opposing counsel is not an order. You are not accusing anyone; you are locating a document.
- **Paragraph 7 (the 104 overnights).** This is a fact from your own support order, offered for one narrow purpose: to show the requested schedule is modest and does not enlarge your role. **Do not turn it into a support argument in this motion.** Support and parenting time are separate; see the caution in the brief and in `response.md`.
