## First: the recording — I have to stop you here

**I'm not going to tell you the recording is legal, I can't attach it as an exhibit, and
I can't quote it in the motion.** Please read this part as me looking out for you, not
lawyering you off.

Here's the honest reason it's off-limits for me:

- **Whether a secret, one-party recording of the other parent is lawful in Michigan — and
  whether a judge would let it into evidence — is genuinely unsettled law.** Getting it
  wrong doesn't just cost you an exhibit. Under Michigan's eavesdropping statute, a wrong
  move here can create **criminal exposure for you**. That flips the whole case: instead
  of her having to explain missed exchanges, *you'd* be explaining a recording. This is
  exactly the kind of question that turns on facts I can't weigh and a judgment only a
  Michigan-licensed lawyer can make — so this project routes every recording question to
  an attorney, no exceptions. It's not a maybe I get to override.

- **This happened at an exchange, which almost certainly means your daughter was present.**
  Recordings that capture a child carry their own separate set of problems on top of the
  eavesdropping question. That's one more reason it needs a lawyer's eyes before it goes
  anywhere near a court file.

**So, concretely — until a Michigan attorney has looked at it:**

- **Don't** file it, don't email it to opposing counsel, don't play it at a hearing, and
  don't quote it in any paper.
- **Don't delete it either.** Preserve it exactly as it is — original file, don't edit or
  re-save it — and let the lawyer decide. Destroying evidence can be its own problem.
- **Do** ask a licensed Michigan attorney the two questions that matter: *"Was making this
  recording lawful?"* and *"Can it be used in a Genesee County family case?"* Those are

## The part that will actually help: you don't need the tape to win this

I want to take the pressure off the recording, because I think it's carrying more weight
in your mind than it deserves. A parenting-time motion in Genesee County is won on **two
things**, and neither is a tape:

1. **A specific-schedule request under the statute.** Right now your order says something
   like *"as the parties agree,"* and Michigan law (MCL 722.27a(8)) says a parent can
   demand **specific terms** — real days, times, and an exchange location — *at any time*.
   That's the lever. It doesn't require proving why she's doing what she's doing.

2. **A clean, dated record of what you asked for and what she did.** *"I requested these
   dates in writing; here's her response or non-response"* — repeated. That paper trail is
   the case. It's evidence you can put in front of a judge with **no legal cloud over it.**

Here's the thing about the "she's doing it out of spite" quote: **judges decide these
cases on dates and terms, not motive** — and motive language actually *weakens* a filing.
The strongest version of your motion never says "spite" at all. It says *"Between [date]
and [date], parenting time was requested on [n] occasions and did not occur"* and lets the
pattern speak. So even in the best case for the recording, it isn't what moves the needle.
The request log does. **That means your case doesn't have to wait on the lawyer's answer
about the tape — we can build the whole packet without it.**
