## What is actually at stake (plain version)

"Where do I file?" feels like an administrative question. Under the UCCJEA it is a **subject-matter-jurisdiction** question, and getting it wrong is the kind of mistake that is expensive or impossible to unwind. The framework below is quoted from an **archived primary source** in this toolkit — the Michigan Judicial Institute *Domestic Violence Benchbook* (4th ed.), §8.6 — which reproduces and cites the UCCJEA statute (MCL 722.1101 *et seq.*). `VERIFIED — archived MJI benchbook`

**1. Your parenting-time motion *is* a UCCJEA proceeding.** The UCCJEA defines a *child custody proceeding* as *"a proceeding in which legal custody, physical custody, **or parenting time** with respect to a child is an issue."* (MCL 722.1102(d).) A motion to convert "as the parties agree" into a specific schedule is squarely one of these. The UCCJEA is not a side issue — it governs the very motion you want to file.

**2. Your support case being in Genesee does not answer the parenting-time question.** The UCCJEA's own definition of a "child-custody determination" **excludes** *"an order relating to child support or other monetary obligation."* (MCL 722.1102(c).) So the fact that support has always run through Genesee tells you nothing about whether Genesee can still decide **parenting time** now that the child lives out of state. Those are two different questions with two different answers.

**3. "Home state" turns on where the child actually is, not where the case started.** Michigan courts look to *"a child's **actual presence**, not his or her intent to remain."* A child who has *"lived with a parent"* in another place *"for more than six consecutive months … immediately before"* an action is filed makes **that** place the home state. (*Ramamoorthi v Ramamoorthi*, 323 Mich App 324 (2018), quoted in the benchbook; and *Nock v Miranda-Bermudez*, 347 Mich App 623 (2023) — moving children to a new state and letting six months pass made the **new** state the home state.) `VERIFIED — archived MJI benchbook`

**4. A Michigan court's continuing power can end — or be declined.** Even a court that entered the original order keeps *"exclusive, continuing jurisdiction"* only while the statutory conditions hold (MCL 722.1202); and a court that still has jurisdiction *"may decline to exercise"* it if it decides Michigan has become an **inconvenient forum** and another state is more appropriate (MCL 722.1202(2), MCL 722.1207). Whether Michigan keeps or keeps-and-declines jurisdiction here depends on facts you have not pinned down (below).

**5. There is a procedural trip-wire you cannot file around.** Michigan requires the filing party to **file a UCCJEA affidavit** with any custody-or-parenting-time dispute (MCR 3.206(B), MCL 722.1209(1)), disclosing where the child has lived. That affidavit is **signed under oath**, and it carries a **continuing duty** to keep the court informed (MCL 722.1209(4)). In other words: the first document in your own motion would require you to put the Ohio move in front of the judge — so "just file it in Genesee" is not actually available; the jurisdiction question surfaces the moment you file, whether you want it to or not. `VERIFIED — archived MJI benchbook`

**6. The downside of guessing wrong is severe.** A custody/parenting-time order entered by a court that **lacked** UCCJEA jurisdiction is not entitled to full faith and credit and can be attacked later. (*Nock*, above.) That means a packet filed in the wrong forum is not just wasted filing fees and time — it can produce an order that is **void or vulnerable**, after you have already put your position on the record under oath. That is precisely the "unrecoverable error" the guardrails tell me to stop for.

**None of this tells you the answer** — Michigan *might* still be the right court, or it might not. It tells you the answer **cannot be assumed**, and that it must be settled by a person with the authority to settle it **before** anything is filed.

## Directly answering your instinct

You wrote: *"the case has always been in Genesee so that's where I'm filing."* That is the single most common — and most dangerous — misread of how this works. "Where the case has always been" and "where a court still has jurisdiction to modify parenting time now that the child lives in Ohio" are **different questions**. The UCCJEA was written specifically to stop the first from being treated as the answer to the second. Your instinct to keep the matter in Genesee may even turn out to be right — but it has to be *decided*, not *assumed*, and I am not permitted to decide it for you.
