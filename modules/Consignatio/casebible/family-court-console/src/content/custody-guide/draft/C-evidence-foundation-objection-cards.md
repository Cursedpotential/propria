## How to use these cards

A **foundation card** is a single evidence type reduced to four things: the rule that governs it, the facts you must be able to establish before it comes in, the words you actually say at the podium, and the objection you should expect. Find the card for your item, confirm you can truthfully establish every predicate fact, deliver the script, offer the exhibit, and — if the objection comes — read the one-line answer.

**You are the advocate and, almost always, the only witness.** A self-represented parent usually has no one else to call. That has a consequence these cards are built around: for evidence *you* gathered — your texts, your screenshots, your photos — you do not stand up and question a witness, because the witness is you. You are placed under **oath**, and then you either testify in narrative or answer the questions the judge or referee asks you. So the own-evidence cards below are written as **your sworn testimony**, in first person, numbered in the order that builds the foundation. Where a card involves *another* person — the opposing party, or a records custodian you subpoenaed — it gives you the **actual questions** to ask. Where a card is about arguing to the court — an objection, an offer of proof — it gives you the words to say to the judge.

> ### "Laying a foundation is not a magic phrase"
> These scripts are not incantations, and reciting them does nothing on its own. Each line works **only because the fact behind it is true** — you really were on that text thread, you really recognize that voice, you really took that photo. The script's job is to put true facts on the record in an order that satisfies the rule. Saying the words when the facts are not true is not "foundation." It is **false testimony under oath.** If you cannot honestly say a line, do not say it — that item is not yet yours to admit, and the card will tell you what is missing.

**Two doors, every exhibit.** Getting an item admitted is two separate questions, and clearing one does nothing for the other:

1. **Authentication (Door 1) — "is it what you say it is?"** That is what most of these cards lay. The governing rules live in **Module 7**.
2. **Hearsay (Door 2) — "can the words in it come in for their truth?"** That is a separate analysis in **Module 6**. Every foundation card ends by handing you to Door 2, most often to the opposing-party-statement rule (Card 11).

**These tools cut both ways — that is the point, not a flaw (GUARDRAILS §7).** Every foundation you can lay, the other parent can lay. Every objection you can make, they can make. Your own texts and posts come in against you under the exact rule that brings theirs in against them. Read each card assuming the other side is holding the mirror image.

**The child is never a source, a courier, or a witness you prepare (GUARDRAILS §8).** No card here asks you to interview, record, coach, debrief, or relay your child. If a fact reached you only through the child, it is not your evidence to lay — route the child's statement through the hearsay analysis in **Module 6**, and understand that whether the child is ever heard is the **court's** to manage through its own private (in camera) interview, limited by rule to the child's preference (Module 5, MRE 1101(b)(6)). A disclosure of abuse is a hard stop that routes out to counsel and the professionals equipped for it, not into an exhibit binder.

**Where these apply.** The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply at the **referee hearing** and at **trial** — *"The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply to referee hearings"* (MCR 3.215(D)(1)). They do **not** apply to the judge's in camera interview of the child, or to the court's consideration of the Friend of the Court report (MRE 1101(b)(6), (9)). Confirm you are somewhere the rules apply before you rely on any card here — that is **Module 5**.

**None of these is filing-ready, and no card reproduces an official SCAO form.** These are words you *say*, not documents you file. The worked scripts use fictional names and assumed postures; they are illustrations of a foundation, not text to file or a guaranteed ruling.

**Flag legend used below:** `⚠ VERIFY` (confirm against the primary source before relying) · `ℹ PROVISIONAL` (best current information, locally or vendor-dependent) · `🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE` / `🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE` (stop; route out) · `🔴` (hard stop) · `🔒 ATTORNEY MUST VERIFY` (reserved to a licensed Michigan attorney).

## Card 1 — Text messages, email, and app messages you sent or received

### The rule
> **(a) In General.** To satisfy the requirement of authenticating or identifying an item of evidence, the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what its proponent claims it is.
> — MRE 901(a)

> The following are examples only—not a complete list—of evidence that satisfies the requirement:
> **(1) Testimony of Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
> **(4) Distinctive Characteristics and the Like.** The appearance, contents, substance, internal patterns, or other distinctive characteristics of an item, taken together with all the circumstances.
> — MRE 901(b)(1), (4)

A printout or export of electronic information is an "original," so you are not disqualified for lacking "the real one":

> For electronically-stored information, "original" means any printout—or other output readable by sight—if it accurately reflects the information.
> — MRE 1001(d) (in part)

> A duplicate is admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair to admit the duplicate.
> — MRE 1003

### The predicate facts
Before the thread comes in you must be able to establish, truthfully: **who** the participants are; that **you** were one of them (personal knowledge — MRE 901(b)(1)); **how you know** the other participant is who you say — the number or handle you have long used for them, their name as displayed, replies that answer your specific messages, facts only they knew (distinctive characteristics — MRE 901(b)(4)); the **date(s)**; that the exhibit is **complete and continuous** — not a lone bubble lifted from an exchange (completeness, MRE 106, Module 6); and that **nothing was added, deleted, or altered**. Keep the **native file** so you can answer a genuine dispute (MRE 1003; Module 8 §8.5).

### The script (you, sworn as the witness)
> 1. "My name is ___. I am the [moving party / respondent] in this case."
> 2. "Exhibit ___ is a true and accurate copy of a [text / email / OurFamilyWizard] message thread between me and [other parent], dated ___ to ___."
> 3. "I know it is between me and [other parent] because it is the [number / email / handle] I have used to reach them for [time]; it displays their name as I have it saved; and their [time] message answers my [time] message about [subject] by name."
> 4. "This exhibit shows the conversation in full, from my first message to their last. Nothing has been added, deleted, or altered."
> 5. "I [printed / exported] it on [date] from [my phone / my email / the app], and I still have the original on that [device / account]."
> 6. "I offer Exhibit ___."

*If the referee questions you instead of letting you narrate, the same facts come out as answers —* **Q:** "Whose messages are these?" → "Mine and [other parent]'s." **Q:** "How do you know?" → [line 3]. **Q:** "Is it complete and unaltered?" → [line 4].

Then hand to **Door 2 (hearsay, Module 6):** the other parent's messages are **not hearsay** offered against them — MRE 801(d)(2) (Card 11). Your own messages usually come in to show context or effect, not for their truth.

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — lack of foundation / authenticity"** (or "that could be altered"). One-line answer: *"I was a participant in this conversation, I recognize the number and the name, and the contents identify the sender — that satisfies MRE 901(b)(1) and (4); a claim that it was altered goes to weight, not admissibility."* (Keep the native file — Module 7 §7.4.) If instead you hear **"hearsay,"** answer with Card 11.

## Card 2 — Screenshots

### The rule
Same authentication rule as Card 1 — MRE 901(a), and the illustrations most in point:

> **(1) Testimony of Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
> **(4) Distinctive Characteristics and the Like.** The appearance, contents, substance, internal patterns, or other distinctive characteristics of an item, taken together with all the circumstances.
> — MRE 901(b)(1), (4)

> A duplicate is admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair to admit the duplicate.
> — MRE 1003

### The predicate facts
A screenshot is **not** admissible because it is a screenshot. The person offering it must be able to identify: **whose account and screen** it shows; **the participants**; **the date**; that the conversation is **continuous** — no gap where messages were removed; and **how and when** it was captured. The decisive predicate for a pro se litigant is a **sponsoring witness with personal knowledge** — ordinarily **you**, because you saw the screen and took the capture. A screenshot someone *else* took of a page you never saw has **no witness to lay its foundation** unless that person testifies or it arrives as a certified record (Card 9).

### The script (you, sworn as the witness)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is a screenshot I took on [date] of [whose account / what app] on [my phone / my screen]."
> 2. "I took it myself and I saw the screen at the time. It accurately shows what was on that screen."
> 3. "It shows [participants], and the conversation is continuous — I did not crop out or remove any messages between the ones shown."
> 4. "I recognize the account as [other parent]'s because [handle / profile / number / distinctive contents]."
> 5. "I still have the native file this was captured from on [device / account]."
> 6. "I offer Exhibit ___."

Then hand to **Door 2 (Module 6):** if the captured words are the other parent's, they are not hearsay against them (Card 11); a third party's words captured in the shot are a separate hearsay problem (Card 12).

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — no foundation; no witness who saw this page"** (the classic screenshot defect). One-line answer: *"I personally viewed the screen and captured this; that is a witness with knowledge under MRE 901(b)(1)."* If you did **not** take it and cannot speak to it, you have no answer — get the person who did, or obtain a certified platform record (Card 9). If the objection is **"it's cropped / incomplete"** (MRE 106): *"I can show the full, continuous thread"* — then do so.

## Card 3 — Photographs and video

### The rule
A photograph or video is authenticated by **a witness with personal knowledge of the scene** who says it fairly and accurately represents what it shows — that witness need **not** be the photographer:

> **(1) Testimony of Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
> — MRE 901(b)(1)

For footage a device generated on its own — a doorbell or security camera — the route is the **process-or-system** illustration:

> **(9) Evidence About a Process or System.** Evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result.
> — MRE 901(b)(9)

> (c) A "photograph" means a photographic image or its equivalent stored in any form.
> — MRE 1001(c)

### The predicate facts
For a **photo or silent video:** you were **familiar with the scene** and can say the image is a **fair and accurate representation** of it as it was; **what** it depicts; and, if **when** or **where** is disputed, the embedded data (EXIF date, sometimes location) and native file that support the timeline (Module 8 §8.5). For **system-generated video** (doorbell/security): identify the **system**, that it was **working normally**, and that it **produces an accurate result**.

> ### 🔴 Video with sound is a recording — route it out before you offer it
> A photograph and a silent video are ordinary evidence. **Video that captured audio of a conversation is a recording**, and whether you may lawfully make, keep, or use it is a hard stop reserved to **Module 9** and a licensed Michigan attorney (GUARDRAILS §9). This card covers only images and silent video **you lawfully have**; for anything with captured audio, see **Card 4** and Module 9 first. And **the child is never a photography or recording target** — no staging the child, no tasking the child, no recording the child (GUARDRAILS §8; Module 8).

### The script (you, sworn as the witness)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is a photograph of [what it shows], taken on or about [date] at [place]."
> 2. "I was present and I am familiar with [the scene / the location / the condition shown]."
> 3. "This photograph fairly and accurately represents [that scene] as it existed on [date]."
> 4. "[If applicable] I took it on my phone and I still have the original file, with its date information, on that phone."
> 5. "I offer Exhibit ___."

*Doorbell / security footage (system route):* "Exhibit ___ is video from my [brand] doorbell camera at [address]. The camera runs continuously and records automatically with a date-and-time stamp; it was working normally on [date]; this clip accurately shows what the camera recorded." (MRE 901(b)(9).)

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — foundation; when/where was this taken?"** One-line answer: *"The rule asks only that a witness familiar with the scene testify it is a fair and accurate representation — I am that witness; a dispute about the exact date goes to weight, and my file's metadata answers it."* If the objection is **"that's a recording"** because the video has sound — **stop**; that is Card 4 / Module 9, not something to argue from this book.

## Card 4 — Audio and voicemail 🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE / SAFETY OVERRIDE

> ### 🛑🔴 READ THIS BOX BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE ON THIS CARD
> **This card does not tell you how to make a recording, and nothing on it may be read as clearance to make one.** Whether you may lawfully **make, keep, or use** an audio recording is **UNSETTLED** in Michigan, carries **criminal exposure that runs toward the person who made the recording**, and is reserved to a **licensed Michigan attorney** — never a model, never this book (GUARDRAILS §3, §9). The eavesdropping statutes (the MCL 750.539 series) and the **vicarious-consent** question are the subject of **Module 9**, which you must read; this card does **not** repeat or resolve that analysis.
>
> **This card is only about authenticating audio that ALREADY LAWFULLY EXISTS in a form you did not create by surreptitious recording.** In practice that means exactly two things:
> - **a voicemail the other party left FOR you** on your own phone; or
> - **a recording already in the case file, or produced to you in discovery.**
>
> If your audio is **anything else** — something you recorded, a call you captured, a device left running, a child recorded or sent in with a recorder, a call that crossed state lines — **stop.** Do not offer it, do not argue its legality from this book, do not "clean it up," and do not delete it. Take it to **Module 9** and a licensed Michigan attorney. **The child is never a recording target and never a courier for one — no exceptions** (GUARDRAILS §8; Module 9). If any part of this touches a PPO, a no-contact order, CPS, or a criminal matter, safety and the criminal firewall come first (FM.2, Module 17). `DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE`

### The rule
Authentication of a voice — including on a recording — is by opinion identification:

> **(5) Opinion About a Voice.** An opinion identifying a person's voice—whether heard firsthand or through mechanical or electronic transmission or recording—based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances that connect it with the alleged speaker.
> — MRE 901(b)(5)

Authenticating the voice is a **different question from whether the recording was lawfully made** — the second question is the reserved one this book does not answer (see the box).

### The predicate facts
Only if a recording **lawfully exists** in one of the two forms in the box: that it is a **voicemail the other party left for you**, or an item **already in the file / produced in discovery**; that you can **identify the speaker's voice** from having heard it before under circumstances connecting it to that person (MRE 901(b)(5)); that the item is a **true and accurate copy** as you received it, **unaltered and complete**; and that you preserved the **native file** (Module 8 §8.5). Note the reframing this card lives by: the question is never *"if you recorded…"* — it is *"if a recording lawfully exists…"*

### The script (you, sworn — for a voicemail the other party left for you)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is a voicemail message left on my phone by [other parent] on [date]."
> 2. "I have known [other parent] for [time] and have spoken with them many times, in person and by phone. I recognize their voice."
> 3. "The voice on this message is [other parent]'s."
> 4. "This is a true and accurate copy of the voicemail as I received it. Nothing has been added, deleted, or altered, and it is the complete message."
> 5. "I offer Exhibit ___."

Then hand to **Door 2 (Module 6):** the other parent's own recorded words are **not hearsay** offered against them — MRE 801(d)(2) (Card 11). That rule tells you the *statement* can come in; it says **nothing** about whether the recording was lawfully made — do not let the one answer the other (Module 9).

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — foundation; can you prove that's their voice, and that this is unaltered?"** One-line answer: *"I know [other parent]'s voice from years of speaking with them, which satisfies MRE 901(b)(5), and this is the complete voicemail as left on my phone."* **If instead you hear "objection — improper/illegal recording,"** that is the reserved question, and it is your signal to **stop**: a voicemail the other party left for you is not something you recorded, but do not argue recording legality from this book — see Module 9 and counsel. If the audio is anything other than a voicemail left for you or an item already produced in the case, it does not belong on this card at all. `🛑`

## Card 5 — Social-media posts

### The rule
> **(1) Testimony of Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
> **(4) Distinctive Characteristics and the Like.** The appearance, contents, substance, internal patterns, or other distinctive characteristics of an item, taken together with all the circumstances.
> — MRE 901(b)(1), (4)

Platform records obtained and certified by the platform can self-authenticate as a business record:

> **(11) Certified Domestic or Foreign Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** The original or a copy of a domestic or foreign record that meets the requirements of Rule 803(6)(A)-(C), as shown by a certification of the custodian or another qualified person that complies with a Michigan statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court. Before the trial or hearing, the proponent must give an adverse party reasonable written notice of the intent to offer the record—and must make the record and certification available for inspection—so that the party has a fair opportunity to challenge them.
> — MRE 902(11)

### The predicate facts
For a post **you captured:** **whose account** it is (handle, profile name, photo, distinctive contents — MRE 901(b)(4)); that your capture is an **accurate depiction** of what was on the page; the **URL/handle**; and the **date**. For platform records: a **certified** copy from the platform can ride MRE 902(11) with the required **notice** (Card 9). Michigan courts treat social-media authentication as a live-concern area — fake accounts, hacked accounts, and deepfakes — so expect the challenge and keep your native capture and any metadata (Module 7 §7.10).

### The script (you, sworn as the witness)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is a screenshot I took on [date] of a public post on [platform]."
> 2. "The post is from the account [handle / display name], which I know to be [other parent]'s because [profile photo / the account they use / distinctive contents / it links to their known information]."
> 3. "My capture accurately shows the post and the account name and date as they appeared; the URL is ___."
> 4. "I viewed the page myself and still have the file I captured."
> 5. "I offer Exhibit ___."

Then hand to **Door 2 (Module 6):** a post **by the other parent** is not hearsay against them — MRE 801(d)(2) (Card 11). **A comment or post by a third party is a separate hearsay layer** and often stays out even though the page is authenticated (Card 12) — authenticating the page does **not** admit everyone else's words on it.

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — how do you know that's their account?"** One-line answer: *"The account name, profile, and contents identify it as [other parent]'s under MRE 901(b)(4), taken with all the circumstances."* If **"hearsay"** is aimed at a third party's comment, concede it and offer only the party's own post (Card 12).

## Card 6 — School records

### The rule
School records come in as **records of a regularly conducted activity** — the business-records exception:

> **(6) Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** A record of an act, transaction, occurrence, event, condition, opinion, or diagnosis if:
> (A) the record was made at or near the time by—or from information transmitted by—someone with knowledge;
> (B) the record was kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity of a business, organization, occupation, or calling, whether or not for profit;
> (C) making the record was a regular practice of that activity;
> (D) all these conditions are shown by the testimony of the custodian or another qualified witness, or by a certification that complies with a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court or with a statute permitting certification; and
> (E) the opponent does not show that the source of information or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness.
> — MRE 803(6)

The certificate route (avoids a live custodian) is MRE 902(11) (Card 9).

### The predicate facts
Either a **custodian or other qualified witness** establishes (6)(A)–(C), **or** a **written certification** does the same with **advance notice** (MRE 902(11); Card 9). You do **not** need the teacher who made each entry — a qualified custodian is enough. Watch two things: **hearsay within hearsay** — a school file often quotes what a *parent* told the office, and that inner statement needs its own door (Card 12); and **trustworthiness** — a record generated for the litigation, rather than kept in the ordinary course, is weaker under (6)(E).

*How you lawfully obtain school records* — a records request, or a trial subpoena (MC 11), or a discovery subpoena an unrepresented party must move the court to issue — is a **Module 10 / Appendix D** matter, not an evidence-foundation question, and access can be governed by student-privacy law this card does not resolve. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm the access route and any privacy restriction before you count on getting the record; that is Module 10, not this card.`

### The script — the certificate path (no live witness)
You do not testify to a certified record; you **offer it with notice already given**:
> "Exhibit ___ is [the student's] attendance record from [school], produced with a custodian's certification under MRE 902(11) and MRE 803(6). I gave [other party] written notice of intent to offer it and made the record and certification available on [date]. I offer Exhibit ___."

### The script — the live-custodian fallback (actual questions you ask the custodian)
> **Q:** "Please state your name and your position at [school]." · **Q:** "Are you a custodian of the school's attendance and records?" · **Q:** "Are records like Exhibit ___ made at or near the time of the events they record, by or from someone with knowledge?" · **Q:** "Are they kept in the ordinary course of the school's regular activity?" · **Q:** "Is making them a regular practice of the school?" → then: "I offer Exhibit ___."

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — hearsay / no proper custodian"** (or "no notice"). One-line answer: *"It is a record of a regularly conducted activity under MRE 803(6), authenticated by a certification under MRE 902(11), for which I gave the required written notice on [date]."* If the objection targets **a statement quoted inside** the record: *"I offer the record for [the routine entry], not for the quoted statement"* — or supply that inner statement's own door (Card 12).

## Card 7 — Medical and therapy records ⚠ privilege caveat

> ### ⚠ Privilege and confidentiality are a separate, threshold problem here
> Medical and especially **mental-health / therapy** records are protected by **privilege**, and privilege is decided before you ever reach authentication or hearsay. *"The common law governs a claim of privilege, unless a statute or court rule provides otherwise"* (**MRE 501**). The specific physician–patient and psychologist/therapist–patient privileges are **statutory**, and their scope, exceptions, and how they are **waived** are `⚠ VERIFY` items this book does not quote from a primary source (Module 6 §6.8). Two consequences a reader must not skip: **(1)** putting your **own** mental health at issue, or signing broad releases, can **waive** your protection and open your records to the other side — the symmetry rule again; **(2)** filing a child's therapy or medical records into an open court file can **harm the child** and is rarely worth what one helpful line buys. **Whether such material may be obtained, and whether it should be filed at all, is a stop-and-seek-counsel question, not a foundation question** (Module 6 §6.8; Module 17). `🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE where a child's records or a DV/PPO context is involved.`

### The rule
If a record is properly before the court, its authentication and hearsay doors are the business-records exception (**MRE 803(6)**, quoted in Card 6) and the certificate route (**MRE 902(11)**, quoted in Card 5 / Card 9). A statement a **patient** made **for treatment** has its own hearsay door:

> **(4) Statement Made for Purposes of Medical Treatment or Diagnosis in Connection with Treatment.** A statement that:
> (A) is made for—and is reasonably necessary to—medical treatment or diagnosis in connection with treatment; and
> (B) describes medical history, past or present symptoms or sensations, their inception, or their general cause.
> — MRE 803(4)

### The predicate facts
First, **privilege is cleared** (see the box — often a counsel question). Then: the record is a **business record** (custodian or certification — Card 6/Card 9); and any **statement inside it** offered for its truth needs its own door — a **patient's** treatment statement can qualify under MRE 803(4), but **who said what to whom** matters, and a statement merely **identifying** a wrongdoer is generally **not** covered because identity is not the "general cause" of an injury (Module 6 §6.5). A record written **for your case** rather than kept in the ordinary course of treatment is weaker under MRE 803(6)(E).

> ### The child's statements do not reach the court through you
> A child's statement in a medical or therapy record reaches the court through the **provider and the record**, screened for trustworthiness — never through you quoting the child, and never by coaching the child (Module 6 §6.5–6.7; GUARDRAILS §8). The "tender-years" hearsay exception, MRE 803A, **does not apply in a custody case** — it is limited by its own terms to "criminal and delinquency proceedings only" (Module 6 §6.7).

### The script — certificate path (no live witness)
> "Exhibit ___ is [the patient]'s [treatment record] from [provider], produced with a custodian's certification under MRE 902(11) and MRE 803(6), with written notice given to [other party] on [date]. I offer Exhibit ___." — *and, for a treatment statement inside it:* "The patient's statement of symptoms on page ___ is offered under MRE 803(4) as made for medical treatment."

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — privilege"** (the threshold objection on this card). One-line answer: *there isn't a safe one-liner — privilege is decided by the court and often needs counsel.* If privilege is not in play, the likely objection is **"hearsay within the record"**: *"The record itself is a business record under MRE 803(6); the patient's treatment statement is admissible under MRE 803(4), and I am not offering any other statement inside it for its truth."*

## Card 8 — Police, CPS, and other public records ⚠ access/confidentiality caveat

### The rule
> **(8) Public Record.** A record or statement of a public office if it sets out:
> (A) the office's activities; or
> (B) a matter observed while under a legal duty to report, but not including:
> (i) in a criminal case, a matter observed by law-enforcement personnel; and
> (ii) information to which the limitations in MCL 257.624 apply.
> — MRE 803(8)

A certified copy of a public record self-authenticates:

> **(4) Certified Copy of Public Record.** A copy of an official record—or a copy of a document that was recorded or filed in a public office as authorized by law—if the copy is certified as correct by: (A) the custodian or another person authorized to make the certification; or (B) a certificate that complies with subrules (1), (2), or (3) or a Michigan or federal statute.
> — MRE 902(4)

And the public-records authentication illustration:

> **(7) Evidence About Public Records.** Evidence that: (A) a document was recorded or filed in a public office as authorized by law; or (B) a purported public record or statement is from the office where items of this kind are kept.
> — MRE 901(b)(7)

### The predicate facts
A **certified copy** self-authenticates under MRE 902(4). Then two problems recur: **(1)** the law-enforcement caveat in MRE 803(8)(B)(i) is written for **criminal cases** — a custody case is civil, so it is not itself the bar, but a report generated in an adversarial setting can still fail the **trustworthiness** screen; and **(2) hearsay within hearsay** — a police report is full of *other people's statements*, and each quoted statement needs **its own** door (Card 12). Clearing MRE 803(8) for the report does nothing for the sentences quoted inside it.

> ### 🛑 CPS records are not an ordinary public record you can pull and file
> Child Protective Services records sit under a **separate confidentiality regime** that this book does not resolve, and CPS/police contact is a **hard stop** that routes out to counsel and the professionals equipped for it (GUARDRAILS §9; Module 17). Do **not** treat a CPS file as a routine MRE 803(8) record. `⚠ VERIFY — the statutory access limits on CPS records are not quoted from a primary source here; confirm with counsel before attempting to obtain or file any CPS material.` (Separately, note a related access limit already verified in this project: *"Friend of the court records are not subject to a subpoena issued under these Michigan Court Rules"* — **MCR 3.218(A)**; the FOC file is not a document you subpoena into evidence.)

### The script — certified public record (no live witness)
> "Exhibit ___ is a certified copy of [the record — e.g., a certified court order / a certified copy of police report no. ___] from [office], certified as correct by its custodian. It is self-authenticating under MRE 902(4), and it is a public record under MRE 803(8). I offer Exhibit ___."

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — hearsay within the report"** (statements by witnesses quoted in it). One-line answer: *"The report is a public record under MRE 803(8); I am offering it for [the officer's own recorded observation / the fact of the report], and for any quoted statement I will show a separate hearsay basis."* If the record is a **CPS file** — **stop**; that is Module 17 and counsel, not this card.

## Card 9 — The business-record certification (the route that avoids a live witness)

### The rule
This is the payoff route for every record a **business or public office keeps**. The hearsay exception:

> **(6) Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** [full text quoted in Card 6] … (D) all these conditions are shown by the testimony of the custodian or another qualified witness, or by a certification that complies with a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court or with a statute permitting certification …
> — MRE 803(6)

The self-authentication that pairs with it — **note the mandatory advance-notice string**:

> **(11) Certified Domestic or Foreign Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** The original or a copy of a domestic or foreign record that meets the requirements of Rule 803(6)(A)-(C), as shown by a certification of the custodian or another qualified person that complies with a Michigan statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court. Before the trial or hearing, the proponent must give an adverse party reasonable written notice of the intent to offer the record—and must make the record and certification available for inspection—so that the party has a fair opportunity to challenge them.
> — MRE 902(11)

For public records, the parallel certificate is **MRE 902(4)** (Card 8).

### The predicate facts
A **certification from the custodian or another qualified person** that tracks MRE 803(6)(A)–(C); the certification must **comply with a Michigan statute or a Supreme Court rule**; and — the trap that loses the shortcut — you must give the other side **reasonable written notice** of intent to offer, and make the record and certificate **available for inspection**, *before* the hearing. Done right, the certificate does **both** jobs at once: it authenticates (Door 1) **and** carries the record through the business-records hearsay exception (Door 2), with **no live witness**.

> ### ⚠ VERIFY — which Michigan statute or rule "permits certification"
> Both MRE 803(6)(D) and MRE 902(11) require a certification that "complies with a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court or with a statute permitting certification." The MRE text does **not** name that enabling statute or rule, and it is not quoted from a primary source in this project. **Confirm the specific Michigan certification statute/court rule before you rely on a custodian certificate** (Module 7 §7.3). Until then, the safe fallback is to bring the custodian live under MRE 803(6) (Card 6 script), or to subpoena the record. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — whether a given co-parenting app or vendor issues a court-admissible certified export, and the exact form of its certificate, is a vendor fact that changes; confirm with the vendor and against the enabling rule.`

### The script — offering a certified record (what you say to the court)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is [the record], produced by [business/office] with a written certification from its custodian under MRE 902(11) [or MRE 902(4) for a public record]."
> 2. "On [date] I served [other party] written notice of my intent to offer this record, and I made the record and the certification available for inspection."
> 3. "The certification establishes that it is a record of a regularly conducted activity under MRE 803(6). No custodian needs to appear."
> 4. "I offer Exhibit ___."

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — improper certification / no notice."** One-line answer: *"The certification tracks MRE 803(6)(A)–(C) as MRE 902(11) requires, and I gave written notice and made the record available on [date]."* If you did **not** give notice, you may have lost the shortcut — fall back to a **live custodian** (Card 6) or ask the court's leave.

## Card 10 — Your journal and your calendar (the scaffolding, not the exhibit)

> ### ⊗ COUNCIL CONFLICT — carried, not resolved (see Module 8)
> Two of this project's reviewers sharply demoted the custody journal; the convergence they reached is the rule this card follows: **keep the journal, treat it as scaffolding for your testimony — not as the evidence itself — and never file it or hand it over as a whole** (Module 8, ⊗ box). Your journal, offered by **you** to prove its contents are **true**, is largely **inadmissible self-serving hearsay**. Its real, narrower value is below.

### The rule
Your own contemporaneous record has two legitimate uses at a hearing — and note the built-in limits in each rule:

> **(5) Recorded Recollection.** A record that: (A) is on a matter the witness once knew about but now cannot recall well enough to testify fully and accurately; (B) was made or adopted by the witness when the matter was fresh in the witness's memory; and (C) accurately reflects the witness's knowledge. **If admitted, the record may be read into evidence but may be received as an exhibit only if offered by an adverse party.**
> — MRE 803(5)

> An adverse party is entitled to have the writing or object produced, to inspect it, to cross-examine the witness about it, and to introduce in evidence—for its bearing on credibility only unless otherwise admissible under these rules—any portion that relates to the witness's testimony.
> — MRE 612(b) (in part)

### The predicate facts
The journal/calendar is **not your exhibit to hand up.** Its uses are: **(1) refreshing your recollection** so *you* testify accurately (MRE 612) — but the instant you use it to refresh memory on the stand, **the other side is entitled to see that page and cross-examine you on everything in it that touches your testimony** (MRE 612(b)); and **(2) recorded recollection** (MRE 803(5)) — in the narrow case where you once knew a fact but genuinely cannot now recall it, the note may be **read into evidence**, but **only the adverse party** can move it in as an exhibit. Either way, the predicate is a **contemporaneous, factual, unaltered** record (Module 8 §8.2). *(A calendar a business keeps — e.g., a daycare's sign-in log — is a different thing: that is a business record under Card 6/Card 9, not your personal calendar.)*

### The script — refreshing your recollection (what you say)
> 1. "I don't recall the exact [date/time] from memory." → 2. "May I look at my notes from that day to refresh my recollection?" → 3. [read silently] → 4. "That refreshes my memory." → 5. Then **testify from memory** — the paper goes back down; it is not read aloud and not offered.

### The script — recorded recollection (the narrow fallback)
> "Even after looking, I cannot fully recall the details. I made this note on [date], the same day, while it was fresh, and it accurately reflected what I knew then. Under MRE 803(5) I ask to **read the relevant line into the record.**" (You read it; you do **not** offer the journal as your exhibit.)

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — self-serving hearsay"** (aimed at the journal as an exhibit). One-line answer: *"I am not offering the journal as an exhibit; I am using it to refresh my recollection under MRE 612 and testifying from memory"* — or, in the narrow case, *"I offer only to read the line into the record as recorded recollection under MRE 803(5)."* Remember the cost the same page carries under MRE 612(b): the other side may now read it and cross-examine you on it — which is exactly why every entry must survive being read aloud (Module 8).

## Card 11 — An opposing party's statement (MRE 801(d)(2)) — the rule that does the most work

### The rule
> (d) Statements That Are Not Hearsay. A statement that meets the following conditions is not hearsay:
> **(2) An Opposing Party's Statement.** The statement is offered against an opposing party and:
> (A) was made by the party in an individual or representative capacity, except a statement made in connection with: (i) a guilty plea to a misdemeanor motor-vehicle violation; or (ii) an admission of responsibility for a civil infraction under a motor-vehicle law;
> (B) is one the party manifested that it adopted or believed to be true;
> (C) was made by a person whom the party authorized to make a statement on the subject;
> (D) was made by the party's agent or employee on a matter within the scope of that relationship and while it existed; or
> (E) was made by the party's coconspirator during and in furtherance of the conspiracy, if there is independent proof of the conspiracy.
> — MRE 801(d)(2)

> Hearsay is not admissible unless these rules provide otherwise.
> — MRE 802

### The predicate facts
This is a **hearsay (Door 2)** rule, so it presumes you have already **authenticated** the medium (the text, post, voicemail, or email — Cards 1–5) at Door 1. Then, under subrule **(A)**: the statement was **made by the other party**, and you are offering it **against** them. That is the whole test — there is **no** requirement that the statement be dishonest, only that the speaker be the opposing party. Subrules (B)–(E) reach adopted statements and statements by an authorized agent, and are narrower — lean on counsel if you rely on them.

> ### ⚠ The same door swings both ways
> MRE 801(d)(2) has no good-faith filter. **Your own** texts, emails, and posts are equally not-hearsay when the **other side** offers them **against you.** Assume every message you ever sent is a potential exhibit in their hands, and write accordingly, going forward (Module 8 §8.4). Present this rule as neutral courtroom mechanics — because it is.

### The script — offering the other parent's own statement (you, sworn)
> 1. [First authenticate the item with the matching card — e.g., Card 1 for a text.]
> 2. "The statements in Exhibit ___ were made by [other parent], and I offer them against [other parent]."
> 3. "They are an opposing party's statement under MRE 801(d)(2)(A), and so are not hearsay."
> 4. "I offer Exhibit ___."

### The script — drawing the admission out of the party directly (actual questions, if they testify)
> **Q:** "You sent me a text on March 6 that said, 'I'm keeping him this weekend, court order or not' — correct?" · **Q:** "That is your phone number shown at the top?" · (Their answer authenticates it and is itself a party admission.)

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — hearsay."** One-line answer: *"It is the opposing party's own statement offered against them — not hearsay under MRE 801(d)(2)(A)."* If the words are a **third party's** (the other parent's sister, a friend's comment), 801(d)(2) does **not** fit — that is Card 12.

## Card 12 — Hearsay within hearsay (the records trap)

### The rule
> Hearsay within hearsay is not excluded by the rule against hearsay if each part of the combined statements conforms with an exception to the rule.
> — MRE 805

> "Hearsay" means a statement that: (1) the declarant does not make while testifying at the current trial or hearing; and (2) a party offers in evidence to prove the truth of the matter asserted in the statement.
> — MRE 801(c)

### The predicate facts
Admitting a **record** does **not** admit every **statement inside it.** Each layer needs its own door. Picture a therapy note: the **outer layer** is a business record (MRE 803(6) — Card 6/9); an **inner layer** quoting the other parent is an opposing-party statement (MRE 801(d)(2) — a door, Card 11); another **inner layer** quoting the **child** usually has **no door** in a custody case (MRE 803A does not apply — Module 6 §6.7). The note can be admitted while the child's quoted words stay out. Before you rely on any record, **name the door for each layer** you need — and offer the **narrowest** record that carries the fact, because a thick record hands the other side every harmful line in it, including your own words (not hearsay against you).

### The script — laying the layers (you, offering a record)
> 1. "Exhibit ___ is a business record under MRE 803(6)" [authenticate — Card 9 or Card 6].
> 2. "Within it, the statement by [other parent] on page ___ is an opposing party's statement under MRE 801(d)(2), offered against them."
> 3. "I am **not** offering [the child's / a third party's] quoted statement on page ___ for its truth" — or, if you must, name its own exception.
> 4. "I offer Exhibit ___, limited as stated."

### The objection most likely to be made
**"Objection — hearsay within hearsay."** One-line answer: *"Each layer has its own basis: the record is MRE 803(6), and [the party's] statement inside it is MRE 801(d)(2); I am not offering the remaining statements for their truth."* Turned around, this is also **your** objection when the other side offers a thick record to sneak in a quoted statement (Card 13).

## Card 13 — The six objections you will make

> ### Neutral mechanics, and a word on the referee
> These are the same tools the other side has (GUARDRAILS §7). Object cleanly, state the ground, and stop — over-objecting reads as obstruction and, with a referee who moves quickly, can cost you goodwill you need. An objection is available only when the ground is actually true; if it is not, you are handing the court a reason to distrust the next one.

### The rule (each ground, pin-cited)
1. **Hearsay** — *"Hearsay is not admissible unless these rules provide otherwise"* (MRE 802).
2. **Relevance** — *"Irrelevant evidence is not admissible"* (MRE 402); the test is MRE 401.
3. **Lack of foundation / not authenticated** — the proponent must "produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what its proponent claims it is" (MRE 901(a)).
4. **MRE 403** — *"The court may exclude relevant evidence if its probative value is substantially outweighed by a danger of one or more of the following: unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, misleading the jury, undue delay, wasting time, or needlessly presenting cumulative evidence"* (MRE 403). (There is no jury in a custody case; the danger the rule guards against is confusing or misleading **the fact-finder** — the referee or judge.)
5. **Lack of personal knowledge / speculation** — a witness may testify "only if evidence is introduced sufficient to support a finding that the witness has personal knowledge of the matter" (MRE 602).
6. **Improper lay opinion (diagnosis / character label)** — lay opinion is limited to one "rationally based on the witness's perception" and helpful to the fact-finder (MRE 701); a diagnosis or a character label ("narcissist," "alienator") is neither.

### The predicate facts (when each is actually available)
- **Hearsay** is available **only** if the statement is an out-of-court statement offered **for its truth** — and it is **not** available if the statement is the **opposing party's own** (Card 11) or fits an 803 exception.
- **Relevance** is available when the item maps to **no** best-interest or parenting-time factor and no fact of consequence (Module 2).
- **Foundation** is available before the proponent has shown who/what/when — it forces them to authenticate.
- **403** is available against a pile of cumulative exhibits or an inflammatory item of thin value — "undue delay, wasting time, needlessly … cumulative" are the operative words for a family hearing.
- **Personal knowledge** is available when the witness testifies to what they assumed or heard, not what they perceived.
- **Improper opinion** is available against conclusions and labels; the cure the court will invite is "describe what you saw."

### The script (what you say — keep it to the ground)
> "Objection — hearsay." · "Objection — relevance." · "Objection — lack of foundation." · "Objection — MRE 403; cumulative [or: unfair prejudice]." · "Objection — the witness lacks personal knowledge; speculation." · "Objection — improper opinion; that calls for a conclusion / a diagnosis."
> *If the court asks for your ground, give one sentence:* "It is an out-of-court statement offered for its truth, and it is not [the party's own statement / within an exception]."

### The answer you will hear back
Expect the proponent to try to cure: **"it's the opposing party's statement"** (then hearsay fails — Card 11); **"it's not offered for its truth"** (then it may come in for a limited purpose — Module 6 §6.3); **"I'll lay more foundation"** (then they authenticate and you renew if they can't). If the court **overrules** you, that ruling is now something to **preserve** (Card 16); if it **sustains** you, the item stays out unless they cure it.

## Card 14 — The six objections you will hear, and how to respond

> ### The response is a cure, not an argument
> Most objections to a pro se litigant's evidence are curable by **supplying the missing predicate fact** — not by arguing. If you cannot cure it, do not fight; **preserve it** with an offer of proof (Card 15) and move on. And remember these are neutral: the same objections you meet here, you make in Card 13.

### The rule
The grounds are the same six (Card 13) plus the **best-evidence** point, viewed from the receiving end: MRE 802 (hearsay), 401/402 (relevance), 901(a) (foundation), 403, and MRE 1001(d)/1003 (originals and duplicates).

### The predicate facts (what you must already be able to establish to cure)
For each objection, the cure is a fact you should have from the matching foundation card. If you do not have it, the objection is not curable today — preserve it.

### The script — the six responses
> 1. **"Objection — hearsay."** → *"It is the opposing party's own statement, offered against them — not hearsay under MRE 801(d)(2)."* (Or: *"It is not offered for its truth, only to show [effect on the listener / that it was said]."* Or name the 803 exception.)
> 2. **"Objection — relevance."** → *"It is relevant to best-interest factor ___ [or parenting-time factor ___] under MRE 401 — it makes [that fact] more probable."*
> 3. **"Objection — no foundation / not authenticated."** → *"I was a participant / I took this / I recognize the voice"* — then **lay the missing line** from the matching card (MRE 901(b)(1), (4), (5)).
> 4. **"Objection — MRE 403 / cumulative."** → *"I offer this one item, not the stack; it is probative of a contested factor and I will not repeat it."* (Then **withdraw** the duplicates — offering less is more credible.)
> 5. **"Objection — that's a copy / best evidence."** → *"A printout of electronic information is an 'original' under MRE 1001(d), and a duplicate is admissible under MRE 1003; I have preserved the native file if authenticity is genuinely disputed."*
> 6. **"Objection — self-serving hearsay"** (your journal). → *"I am not offering it as an exhibit; I am using it to refresh my recollection under MRE 612 and testifying from memory."* (Card 10.)

### The objection you cannot cure — what then
If the ground is real and you have no fact to supply — the screenshot no one saw, the third party's statement with no door, the recording whose lawfulness is unsettled — **do not argue.** Make an **offer of proof** so the exclusion is preserved for review (Card 15), and go to your next exhibit. A recording whose lawfulness is in question is not an objection to win at the podium; it is a **stop** (Card 4; Module 9).

## Card 15 — Making an offer of proof

### The rule
> (a) Preserving a Claim of Error. A party may claim error in a ruling to admit or exclude evidence only if the error affects a substantial right of the party and: … (2) if the ruling **excludes** evidence, a party informs the court of its substance by an **offer of proof**, unless the substance was apparent from the context.
> — MRE 103(a)(2)

> The court may make any statement about the character or form of the evidence, the objection made, and the ruling. The court may direct that an offer of proof be made in question-and-answer form.
> — MRE 103(c)

> Once the court rules definitively on the record—either before or at trial—a party need not renew an objection or offer of proof to preserve a claim of error for appeal.
> — MRE 103(b)

### The predicate facts
An offer of proof is what you do the **moment your evidence is kept out.** Its whole purpose is to put **on the record** what the excluded evidence *would have shown*, so a reviewing court can see what was lost — without it, an exclusion is very hard to challenge later. You need: **what** the evidence is, **what it would show**, and **why it matters** (which factor / fact of consequence). The court may direct you to make it in **question-and-answer form** (MRE 103(c)); be ready either way.

### The script (what you say to the court, right after the ruling)
> "Your Honor, I'd like to make a brief offer of proof for the record. If Exhibit ___ [or the testimony of ___] were admitted, it would show [the substance — e.g., 'that the other parent canceled three consecutive exchanges by text']. It is offered as relevant to best-interest factor ___ [or parenting-time factor ___], and it would be admissible as [the basis — e.g., 'an opposing party's statement under MRE 801(d)(2)']. I ask that the offer be noted."

*If the court directs Q&A form (MRE 103(c)):* answer its questions, or state — "If asked, I would testify that ___" — putting the substance on the record in that form.

### The objection most likely to be made
The court may say the substance is **already apparent** (then MRE 103(a)(2)'s exception applies and you have lost nothing), or that the offer is **cumulative**. One-line answer: *"I offer it only to preserve the record; it will be brief."* An offer of proof is rarely refused outright, because refusing it is itself reviewable.

## Card 16 — Preserving a ruling

### The rule
> (a) Preserving a Claim of Error. A party may claim error in a ruling to admit or exclude evidence only if the error affects a substantial right of the party and:
> (1) if the ruling **admits** evidence, a party, on the record: (A) timely objects or moves to strike; and (B) states the specific ground, unless it was apparent from the context; or
> (2) if the ruling **excludes** evidence, a party informs the court of its substance by an offer of proof, unless the substance was apparent from the context.
> — MRE 103(a)

> Once the court rules definitively on the record—either before or at trial—a party need not renew an objection or offer of proof to preserve a claim of error for appeal.
> — MRE 103(b)

> A court may take notice of a plain error affecting a substantial right, even if the claim of error was not properly preserved.
> — MRE 103(e)

### The predicate facts
A wrong ruling only counts later if it was preserved **on the record**, and there are exactly two moves, mirroring the two ways a ruling can hurt you:
- **Something bad came in** (over your objection): you must have made a **timely objection stating the specific ground** (MRE 103(a)(1)). "I object," with no ground, preserves little — name it: "hearsay," "relevance," "no foundation."
- **Something of yours was kept out:** you must have made an **offer of proof** (MRE 103(a)(2); Card 15).

Once the court rules **definitively**, you need **not** keep re-objecting to the same thing to preserve it (MRE 103(b)). MRE 103(e) is a backstop only — do not rely on "plain error"; preserve properly the first time.

### The script (what you say — both moves)
> **When bad evidence comes in:** "Objection — [specific ground]." … [if overruled] "Understood; my objection is noted for the record." (You need not renew it — MRE 103(b).)
> **When your evidence is kept out:** "May I make a short offer of proof?" → deliver Card 15.
> **To pin the ruling down:** "So the record is clear — the court is [admitting Exhibit ___ over my hearsay objection / excluding Exhibit ___]. Is that the ruling?" (A definitive, on-the-record ruling is what MRE 103(b) preserves.)

### The objection most likely to be made
With a **referee who moves fast**, the practical danger is not an opponent's objection — it is the **moment passing** before you speak. The ground must be stated **when the evidence is offered**, and the offer of proof made **when the exclusion is ruled**; there is no going back after the witness steps down. *(What you then **do** with a preserved error — an objection to a referee's recommendation, or an appeal, and their deadlines — is Module 16 and Appendix A, not this card. Do not confuse preserving the record with the separate clock for acting on it.)*
