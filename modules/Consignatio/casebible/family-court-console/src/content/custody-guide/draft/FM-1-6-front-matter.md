# FM.1 — What this book is, and what it cannot be

You are holding **legal information. It is not legal advice, and it never becomes legal advice** — not on the hardest page, not when you are out of time, not when you wish it would. Reading it creates no lawyer-client relationship with anyone. It cannot tell you what to do in your case, because what happens in your case turns on your facts, your evidence, and your judge — and a book cannot see any of the three.

Michigan statutes, court rules, local administrative orders, forms, and fees change. Some of what is true today will not be true when you read it. Before you rely on anything here, confirm it at the source:

- **Statutes (MCL):** [legislature.mi.gov](https://www.legislature.mi.gov)
- **Court rules, forms, and evidence rules:** [courts.michigan.gov](https://www.courts.michigan.gov)
- **Genesee County / 7th Circuit local practice:** the [7th Circuit Court](https://7thcircuitcourt.com/) and the Genesee County Clerk and Friend of the Court — see **FM.4**.

## What a lawyer does that this book cannot

A book can hand you the rules. It cannot do the four things a lawyer actually sells:

1. **Read your specific judge.** Local practice varies by the judge you drew. This book flags where that matters; it cannot tell you how your judge, this month, treats your issue.
2. **Make the judgment calls.** Whether to accept a settlement, whether to object, when to push and when to wait — these are strategic decisions that depend on facts a book never sees. This book gives you the tradeoffs and the questions to ask. It does not choose for you.
3. **See the issue you don't know is there.** The most expensive mistakes are the ones you never spotted — a jurisdiction problem, a deadline hidden in a rule you didn't read, an admission you didn't know you were making. This book points at the common traps. It cannot catch the one peculiar to your file.
4. **Speak for you.** Advocacy, cross-examination, reading the room — these are skills, and this book can only rehearse you, not stand in for you.

## When continuing on your own is a mistake

Doing this without a lawyer is a real choice, and for many people a forced one. This book is built to make that choice survivable. But some situations are **not** survivable from a book alone, and pretending otherwise would be the cruelest thing this book could do. Stop and get a person on your side — starting with the free and low-cost options in **FM.4** and **Appendix K**, not with a retainer you cannot pay — if any of these is in your case:

- **CPS or the police have contacted you, or you have been accused of abuse.** Anything you write in your custody case can be used against you in a criminal one. 🛑
- **There is a PPO and you are the respondent**, or a no-contact/bond condition touches your contact with the other parent. 🛑
- **Another state is involved**, or the child has been taken out of Michigan (a jurisdiction / UCCJEA question). 🛑
- **There is any Native American / tribal heritage** in the family (ICWA / MIFPA changes the rules). 🛑
- **You are trying to catch an appeal deadline.** It is jurisdictional — no judge can give the time back. 🛑
- **Any question about recording a call or conversation.** Michigan law here is unsettled and carries criminal exposure. This book will not tell you a recording is legal.

These are not formalities. Each marks a place where getting it wrong is hard or impossible to undo. **Appendix K** collects them with where to go.

*Every module in this book repeats the disclaimer that fits its own risk — the safety page differs from the trial-brief page differs from the recording page. That is on purpose. A single boilerplate paragraph at the front is not enough, because the risk is not the same everywhere.*

# FM.2 — Safety comes first 🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE

**If you or a child are in danger right now, stop reading and call 911.**

- **National Domestic Violence Hotline: 1-800-799-7233**, 24 hours a day, free and confidential (call, or text "START" to 88788).
- **Crisis support (U.S.): call or text 988**, 24 hours.

Nothing in this book comes before your safety or your child's. Every procedural step here can wait — a hearing can be rescheduled, a filing can be re-filed. A safety decision often cannot be undone. When safety and procedure point in different directions, **safety wins**, every time, without exception.

**Filing is not a safe, neutral act.** Handing a custody motion or a PPO petition across the counter can itself be the thing that escalates a dangerous person. If that is your situation, do your safety planning **before** anything goes to the court — see **FM.5** — and get a domestic-violence advocate involved before you need one, not after.

**Free help in Genesee County** *(confirm each address, room, and hours before you rely on it — `ℹ PROVISIONAL`)*:

- **YWCA Greater Flint** — PPO-filing assistance, on-site at the courthouse, Room 203.
- **Genesee County Legal Resource Center** — the self-help center at 900 S. Saginaw St. ([Michigan Legal Help](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/self-help-centers/genesee-county-legal-resource-center)).
- **Michigan's statewide DV resources** and how to find a local shelter and advocate: [michigan.gov](https://www.michigan.gov) and the National Hotline above.

This safety notice repeats at the head of every part of this book that touches domestic violence, PPOs, CPS, or exchanges. If you skipped straight here from the router, you are in the right place.

# FM.3 — Edition, verification, and what the flags mean

**Edition verified as of August 2026.** Every statement of statewide Michigan law in this book was checked against the primary source — the actual statute, court rule, or evidence rule — as of that date, and the archived copies live in `sources/primary/`. That does not freeze the law. **Confirm anything you are about to file or rely on**, especially anything carrying a flag.

**Two clocks, not one.** The statewide legal content (statutes, court rules, published cases) changes slowly — over years. The **Genesee County local facts** — fees, judge and referee assignments, FOC procedure, form revisions, filing-method status — change in **months**. Treat any local fact older than about six months as stale and re-check it. The change log for this edition is Appendix M.

**A note on the evidence rules.** Michigan restyled its Rules of Evidence effective January 1, 2024. Older guides, forms, and even some court materials still quote the pre-2024 wording and numbering. Where this book quotes an evidence rule, it uses the current text as archived; if you find a different version elsewhere, check the date.

# FM.4 — Genesee County quick-reference card ℹ LOCAL PRACTICE

*Everything on this card is a local fact with a short shelf life. It is sourced, but confirm the specific item before you rely on it. `ℹ PROVISIONAL`.*

**The court**
- **7th Judicial Circuit Court, Family Division** — 900 S. Saginaw St., Flint, MI 48502 · (810) 424-4355 · [7thcircuitcourt.com](https://7thcircuitcourt.com/)
- **Genesee County Friend of the Court (FOC)** — 630 S. Saginaw St., Ste. 2500, Flint, MI 48502 · (810) 257-3300 · toll-free (877) 543-2660 · [FOC on Michigan Legal Help](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/courts-and-agencies/genesee-county-friend-of-court) · [FOC Handbook 2022](https://cms7files.revize.com/genessecountymi/New%20Handbook%202022.pdf)

**Where you file — this trips people up**
- File at **Circuit Court Records, 2nd Floor** — **not** at the FOC office. The FOC does not accept your filings ([Genesee custody motion packet](https://cms7files.revize.com/genessecountymi/Document_Center/Courts%20and%20Law%20Enforcement/Friend%20of%20the%20Court/Online%20Forms/Pro%20Per/fillable%20Custody%20motion.pdf)).
- Genesee is **not** a MiFILE (e-filing) court **(✅ VERIFIED 2026-09-07, owner confirmation — no longer PROVISIONAL)**. **Paper filing is the default.** Administrative Order **AO 2026-3** authorizes a 7th Circuit pilot for electronic **service** — that is service, not filing ([AO 2026-3](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/rules-instructions-administrative-orders/administrative-orders/aos-responsive-html5.zip/AOs/Administrative_Orders/AO_No._2026-3_%E2%80%94_Establishing_Pilot_Project_for_Implementing_Expanded_Electronic_Service.htm)). The pilot's live/enforced status is still `ℹ PROVISIONAL`.
- **Motion day is Monday.** Contested motions are usually in person; uncontested or stipulated matters are often by Zoom. Confirm your assigned judge's practice.

**The two deadlines that are not in the same place** ⚠

Getting a motion *heard* in Genesee County takes four things, and the rules scatter them:

1. **Serve it in time.** The motion, notice of hearing, and any brief or affidavits must be **served at least 9 days before the hearing if you mail them, or 7 days if you deliver them** — MCR 2.119(C)(1). *This is statewide law, not a stricter Genesee rule.*
2. **File it in time — a separate deadline.** The motion must be **filed at least 7 days before the hearing** — MCR 2.119(C)(4).
3. **Attach a proposed order** — Genesee **LCR 2.119(B)** requires a proposed order attached to *and served with* the motion.
4. **Attach a concurrence certificate** — Genesee **LCR 2.119(A)** requires it of "the attorney of record **or the party in propria persona**" — meaning it binds you even without a lawyer. It states that you personally contacted the other side to ask them to agree and they declined, or that you made reasonable and diligent attempts.

> ⚠ **The trap on the county's own website.** Genesee posts a 2019 "Notice Regarding Motion Practice" that tells you to file **7 days** before the hearing. That is a true statement of the *filing* rule (C)(4) — and it leaves out the *service* rule (C)(1). If you follow the county notice and **mail** your motion 7 days out, **you are two days late on service.** Serve early, file early. ([Local Court Rules — Circuit, eff. 6/1/2025](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/492c71/siteassets/rules-instructions-administrative-orders/local-court-rules/local-court-rules-circuit-court.pdf))

*Response deadlines run the other direction: a response is served ≥5 days (mail) / ≥3 days (delivery) and filed ≥3 days before the hearing — MCR 2.119(C)(2), (C)(4).*

**A praecipe** (a card that puts your motion on the hearing calendar) is filed **≥7 days ahead** — *`ℹ PROVISIONAL`: this is Genesee practice, not written into LCR 2.119; confirm the current card, color, and timing with the clerk.*

**Fees** *(all `ℹ PROVISIONAL` — confirm at the [Genesee County Clerk Legal Division fees page](https://www.geneseecountymi.gov/departments/county_clerk/legal_division/fees.php))*
- General motion: **$20** · Custody / parenting-time motion: **$100** total · Support-only motion: **$60**
- **Can't afford the fee?** Ask the court to waive it with form **MC 20** (fee-waiver), if you receive means-tested benefits (Medicaid, SSI, SNAP) or meet the income test ([MC 20](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/forms/scao-approved/mc20.pdf); [DIY Fee Waiver](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/resources/going-court/do-it-yourself-fee-waiver)). A fee waiver is **not** the same as asking the other side to pay your attorney fees — that is a different request (Module 24).

**Free and low-cost help** *(confirm hours — `ℹ PROVISIONAL`)*
- **Genesee County Legal Resource Center** — 900 S. Saginaw St., self-help center ([Michigan Legal Help](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/self-help-centers/genesee-county-legal-resource-center))
- **Legal Services of Eastern Michigan** — civil legal aid ([lsem-mi.org](https://lsem-mi.org/))
- **State Bar of Michigan Lawyer Referral** — ask specifically about **limited-scope** help (a lawyer for one hearing or one document, not your whole case) ([lrs.michbar.org](https://lrs.michbar.org/); Form [MC 516](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4aa4e1/siteassets/forms/scao-approved/mc516.pdf))
- **Michigan Legal Help** — free statewide forms and step-by-step tools ([michiganlegalhelp.org](https://michiganlegalhelp.org))

**Remember:** clerks, referees, and self-help navigators can tell you *how* the process works, but they **cannot give you legal advice** — they cannot tell you what to do. That is not them being unhelpful; it is the line the law draws.

# FM.5 — Safety planning, before you file anything

**Do this before the first document goes to the counter — not after.** For most readers this is ordinary preparation. If there is any history of violence, threats, stalking, or control in your relationship, it is essential: the act of filing can itself be an escalation trigger, and the earlier version of this material treated safety as an afterthought. It comes first here on purpose.

**What you are trying to do:** recognize filing as an event the other person will react to; secure your home, phone, accounts, workplace, and the child's school and daycare; set up a way to receive court mail that does not expose where you live; and have an advocate's number in your pocket before you need it.

## Pre-filing safety checklist

Work through every line. Mark each one **done** or **not applicable** with a one-line reason.

- ☐ **Locks and entry** — changed or re-keyed where you can; windows and garage secured.
- ☐ **Phone and devices** — new passcodes; check for location sharing and tracking (Find My, Life360, shared Google/Apple accounts); turn off what you did not knowingly turn on.
- ☐ **Accounts and passwords** — change passwords on email, banking, and anything the other person may know or guess; set up two-factor authentication; use a device the other person has never had access to.
- ☐ **School and daycare** — tell them in writing who is authorized to pick up the child; ask what they require to update the list; give them a copy of any order that controls pickup.
- ☐ **Workplace** — tell a trusted person at work; describe the vehicle and person to watch for; ask about screening calls and visitors.
- ☐ **A go-bag** — copies of IDs, the children's documents, medications, some cash, and a spare phone or charger, kept somewhere you can reach quickly.
- ☐ **Safe contacts** — two people who know your situation and can be called.
- ☐ **A confidential address** — a way to receive court mail that is not your home. Ask the Legal Resource Center about Michigan's **Address Confidentiality Program** and about keeping your address out of court filings.
- ☐ **A safe way to be served / to serve** — plan how papers reach the other side, and reach you, without a face-to-face confrontation. Ask the clerk about service by an officer or a process server.
- ☐ **An advocate** — the name and number of a domestic-violence advocate (start with the YWCA Greater Flint, FM.2), entered before you need them.

**You are done with this checklist when every line is either completed or marked "not applicable" with a reason.**

**Stop and get help today — do not file first —** if there is any *current* threat, any weapon involved, or any stalking behavior. 🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE

# FM.6 — How to use this book

**Do not read this book front to back.** It is built to be entered at the point that matches your situation and followed along a path, not read like a novel. If you have not already, start at **FM.0 — "What Happened Today?"** It routes you to the module you need right now.

**Reading order is by pathway, not page number.** Each module carries a *route tag* (who it is for) and a *stage* label that tells you where you are in the arc of a case:

- **Start** — figure out which road you are on and what standard governs it (FM, Modules 1–4).
- **Stabilize** — deal with what is already moving: an ex parte order, a temporary order, a hearing this week.
- **Build Proof** — the evidence spine (Modules 5–10). In this system, proof comes *before* tactics: what you can prove decides what you should ask for.
- **Ask / Respond / Resolve** — motions, the Friend of the Court, the referee hearing, negotiation (Modules 11–20).
- **Try** — prepare and present the adjudicated case (Modules 21–23).
- **After the Order** — support, enforcement, make-up time, modification, and moving on (Modules 24–29).

**How the learning is built.** Each module is meant to leave you holding a real work-product — a filled-in template, a completed log entry, a drafted objection — not just having read. The exercises come in a fixed set of types, so you learn the shape once and reuse it:

| Type | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| **Fill-in template** | An actual document to complete, with bracketed prompts |
| **Worked example** | The same document, filled in with realistic (fictional) facts, to copy the shape from |
| **Checklist** | A sequence so nothing is missed |
| **Scenario exercise** | Practice applying a legal standard to facts |
| **Self-assessment** | Questions that tell you which path is yours |
| **Evidence log / journal** | A documentation habit you start on day one |
| **Practice script** | Rehearsal for speaking in the room |
| **Red-flag spotting** | Training to see a tactic or an evidentiary opening |

**Things are taught once and referred to after.** A template introduced in one module (the custody journal, the Best-Interest Evidence Matrix, the deadline tracker) is not re-taught later — it is referenced by section number. **Appendix O** maps which module owns which concept, so you can always find the full explanation.

**Every legal term is defined the first time it appears in a module**, and all of them are collected in the glossary (**Appendix N**). You do not need to already know the words.
