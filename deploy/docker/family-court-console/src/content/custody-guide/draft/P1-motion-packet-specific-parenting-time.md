## Route tag

**Postjudgment.** An order already exists in a Genesee County case. You are asking the court to make an existing parenting-time provision specific, and to set the logistics that make it work. This is not an appeal, not a reconsideration, and not a request to change custody.

**⚠ Where a related new request must go.** If a circuit court action about support, custody, or parenting time is pending, or the court has continuing jurisdiction from a prior action, **a new request about the same child must be filed as a motion in the earlier case**, not as a new complaint, if the relief was available in the original action — MCR 3.204(A)(1). Postjudgment motions in domestic relations cases are governed by MCR 2.119 — MCR 3.213. *(VERIFIED, `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`.)*

## What you are asking for, in one sentence

> **You are asking the court to state your parenting time in specific terms — dates, times, and places — and to set the conditions that make those terms work, so the order can be followed and enforced without anyone having to agree to anything.**

Write that sentence at the top of your own notes and keep coming back to it. Every time the drafting drifts toward what happened at the last hearing, or what the other parent is like, that sentence pulls it back.

# Part A — The law you are standing on

## A.1 The lead authority: parenting time "in specific terms," on request

> **MCL 722.27a(8): "Parenting time shall be granted in specific terms if requested by either party at any time."**

*(VERIFIED — complete text of MCL 722.27a retrieved from legislature.mi.gov on August 9, 2026; site banner "Michigan Compiled Laws Complete Through PA 2 of 2025"; section history shows last amendment 2016 PA 96, eff. Aug. 1, 2016.)*

Read the words one at a time, because each one is doing work:

| The word | What it does |
|---|---|
| **"shall"** | Mandatory voice, not "may." |
| **"in specific terms"** | The thing you are asking for is named in the statute. |
| **"if requested"** | It happens on request. You have to actually ask. |
| **"by either party"** | Either parent. Not only the parent with more time. |
| **"at any time"** | No window closes. Ten months, two years — the subsection sets no deadline. |

This subsection is why the motion exists. The related provision confirms the point from the other direction: the court may **"[p]rovide for reasonable parenting time of the child by the parties involved … by general or specific terms and conditions"** — MCL 722.27(1)(b), verified. Michigan law expressly allows an order to be written in *general* terms. And MCL 722.27a(8) is the mechanism by which either parent converts general terms into specific ones.

> ### ⚠⚠ THE HONEST LIMIT ON THIS ARGUMENT — read before you build on it
>
> **The statutory text above is verified. The case law is not.**
>
> No Michigan appellate opinion has been read for this project on how MCL 722.27a(8) interacts with the "proper cause or change of circumstances" gate that applies to **modifying** an existing order (MCL 722.27(1)(c)). It is possible a court would say (8) is a right that operates on request. It is also possible a court would say that any change to an existing parenting-time provision must first clear the threshold, and that (8) governs only what the order must look like once the court reaches the merits.
>
> **We do not know which, and no one should tell you they do.**
>
> **What follows from that is not weakness — it is the structure of the motion.** You plead **both**: (8) as the lead, and proper cause / change of circumstances **in the alternative**. If the court agrees that (8) applies directly, you win on the shorter path. If the court says you must clear the threshold first, you have already pleaded it and you do not lose on a technicality.
>
> **Do not write, say, or imply that MCL 722.27a(8) is settled law that removes the threshold.** If asked, say what is true: the statute says what it says, and you are also prepared to meet the threshold. `UNVERIFIED — case law on subsection (8) not read`

## A.2 The alternative path: the threshold, and why it may be lower than you fear

If the court asks you to clear a threshold, this is the ground you stand on.

### Step 1 — What is the established custodial environment?

> **MCL 722.27(1)(c):** "Subject to subsection (3), modify or amend its previous judgments or orders for proper cause shown or because of change of circumstances until the child reaches 18 years of age… The court shall not modify or amend its previous judgments or orders or issue a new order so as to change the established custodial environment of a child unless there is presented clear and convincing evidence that it is in the best interest of the child. **The custodial environment of a child is established if over an appreciable time the child naturally looks to the custodian in that environment for guidance, discipline, the necessities of life, and parental comfort.** The age of the child, the physical environment, and the inclination of the custodian and the child as to permanency of the relationship shall also be considered."

*(VERIFIED — full text of MCL 722.27 retrieved from legislature.mi.gov, August 9, 2026.)*

**The established custodial environment — the "ECE" — is a question of fact about how the child actually lives.** Not about what the order says. Not about who was right. It can exist with one parent, both, or neither.

> ### ⚠ The hard part, stated plainly
>
> **Time builds the other side's ECE, not yours.** Every month the current arrangement continues, the factual condition described in that statute deepens — regardless of how the arrangement came about. That is the real reason this is urgent. It is not a deadline. There is no cliff. It is a fact about a child that gets more true the longer it lasts.
>
> This also cuts against you if you wait, and it is the honest reason not to spend more months on the history of the last hearing.

### Step 2 — Does what you are asking for *change* the ECE?

This is the question the whole threshold turns on, and it is the question you must answer in your papers.

> ***Shade v Wright*, 291 Mich App 17 (2010)** (published; read in full for this project):
>
> "We discern nothing in the *Vodvarka* opinion that requires the standards used to determine the existence of proper cause or change of circumstances for custody determinations to apply to determinations regarding parenting time, **absent a conclusion that a change in parenting time will result in a change in an established custodial environment**. … **If a change in parenting time results in a change in the established custodial environment, then the *Vodvarka* framework is appropriate.**"

*(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1; opinion read in full.)*

So:

- **If the relief you ask for would change the ECE** → the full *Vodvarka* framework applies, and the burden is **clear and convincing evidence**. Re-labeling the motion "parenting time" does not lower the bar. *Shade* says so directly.
- **If it would not** → *Shade*'s more expansive approach governs, and the burden is **preponderance of the evidence**.

> ***Pierron v Pierron*, 486 Mich 81, 92–93; 782 NW2d 480 (2010)** — **Michigan Supreme Court, binding**: where the proposed change **alters** the established custodial environment, the standard is **clear and convincing evidence**; where it does **not** alter the ECE, the standard is **preponderance of the evidence**, and the burden is on the parent proposing the change.

*(VERIFIED as to citation and as to this proposition through *Shade*'s own text — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1. The full *Pierron* opinion has not been read for this project. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — holding confirmed through a published opinion quoting it, not from the opinion itself`)*

**Where a motion for specific terms usually sits.** Asking the court to write down when you already have time — converting "as the parties agree" into a calendar — does not by itself move the child's home, change who the child looks to day to day, or shift primary care. That is the argument you make. **But it is an argument about your facts, not an automatic result**, and it gets weaker the more time you ask for. If what you are really asking for is a change in where the child primarily lives, say so honestly and understand that you are in the clear-and-convincing lane.

### Step 3 — If you must show proper cause or change of circumstances

> ***Vodvarka v Grasmeyer*, 259 Mich App 499, 511, 513–514; 675 NW2d 847 (2003)** (published):
>
> "proper cause means one or more appropriate grounds that have or could have a **significant effect on the child's life** to the extent that a reevaluation of the child's custodial situation should be undertaken."
>
> "in order to establish a 'change of circumstances,' a movant must prove that, **since the entry of the last custody order**, the conditions surrounding custody of the child, which have or could have a significant effect on the child's well-being, **have materially changed**. … the evidence must demonstrate **something more than the normal life changes** (both good and bad) that occur during the life of a child, and there must be at least some evidence that the material changes **have had or will almost certainly have an effect on the child**."

*(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

And the limit *Shade* placed on its own holding, which you must carry if you cite *Shade*:

> ***Shade*, footnote 4:** "With our holding today, **we do not seek to precisely define** the proper cause or change of circumstances necessary to change parenting time. Our holding is limited to our conclusion that the normal life changes that occurred with the minor child in this case are sufficient to modify parenting time."

*(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

**Do not overstate *Shade*.** It is a narrow holding plus a widely quoted rationale — that "the very normal life change factors that *Vodvarka* finds insufficient to justify a change in custodial environment are precisely the types of considerations that trial courts should take into account in making determinations regarding modification of parenting time." Cite it for what it says.

**What the change-of-circumstances facts look like here** — dated and neutral, never characterized:

- An order provision that, as written, requires agreement in order to operate, and which has produced no schedule over a period of months.
- A documented record of specific written requests and what followed (Document 1). This is why Document 1 comes first.
- The child's own passage of time and developmental change since the order.

Whether those facts clear the bar is for the court. Plead them as facts with dates and let the court do the labeling.

## A.3 The framing provisions — the presumption and the right

> **MCL 722.27a(1):** "Parenting time shall be granted in accordance with the best interests of the child. **It is presumed to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents.** Except as otherwise provided in this section, parenting time shall be granted to a parent **in a frequency, duration, and type reasonably calculated to promote a strong relationship** between the child and the parent granted parenting time."
>
> **MCL 722.27a(3):** "A child **has a right** to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by **clear and convincing evidence** that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health."

*(VERIFIED — legislature.mi.gov full-section fetch, August 9, 2026.)*

These two do a specific job in your papers. Subsection (1) tells the court what the parenting time must be *shaped* to do — promote a strong relationship. An open-ended provision that has produced no time is not, on its face, "reasonably calculated" to do that. Subsection (3) puts the burden of curtailing contact on whoever asks to curtail it, at the clear-and-convincing level.

**The limiter in the same breath:** subsection (3) is a burden allocation, not a shield. If there is evidence of endangerment, this provision is exactly the frame in which that evidence gets heard. And a court **may** consider **"(c) The reasonable likelihood of abuse or neglect of the child during parenting time"** and **"(d) The reasonable likelihood of abuse of a parent resulting from the exercise of parenting time"** — MCL 722.27a(7)(c), (d), verified verbatim. Know what the record contains before you invoke (3).

## A.4 The relief menu — what you may actually ask for

> **MCL 722.27a(9):** "A parenting time order **may contain any reasonable terms or conditions that facilitate the orderly and meaningful exercise of parenting time by a parent**, including 1 or more of the following:
> (a) Division of the responsibility to transport the child.
> (b) Division of the cost of transporting the child.
> (c) Restrictions on the presence of third persons during parenting time.
> (d) Requirements that the child be ready for parenting time at a specific time.
> (e) Requirements that the parent arrive for parenting time and return the child from parenting time at specific times.
> (f) Requirements that parenting time occur in the presence of a third person or agency.
> (g) Requirements that a party post a bond to assure compliance with a parenting time order.
> (h) Requirements of reasonable notice when parenting time will not occur.
> (i) Any other reasonable condition determined to be appropriate in the particular case."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — legislature.mi.gov, August 9, 2026.)*

Two more that must be in any parenting-time order you propose:

> **MCL 722.27a(10):** "Except as provided in this subsection, a parenting time order **shall contain a prohibition on exercising parenting time in a country that is not a party to the Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction.** This subsection does not apply if both parents provide the court with written consent…"
>
> **MCL 722.27a(11):** "During the time a child is with a parent to whom parenting time has been awarded, **that parent shall decide all routine matters concerning the child**."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — same.)*

Subsection (10) uses "shall contain." Put it in the proposed order. Leaving it out is a defect in a proposed order, and putting it in costs you one sentence.

Subsections (9)(d) and (9)(e) are the quiet workhorses. They convert "he was late again" from an argument into a breach of a written term.

## A.5 Why specific terms are the whole point — the enforcement provision

This is the sentence that explains why a vague order is worse than no order.

> **MCL 552.641(2):** "Notwithstanding the requirement of subsection (1), the office of the friend of the court **may decline to respond** to an alleged custody or parenting time order violation under any of the following circumstances: … **(c) The custody or parenting time order does not include an enforceable provision that is relevant to the custody or parenting time order violation alleged in the complaint.**"

*(VERIFIED verbatim — full text of MCL 552.641 retrieved from legislature.mi.gov, August 9, 2026.)*

An order that says parenting time happens "as the parties agree" contains no term that can be violated. Nothing was promised, so nothing can be broken. **The Friend of the Court has a lawful basis to decline your complaint, and enforcement is unavailable to you until specific terms exist.**

That is the whole sequence in one line: **specific terms now are enforcement later.** Every enforcement tool in Michigan law — makeup time, contempt, the FOC complaint process — needs a term to hang on.

> **The same provision cuts at you.** MCL 552.641(2)(a) lets the FOC decline where a complainant has submitted **two or more** complaints found unwarranted, costs were assessed, and those costs are unpaid; and (2)(b) lets it decline where the violation is **more than 56 days** old. The statute says "may decline" — discretionary, not jurisdictional — but assume the FOC will use it. Over-complaining can close the door you are trying to open.

## A.6 The support connection — a note, not a request in this motion

Michigan's child support formula computes an offset from the number of overnights each parent has. Two provisions to know now, and to act on **later**:

- **2025 MCSF §3.03(E):** every child support order must state whether it includes a parental time offset and **the number of overnights used.** Find your support order and read what number it recites.
- **2025 MCSF §3.03(D):** a substantial difference between the ordered and actual overnights — **at least 21 overnights**, or one exceeding the §4.05 modification threshold — supports a motion to modify support.

*(VERIFIED — `sources/primary/2025-michigan-child-support-formula_eff-2025-01-01.pdf`, §3.03; `verification_ledger.md` §2.6.)*

> ### ⚠ Do not put support in this motion
>
> Parenting time and support are independent. Asking for both in one breath invites the response that you want time in order to reduce a payment. Get the schedule first. Support modification is a separate motion, sequenced after — and the §3.03(D) hook is the reason the sequence is in that order, not the reverse.
>
> Also verified and worth knowing: MCSF §3.03(C)(4) directs that overnights a child "lawfully and actually" spends with a parent be credited, **but "Do not consider overnights exercised in violation of an order."** A parent cannot bank withheld overnights.

# Part B — Before you draft: three traps

## B.1 The FOC 65 form trap

SCAO publishes a **Motion Regarding Parenting Time (FOC 65)**. Use the official form — this guide does not publish competing versions of official forms, and you should not either. Find the current revision on the Michigan courts forms site or through Michigan Legal Help. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — form number, current title, and revision date not confirmed this pass. Court website URLs contain generated path segments that have already changed once during this project; a 404 means moved, not withdrawn.`

**Here is the trap.** In *Shade v Wright*, Judge Shapiro's concurrence observed that the SCAO parenting-time motion form **does not ask the movant for proper cause or change of circumstances at all**, while the custody motion form does. *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §4.1.)*

So a self-represented parent can fill out the official form completely and correctly, file it, and **never be prompted for the showing the court may require.** The form does not warn you. Nothing warns you.

**What to do:** complete the official form, and **attach** a brief in support that makes the showing anyway. The form is the container. The brief is the case.

## B.2 The mandatory pleading-content rule for a change of parenting time

> **MCR 3.207(B)(1)(a):** "A verified pleading or an affidavit attached to a motion or pleading that requests an ex parte custody or parenting time order **or that requests a change of custody or parenting time** must include the following information:
> (i) facts establishing whether the child has an established custodial environment with either parent, both parents, neither parent, or a third party; and
> (ii) either facts establishing that entry of the requested order **will not change** the child's established custodial environment, facts establishing that clear and convincing evidence exists that the change in the child's established custodial environment is in the child's best interest, or if the request is from a third party, facts establishing by clear and convincing evidence that it is not in the child's best interests for the parent(s) to have custody."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 3 updated May 1, 2026.)*

**A reading question you should know about.** This subrule sits underneath MCR 3.207(**B**) "Ex Parte Orders," and under (B)(1), which is about entering an ex parte order. But the text of (a) itself covers a verified pleading or affidavit attached to a motion "that requests a change of custody or parenting time," with no ex parte limitation on that branch. Whether it binds an ordinary noticed motion is a fair question. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — rule text verified; scope reading not confirmed by any case or by the clerk`

**It does not matter, practically. Include the ECE facts either way.** They cost you a paragraph, they are the exact facts the court must find under MCL 722.27(1)(c) anyway, and they are what puts you in the *Shade*/preponderance lane. Omitting them is the only way this question can hurt you.

Note how neatly (a)(ii)'s first option fits: *"facts establishing that entry of the requested order will not change the child's established custodial environment."* That is your motion's central factual assertion, and the rule invites you to plead it.

## B.3 The service trap that already cost you once

A prior filing in this case failed on service. **Do not draft one more word of a motion until you have read Packet 1 Document 3.** Service in a postjudgment domestic relations case is not obvious, the rules interact, and the intuitive answer — "the other side has a lawyer, so serve the lawyer" — is **not** the complete answer. See Document 3, §"Who must be served."

## C.2 The motion — drafting aid

> **This is a drafting aid, not a form, and not filing-ready.** It assumes: an existing Genesee County postjudgment order; a parenting-time provision stated in open-ended terms; no pending appeal; no PPO or criminal matter in the way; and that you have read Document 3 on service.
>
> ### ⛔ Do not use this if:
> - You are actually asking to change **custody** (where the child primarily lives). Different standard, different burden, different motion — and MCR 3.207(B)(1)(a)(ii)'s clear-and-convincing branch applies to you.
> - A PPO, no-contact order, bond condition, or open criminal or CPS matter touches these facts. **Stop and get help.**
> - Another state, or another country, is or has been involved. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`

**Drafting rules for the numbered paragraphs:**

- **One fact per paragraph**, each with a date.
- **Quote the order; never characterize it.**
- **No adjectives about a person.** Not one. If a sentence would change meaning when read aloud in a flat voice by someone who does not know you, rewrite it.
- **Nothing about the other parent's character, motives, mental state, or new relationships.**
- **The banned words appear nowhere:** narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, coercive control, weaponized, personality disorder. A filing containing them becomes a filing about the person who wrote it.
- **The history gets one sentence, if any.** "The moving party did not contest the terms entered on [date]" is a sentence. Anything longer is a different case, and every window to relitigate it closed months ago.

```
     - MCL 722.27a(9)(a)-(i), quoted, tied item by item to the
       proposed order's paragraphs.
     - MCL 722.27a(10): the Hague provision is mandatory content.
     - MCL 722.27a(11): routine matters during parenting time.

     - MCL 552.641(2)(c), quoted. An order with no enforceable

```

## C.4 The proposed order — the specific schedule

**The test for every line: could a stranger execute it without asking a question?** If the answer is no, rewrite the line.

> ### ⚠ Schedule design depends on the child's age, and this draft cannot supply that
>
> MCL 722.27a(7)(b) directs that the court may consider "[w]hether the child is a nursing child less than 6 months of age, or less than 1 year of age if the child receives substantial nutrition through nursing." For a very young child, a schedule of frequent shorter periods is a different proposal from alternating weekends, and asking for the wrong shape is a real risk.

> A graduated schedule — shorter periods that lengthen on stated dates, with no further motion required — is one drafting approach that removes the need to come back to court. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — no authority states courts prefer this; it is a way to write an order, not a rule of law`

## C.5 The concurrence certificate — Genesee LCR 2.119(A)

> **SEVENTH JUDICIAL CIRCUIT (GENESEE), Rule 2.119(A) Motion Certification by Attorney.** "The following certificate signed by the attorney of record **or by the party in propria persona** shall be attached to or incorporated in the motion and notice of hearing filed with the clerk:
>
> *I hereby certify that I have made personal contact with \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_[name] on \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_[date], requesting concurrence in the relief sought with this motion and that concurrence has been denied, or that I have made reasonable and diligent attempts to contact counsel requesting concurrence in the relief sought with this motion.*"

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/local-court-rules-circuit_2025-06-01.txt`, "Updated with MSC order(s) effective on 6/1/2025".)*

**Three things a self-represented parent must understand about this rule:**

1. **The heading says "by Attorney." The text says "or by the party in propria persona." The text controls.** You are covered by this rule. If you leave the certificate off, your motion is out of compliance with a binding local rule.
2. **You have to actually do it before you can certify it.** The certificate has two branches: concurrence was requested and denied, **or** you made reasonable and diligent attempts to contact counsel. If communication in your case runs through the other parent's attorney, that is who you contact, and **that contact is the second branch of this certificate** — which is convenient, because it is also exactly what Document 1 has you doing already.
3. **Sign the certificate honestly.** Your signature carries MCR 1.109(E)(5): it certifies you read the document, that to the best of your knowledge after reasonable inquiry it is well grounded in fact and warranted by existing law or a good-faith argument to extend it, and **that it is not interposed for any improper purpose, such as to harass or to cause unnecessary delay.** MCR 1.109(E)(6) requires the court to impose an appropriate sanction for a violation. *(VERIFIED.)*

**Practical:** send a short concurrence request by certified mail — its own letter, separate from a parenting-time request — that encloses the proposed order, asks whether the other side will concur, and states a date by which you need an answer. Then the certificate writes itself, and the mailing receipt proves it.

`ℹ PROVISIONAL — whether a Genesee judge expects the certificate on its own page or incorporated in the motion, and whether the courthouse expects a **praecipe** (the local rule contains no praecipe requirement), must be confirmed at the Circuit Court Records counter. See Document 3.`

## C.6 The notice of hearing

MCR 2.119(A)(3): a motion and the notice of hearing **may be combined in the same document.** MCR 2.119(C)(1) sets the service deadline — **9 days by first-class mail, 7 days by delivery** — and MCR 2.119(C)(4) sets a separate **filing** deadline of 7 days. These are different obligations. **Both apply. Document 3 works the calendar backward.** *(VERIFIED — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`.)*

**You cannot notice a hearing for a date you invented.** How motion hearing dates are obtained in Genesee — whether by contacting chambers, a scheduling clerk, a praecipe, or a posted motion day — is a local practice question this project has not confirmed. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — ask the Circuit Court Records counter, and ask which referee or judge hears parenting-time motions in your case.`

## C.7 Communication-protocol relief, in this same motion

You are already asking the court to write down when parenting time happens. Ask, in the same motion, for the court to set **how** the parents exchange the information that makes it work.

**The statutory basis:** MCL 722.27a(9)(i) — "Any other reasonable condition determined to be appropriate in the particular case" — together with (9)(h), which contemplates orders requiring notice when parenting time will not occur. A notice requirement is unworkable without a channel through which notice can be given and proved. *(Statutory text VERIFIED. The application is an argument, not a holding.)*

**How to frame it — and how not to:**

| Ask for | Do not ask for |
|---|---|
| A **written, date-stamped channel** for child-related logistics, with records both parents can retrieve | "Removal of the communication restriction" |
| Communication **limited to matters concerning the child** | An open channel |
| A stated **response window** for scheduling messages | Anything that reads as access to the other parent |
| Notice of a missed period, in writing, a stated number of hours ahead | Anything framed as a grievance about the current channel |

**Say why in child-centered terms:** a schedule with start times, end times, an exchange location, and a notice requirement needs a channel through which notice can actually be given and proved. Mail delivered in three to five days cannot carry notice of a same-day problem. That is a logistics argument, and it is true.

> ### ⚠ The limiter, in the same section
>
> **If the restriction exists for a safety reason, asking to loosen it will be heard as asking to loosen a safety measure.** Do not ask for less structure. Ask for **different** structure: a written, recorded, child-only channel is *more* accountable than mail, not less. If there is any safety history in this case, say plainly in the motion that you are not asking for direct contact and that the requested channel is written and recorded.
>
> A co-parenting application is one way to do this. **Treating any commercial application as inherently court-favored is unsupported**, and cost, access, disability, and safety all matter. If you propose one, propose it as a generic option — "a written communication platform that retains records, such as [name], or another method the Court directs" — and be ready for the court to pick something else, including simply "email limited to matters concerning the child." `ℹ PROVISIONAL — no verified information about what any Genesee judge or referee uses or prefers`

**Open question to the owner:** whether the restriction is in the **order** or only in **counsel's correspondence** changes this request materially. If it is in the order, you are asking the court to modify its own provision. If it is only counsel's letter, you are asking the court to set a protocol where the order is silent — and you should say so, neutrally, in one sentence, attaching the letter. Read the order and the letter and note the date and signer of each.

# Part D — The other side, and yourself

## D.1 What the other side will likely argue, and where you stand

| Their argument | Your answer | Honest risk |
|---|---|---|
| **"No proper cause or change of circumstances."** | Lead: MCL 722.27a(8) grants specific terms on request, at any time. Alternative: the relief does not alter the ECE, so *Shade* governs and *Pierron* sets the burden at preponderance; and the dated facts meet it. | Real. This is the most likely attack and the one that kills most pro se modification motions. It is the reason the alternative pleading is not optional. |
| **"He never exercised his time."** — MCL 722.27a(7)(g) | The request record from Document 1: dated letters, certified mail receipts, delivery cards, replies. | This argument is strong against a parent with **no** written record, and weak against one with a thick one. That asymmetry is the entire reason Document 1 comes first. |
| **"The current arrangement is what the child is used to."** | That is an ECE argument, and you meet it head-on: specifying times within the existing arrangement does not change where the child primarily lives or who provides day-to-day care. | Real, and it grows every month. |
| **Renewed allegations about safety.** | Your motion does not depend on scoring the other parent. It depends on (8), the (1) presumption, the (3) right, and your own documented record. | Serious. Factor (j) **cannot** be scored against a parent for reasonable protective action against sexual assault or domestic violence — MCL 722.23(j). If you built your case on (j), it collapses here. If you built it on the request record, it does not. |
| **"This filing is harassment."** | The record: one motion, requesting statutory relief, after documented written requests through the permitted channel. | Real if you over-file. MCR 1.109(E)(6)–(7) and MCL 600.2591 sanction frivolous filings; MCR 3.206(D)(2)(b) shifts fees; MCL 552.641(2)(a) can close the FOC route. **One good motion beats four.** |

## D.2 When NOT to file

Do not file if any of these is true — fix it first:

- You have **not** read the existing order word for word.
- You have **no** written request record yet. File after you have one, not before. A motion supported by three certified letters is a different motion from one supported by a story.
- You have contacted the other parent outside the permitted channel and it is not addressed.
- You are asking for a change of primary residence but calling it parenting time. *Shade* forecloses that: the ECE, not the caption, decides the standard.
- There is an active PPO, CPS, or criminal matter you have not told anyone about. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
- You cannot say, in one sentence and without adjectives, what you want the order to say.

## D.3 Your own audit, before you file

1. Read the motion out loud in a flat voice. Does any sentence describe a person rather than an event? Cut it.
2. Search your draft for the banned words. Also search for: *always, never, refuses, deliberately, obviously, clearly, of course, as usual.* Cut them.
3. Count paragraphs about the past. If more than one, cut to one.
4. Check that every fact has a date and a document behind it.
5. Check that the proposed order has no "as agreed" anywhere.
6. Confirm the proposed order is **attached** to the motion and will be **served with** it — Genesee LCR 2.119(B).
7. Confirm the concurrence certificate is present and truthful.
8. Confirm you have read Document 3 and know exactly who gets served and by what date.
