## Route tag

**Postjudgment.** An order already exists. You are not starting a case; you are preparing to ask the court to make an existing order specific and workable. This document also applies at the pending and temporary-order stages, and it applies whether or not you ever file anything.

---

## The short version

An order exists. It does not say when you see your child. Every month that passes with no written record is a month that can be described later as a month you did not ask.

This document fixes that, and it costs the price of postage.

**You are building three things at once:**

1. **A record of asking.** Dated, specific, written requests, sent through the channel you are permitted to use.
2. **A record of what happened next.** Answered, refused, ignored, or agreed and then not honored.
3. **A record of your own compliance.** You followed the restriction exactly, every time, while you disagreed with it.

The third one is the one people skip, and it is often the one that decides how a judge or referee reads the first two.

---

## Rule 1 — The permitted channel only. No exceptions.

**Use the narrowest channel anyone has told you to use, until a court tells you otherwise.**

If your order routes communication through the other parent's attorney, use that. If the attorney has since told you to use US mail only, use US mail only. It does not matter whether that narrowing came from the judge or from the attorney's own letter — **you comply with the narrowest instruction you have received while you challenge it in court.** You do not get to decide which instruction was properly given. A judge does.

That means, starting today and until an order changes it:

- **No** texts, calls, emails, or direct messages to the other parent.
- **No** contact through the other parent's family, friends, or new partner.
- **No** social-media posts, comments, replies, likes, or stories about the other parent, the case, the order, the attorney, the judge, or the referee. None. Not private. Not "just to my friends."
- **No** showing up anywhere to talk in person.
- **No** message, note, question, or explanation carried by the child, in either direction.

> ### ⚠ Why this rule is not optional
>
> Every one of the tools in this guide can be pointed back at you. Michigan defines a "course of conduct" for stalking purposes as **"a pattern of conduct composed of a series of 2 or more separate noncontinuous acts evidencing a continuity of purpose"** (MCL 750.411h, verified verbatim — `research_interference_coercive_control.md` §6.2). Two contacts is a pattern. The same statute excludes **"conduct that serves a legitimate purpose,"** which is why a small number of polite, child-focused written requests through the permitted channel is a different thing from a stream of messages — but you do not want to be the person arguing about where that line is.
>
> The practical point is simpler than the law. If you break the restriction once, the next hearing is about you breaking the restriction. It is no longer about your child's schedule.

**The flip side, in the same breath:** exact compliance is itself evidence. When you ask the court for specific parenting time, the court may consider **"[w]hether a parent can reasonably be expected to exercise parenting time in accordance with the court order"** — MCL 722.27a(7)(f), verified verbatim. Ten months of documented, exact compliance with a communication restriction you disagree with is the cleanest possible answer to that question.

**One more thing about a restriction you think is unfair.** A provision routing communication through counsel is a *channel* rule. It is not a court finding that you are dangerous, and complying with it is not an admission of anything. Do not spend a single letter arguing about why it exists. The way a channel restriction ends is that a court replaces it — Document 2's motion asks for a defined, direct, written method for parenting-time logistics, and the cleanest supporting fact is your own record of using the imposed channel exactly, while it produced no parenting time.

### When the channel is "through counsel" — and counsel's letter says "mail only"

Two instructions from two different sources often stack here. Keep them straight in your records, because Document 2 treats them differently:

- **"Communicate through the attorney"** — if that is in a **court order**, it came from the judge, and only a judge can change it.
- **"US mail only"** — if that arrived in a **letter from the attorney**, it is counsel's instruction, not the court's. You still follow it while it stands (narrowest-instruction rule, above), but the motion may accurately describe its source and date when asking the court to set a workable method.

In practice:

1. **Every request that matters goes by certified mail, return receipt requested.** The mailed letter *is* the request. It satisfies the strictest instruction anyone has given, and it is the strongest proof you can buy for the price of postage.
2. **A same-day courtesy copy by email is a choice, and it has a tradeoff.** For it: an instant timestamp, and a channel some offices actually answer. Against it: if counsel has already written "mail only," even a one-line courtesy copy hands them a small "does not follow instructions" point. Small — the letter *was* mailed as instructed — but small points accumulate. The cautious course is mail alone. If you send the courtesy copy, it says one thing and nothing else: *"A copy of this letter was mailed to your office today by certified mail, tracking no. ___. This email is a courtesy copy."* If counsel then writes "stop emailing," stop. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — no rule governs this; both courses comply with the order`
3. **Short, specific, professional questions are the ones that get answers.** However the past correspondence has gone, a question that can be answered in one sentence — *does your client agree to the exchange time in my letter of [date]?* — is easier to answer than a narrative, and either outcome helps you: an answer is progress, and a non-answer to a one-sentence question is easy for a referee to see.
4. **The channel-narrowing letter is itself evidence.** Keep the letter that said "mail only," note its date and signer, and log it. It documents how the communication funnel was built — dates, not characterizations.

## Rule 2 — The child is not part of this

The child does not carry messages. The child is not asked what the other parent said. The child is not asked what happened at the other house. The child is not told about court, the order, the attorney, or money. The child is not asked to remember anything, choose anything, or repeat anything.

This is not a balancing test and it does not bend for a good reason. A parent who uses a child as a source is the parent the court starts watching.

**What you may write down:** what *you* saw and what *adults* said to *you*. Nothing that begins "she told me that Mom said."

---

## Rule 3 — Everything in writing, and keep the proof of sending

For each request, you keep four things:

| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| A **copy of the exact letter you mailed** | So you can prove what you asked, not just that you asked |
| The **certified mail receipt** with the tracking number | Proof of the date you mailed it |
| The **return receipt card** (or the electronic delivery record) | Proof it arrived, and when |
| **Any reply**, in full, unedited | Replies from the other side are usable in a way your own letters are not — see the evidence section |

Do not edit, crop, retype, or "clean up" anything. Keep the original of everything. If you scan documents, keep the paper too.

## If you already asked — assembling the record that exists

Most people arrive at this document having already asked, usually by email, usually less carefully than the letters above. **That record is not a do-over. It is an asset.** Dated written requests are dated written requests, whatever their format, and the replies they drew are the other side's own words. Assemble it now, before drafting anything for court:

1. **Export or print every message you sent through the permitted channel, and every reply — in full, with the header visible** (date, time, from, to, subject). Including, especially, every reply that says only some version of *"communicate by mail."*
2. **Do not retype, excerpt, annotate, or clean anything up.** The copy that goes in the folder is the whole message, as sent. And **delete nothing** — not even messages you regret. A gap in a record reads as what it is, and the messages exist on the other side's server regardless.
3. **Log the old requests retroactively** in the request log below — one row per request, in date order, channel noted. The no-adjectives rule applies with extra force to messages written when you were angry: the log *describes* what was asked and what came back; it never characterizes.
4. **A reply that only redirects the channel is still evidence.** It proves the request arrived, the date it arrived, and what happened next. A stack of specific requests answered only with *"use the mail"* is a documented pattern with dates attached. You will never need to argue about it — laid end to end, the dates do the arguing.
5. **Read your own old messages before anyone else does.** Anything in them you would not want read aloud belongs in your conduct audit (below), today, so it is a known fact and not a surprise. It does not undo the record; it tells you how to build the case around it.

Then the certified-mail practice starts today, on top of that base — the old record shows the asking began long before this protocol did, and the new record shows what it looks like done exactly right.

Three provisions do the work. All three are quoted verbatim from primary sources.

### 1. The statute presumes your child should have a strong relationship with you

> **MCL 722.27a(1):** "Parenting time shall be granted in accordance with the best interests of the child. It is presumed to be in the best interests of a child for the child to have a strong relationship with both of his or her parents. Except as otherwise provided in this section, parenting time shall be granted to a parent in a frequency, duration, and type reasonably calculated to promote a strong relationship between the child and the parent granted parenting time."
>
> **MCL 722.27a(3):** "A child has a right to parenting time with a parent unless it is shown on the record by clear and convincing evidence that it would endanger the child's physical, mental, or emotional health."

*(VERIFIED — full text of MCL 722.27a retrieved from legislature.mi.gov, August 9, 2026; compilation banner "Complete Through PA 2 of 2025"; section last amended 2016 PA 96.)*

Read those two sentences together. The presumption runs in favor of the relationship, and the burden to cut contact sits on the person asking to cut it, at the highest civil standard Michigan uses.

### 2. There is a factor about failing to show up — and it can be aimed at you

> **MCL 722.27a(7):** "The court **may** consider the following factors when determining the frequency, duration, and type of parenting time to be granted: … (f) Whether a parent can reasonably be expected to exercise parenting time in accordance with the court order. (g) Whether a parent has frequently failed to exercise reasonable parenting time."

*(VERIFIED — same source. Note the verb: "may consider." These factors are permissive, not mandatory.)*

**⚠ Citation trap.** Older Michigan opinions cite this list as MCL 722.27a**(6)**(a)–(i). The current statute places it at **(7)**(a)–(i). Both are correct for their dates. If you follow a case citation to subsection (6), you will land on the wrong provision.

Factor **(g)** is the reason this document exists. Ten quiet months, with nothing written down, can be described later as ten months of not asking. Ten months of dated certified letters proposing specific dates cannot.

### 3. Failing to help the relationship is a best-interest factor — with a hard limit

> **MCL 722.23(j):** "The willingness and ability of each of the parties to facilitate and encourage a close and continuing parent-child relationship between the child and the other parent or the child and the parents. A court may not consider negatively for the purposes of this factor any reasonable action taken by a parent to protect a child or that parent from sexual assault or domestic violence by the child's other parent."

*(VERIFIED verbatim — `research_interference_coercive_control.md` §2; MCL 722.23 independently confirmed against legislature.mi.gov, current through PA 2 of 2025, last amended 2016 PA 95.)*

> ### ⚠ The limiter that travels with factor (j) — read this before you rely on it
>
> The second sentence is not decoration. If the other parent's conduct was **reasonable protective action** against sexual assault or domestic violence, factor (j) **cannot** be scored against them, no matter how it looks to you.
>
> **So do not build your case on factor (j).** Build it on the statute that says parenting time shall be granted in specific terms on request (Document 2), on the presumption in MCL 722.27a(1), on the child's right in (3), and on your own documented record of asking. Factor (j) is supporting material. If it turns out there is a protective-action explanation, a case built on (j) collapses, and a case built on the request record does not.

## Rule 4 — What a good request letter does, and what a bad one does

A good letter is **short, dated, factual, specific, and answerable.** A judge or referee reading it six months from now should be able to say: this parent asked clearly, proposed real dates, and asked for a written answer.

**A good letter:**

- States the date at the top.
- Names the order it is written under (case number, date of the order).
- Proposes **specific dates, specific times, and a specific exchange location.** Not "some time this month."
- Offers at least one alternative.
- Asks for a **written response by a stated date.**
- Is polite, even when it costs you something to be polite.
- Fits on one page.

**A bad letter:**

- Argues about the past hearing, the last order, or who said what.
- Uses any word that describes the other parent's character, mind, motives, or mental health. The banned list is absolute: **narcissist, alienator, abuser, gaslighting, parental alienation, PAS, personality disorder** — and any similar term. These words do not appear in your letters, your motion, your testimony, or your notes.
- Threatens: "if you don't answer I'll file contempt."
- Mentions money or support. Parenting time and support are separate. Mixing them in one letter invites the answer that you are using one to get the other.
- Asks about the other parent's household, partner, job, or plans.
- Repeats a request that was answered three days ago.

**Cadence.** One letter per proposed period, spaced so each one is a real request and not a campaign. A holiday or school break gets its own letter, further ahead. `ℹ DRAFTING JUDGMENT — this is a practical recommendation, not a legal rule; no statute or court rule sets a frequency`

## Drafting aid — the written request

> **This is a drafting aid, not a form and not a filing.** It is not filed with any court. It does not start any clock. Sending it is not "service" of anything, and it is not a substitute for service of court papers (see Packet 1 Document 3). It assumes: an existing Genesee County order; parenting time stated in open-ended terms rather than a schedule; and a restriction routing your communication through the other parent's attorney or through the mail.
>
> ### ⛔ Do not use this if:
> - A PPO, no-contact order, or bond condition prohibits the contact. **Stop.**
> - You are not sure exactly what your order says about communication. Read the order first, word for word.
> - You are a respondent in a PPO case or have any active police or CPS contact. Different rules apply, and this guide stops there.
> - You have already been told in writing by the court to communicate a different way.
> - You want to say anything in it other than a request about parenting time.
> - There is an active criminal matter in which anything you write could be used.

```
[Your name]
[Your mailing address]
[Your phone]

[Date]

[Attorney name]
[Firm name]
[Firm mailing address]

Sent by certified mail, return receipt requested
Tracking number: ______________________

Re:  [Case name as it appears on the order], Genesee County Circuit Court
     Case No. [number]
     Request for parenting time — [month/period]

Dear [Attorney name]:

I am writing through your office because the communication in this
case is directed through you. I am not writing to your client
directly.

The order entered [date of order] provides for parenting time
[quote the exact words of the parenting-time provision, in
quotation marks].

I am requesting parenting time with our child as follows:

     First choice:   [day], [date], from [time] to [time],
                     exchange at [specific location].
     Alternative:    [day], [date], from [time] to [time],
                     exchange at [specific location].

I will provide transportation [both ways / to the exchange /
as follows: ____________].

Please let me know in writing by [date — allow a reasonable
period, and say what it is] whether either of these times works.
If neither works, I would appreciate a proposed alternative
date and time.

Thank you for your time.

Sincerely,

[Signature]
[Printed name]
```

**Notes on filling it in:**

- **Quote the order exactly.** Copy the parenting-time words character for character. This single habit is worth more than any argument you could write, because it forces every later conversation to be about the words actually in the order.
- **Specific means specific.** A date, a start time, an end time, and a place a stranger could find. "Saturday afternoon" is not a request; it is an invitation to say no.
- **Give a real response window.** Long enough to be reasonable, short enough to be a date.
- **You may use your child's first name** in your own letter. Do not put your child's name, birthdate, school, or photograph into anything you post, share, or upload anywhere.

## Drafting aid — the request log

Keep one row per request. Keep it in a spreadsheet or a notebook — whichever you will actually maintain. Do not keep it in a phone app you might lose access to.

| # | Date mailed | Sent to | Certified tracking no. | What was requested (dates/times/place) | Date delivered (green card) | Response received? Date? | What actually happened | Where the proof is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | | | |
| 2 | | | | | | | | |
| 3 | | | | | | | | |

**Rules for the log:**

- **One line, one fact.** "Requested Oct 18, 10:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m., Flint Twp PD lobby. No response as of Oct 20." Not "ignored me again."
- **No conclusions, no adjectives, no motives.** You are writing the exhibit, not the argument. Assume it will be read out loud by someone who is not on your side.
- **"What actually happened" is what you observed.** Where you went, what time, how long you waited, whether the child was produced. Not what anyone told you the child said.
- **Fill it in the same day.** A log written months later is worth much less, and it looks like what it is.

| # | Date mailed | Sent to | Tracking no. | Requested | Delivered | Response | What happened | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

> **⚠ Check your own day-of-week labels.** Every date in these examples has been checked against a calendar. In a document whose whole purpose is date precision, writing "Saturday" next to a date that falls on a Sunday tells the reader — and later, the court — that you do not check. Open a calendar and verify every single one before it leaves your hands.

## Evidence — what these documents actually are, and what they are not

This is the part most self-help material skips, and it is the part that decides whether your folder is useful in a hearing room.

**The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply at a referee hearing.**

> **MCR 3.215(D)(1): "The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply to referee hearings."**

*(VERIFIED — `sources/primary/michigan-court-rules_2026-07-31.txt`, Chapter 3 updated May 1, 2026.)*

So the folder has to survive the rules. Here is the honest picture:

| The document | The evidence problem | The realistic route |
|---|---|---|
| **Your own letters** | Your own out-of-court written statements. If you offer them **to prove the facts stated inside them**, that is hearsay under MRE 801(c). | Offered instead **to prove that the request was made, what it said, and when** — not to prove the truth of anything asserted — they are not being offered "to prove the truth of the matter asserted." You also testify to sending them. Expect the other side to object; be ready to state the non-hearsay purpose out loud. `ℹ DRAFTING NOTE — this is the standard framing; no Michigan case has been read to confirm its application on these facts` |
| **The certified mail receipt and return receipt** | Authentication. | You testify: this is the receipt the post office gave me, this is the card that came back. A witness with knowledge is the route (MRE 901). |
| **A reply letter from the other parent** | None, in the usual sense — this is the strongest paper you will have. | **MRE 801(d)(2): "An Opposing Party's Statement. The statement is offered against an opposing party and: (A) was made by the party in an individual or representative capacity…"** A statement by the opposing party, offered against them, **is not hearsay.** *(VERIFIED verbatim — `sources/primary/michigan-rules-of-evidence_2026-01-28.txt`.)* |
| **A reply letter from the attorney** | Whether the attorney's letter counts as the party's statement. | **MRE 801(d)(2)(C)/(D)** cover statements by a person the party authorized to speak on the subject, or by the party's agent within the scope of the relationship while it existed. That is the argument. It is not automatic and it can be contested. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — rule text verified; application to a specific letter is a live question` |
| **Your log** | Your own out-of-court statements again, and it is not a business record. | Its real value is that it lets you **testify accurately from memory refreshed**, and it tells you which documents to pull. Do not assume the log itself comes in. Build the folder so you never need it to. |
| **Screenshots, app exports, message printouts** | Authentication. | **Michigan has no self-authentication route for digital records.** MRE 902 ends at (11); there is no Michigan version of the federal 902(13)/(14). *(VERIFIED — `verification_ledger.md` §2.1.)* Every digital exhibit needs a live witness who can say what it is. |

**⚠ The single most useful consequence:** the paper *they* write is worth more, evidentially, than the paper *you* write. That is a reason to write letters that invite a written answer, and a reason never to send anything you would not want read back to you.

**⚠ And a warning about recordings.** This guide does not tell you that any particular recording is lawful, because Michigan law in this area is unsettled and the criminal exposure is real. Do not record a conversation you are not part of. Do not send a child anywhere with a recording device. Do not record the child. Do not place a device in the other parent's home or car. If a recording already exists, that is a question for a lawyer before it is a question for a filing. **`DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE` on any recording question.**

## Your own conduct audit — do this before you rely on any of the above

Every hook in this document points both ways. Answer these honestly, on paper, for yourself:

1. Have I contacted the other parent outside the permitted channel, even once, even briefly, since the restriction started? What is the date?
2. Is there anything on any social-media account of mine — including old posts, comments, and replies — about the other parent, the case, or the child's living situation?
3. Have I asked the child anything about the other parent's household?
4. Have I said anything about the other parent in front of the child?
5. Is there a protective-action explanation for the other parent's conduct that a court might accept? What are the actual dated facts, not my interpretation of them?
6. Have I missed a time that was offered? On what date, and why?
7. Am I current on support? (You may not withhold either support or parenting time because of the other. They are independent obligations. Being behind will not defeat this motion, but it will be raised, and you should know what the record shows before someone else says it out loud.)

If question 1 or 3 has a "yes," you need to know that before a hearing, not during one. If question 5 has a "yes," a case built on factor (j) is the wrong case to build — see the boxed limiter above.

## Exercise — done when

**Do this now, before you draft anything for court.**

1. Read your order and copy out the parenting-time provision **word for word** into the top of your log file.
2. Read your order and copy out the communication provision **word for word**, if there is one.
3. Find the letter or order that narrowed the channel to mail, if one exists. Note its date and who signed it.
4. Set up one folder — paper or digital — named for the case, with three sections: **Letters sent**, **Proof of mailing and delivery**, **Replies**.
5. Draft and mail request #1.
6. Enter row 1 in the log the same day.

**You are done with this exercise when:**

- The exact words of the parenting-time provision are written down where you can see them, and
- You can state, in one sentence, whether the communication restriction came from the court or from a letter — or that you cannot tell yet and have written down which document you need in order to tell, and
- Row 1 of the log is complete through "date mailed," with the tracking number recorded, and
- A person who has never met you could open the folder and say what was asked, when, and what happened.

- **A PPO, no-contact order, or bond condition** touches your contact with the other parent. `DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE`
