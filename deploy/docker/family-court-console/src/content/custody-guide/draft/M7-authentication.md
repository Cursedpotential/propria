# Why this module exists

A custody case runs on ordinary, everyday evidence: text messages, app messages, emails, photos, screenshots, call logs, social-media posts, and school, medical, and business records. Almost none of it proves *itself*. Before a judge or referee will look at a single text, someone has to stand up and show that the text is **what you say it is** — a real message, from the person you say sent it, on the date you say, not edited. That step is called **authentication**, and Michigan gives you no way around it for the material you gathered yourself.

The trap this module exists to prevent is simple and common. A parent prints a stack of texts, three-hole-punches them into a binder, and walks to the podium — and the other side says "objection, foundation," and the binder never comes into evidence. The facts inside it may be true. They still do not get considered, because no one established that the pages are what the parent claims.

Two ideas carry the whole module:

1. **Authentication is a door, and it is a low door.** You only have to show *enough* for a reasonable fact-finder to conclude the item is genuine — not prove it beyond doubt. But the door does not open by itself.
2. **Authentication and hearsay are two different doors.** Getting a text authenticated does **not** make it admissible; it can still be barred as hearsay, and hearsay is a separate analysis that lives in **Module 6**. A "foundation script" that teaches magic words without teaching both doors will walk you into the second objection right after you clear the first.

Where the Michigan Rules of Evidence even apply is its own question — they apply at referee hearings and at trial, and they do **not** apply to the in-camera child interview (**Module 5**). This module assumes you are somewhere the rules apply, which for most Genesee pro se parents means the referee hearing that functions as the real trial.

> ### ⚠ BOXED WARNING — MICHIGAN HAS NO "CERTIFIED ELECTRONIC RECORD" SHORTCUT
> **This is one of the highest-value discoveries in the whole guide. Read it before you plan how to get your phone evidence in.**
>
> Michigan's self-authentication list, MRE 902, runs from **(1) through (11) and stops there.** It has **no** analogue to the federal provisions that let a certificate self-authenticate electronic records — FRE 902(13) (records generated by an electronic process or system) and FRE 902(14) (data copied from an electronic device or storage medium). *(The Michigan side — that MRE 902 ends at (11) — is VERIFIED from the rule text quoted below. ⚠ The federal comparison is stated from the outline; confirm FRE 902(13)–(14) against the federal rule if you rely on the contrast in argument.)*
>
> **What that means in practice:** there is no certificate you can buy or download that makes *your own* screenshots, *your own* phone's text export, or a photo off *your own* camera roll self-proving. For the material you captured yourself, authentication happens **the old way — a live witness with personal knowledge testifies under MRE 901.** Usually that witness is you.
>
> **The one certificate route that *does* exist is narrow and does not cover your screenshots.** MRE 902(4) and 902(11) let a **records custodian's certification** self-authenticate records a *business or public office keeps* — a phone carrier's call-detail records, a co-parenting app's certified export, a bank, a school, a clinic, the FOC's own file. That route (Section 7.3) can spare you from dragging a custodian into court. It cannot rescue a screenshot you took at your kitchen table.

## 7.1 What authentication is — and what it is not

Here is the rule, verbatim:

> **(a) In General.** To satisfy the requirement of authenticating or identifying an item of evidence, the proponent must produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what its proponent claims it is.
> — MRE 901(a)

Three things follow, and they change how you should think about your evidence.

**The burden is low.** As the MJI Evidence Benchbook summarizes the leading civil case, the proponent must make "only . . . a prima facie showing that a reasonable juror might conclude that the proffered evidence is what the proponent claims it to be," and "evidence supporting authentication may be direct or circumstantial and need not be free of all doubt" (*Mitchell v Kalamazoo Anesthesiology, PC*, 321 Mich App 144, 155 (2017), as quoted in the Benchbook. ⚠ read the opinion before quoting it in a filing). The Benchbook's own committee tip puts it plainly: "Care should be taken not to demand too much when it comes to authentication. Authentication is a low, not high, burden to carry." This is good news for a pro se parent — you do not need an expert or a forensic report to get a text into evidence. You need a witness who can honestly say what it is.

**"Authenticated" and "genuine" are two separate questions — and the split is your friend against an altered-image objection.** The Benchbook, quoting *Mitchell*, explains that authenticity "involve[s] two related, but distinct, questions. The first question is whether the evidence has been *authenticated* — whether there is sufficient reason to believe that the evidence is what its proponent claims for purposes of admission into evidence. The second question is whether the evidence is *actually authentic* or *genuine* . . . for purposes of evidentiary weight and reliability" (321 Mich App at 154). The first is decided by the judge, for admission. The second — is it *really* genuine, or was it doctored? — is for the fact-finder, and it goes to **weight, not admissibility.** So when the other side cries "that screenshot could have been Photoshopped," that argument usually does **not** keep your exhibit out; it is an argument about how much the exhibit is worth, made after it comes in. (It still means you should preserve the original file — see Section 7.4.)

**Authentication does not cure hearsay.** This is the discipline that a "foundation script" alone will not give you. Proving a text is really from the other parent gets you through the authentication door. Whether the *words* in it can be offered for their truth is a **hearsay** question answered in **Module 6** — most often by MRE 801(d)(2), the opposing-party's-statement rule, which makes anything the other parent said, texted, emailed, or posted **not hearsay** when you offer it against them. Two doors, every time:

```
YOUR EXHIBIT (a text, photo, record, post)
        │
        ▼
  DOOR 1 — AUTHENTICATION (this module, MRE 901 / 902)
  "Is it what you say it is?"  ── fail → excluded
        │ pass
        ▼
  DOOR 2 — HEARSAY (Module 6, MRE 801–807)
  "Can the words come in for their truth?" ── fail → excluded (or comes in for a non-truth purpose)
        │ pass
        ▼
  Relevance / MRE 403 balancing (Module 6) → admitted
```

## 7.2 The MRE 901 toolbox — the ways you actually prove an item is genuine

MRE 901(b) gives a menu. It is expressly **not** a closed list — "The following are examples only — not a complete list — of evidence that satisfies the requirement." The Benchbook committee tip draws the contrast you should keep in mind: MRE 901(b) is "a list of possibilities; the latter [MRE 902] is a finite list." Here are the tools a custody litigant reaches for most, verbatim:

> **(1) Testimony of Witness with Knowledge.** Testimony that an item is what it is claimed to be.
>
> **(4) Distinctive Characteristics and the Like.** The appearance, contents, substance, internal patterns, or other distinctive characteristics of an item, taken together with all the circumstances.
>
> **(5) Opinion About a Voice.** An opinion identifying a person's voice — whether heard firsthand or through mechanical or electronic transmission or recording — based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances that connect it with the alleged speaker.
>
> **(6) Evidence About a Telephone Conversation.** For a telephone conversation, evidence that a call was made to the number assigned at the time to: (A) a particular person, if circumstances, including self-identification, show that the person was the one called; or (B) a particular business, if the call was made to a business and the call related to business reasonably transacted over the telephone.
>
> **(7) Evidence About Public Records.** Evidence that: (A) a document was recorded or filed in a public office as authorized by law; or (B) a purported public record or statement is from the office where items of this kind are kept.
>
> **(9) Evidence About a Process or System.** Evidence describing a process or system and showing that it produces an accurate result.
>
> **(10) Methods Provided by a Statute or Rule.** Any method of authentication or identification allowed by a Michigan statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court.
> — MRE 901(b)(1), (4), (5), (6), (7), (9), (10)

Two of these do most of the work in a custody case:

- **901(b)(1), witness with knowledge**, is the everyday route. You were a party to the text thread; you received the email; you saw the scene in the photograph. You testify to that.
- **901(b)(4), distinctive characteristics**, is the quiet workhorse for digital messages, and it is more powerful than it looks. The Benchbook committee tip: "the contents of a letter may be used to establish the author of a letter. This is a common form of authentication of a letter, whether in hard copy, text, email, or otherwise." A message can authenticate *itself* by what is in it — the sender's number, their name, a nickname only they use, a reply that answers the exact question you asked, facts only that person knew. Taken "together with all the circumstances," those details let a reasonable fact-finder conclude who wrote it.

## 7.3 The certificate route — where a custodian or a certification avoids live testimony (MRE 902)

MRE 902 lists items that are **self-authenticating** — they "require no extrinsic evidence of authenticity in order to be admitted." For a custody litigant, two entries matter, because they let a piece of paper stand in for a live witness:

> **(4) Certified Copy of Public Record.** A copy of an official record — or a copy of a document that was recorded or filed in a public office as authorized by law — if the copy is certified as correct by: (A) the custodian or another person authorized to make the certification; or (B) a certificate that complies with subrules (1), (2), or (3) or a Michigan or federal statute.
>
> **(11) Certified Domestic or Foreign Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** The original or a copy of a domestic or foreign record that meets the requirements of Rule 803(6)(A)-(C), as shown by a certification of the custodian or another qualified person that complies with a Michigan statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court. Before the trial or hearing, the proponent must give an adverse party reasonable written notice of the intent to offer the record — and must make the record and certification available for inspection — so that the party has a fair opportunity to challenge them.
> — MRE 902(4), (11)

This is the payoff the boxed warning pointed to. Records that a **business or public office keeps** can walk in on a certificate, without the custodian appearing:

- **902(4)** covers certified copies of **public records** — a court order, a police report obtained as a certified copy, a filed document. (Police reports carry a separate hearsay caveat for law-enforcement observations in criminal matters — that is a Module 6 issue.)
- **902(11)** covers certified **business records** — a phone carrier's call-detail records, a bank statement, a school file, a medical chart, and, importantly, a **certified export from a co-parenting app**. The certificate does two jobs at once: it self-authenticates the record (Door 1) *and*, because 902(11) tracks the business-records hearsay exception MRE 803(6), it can also carry the record through Door 2 (hearsay — Module 6). But 902(11) has a **string attached**: you must give the other side **reasonable written notice** ahead of time and make the record and certificate available. Miss the notice and you can lose the shortcut.

The Benchbook records exactly this playing out with social-media evidence: in *People v Dingee* (Mich App 2025), the trial court admitted **Facebook records as self-authenticating under MRE 902(11)** once they were certified, even though no separate formal notice was sent, because the party had disclosed the certified records and the context made clear what they were. *(⚠ *Dingee* is recent; read the opinion before relying on its notice analysis.)*

> ### ⚠ The certificate covers records a company keeps — not the screenshot you took of them
> A **certified export** that OurFamilyWizard, TalkingParents, or AppClose produces *as the record-keeper* is a business record that can ride MRE 902(11). A **screenshot you took of the same app on your phone** is not — it is your capture, and it goes back to live testimony under MRE 901. Same conversation, two very different foundations. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — whether a given app offers a court-admissible certified record, and the exact form of its certificate, is a vendor fact that changes; confirm with the app and against the certification that "complies with a Michigan statute or a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court" before you count on it.`

> ### ⚠ VERIFY — which Michigan statute or rule "permits certification"
> Both MRE 803(6)(D) and MRE 902(11) require a certification that "complies with a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court or with a statute permitting certification." The rules do not name that statute or rule inside the text quoted here, and the enabling provision is not in this module's sources. **Confirm the specific Michigan certification statute/court rule before drafting or relying on a custodian certificate.** Until then, the safer path is to bring the custodian (or a qualified witness) live under MRE 803(6), or to subpoena the record.

## 7.4 Originals, duplicates, and screenshots — the best-evidence layer (MRE 1001–1004)

People assume a screenshot is second-best to "the real thing." Michigan's definitions say otherwise for electronic information. Verbatim:

> **(d)** An "original" of a writing or recording means the writing or recording itself or any counterpart intended to have the same effect by the person who executed or issued it. For electronically-stored information, "original" means any printout — or other output readable by sight — if it accurately reflects the information. An "original" of a photograph includes the negative or any print from it.
> **(e)** A "duplicate" means a counterpart produced by a mechanical, photographic, chemical, electronic, or other equivalent process or technique that accurately reproduces the original.
> — MRE 1001(d), (e)

> An original writing, recording, or photograph is required in order to prove its content unless these rules or a statute provides otherwise.
> — MRE 1002

> A duplicate is admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair to admit the duplicate.
> — MRE 1003

What this means for digital evidence:

- **A screenshot or printout of a text or email is an "original"** for best-evidence purposes, *so long as it accurately reflects the information* (MRE 1001(d)). You are not disqualified for lacking "the real one." The pressure point is the phrase **"accurately reflects"** — which loops straight back to authentication and to the genuineness question in Section 7.1.
- **Duplicates come in freely — until authenticity is genuinely disputed.** MRE 1003 lets a duplicate stand in for an original "unless a genuine question is raised about the original's authenticity or the circumstances make it unfair." That "genuine question" is where **metadata and the native file** start to matter.
- **Preserve the native file, not just the screenshot.** When the other side raises a real dispute about whether a message was edited or invented, the thing that answers it is the **native file** — the message as it lives in the app or on the device, carrying its **metadata** (timestamps, sender data). A cropped screenshot cannot answer a genuine forgery claim; the underlying file often can. Keeping the native source is a **Module 8** preservation task, but the reason to keep it is this rule. If originals are truly lost, MRE 1004 provides limited off-ramps — but do not manufacture the loss; MRE 1004(a) only forgives destruction that is "not by the proponent acting in bad faith."

## The step-by-step foundations

For each common exhibit type below: **who** can lay the foundation, **what** you are putting in front of the court, **how** you show it is what you claim (which MRE 901/902 route), the **hearsay** hand-off to Module 6, and the **trap**. The child is never the source, the courier, or the recording target of any of this (GUARDRAILS §8; Module 8).

### 7.5 Text messages and co-parenting-app messages (OurFamilyWizard / TalkingParents / AppClose)

- **Who:** you, as a participant in the conversation (personal knowledge — MRE 901(b)(1)). No one else is needed for messages you sent and received.
- **What:** the thread, shown **complete and in context** — not a lone bubble lifted out of an exchange. (Completeness has its own rule, MRE 106, in Module 6; a fragment invites the other side to demand the rest at the worst moment.)
- **How you know it is what you claim:** you were on the thread; you recognize the other parent's number/handle; the contents carry distinctive characteristics — their name, their phone number displayed, replies that answer your specific messages, facts only they knew (MRE 901(b)(1) + (b)(4)).
- **Certificate alternative:** a **certified export** from the app as record-keeper can self-authenticate under MRE 902(11) with notice (Section 7.3) — this is the co-parenting app's selling point. Your own screenshot of the app does not.
- **Hearsay (Module 6):** the other parent's messages are **not hearsay** offered against them — MRE 801(d)(2). Your own messages are usually offered to show context or effect, not for their truth.
- **Trap:** an incomplete thread; a screenshot with the date or sender cut off; assuming the app export and your screenshot are interchangeable (they are not — different foundations).

### The foundation-script template (a skeleton you fill in for any digital item)

This is a model of the testimony a court is listening for, in the witness's own words. It is not a filing and not a fill-in-the-blank form to file — it is what a person *says* on the stand to open Door 1. Sections in brackets change with the item.

> 1. **Who I am / my knowledge.** "My name is ___. I am the [petitioner/respondent] in this case. I am familiar with [the item] because I [was a participant in the conversation / took the photograph / received the email / keep this record]."
> 2. **What this is.** "Exhibit ___ is [a true and accurate copy of a text-message thread between me and the other parent / a photograph of ___ / an email I received from ___] dated ___."
> 3. **How I know it is genuine.** "I recognize it because [it is from the number/handle I know to be the other parent's; it contains ___ that identifies them; I was present and it accurately shows what I saw; I generated it from ___]."
> 4. **Accuracy / completeness.** "This exhibit fairly and accurately reflects [the conversation / the scene / the record] as it existed on ___. Nothing has been added, deleted, or altered, and the thread is shown in full from ___ to ___."
> 5. **How and when I captured it.** "I [screenshotted / printed / exported] it on ___ from [my phone / the app / my email], and I still have the original on [device/account]."
> 6. **Then, separately, the offer and the hearsay basis (Module 6).** "I offer Exhibit ___. The other parent's statements in it are offered as an opposing party's statement under MRE 801(d)(2)."

### Worked example — foundation for a text thread *(fill-in illustration)*

*Assumed posture and facts (fictional):* a post-judgment parenting-time enforcement hearing before a referee; the moving parent, "Alex Roe," offers a thread with the other parent, "Pat Doe," about a missed Friday exchange. The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply (referee hearing — Module 5).

> "My name is Alex Roe; I am the moving party. Exhibit 3 is a true and accurate copy of a text-message thread between me and Pat Doe from Friday, March 6, 2026, between 4:11 p.m. and 6:02 p.m. I know it is between Pat and me because it is the mobile number I have used to reach Pat for the last three years, it displays Pat's name as saved in my phone, and Pat's 4:52 p.m. message answers my 4:40 p.m. question about the exchange by name. The exhibit shows the conversation in full, from my first message to Pat's last; nothing is added, deleted, or altered. I made this printout from my own phone on March 9, 2026, and I still have the thread on that phone. I offer Exhibit 3; Pat's messages are offered as an opposing party's statement under MRE 801(d)(2)."

*Do not use if:* the rules of evidence do not apply to your proceeding (Module 5); the thread is incomplete or you cannot speak to part of it; the messages you want are from someone other than the opposing party (the 801(d)(2) basis will not fit — see Module 6); or you have not confirmed your own facts. This is an illustration of a foundation, **not** filing-ready text.

### 7.6 Screenshots — the highest-trap category

A screenshot is not admissible **because it is a screenshot.** The outline's warning is the whole point: to authenticate one, the person offering it has to be able to identify **the account, the participants, the date, the continuity of the conversation, and the method of capture.**

- **Who:** the person who **took** the screenshot and can speak to what was on the screen — ordinarily you. This is exactly where foundation collapses: a screenshot someone *else* took, that you cannot personally speak to, has **no witness to lay its foundation** (unless that person testifies or it arrives as a certified record).
- **What / how:** identify whose account and screen it shows; identify the participants; show the date; show that the conversation is **continuous** (no gap where messages were removed); and explain how and when you captured it (MRE 901(b)(1) + (b)(4)).
- **Trap 1 — no sponsoring witness.** If you did not take it and cannot authenticate it, forwarding a friend's screenshot to yourself does not fix it. Someone with knowledge must sponsor it, or it must come in as a certified record.
- **Trap 2 — continuity.** A cropped or spliced thread invites the objection that context was removed (MRE 106, Module 6). Capture the full exchange.
- **Trap 3 — the altered-image objection.** As Section 7.1 explained, "it was doctored" usually goes to **weight, not admissibility** — the exhibit can still come in. But keep the **native file with its metadata** so you can answer a genuine dispute (MRE 1003; Section 7.4). The Michigan cases warn courts to stay "vigilant" about "fake . . . accounts, hacked accounts, and so-called deep fakes" (*People v Smith*, 336 Mich App 79, 107 (2021), per the Benchbook. ⚠ read before quoting) — so expect the challenge and be ready to meet it, not surprised by it.

### 7.7 Emails

- **Who:** the sender or a recipient with personal knowledge (MRE 901(b)(1)).
- **How:** distinctive characteristics — the sending address, the display name, the signature block, a reply chain that quotes your message, contents only the author would know (MRE 901(b)(4)).
- **Practical note:** print or export the **full header and the full chain**, not just the body; the header carries the address and date that do your authenticating work, and the chain shows continuity.
- **Hearsay (Module 6):** the other parent's email is an opposing-party statement (MRE 801(d)(2)); a forwarded email written by a third party adds a second hearsay layer (MRE 805 — Section 7.12).

### 7.8 Photographs

The foundation for a photo is old and forgiving. As the Benchbook states the rule: "someone who is familiar from personal observation of the scene or person photographed [must] testif[y] that the photograph is an accurate representation of the scene or person" (*In re Robinson*, 180 Mich App 454, 460–461 (1989), per the Benchbook. ⚠ read before quoting).

- **Who:** **anyone** with personal knowledge of the scene — **not** necessarily the photographer. If you were there and the photo fairly shows what you saw, you can lay it.
- **What / how:** identify what the photo shows, and testify it is a fair and accurate representation of that scene as it was (MRE 901(b)(1)).
- **Metadata:** if **when** or **where** is disputed, the photo's embedded data (EXIF: date, sometimes location) and the native file support the timeline — keep them (Section 7.4; Module 8).
- **Trap:** a fight over the date or over alteration. Accurate-representation testimony gets it admitted; the date/alteration fight is weight (Section 7.1), answered by metadata and the native file.
- **The child line:** photographs of a child's living conditions or injuries can be relevant, but this guide does **not** turn the child into an evidence-gathering project — no staging, no tasking the child, no recording the child (GUARDRAILS §8; Module 8). Injury or abuse disclosures route out (the hard stops below and in Module 6).

### 7.9 Call logs

- **Your phone's log** shows a call happened, its number, direction, time, and duration — authenticated by you as the phone's user (MRE 901(b)(1) + (b)(4)). It shows the **fact and metadata of a call, not its content.**
- **The carrier's call-detail records** are a **business record**: a certified copy self-authenticates under MRE 902(11) and can clear hearsay through MRE 803(6) with notice (Section 7.3), or a custodian can be subpoenaed.
- **The content of a call is a different problem.** What was *said* on a call is not in the log, and capturing call *audio* raises recording-legality questions that this module does not touch — see the hard stop in Section 7.11.

### 7.10 Social-media posts

- **Who / how:** the person who viewed and captured the post, identifying **whose account** it is, that the capture is an **accurate depiction** of what was on the page, the **URL/handle**, and the **date** (MRE 901(b)(1) + (b)(4)). Michigan courts treat this as authentication's frontier and flag "fake . . . accounts, hacked accounts, and so-called deep fakes" as live concerns (*Smith*, 336 Mich App at 107, per the Benchbook).
- **Certificate route:** platform records obtained and **certified** can self-authenticate under MRE 902(11) — this is what happened with the Facebook records in *Dingee* (Section 7.3).
- **Hearsay (Module 6) — read this twice for posts:** a post **by the other parent** is an opposing-party statement (MRE 801(d)(2)) and comes in against them. A post or **comment by a third party** is a separate hearsay problem — in *Smith*, third-party Facebook **comments** were held to be inadmissible hearsay even though the posts were authenticated (per the Benchbook). Authenticating the post does not admit everyone else's words on it.
- **Trap:** confusing "I authenticated the page" with "everything on the page comes in." Different doors, and third-party content often fails Door 2.

### 7.11 Audio and video

> ### 🔴 DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE — RECORDINGS ARE ROUTED TO MODULE 9 AND COUNSEL
> **Whether you may lawfully make, keep, or use a recording — audio or video — is a hard stop.** Michigan recording law carries criminal exposure and unsettled questions, and this guide does **not** resolve any of it. That entire subject belongs to **Module 9** and to a licensed Michigan attorney (GUARDRAILS §9; outline §0.4).
>
> This section is **only** about the mechanics of **authenticating a recording you already lawfully possess** — for example, **a voicemail the other parent left on your phone**, or a video the other parent themselves posted publicly. It does **not** tell you to create a recording, and nothing here should be read as clearance to do so. **The child is never a recording target** (GUARDRAILS §8). If your evidence would require you to record someone, or if you are unsure whether a recording was lawfully obtained, **stop and go to Module 9.**

Assuming an item you lawfully have:

- **Voicemail / audio:** identify the speaker's **voice** — an opinion identifying a person's voice "based on hearing the voice at any time under circumstances that connect it with the alleged speaker" (MRE 901(b)(5), quoted in Section 7.2). You know the other parent's voice; you say so.
- **Video:** the same accurate-representation foundation as a photograph (a witness with personal knowledge that it fairly shows the scene — MRE 901(b)(1)), and, for system-generated footage like a doorbell or security camera, the **process-or-system** route (MRE 901(b)(9)) — evidence describing the system and that it produces an accurate result.
- **Preserve the native file** for the same reasons as any digital item (Section 7.4).

### 7.12 Business, medical, and school records — the certificate payoff

This is where the custodian certificate saves you the most work. The business-records hearsay exception, verbatim:

> **(6) Record of a Regularly Conducted Activity.** A record of an act, transaction, occurrence, event, condition, opinion, or diagnosis if:
> (A) the record was made at or near the time by — or from information transmitted by — someone with knowledge;
> (B) the record was kept in the course of a regularly conducted activity of a business, organization, occupation, or calling, whether or not for profit;
> (C) making the record was a regular practice of that activity;
> (D) all these conditions are shown by the testimony of the custodian or another qualified witness, or by a certification that complies with a rule prescribed by the Supreme Court or with a statute permitting certification; and
> (E) the opponent does not show that the source of information or the method or circumstances of preparation indicate a lack of trustworthiness.
> — MRE 803(6)

- **Who:** the **custodian or "another qualified witness"** — and that witness does **not** have to be the author. The Benchbook, quoting a 2025 case, states that these "foundational requirements do not require the party seeking admission to produce the author of the records or even a person who can interpret the content of the records"; the qualified witness "need only show that the records were made in the ordinary course of business and were made at or near the times of the transactions" (*People v Englebrecht*, Mich App 2025, per the Benchbook. ⚠ read before quoting).
- **The shortcut:** under MRE 803(6)(D) and 902(11) together, a **written certification** from the custodian can do the whole job — authenticate the record **and** satisfy the hearsay exception — with **no live witness**, provided you give the required **notice** (Section 7.3, and the ⚠ VERIFY note there about which statute/rule the certificate must comply with).
- **Hearsay within hearsay (MRE 805 — Module 6):** admitting the record does **not** admit every statement inside it. A school or medical file often contains **what a parent told the intake nurse or counselor** — that inner statement needs its own hearsay basis. A record admitted under 803(6) is not a laundry chute for the other parent's out-of-court narrative.
- **Trap — records built for the fight.** The business-records exception rests on routine reliability, and it weakens when a record is "prepared in anticipation of litigation" (the Benchbook collects *Solomon v Shuell* and *People v McDaniel* on this point. ⚠ read before quoting). A letter a provider writes *for your case* is on far shakier ground than the routine chart kept in the ordinary course of treatment.
- **Police reports:** a certified copy self-authenticates under MRE 902(4); but the **law-enforcement caveat** to the public-records hearsay exception (MRE 803(8)) is a Module 6 issue — authentication and hearsay, two doors again.

## Templates and tools

**1. The foundation-script template (Section 7.5).** The six-step skeleton — who I am / what this is / how I know it is genuine / accuracy and completeness / how and when I captured it / offer and hearsay basis. Introduced once here; the full **one-page scripts** for each exhibit type (text thread, screenshot, email, photograph, video, voicemail, app export, social-media post, school record, medical record, police report, calendar, business-record certification, opposing-party statement, hearsay-within-hearsay, offer of proof, and preserving a ruling) live in **Appendix C** and are cross-referenced from **Module 6** (offer of proof) and **Module 16** (hearing).

**2. The Evidence-Authentication Planner.** One row per exhibit, filled before any hearing. This is the column the **Best-Interest Evidence Matrix** in Module 2 calls the "admissibility route."

| Exhibit | What it is | Who lays it (personal knowledge?) | MRE 901 route | Certificate available? (902(4)/(11)) | Hearsay route (Module 6) | Native file preserved? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. text thread | | you (participant) | 901(b)(1)+(4) | no (your capture) | 801(d)(2) | yes / where |
| e.g. carrier call log | | custodian | 901(b)(9) | **yes — 902(11)+803(6), notice** | 803(6) | n/a (certified) |
| … | | | | | | |

*You are done with a row when every cell is answered — and an exhibit with an empty "who lays it" cell and no certificate is not yet evidence, no matter how true it is.*

**3. Certificate-vs.-live-testimony decision guide.**
*Did a business or public office keep this record? → If yes → can you get a custodian certification (902(4)/(11)) and give notice? → If yes → certificate, no live witness needed (confirm the certification statute — ⚠ VERIFY, Section 7.3). → If no, or you captured it yourself → live testimony under MRE 901, with you as the witness.*

**4. Native-file preservation note (links to Module 8).** For every digital exhibit, keep the source where it lives — the message in the app, the photo in the camera roll with its metadata, the email in the account — not only the screenshot. The reason is MRE 1003: a duplicate is fine until authenticity is genuinely disputed, and only the native file answers a genuine dispute.

## Exercises

1. **Lay the text-thread foundation out loud, three times *(practice script).*** Use the six-step template (Section 7.5) on one real thread from your own materials. *Done when you can deliver all six steps — who you are, what it is, how you know it is genuine, accuracy and completeness, capture method and date, offer plus the 801(d)(2) hearsay basis — without reading the card.*
2. **Build one complete, chronologically numbered text exhibit *(evidence-log exercise).*** Assemble a single thread in full, in date order, with a source note for each page (device, date captured, native file location). *Done when the exhibit shows the conversation continuously with no gap, every page is dated and sourced, and you have preserved the native file.*
3. **Spot the authenticity defect in three screenshots *(red-flag spotting).*** For each sample, name the defect and the fix:
   - **(a)** A screenshot a friend texted you of the other parent's post — you never saw the page yourself. *(Defect: no sponsoring witness with personal knowledge — MRE 901(b)(1). Fix: the friend authenticates it, or obtain a certified platform record — 902(11).)*
   - **(b)** A single message bubble with the date bar and the sender's name cropped out. *(Defect: no date, no participant, no continuity — fails the screenshot foundation and invites the completeness objection, MRE 106. Fix: recapture the full thread with headers and dates visible.)*
   - **(c)** A printed text the other side says was edited, where you kept only the printout. *(Defect: no native file to answer a genuine authenticity dispute — MRE 1003. The objection likely goes to weight, not admissibility (Section 7.1), but you have nothing to rebut it. Fix: preserve and produce the native message with metadata.)*
   *Done when you have named the defect, the governing rule, and the fix for all three.*

- **Any recording — audio or video — you are thinking of making, keeping, or using.** 🔴 Recording legality is a hard stop routed to **Module 9** and a licensed Michigan attorney (GUARDRAILS §9). This module only authenticates recordings you **already lawfully have**.
- **Any evidence that would require accessing the other party's phone, accounts, email, or a tracking device.** Whether that material was lawfully obtained is outside this guide — account access and trackers route to **Module 9** and counsel (outline §0.4). Do not gather what you cannot lawfully hold.
- **Anything involving the child as a source, courier, or subject of a recording or photograph staged for the case.** Not a balancing test — it does not happen (GUARDRAILS §8; Module 8).
- **A child's disclosure of abuse, or any CPS or police contact surfacing in your records.** Stop the self-help workflow; this routes out (outline §0.4; Module 6's child-hearsay warning). 🛑
- **If preserving or presenting evidence intersects with domestic violence or a PPO** — for example, evidence that would require contact or account access — safety comes first (**FM.2**, **Module 17**); do not let an evidence task override a safety plan. 🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE
