## Why this module comes first

You can understand the law perfectly and still lose because you filed at the wrong counter, served two days late, or forgot a slip of paper the local rule requires. **Module 2 tells you what standard decides your case. This module tells you how to get in front of the person who applies it** — who the players are, which of two tracks your motion rides, how a filing physically moves through the Genesee County courthouse, what it costs, and how to get help without paying for a lawyer you cannot afford.

Nothing here changes the law. All of it changes whether the court ever reaches your law. Two clocks run under everything in this module. The **statewide court rules** below change slowly and were checked against primary sources. The **Genesee local facts** — where the counter is, what the praecipe looks like, which judge you drew, the office hours — change in months, and this book cannot confirm them for the day you are reading. Every local fact is marked `ℹ PROVISIONAL`, which means one thing: *confirm this specific item with the clerk or the Friend of the Court before you quote it in a filing or rely on it at the counter.* Everything not flagged was checked against primary Michigan law.

> ### 🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE — filing is not a neutral act
> Handing a custody motion across the counter can itself be the thing that escalates a dangerous person. If there is any history of violence, threats, stalking, or control in your relationship, do your safety planning **before** the first document goes to the counter — see **FM.5** — and get a domestic-violence advocate involved before you need one (**FM.2**). ## 4.1 Who is who — the cast of the courthouse

The single most common orientation mistake is not knowing who has power to *decide* and who only *recommends, investigates, or explains*. Talking to the wrong person, or expecting a decision from someone who cannot make one, wastes time you may not have. Here is the short version. The deep version — especially what each person's report is and is not worth as **evidence** — is **Module 16** (roles), **Module 15** (the FOC report), and **Module 19** (evaluators and guardians).

| Who | What they *can* do | What they *cannot* do |
|---|---|---|
| **Judge** (your *assigned judge* — the one judge assigned to your family's case) | Enter orders and the final judgment; decide contested custody, parenting time, and support | Give you legal advice; decide your case on paperwork you never got admitted as evidence |
| **Domestic-relations referee** (a court officer who hears family matters and recommends) | Hold a hearing and issue a **recommendation**; the Michigan Rules of Evidence apply at that hearing | **Enter a final order alone** — the referee recommends, the judge decides; you have a limited window to object and get the judge to review it (see 4.2, **Module 16**, **FM.0**) |
| **FOC caseworker / enforcement** (the Friend of the Court is the office that investigates, recommends, and enforces) | Administer support and parenting-time enforcement; provide services | **Decide** custody — the court does; the FOC does not |
| **FOC investigator** | Investigate and write a report with a recommendation (**Module 15**) | Make it *evidence* by itself — the report is not evidence unless both sides agree (the report paradox, **Module 15**) |
| **Clerk / Circuit Court Records** | Accept filings, take fees, stamp and docket, issue a subpoena on request | **Give legal advice** — a clerk may tell you *how*, never *what to do* |
| **LGAL** (lawyer-guardian ad litem — a lawyer appointed for the child) | Represent the child's **best interests** (**Module 19**) | Be your lawyer, or the other parent's; the LGAL's report is not automatically evidence (**Module 19**) |
| **GAL / attorney for the child** | Assist the court on best interests (GAL) or advocate the child's **expressed preference** (attorney for the child) (**Module 19**) | Substitute for your own case |
| **Custody evaluator** | Evaluate and recommend, when appointed (**Module 19**) | Decide; the recommendation is not the order |
| **Mediator** | Help the parents try to reach an agreement, confidentially (**Module 20**) | Decide anything, or report the content of mediation to the judge |
| **Legal Resource Center navigator** | Explain the process, point you to the right form (4.7) | Give legal advice or tell you what to do |

> **The line that runs through the whole table: information versus advice.** Clerks, referees, FOC staff, and self-help navigators can explain *how the process works.* None of them can tell you *what to do in your case* — that is the line the law draws between legal information and legal advice (**FM.1**, 4.7). This book is on the same side of that line: it gives information and decision frameworks, never advice.

## 4.2 The two tracks — and why the referee hearing is often the real trial

Most contested family motions in Genesee County ride one of two tracks, and knowing which one you are on tells you where the real contest happens.

- **The referee track.** Many custody, parenting-time, and support matters are first heard by an **FOC domestic-relations referee**, who holds a hearing and then issues a **recommendation** to the judge. If nobody objects in time, that recommendation becomes the court's order. If you object in writing within the deadline, you can get the judge to review it — often on the record already made, so what you built at the referee hearing is what the judge looks at.
- **The judge track.** Some motions go straight to the assigned judge on motion day.

> **Why this matters more than it looks.** For a self-represented parent, the referee hearing is frequently the **first and only contested hearing where evidence is actually taken** — which makes it, in practice, the real trial. **The Michigan Rules of Evidence apply at a referee hearing** — this is not an informal chat, and it is where authentication, hearsay, and foundation decide what the judge ever sees. The evidence spine that governs it is **Module 5** (where the rules apply) through **Module 7** (authentication); the mechanics of objecting to a referee's recommendation, and the tight clocks for doing it, are **Module 16** and the router at **FM.0**. Do not treat the referee step as a warm-up. `⚠ TIME-CRITICAL`

The deadlines that attach to the referee track — the **21-day** written objection window and the **7-day** window to object to a proposed recommended order — are jurisdiction-flavored forfeitures. They live in **FM.0** and **Appendix A**, and missing them is how a recommendation quietly becomes a final order. Do not manage them from this orientation module; go to **Module 16**.

## 4.3 Filing mechanics — how a motion physically moves

This is the "how do I actually file" section. The exact addresses, phone numbers, and the two-deadline math live on the **FM.4** card and in **FM.0**; this section teaches the *sequence* so the card makes sense.

**First, the one that trips everyone up: where you file.** In Genesee County you file your motion **with the clerk, at Circuit Court Records — not at the Friend of the Court.** The FOC investigates, recommends, and enforces, but it **is not a filing counter**. Handing your motion to the FOC does not file it. (For the floor and street address, see **FM.4**.) `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm the current filing counter with Circuit Court Records`

**Second, the pieces of a heard motion.** In Genesee County a motion generally needs more than the motion itself before it can be heard. The rules scatter the pieces, so here they are in one list:

1. **The motion**, in the correct form — caption (court, county, case number, your assigned judge, the parties, and the document title) and format under **MCR 1.109**.
2. **A notice of hearing** — the paper that tells everyone when and where the motion will be heard — served *with* the motion.
3. **A proposed order**, attached to and served with the motion. This is a **Genesee local requirement** under the county's local court rule, LCR 2.119 — see **FM.4**. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — the exact operative text of Genesee LCR 2.119 could not be machine-confirmed from the consolidated rule PDF; read the current rule or ask the clerk before you rely on the wording`
4. **A concurrence certificate** — also Genesee LCR 2.119, and it **binds you even without a lawyer**: the rule reaches "the attorney of record *or the party in propria persona*." It states that you personally contacted the other side to ask them to agree to the relief and they declined, or that you made reasonable and diligent attempts. See **FM.4**. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — exact LCR 2.119 wording`
5. **A praecipe** — the local scheduling slip you file with the clerk to get the motion onto the docket. Genesee is reported to use a **color-coded** praecipe system (for example, a domestic-relations motion praecipe distinct from a contested at-issue slip and from general civil), filed **at least 7 days before the hearing** and listing the parties, case number, nature of the motion, attorneys, hearing date, and assigned judge. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — the color scheme and the 7-day praecipe timing are Genesee practice, are not written into LCR 2.119, and could not be independently confirmed; ask the clerk for the current card, its color, and its timing`
6. **Proof of service** — the document proving you delivered the papers to the other side. Without it, a motion may not be heard. This is the one place in this logistics module where *evidence* discipline bites: proof of service is your own evidence that you did what the rules require, so keep it.
7. **The fee, or a fee waiver** (4.5).

**Third, the two deadlines that are not in the same place.** Getting a motion *heard* means meeting a **service** deadline and a separate **filing** deadline, and they are different numbers:

- **Serve** the motion, notice, and any brief or affidavits **at least 9 days before the hearing if you mail them, or 7 days if you deliver them** — MCR 2.119(C)(1). *This is statewide law, not a stricter Genesee rule.*
- **File** the motion **at least 7 days before the hearing** — MCR 2.119(C)(4). *A separate deadline from service.*

> ### ⚠ The trap on the county's own website
> Genesee posts a 2019 "Notice Regarding Motion Practice" that tells you to file **7 days** before the hearing. That is a true statement of the *filing* rule (C)(4) — and it **leaves out** the *service* rule (C)(1). If you follow the county notice and **mail** your motion 7 days out, **you are two days late on service.** Serve early, file early. FM.0 and **FM.4** carry this trap in full; it is repeated here because it is the single most common way a Genesee pro se motion gets knocked off the calendar. `⚠ TIME-CRITICAL` (Whether the 2019 notice is still posted is itself `ℹ PROVISIONAL` — confirm current motion practice with the clerk.)

*(Response deadlines run the other direction — a response is served ≥5 days by mail / ≥3 days by delivery and filed ≥3 days before the hearing, MCR 2.119(C)(2) and (C)(4). The full set lives in FM.4 and Appendix A; do not recompute them from here.)*

## 4.4 Filing-method currency — check before every filing

Filing method is a moving target, so treat it as something to **verify, not remember.**

As of this edition, **Genesee is not a MiFILE court** (MiFILE / e-filing / electronic service all mean handling documents online). **Paper filing is the default.** Administrative Order **AO No. 2026-3** authorizes a 7th Circuit pilot for *expanded electronic service* — that is **service, not filing.** Do not assume you can e-file because you saw the court mention electronics; service and filing are different things, and so are document-imaging systems (OnBase, JIS, case search) which are **not** the same as e-filing. ~~`ℹ PROVISIONAL` — the current status is on the **FM.4** card and can change without notice.~~ `✅ VERIFIED 2026-09-07 (owner confirmation)` — Genesee not using MiFILE is settled, not a working assumption; the AO 2026-3 e-service pilot's live/enforced status remains the only open piece on the FM.4 card.

**Verification habit — run this before every filing:**

- ☐ Check the [MiFILE Available Courts](https://mifile.courts.michigan.gov/availablecourts) list for Genesee / 7th Circuit before you assume paper-only.
- ☐ If the list has changed, ask the clerk whether e-filing is **available and whether it is mandatory** for your case type.
- ☐ Confirm the filing counter is still Circuit Court Records (4.3).
- ☐ Do not treat a local imaging or case-search system as e-filing.

## 4.5 Fees, waivers, and the real cost of a case

**The motion fees** (the general-motion, custody/parenting-time, and support-only amounts) are on the **FM.4** card, all `ℹ PROVISIONAL` — confirm them at the [Genesee County Clerk Legal Division fees page](https://www.geneseecountymi.gov/departments/county_clerk/legal_division/fees.php) before you rely on a number. This section is about the two things the card does not have room for: **the fee waiver, and the money a case costs beyond the filing fee.**

**If you cannot afford the fee, ask the court to waive it.** A **fee waiver** is asking the court not to charge you filing fees because you cannot afford them, using SCAO form **MC 20** (linked from **FM.4**; do not use a recreated version). Eligibility commonly rests on receiving a means-tested benefit (for example Medicaid, SSI, or SNAP) or on meeting an income test. Prepare, do not improvise:

- Gather proof of the benefit you receive, or of your income and expenses, before you fill out MC 20.
- File the fee-waiver request **with** the filing it is meant to cover, at the same counter.

> ### 🔴 If your fee waiver is denied, a short clock starts
> If the court **denies** your fee waiver, you generally have **14 days from the date the clerk sends the notice** to pay the fee — **or your filing is rejected and you lose the filing date.** (Michigan's fee-waiver rule, MCR 2.002; the court is to rule on the request within a few business days.) Losing a filing date can mean losing a deadline you thought you had met. This deadline is why the **FM.0** router sends a denied fee-waiver here; it belongs on your tracker and in **Appendix A**. `🔴 ACT TODAY` / `⚠ TIME-CRITICAL — confirm the exact date on your notice`

**The real cost of a case is more than the filing fee.** Plan for the whole thing, so a cost you did not see coming does not stop your case midway:

| Likely cost | When it hits |
|---|---|
| Copying and printing (motions, exhibits, courtesy copies) | Every filing |
| Serving papers (process server or mail/certified mail) | Every motion |
| **Transcripts** (for an objection to a referee recommendation, or an appeal) | If you object or appeal |
| Mediator fees | If the case goes to mediation (**Module 20**) |
| Custody-evaluator fees | If an evaluation is ordered (**Module 19**) |
| LGAL / GAL fees | If one is appointed (**Module 19**) |
| Subpoena and witness fees | If you subpoena witnesses or records (**Module 10**) |

> **A fee waiver is not the same as making the other side pay.** Waiving *your* filing fee (MC 20) is a completely different thing from asking the court to order the **other party** to pay *your* attorney fees or costs. Those fee-shifting requests run through separate rules and belong to **Module 24** — do not confuse the two, and do not assume a fee waiver gets you anything from your ex.

## 4.6 Motion day, judge-specific policies, and courtroom conduct

**Motion day is Monday.** A sitting 7th Circuit judge has described it plainly: *"Mondays are always our motion day."* Contested motions are generally heard **in person**; uncontested or stipulated matters are often handled by **Zoom**. `ℹ PROVISIONAL — confirm your assigned judge's current motion day, time, and whether your matter is in person or remote` ([ICLE Q&A with Judge Anthony J. McDowell](https://community.icle.org/blogs/lindsey-a-dicesare/2026/05/18/qa-with-judge-anthony-j-mcdowell-7th-circuit-court)).

> ### ⚠ One judge's policy is not every judge's policy
> This is the part of Genesee practice that is **most specific to the individual judge you drew**, and the part this book can least confirm for your day. Treat everything below as an *illustration of the kinds of things that vary* — not as the rule for your judge. **Confirm your own assigned judge's current written policies before you file or appear.**

**What varies by judge — the categories to check.** As a concrete illustration only, one 7th Circuit judge's published policies have set things like: **courtesy copies** of motions delivered to chambers (a *courtesy copy* is an extra copy given straight to the judge, on top of the copy you file with the clerk); a deadline for **motions in limine** (evidence questions raised before trial) weeks ahead of trial; a limit on how many **adjournments** you get without re-filing; and a **time-blocked Monday schedule** that sorts the day by matter type and by the parties' last-initial (for example, PPO motions, then show-cause and pretrials, then objections to referee recommendations in the morning, and contested motions split A–K / L–Z in the afternoon). `ℹ PROVISIONAL` `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — these specifics are one judge's published policy, were not machine-confirmed for this edition, and change; read your assigned judge's own policy page` ([Judge Hood Policies and Procedures](https://cms2.revize.com/revize/geneseecountyjudicialcourt/Documents/General%20Information/Judges/Hood-Policies-and-Procedures.pdf)).

**Who your judge is may itself need confirming.** The Genesee family-division bench is genuinely in flux, and this book could not resolve it to a single current roster. A 2024 case-assignment order (C07 2024-03) listed family-division judges **Gadola, Hanible, Heath, and Weier**; the FOC staff page has listed domestic referees (reported names include **Butler, Cottington-Heath, Kerr, and O'Brien**); and at least two people (**Hood** and **McDowell**) appear to have moved onto the family bench from FOC referee/director roles *after* that 2024 order, so the roster and the assignment order do not fully agree. The 2025 Family Court Plan (C07 2025-04J / P25 2025-02J) sets a **one-family-one-judge** objective where practicable. `ℹ PROVISIONAL` `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — confirm your actual assigned judge and referee from your case notice and the current court directory; do not rely on any dated roster`

**Courtroom conduct — the register the room expects.** How you carry yourself is read as information about you. None of this is optional politeness; it is how you avoid handing the other side an easy point:

- Address the judge as **"Your Honor."** Stand when the judge enters and when you speak, unless told otherwise.
- **Do not interrupt** — not the judge, not the referee, not the other party or their lawyer. Wait, then speak.
- Refer to the other parent **neutrally** — by name or as "the other party" — never with a label. The book bans clinical labels ("narcissist," "alienator," and the like) in filings and argument for a reason: they read as *your* instability, not the other parent's, and Michigan has no such cause of action anyway (see **Module 2** and the guardrail on banned vocabulary). You plead dated conduct; you let the court name the pattern.
- Keep to **time discipline** — motion dockets move fast; make your two or three best points and stop.
- Judges read chaos — over-talking, arguing with the ruling, emotional escalation — as a signal about how you will co-parent. Calm is strategic.

## 4.7 Getting help without hiring full counsel

You do not have to choose between "represent yourself entirely" and "pay a full retainer." Between them sits **limited-scope representation** — hiring a lawyer for **one task** instead of the whole case.

- **Limited-scope representation** is authorized by **MCR 2.117(B)** (with MCR 2.107), using SCAO form **MC 516**, Notice of Limited Scope Appearance (linked from **FM.4**). A lawyer can appear for one hearing, or handle one motion, and then withdraw. *(Cite MCR 2.117(B) — not MCR 5.117, which is the probate analogue and does not apply here.)*
- **Ghostwriting** — a lawyer drafting a document you file under your own name — is permitted within that limited-scope framework (MCR 2.117(B) and MCR 2.107). A **consulting-only** arrangement, where a lawyer advises you behind the scenes without ever appearing, is also an option.
- A limited-scope dollar is often the highest-value dollar a pro se parent spends: one hour of a lawyer's eyes on your referee-objection strategy or your parenting-time relief can be worth more than the filing itself.

**Free and low-cost help in Genesee County** *(all hours and rooms `ℹ PROVISIONAL` — confirm before you go; staffing and hours change):*

- **Genesee County Legal Resource Center** — the self-help center at 900 S. Saginaw St. Reported walk-in hours have been Monday–Friday, 8:30–4:30, with a Legal Aid of Eastern Michigan clinic on Mondays and YWCA PPO help on scheduled days in Room 203 ([Michigan Legal Help — Genesee Legal Resource Center](https://michiganlegalhelp.org/self-help-centers/genesee-county-legal-resource-center)). `⚠ VERIFY BEFORE RELYING — confirm current days, hours, and room by phone before relying on any of these`
- **Legal Services of Eastern Michigan** — civil legal aid ([lsem-mi.org](https://lsem-mi.org/)).
- **State Bar of Michigan Lawyer Referral** — ask specifically for **limited-scope** help ([lrs.michbar.org](https://lrs.michbar.org/)).
- **Michigan Legal Help** — free statewide forms and step-by-step tools ([michiganlegalhelp.org](https://michiganlegalhelp.org)).

**And the line again, because it matters most where you are most desperate for an answer:** the clerk, the referee, the FOC staff, and the self-help navigator can tell you *how* — never *what to do.* When you need someone who can tell you what to do, you need a lawyer, even for one hour. The situations where that stops being optional are in **FM.1** and **Appendix K**.

## Stop and seek counsel 🛑

This module is logistics, but some situations are not logistics — they are places where getting it wrong is hard or impossible to undo. Stop using this book alone, and get a lawyer (start with the **free** options in 4.7 and **Appendix K**, not a retainer you cannot pay), if:

- **CPS or the police have contacted you, or you have been accused of abuse.** Anything you file in the custody case can be used in a criminal one. 🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE
- **There is a PPO and you are the respondent,** or a no-contact or bond condition touches your contact with the other parent — that order overrides parenting-time logistics until it is modified. 🛑 SAFETY OVERRIDE / 🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE (**Module 17**)
- **Another state is involved, or the child was taken out of Michigan** — jurisdiction may not be Michigan's, and nothing in this filing module fixes that (**Module 3**). 🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE
- **An appeal or objection clock is running** — a jurisdictional deadline you miss cannot be given back (**FM.0**, **Module 16**, **Module 28**). 🔴 ACT TODAY
- **Any question about recording a call or conversation** — Michigan law here is unsettled and carries criminal exposure; this book will not tell you a recording is legal (**Module 9**). 🛑 DO NOT USE THIS BOOK ALONE
