### 1.2 The biggest gap found in the entire project — MCL 722.27a(8)

> "Parenting time **shall** be granted **in specific terms** if requested by **either party at any time**."

**Nobody had it.** Not the three v1 council reports, not the four toolkit council reports, not either research compendium, not outline v2, not my own interference addendum. Seven documents analyze the vague-order / "as parties agree" problem — the exact problem the statute answers in one sentence — and every one of them routes the reader through modification thresholds without mentioning the subsection that grants specific terms **on request**.

This is also a process lesson worth recording: every layer of review inherited the prior layer's excerpting of MCL 722.27a. The council models analyzed the subsections the research file quoted — (1)–(3) and (7) — and no pass re-read the whole statute until tonight. **Rule for drafting: when a module turns on a statute, the drafter reads the entire section from the archive, not the quoted excerpts.** Added to the handoff instructions.

## PASS 2 — Predictions, concerns, improvements

### 2.1 Predictions (what the other side does when the motion lands)

1. **Threshold attack** — "no proper cause or change of circumstances." Answer is layered: lead with the **(8) mandate** (specific terms on request, "at any time"); argue in the alternative that converting "as parties agree" to a specific schedule does not alter the ECE, so ***Shade*'s expansive standard** governs, not *Vodvarka*; and under *Pierron*, the burden is **preponderance**, not clear-and-convincing. Ten months of a structurally unworkable arrangement is itself the changed circumstance.
2. **"He never exercised his time"** — the (7)(g) flip. Predictable and currently dangerous: ten quiet months read as acquiescence. The antidote is the **interim documentation protocol** (start now, before filing): dated, polite, specific written requests through the permitted channel, certified mail, return receipt. Every unanswered request converts from his weakness into factor (j) evidence about her and (7)(f)/(g) protection for him.
3. **Renewed allegations.** If protective claims are made and credited, factor (j)'s second sentence means her protective actions cannot be scored against her — so his filing must not depend on scoring them. It depends on (8), the (1) presumption, the (3) right, and the documented request record. Conduct and dates, never labels: the words "weaponized," "narcissist," "power and control" appear in **no** filing, no matter how true they feel — the parent who uses them is the one the court watches.

### 2.2 Concerns (ranked)

1. **Contempt/PPO exposure between now and the hearing.** Any direct contact with the other parent while the restriction stands — text, call, message through the child, social-media contact or commentary — hands the other side exactly what it needs. **Hard rule: the permitted channel only, until a court changes it.** Comply exactly while challenging; the compliance itself becomes evidence of good faith.
2. **The clock.** Every additional month deepens the child's established custodial environment with the other parent (MCL 722.27(1)(c) is a factual test — *how the child actually lives* — regardless of how the arrangement arose). The urgency is real and the direction of the owner's "12-month" instinct is correct. **But no 12-month statutory cliff was found or verified.** Candidates for what the figure refers to: ECE "appreciable time" hardening; the child crossing the (7)(b) nursing-age lines (6 months / 1 year); an FOC review cycle; something said by opposing counsel. **Open question to the owner — but nothing waits on the answer.**
3. **Service.** The last filing died on service, and the opposing party is represented — MCR 2.107 requires service on the attorney. The service pack in Packet 1 is not boilerplate; it is the single most likely failure point repeating.
4. **The support/overnights loop.** MCSF §3.03(E) requires the support order to state the overnights used. If support was computed on near-zero overnights because parenting time is "as agreed," then winning a specific schedule later supports a §3.03(D) support modification (≥21-overnight delta). Owner should locate the support order and check what overnight number it recites.
5. **Energy spent backward.** The missed first hearing and what was said there are 10 months stale — reconsideration, objection, and appeal windows are all long closed, and a parenting-time-only order was likely never appealable of right anyway (MCR 7.202(6)(a)(iii)). The forward path is the only path. Filings acknowledge the history in one factual sentence if needed, and litigate the future.

### 2.3 Improvements (adopted into the handoff)

1. **Whole-statute rule** (from §1.2 above): the drafter reads the full statute from the archive whenever a module turns on it.
2. **Packet structure over module structure** for the owner's case: draft the *work products in filing order* (documentation protocol → motion packet → service pack → hearing prep), pulling module content as needed, rather than drafting modules in catalog order.
3. **Hearing prep travels with the packet**: the referee hearing is an evidentiary hearing (MRE apply, MCR 3.215(D)(1)); the record must be built there (the 16.5 lesson); request findings on the record.
4. **After the specific order enters**: compliance log from day one; the make-up-time and enforcement machinery (M26) only becomes usable once specific terms exist — which is the point of the whole sequence.
