# Council Review: `custody_guide_outline.md`

**Model:** GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Review date:** August 9, 2026  
**Scope:** The project outline only. The plan and research compendium were read as context but are not revised here.

## Executive verdict

**Verdict: promising as a subject inventory, not yet safe or executable as a drafting specification.**

The outline has real strengths: it is Michigan-specific, uses a cumulative evidence matrix, distinguishes custody from parenting time, recognizes domestic violence and referee review, and sensibly reframes “parental alienation” as conduct relevant to statutory factors rather than as a freestanding claim. But a drafting model following it literally would produce a polished book that teaches strategy before it teaches jurisdiction, proof, deadlines, and pathway selection. That is a dangerous failure mode: the reader may understand *why* a fact matters yet still lose because the evidence is inadmissible, a subpoena was never validly issued, an ex parte objection was late, an FOC complaint was stale, or an appeal/stay was not sought in time.

The central design error is that the outline mirrors an attorney CLE’s lesson numbering instead of modeling the actual decision sequence of a first-time self-represented litigant. “Foundational Legal Standards” appears as Lesson 7, after the reader has supposedly drafted motions, mediated, tried the case, and entered judgment. Evidence is reduced to a few objectives in the trial module even though admissibility determines what the reader must preserve and obtain months earlier. Enforcement, relocation, emergency orders, and appellate triage are either compressed into passing references or omitted.

**Publication decision:** do not send the existing skeleton directly to a drafting model. Replace it with a pathway-based outline, require source-level verification before each procedural module is drafted, and subject filing templates and deadline tables to Michigan family-law attorney review.

# 1. Where the present outline would fail in practice

## 1.1 It does not tell the reader which procedural road they are on

The book needs an opening triage that separates at least:

1. no existing case/order;
2. pending original action;
3. temporary-order stage;
4. postjudgment modification;
5. enforcement of an existing order;
6. objection to an ex parte order;
7. objection to a referee recommendation;
8. emergency/PPO/CPS/criminal overlap;
9. domicile or interstate-jurisdiction dispute; and
10. appeal or stay.

Those paths invoke different standards, forms, service rules, and clocks. The current outline begins with “strategy” and then uses “motion” as though motions were interchangeable. They are not. A postjudgment custody motion must clear the proper-cause/change-of-circumstances gate; a parenting-time-only request may use the lower *Shade* framework if it does not alter the established custodial environment; an emergency ex parte request is governed by a distinct irreparable-harm/notice standard; and an objection to an ex parte order currently has a 14-day service-based deadline under the revised MCR 3.207 forms and rule materials ([Michigan Supreme Court’s 2025 amendment order](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49e151/siteassets/rules-instructions-administrative-orders/proposed-and-recently-adopted-orders-on-admin-matters/adopted-orders/2021-27_2025-06-18_formor_amdmcr3.207-3.210.pdf); [FOC 138](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/498596/siteassets/forms/scao-approved/foc138.pdf)).

## 1.2 It teaches evidence too late and too superficially

“Lay a foundation” is not enough. A pro se litigant needs a pre-litigation evidence workflow:

- relevance and personal knowledge;
- hearsay versus nonhearsay purpose;
- an opposing party’s statement under MRE 801(d)(2);
- hearsay within hearsay;
- authentication under MRE 901;
- original/duplicate rules under MRE 1001–1008;
- business-record foundation and certification;
- public records;
- privileges and confidentiality;
- completeness and context;
- exhibit disclosure deadlines;
- preservation of native files and metadata; and
- how to make and preserve an objection and an offer of proof.

The current Michigan Rules of Evidence require sufficient proof that an item is what its proponent claims it is, and expressly recognize voice identification and process/system evidence as authentication methods ([Michigan Rules of Evidence, MRE 901](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/492ca5/siteassets/rules-instructions-administrative-orders/rules-of-evidence/michigan-rules-of-evidence.pdf)). The same source defines originals and duplicates for electronic evidence and generally permits duplicates unless authenticity or fairness is genuinely disputed. A screenshot therefore is not “admissible because it is a screenshot”; the reader must be able to identify the account, participants, date, continuity, and method of capture, and must address hearsay separately.

The proposed “exhibit foundation script” risks teaching ritual words rather than the actual predicates. It also invites a false belief that authentication cures hearsay. It does not.

## 1.3 Discovery is incomplete and contains a pro se trap

The current discovery module lists party discovery but omits:

- scope, proportionality, privileges, objections, supplementation, protective orders, motions to compel, and sanctions;
- preservation letters;
- nonparty records;
- subpoenas for records versus subpoenas for testimony;
- witness fees and service;
- medical, school, CPS, police, employer, and platform records;
- deposition costs and transcript use;
- the domestic-relations exemption from ordinary initial disclosures; and
- the special rule for an unrepresented person seeking a nonparty discovery subpoena.

Domestic-relations actions are exempt from the general initial-disclosure regime under MCR 2.302(A)(4), so the reader cannot assume the other side must volunteer the ordinary civil disclosures ([Michigan Judicial Institute, Disclosure](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4aeeef/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/civil/civilresponsivehtml5.zip/Civil/Ch_5_Discovery/Disclosure.htm)). More importantly, an unrepresented party may have to move the court for issuance of a nonparty discovery subpoena; a lawyer’s ability to issue one should not be copied into a pro se template ([Michigan Judicial Institute, Request for Documents](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a4e88/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/civil/civilresponsivehtml5.zip/Civil/Ch_5_Discovery/Request_for_Documents.htm)). That is exactly the kind of lawyer-to-pro-se translation the outline is supposed to make, and it currently misses it.

## 1.4 “One-party consent generally” is too hazardous

The proposed recording-law sentence should not survive drafting. Michigan appellate authority has treated a participant’s recording as outside “the private discourse of others,” but the statutory wording refers to permission of all persons, the issue has generated serious interpretive litigation, MCL 750.539d separately restricts devices used in a private place, and interstate calls may implicate another state’s law ([MCL 750.539a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-750-539a); [MCL 750.539d](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-750-539D); [*Lewis v LeGrow*](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/OPINIONS/FINAL/COA/20030821_C234723_55_161O.234723.OPN.COA.PDF)). The guide should state the participant-recording rule cautiously, then impose a **stop-and-get-specific-advice** gate for hidden devices, recordings in another person’s home, calls crossing state lines, recording a child, or any recording touching a PPO, criminal allegation, therapy, school, or medical setting.

This is not a “VERIFY BEFORE RELYING” issue. It is a potential felony/privacy/safety issue requiring an affirmative warning not to act from a generic summary.

## 1.5 Referee review is oversimplified

The outline says the judge “rehears the matter fresh.” That is incomplete. MCL 552.507 permits a new decision based entirely on the referee record, entirely on new hearing evidence, or on a supplemented record; reasonable restrictions may apply, while live evidence must remain available as the rule permits ([MCL 552.507](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-552-507); [*Butters v Butters*](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49f1dc/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20220728_c359665_49_359665.opn.pdf)). The objection must identify specific findings or legal applications, not merely say “I disagree.” The present module also fails to teach preservation at the referee hearing, although the de novo court may restrict evidence or issues that were not properly identified.

## 1.6 It ends when the most unforgiving clocks begin

Appeals cannot be relegated to an appendix. Michigan’s civil appeal guide identifies 21 days as the key period for a claim of appeal from a final order or an application for leave, and identifies postjudgment orders granting or denying a change of legal custody, physical custody, or domicile as final orders for this purpose ([Michigan Court of Appeals, Guide to Handling a Civil Appeal](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49b19f/siteassets/publications/manuals/coa/guide-to-handling-a-civil-appeal.pdf)). Filing an appeal ordinarily does not itself stay the order, and a stay request generally must first be decided by the trial court ([MCR 7.209; same Court of Appeals guide](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49b19f/siteassets/publications/manuals/coa/guide-to-handling-a-civil-appeal.pdf)).

A self-help guide may properly decline to teach appellate brief writing, but it must teach immediate issue preservation, ordering the record, the 21-day triage, the difference between appeal by right and leave, and stay practice. “Get appellate counsel immediately” is appropriate; silence is not.

# 2. Substantive gaps

## Critical omissions

| Gap | Why it matters | Required outline response |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence and admissibility** | The reader may collect emotionally powerful but unusable proof. A journal is usually a memory aid or source map, not automatically admissible substantive evidence. | A full early evidence module plus exhibit-specific foundation cards and an admissibility worksheet. |
| **Subpoenas and nonparty records** | Teachers, doctors, employers, police, and platforms will not necessarily appear or release records voluntarily. Pro se issuance differs from attorney issuance. | Separate records request, discovery subpoena, and trial subpoena workflows; MC 11; service/witness fees; objections; HIPAA/FERPA/confidentiality cautions. |
| **Parenting-time enforcement and contempt** | Generic “contact FOC or file contempt” omits the 56-day FOC complaint limit and available make-up time. | A postjudgment enforcement module with complaint-versus-motion decision tree. |
| **Emergency ex parte relief and response** | The outline defines “ex parte” but does not teach the heightened factual affidavit, ECE findings, current forms, 14-day objection, or continuing duty to obey. | A two-sided emergency module: requesting relief and responding to it. |
| **Appeals and stays** | A reader can lose appellate review or allow an immediately harmful order to operate. | A limited but mandatory appellate triage module. |
| **Change of domicile / 100-mile rule** | Relocation is common and can trigger a multi-step custody analysis. | A dedicated domicile module before general modification. |

The FOC may decline a parenting-time complaint when the alleged violation is more than 56 days old, while MCL 552.642 requires each circuit to maintain a make-up-parenting-time policy with same-type/same-duration replacement time and specified notice periods ([MCL 552.641](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-552-641); [MCL 552.642](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-552-642)). If contempt is established without good cause, available remedies can include make-up time, conditions, fines, and other sanctions ([Michigan Judicial Institute, Contempt for Violation of Parenting Time Order](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49aeda/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/contempt/contemptresponsivehtml5.zip/Contempt/Ch_5_Common_Forms_of_Contempt/Contempt_for_Violation_of_Parenting_Time_Order.htm)). Genesee’s site provides a parenting-time complaint and accepts complaints through MiChildSupport or its form process ([Genesee FOC forms](https://www.geneseecountymi.gov/courts_and_law_enforcement/friend_of_the_court/online_forms.php); [Genesee FOC FAQ](https://www.geneseecountymi.gov/courts_and_law_enforcement/friend_of_the_court/frequently_asked_questions.php)).

MCL 722.31 generally restricts moving a child’s legal residence more than 100 miles from the residence at commencement of the action, subject to consent, court permission, sole-legal-custody and other statutory exceptions ([MCL 722.31](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-31)). The SCAO checklist confirms that out-of-state moves and the 100-mile analysis require careful attention to the order, legal custody, consent, and exceptions ([SCAO Changing Child’s Legal Residence Checklist](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/48dc1e/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/qrms/family/domestic-relations/changing-childs-legal-residence-checklist.pdf)).

## Major omissions

### Attorney fees and litigation expenses

The book discusses fee waivers but not a request that the other party contribute attorney fees or expenses. Current MCR 3.206(D) permits a request based on need/ability to pay or fees caused by noncompliance or discovery misconduct ([SCAO Divorce Proceeding Checklist quoting MCR 3.206(D)](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4ab2c2/siteassets/publications/benchbooks/qrms/family/domestic-relations/divorce-checklist.pdf)). A pro se reader may still incur limited-scope fees, transcript fees, evaluator fees, subpoena costs, or costs after obtaining counsel. The outline should cover the evidentiary showing, invoices, reasonableness, nexus to misconduct, and the distinction between attorney fees, taxable costs, sanctions, and fee waivers.

### GAL versus LGAL

The outline teaches only the LGAL and risks treating every child representative as the same. Under the Child Custody Act, a GAL is an individual appointed to assist the court in determining best interests and need not be a lawyer, while an LGAL is an attorney who represents the child; MCL 722.24 authorizes a discretionary custody-case LGAL and bars admission of the LGAL report absent all-party stipulation ([Child Custody Act PDF](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-act-91-of-1970.pdf); [MCL 722.24](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-722-24)). The guide should separately explain:

- GAL: assists court; may investigate/recommend; role depends on appointment;
- LGAL: attorney for child, statutory duties;
- FOC investigator/evaluator: court-office function, not child’s lawyer;
- community professional evaluator: expert/report role;
- therapist: treating professional, not a custody evaluator; and
- child-protective LGAL: mandatory framework in a separate proceeding.

GAL reports are not immune from evidence rules; hearsay in a GAL report may remain inadmissible over proper objection ([Michigan Judicial Institute summary of *Kuebler v Kuebler*](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/49add7/siteassets/publications/impact/written/family/impact-e-mail-5-22-23-family.pdf)).

### Custody evaluations and report challenges

The outline tells the reader how to “prepare” for an evaluation but not how to evaluate neutrality, scope, data sources, collateral contacts, testing limits, fees, report access, objections, cross-examination, or a request for underlying material. MCR 3.219 requires distribution of community-resource findings and permits an unrepresented party to object before the custody decision ([Michigan Judicial Institute training on reports](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a7d51/siteassets/educational-materials/mji/court-professional/videos-and-webinars/2023-2024/what-jurists-look-for-in-custody-and-parenting-time-reports/mjifocb_decoster-and-martinez-august-2024_material.pdf)). “Share the custody journal with the evaluator” should not be generic advice; selective, inflammatory, privileged, or child-generated material can backfire.

### CPS overlap

“CPS may run parallel” is not enough. The outline needs a bounded CPS module explaining:

- custody litigation and CPS/child-protective proceedings are separate tracks;
- CPS records are confidential and access is controlled;
- a CPS disposition is not automatically the family court’s custody finding;
- a person placed on the Central Registry has separate notice, review, expungement, and administrative-hearing rights;
- statements made to CPS may affect custody, criminal, or PPO matters;
- do not coach or repeatedly interview the child; and
- seek counsel immediately if removal, a petition, police contact, or Central Registry placement is possible.

MDHHS states that a person placed on the Central Registry has notice, record-review, expungement-request, and administrative-hearing rights ([MDHHS Central Registry](https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs/adult-child-serv/abuse-neglect/childrens/investigation/results/central-registry)). CPS records remain confidential subject to statutory access rules ([MDHHS Central Registry Clearance Requests](https://www.michigan.gov/mdhhs/adult-child-serv/abuse-neglect/accordion/forms/central-registry-clearance-requests)).

### Tax dependency and child-related credits

The allocation of the dependency/child-tax benefit belongs with support and judgment drafting. Michigan appellate authority treats the allocation as part of child support, and the current federal mechanism generally requires the custodial parent’s Form 8332 release for the noncustodial parent to claim specified benefits ([*Onyejekwulum v Onyejekwulum*](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/OPINIONS/FINAL/COA/20230316_C361167_33_361167.OPN.PDF); [IRS Form 8332](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8332.pdf)). Form 8332 does not transfer head-of-household status, earned-income credit, dependent-care credit, or dependent-care exclusion ([IRS Dependents FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/filing-requirements-status-dependents/dependents/dependents-3)). A judgment provision that merely says “Father claims the child” may therefore be operationally incomplete.

### False accusations

The current module treats a false accusation mostly as a rebuttal worksheet. It needs a **parallel-proceeding firewall**:

- preserve records; do not alter, delete, or fabricate;
- do not contact or intimidate the accuser or collateral witnesses;
- do not interrogate the child or seek recantation;
- obey PPO, bond, no-contact, school, and custody orders even if believed false;
- separate family-court response from CPS administrative response and criminal defense;
- avoid broad written narratives before obtaining advice where criminal exposure exists;
- request particulars and use formal process rather than social-media rebuttal;
- distinguish “unsubstantiated” from an affirmative finding that an allegation was knowingly false; and
- use neutral language in court.

This is one of the points where the book must stop being a workbook and direct the reader to counsel.

### Psychological and self-management dimension

The target reader is likely to be dysregulated, sleep-deprived, hypervigilant, grieving loss of contact, or tempted to answer every provocation. That is not ancillary “wellness”; it affects credibility, deadlines, communication, and child safety. The outline needs:

- a litigation operating system: one calendar, one document repository, one communication channel, weekly review;
- a 24-hour pause rule for nonemergency messages;
- a “facts/inference/emotion/action” separation worksheet;
- hearing rehearsal and grounding techniques;
- rules against using the child as confidant, messenger, investigator, or emotional regulator;
- a support-team map that preserves privilege boundaries;
- burnout and crisis triggers; and
- a plan for complying with an adverse order while preserving objections.

The material must avoid diagnosing either parent and must not imply that calm presentation proves truth or that trauma responses prove unreliability.

## Additional non-obvious gaps

1. **Jurisdiction/UCCJEA and interstate enforcement:** MC 416 is listed as an attachment, but the reader is not taught home-state jurisdiction, simultaneous proceedings, emergency jurisdiction, registration, or when Michigan may lack power.
2. **Initial-action basics:** complaint, summons, answer, counterclaim, default, service, proof of service, confidential identifiers, prior-case disclosure, paternity/marriage gatekeeping, and final-default safeguards.
3. **Order precision:** transportation, exchange location, holidays, school breaks, virtual contact, right of first refusal, decision deadlocks, information access, passports/travel, medication, extracurriculars, relocation notice, communication protocol, and safety exceptions.
4. **Privileges and child records:** therapist-patient privilege, school and medical records, releases, confidential addresses, redaction, sealed records, and consequences of filing sensitive material publicly.
5. **Sanctions and frivolous filings:** repeated emergency motions, overbroad discovery, unsupported “alienation” accusations, and bad-faith contempt can create fee exposure and credibility damage.
6. **Settlement mechanics:** authority to settle, complete term sheets, on-record settlements, voluntariness, review before assent, tax/support interaction, and avoiding an agreement too vague to enforce.
7. **Record preservation:** ensuring proceedings are recorded, obtaining transcripts, making offers of proof, and securing a ruling on objections.
8. **Special contexts:** military deployment, incarceration, disability/accommodation, interpreters, tribal/ICWA indicators, nonparent custody, and child conceived through sexual assault should at least appear in a routing appendix.

# 3. Structural critique

## 3.1 Sequencing

The current sequence is inverted. The reader encounters discovery before a complete explanation of burdens; trial before a full evidence module; judgment before enforcement detail; and “pro se survival” after most filing work is supposedly complete. Legal standards should be taught immediately after pathway triage, and evidence/preservation should precede discovery and motions.

## 3.2 Granularity

Some modules are too broad to draft safely:

- “Manage the Pretrial Phase and Discovery” is at least four modules.
- “Present Proofs at Trial” improperly combines evidence doctrine, witness examination, financial proof, objections, and trial sequence.
- “Enter the Judgment” combines proposed-order practice, substantive drafting, compliance, modification, and enforcement.
- “Experts and Court-Ordered Evaluations” combines four legally distinct actors and processes.

Conversely, the outline duplicates Genesee filing mechanics across 2.3, 6.2, 6.3, and 6.4. One master local-procedure module and one maintained deadline/form appendix would reduce contradiction risk.

## 3.3 ICLE numbering

Mirroring Lessons 1–3 has editorial provenance value but low user value. A pro se reader does not care which chapter resembles an attorney CLE; they care what to do next. Retain an internal crosswalk appendix for project management, but use reader-facing stage and pathway labels:

**Start → Stabilize → Build Proof → Ask/Respond → Resolve → Try → Enforce/Change → Review.**

## 3.4 Appendices

The appendices are missing the materials most likely to prevent harm:

- deadline and trigger table;
- pathway/form decision tree;
- evidence foundation cards;
- service methods and proof table;
- order-clause library;
- risk/escalation matrix;
- judge/local-practice verification log;
- source currency log; and
- appellate/CPS/PPO/recording-law referral card.

The proposed “Sample Documents Library” should not contain apparently “ready-to-adapt” full motions without an issue checklist, assumptions, source date, and warning that facts control. It should instead provide annotated drafting aids paired with official forms. The form directory should remove uncertain entries until their titles and functions are confirmed. FOC 57 is now identifiable as “Referee Findings and Recommendation After Hearing on Alleged Custody/Parenting Time Violation” ([FOC 57](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a7bf6/siteassets/forms/scao-approved/foc57.pdf)); the outline should not publish an unresolved guess.

# 4. Risk review

## Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Likelihood | Why the current outline invites it | Required control |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Missed ex parte/referee/appellate deadline | 5 | 4 | Deadlines scattered or omitted | Front-matter “deadline emergency” card; trigger-based tables; no disclaimer substitution |
| Illegal or unsafe recording | 5 | 3 | “One-party consent generally” | Remove shorthand; decision tree; hard-stop counsel warning |
| Inadmissible evidence / no preserved record | 5 | 5 | Evidence taught at trial | Early evidence module; exhibit pipeline; offers of proof/transcripts |
| Invalid subpoena or privacy violation | 4 | 4 | Generic discovery templates | Pro se issuance workflow; custodian/certification; privilege and notice gates |
| Wrong procedural vehicle | 5 | 4 | No pathway triage | Case-stage diagnostic; form/standard crosswalk |
| Harmful emergency motion | 5 | 3 | No ex parte merits/response module | Specific-facts affidavit; ECE analysis; adverse-action/safety screening; attorney review |
| Child coaching or triangulation | 5 | 4 | Child treated as evidence source | Child-safe protocol throughout; no interrogation; professional referral |
| CPS/criminal self-incrimination or witness contact | 5 | 3 | False-allegation module encourages detailed response | Parallel-proceeding firewall; immediate counsel triggers |
| Vague, unenforceable judgment | 4 | 4 | Judgment checklist too generic | Clause library; ambiguity audit; calendar stress test |
| Fee/sanctions exposure | 3 | 4 | Aggressive discovery/contempt templates | Proportionality, good-faith certification, sanctions module |
| Safety breach through address/service/mediation | 5 | 3 | Ordinary co-parenting assumptions | Confidential-address and safe-service protocol; DV-informed mediation choices |
| Unauthorized-practice-equivalent product behavior | 4 | 3 | Templates plus outcome predictions can look individualized | General information framing, alternative paths, assumptions, attorney-review gates |

## Why “VERIFY BEFORE RELYING” is not enough

A currency flag helps with phone numbers and fees. It does not adequately control:

- jurisdictional or near-jurisdictional deadlines;
- criminal/privacy exposure from recordings;
- immediate-danger decisions;
- conflict between custody, PPO, CPS, and criminal orders;
- privilege waiver;
- child interviewing;
- appellate final-order classification;
- use of experts or testing;
- settlement waiver; or
- interstate jurisdiction.

For those subjects, use three stronger labels:

1. **DEADLINE — ACT TODAY:** identify the triggering event, current default period, official source, and where to obtain same-day help.
2. **DO NOT USE THIS GUIDE ALONE:** recording, criminal/CPS overlap, child removal, interstate abduction risk, complex jurisdiction, appeal/stay, and expert testing.
3. **SAFETY OVERRIDE:** no direct contact, mediation, exchange, or service step should supersede a PPO, no-contact order, law-enforcement direction, or individualized safety plan.

## Strategy traps

- Calling all resistance “alienation” can obscure abuse, developmental needs, or a child’s independent experience.
- Equating an unsubstantiated CPS report with a fabricated report overstates the evidence.
- Requesting sole custody merely because conflict exists may reward conflict manufacture or expose the requester under factor (j).
- Over-documenting every slight can bury the material facts and make the writer look obsessive.
- Sending the entire custody journal to an evaluator or attaching it wholesale to a motion may disclose hearsay, privileged material, strategy, or inflammatory commentary.
- Treating a co-parenting app as inherently court-favored is unsupported; it is only a tool, and access, cost, disability, safety, and order language matter.
- Filing contempt when the order is vague may fail; the better first objective may be clarification or modification.
- Assuming an appeal pauses an adverse order is dangerous; it ordinarily does not ([MCR 7.209](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/rules-instructions-administrative-orders/michigan-court-rules/michigan-court-rules-responsive-html5.zip/Michigan_Court_Rules/Court_Rules_Chapter_7/Court_Rules_Chapter_7.htm?rhtocid=_7)).

# 5. Revised executable outline

## Drafting contract for every module

Every module must contain, in this order:

1. **Route tag:** original case, pending case, postjudgment, enforcement, emergency, or appeal.
2. **Authority panel:** current statute/rule/form and “verified as of” date.
3. **Plain-language rule.**
4. **Decision tree.**
5. **Evidence needed and admissibility issues.**
6. **Genesee local steps.**
7. **Safety/privilege/deadline traps.**
8. **Official forms, not recreated substitutes.**
9. **Drafting aid + worked example.**
10. **Exercise with completion criterion.**
11. **When to stop and seek counsel.**
12. **Glossary terms.**

No filing template may be labeled “filing-ready.” Every sample must state its assumed procedural posture and facts.

---

## Front matter: Use the guide without losing a right

**Objectives**
- Identify a deadline emergency before reading linearly.
- Distinguish legal information from individualized advice.
- Apply the safety override and parallel-proceeding warning.
- Use the case-path map and source-date system.

**Templates/tools**
- One-page “What happened today?” triage card: served with ex parte order; referee recommendation; final custody/domicile order; CPS/police contact; PPO; denied parenting time; move notice.
- Master deadline tracker with trigger, service date, filing date, source, and proof.
- Safety and confidential-address card.
- Edition/source-change log.

**Exercise**
- Enter the last five docket events and calculate every possible response deadline; completion means each date has an official source and a calendar reminder.

**Glossary**
Service, entry, filing, calendar day, court day, jurisdiction, order, judgment, stay.

## PART I — Choose the correct path

### Module 1. Case-stage and jurisdiction triage

**Objectives**
- Separate original action, pending action, temporary order, postjudgment modification, enforcement, emergency relief, and appeal.
- Identify marriage/paternity/order prerequisites without attempting to teach all paternity or divorce law.
- Screen for UCCJEA home-state, other-state order, simultaneous case, tribal/ICWA, nonparent, and international issues.
- Understand that MC 416 is jurisdictional information, not a routine afterthought.

**Templates/tools**
- Case-stage flowchart.
- Existing-orders inventory.
- Child residence/case history table.
- “Outside this guide” referral triggers.

**Exercises**
- Classify ten scenarios by procedural path.
- Complete a five-year residence and case-history chronology.

**Glossary**
Original action, postjudgment, UCCJEA, home state, emergency jurisdiction, paternity, standing, subject-matter jurisdiction.

### Module 2. Michigan custody, parenting-time, and support standards

**Objectives**
- Distinguish legal/physical and joint/sole custody.
- Apply all MCL 722.23 factors without scorekeeping.
- Identify ECE and the applicable burden.
- Separate *Vodvarka* from *Shade*.
- Understand MCL 722.27a parenting-time principles and MCL 722.26a joint custody.
- Frame requested relief precisely.

**Templates/tools**
- Best-interest evidence matrix.
- ECE/burden decision tree.
- Custody-versus-parenting-time relief worksheet.
- Proper-cause/change worksheet.

**Exercises**
- Apply the complete threshold/ECE/merits sequence to four scenarios.
- Draft a one-paragraph neutral statement of requested relief.

**Glossary**
ECE, proper cause, change of circumstances, preponderance, clear and convincing, best interests, legal custody, physical custody.

### Module 3. Genesee court map and local practice

**Objectives**
- Distinguish judge, referee, clerk/Circuit Court Records, FOC, Legal Resource Center, and mediator.

# 7. Drafting-model acceptance tests

The revised outline is executable only if the drafting model passes these tests for every module:

1. **Path test:** a reader can tell whether the module applies to an original, pending, postjudgment, enforcement, emergency, or appellate posture.
2. **Authority test:** every legal claim has a primary source and verification date.
3. **Deadline test:** every period identifies the triggering event—entry, mailing, service, filing, or occurrence.
4. **Evidence test:** every proposed exhibit identifies authentication, hearsay, privilege, disclosure, and witness issues.
5. **Form test:** the official form is linked and its current revision checked.
6. **Local test:** statewide law is not mislabeled as Genesee practice, and one judge’s policy is not generalized to all judges.
7. **Safety test:** ordinary communication, service, mediation, and exchange guidance yields to PPO/no-contact/safety constraints.
8. **Child test:** no exercise asks the reader to interview, recruit, coach, diagnose, or emotionally rely on the child.
9. **Adversarial-fairness test:** each strategy includes the likely counterargument and the reader’s own-conduct audit.
10. **Escalation test:** criminal, CPS, appellate, jurisdictional, recording, and immediate-danger issues stop the self-help workflow and refer outward.
11. **Operational-order test:** proposed relief can be placed on a calendar and enforced without guessing.
12. **Maintenance test:** time-sensitive facts live in a change-controlled appendix rather than being repeated throughout prose.

## Bottom line

The existing outline is unusually thoughtful for an early skeleton, but it is organized around what lawyers study rather than what self-represented parents must decide and do. Its most consequential weakness is not a missing topic; it is the absence of a procedural and evidentiary control system. Rebuilding around pathways, proof, hard deadlines, and escalation gates would turn it from an ambitious content list into a drafting specification capable of producing a genuinely useful—and materially safer—Michigan pro se treatise.
