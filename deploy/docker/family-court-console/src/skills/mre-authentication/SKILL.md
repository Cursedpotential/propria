# MRE 901 Evidence Authentication

Framework for authenticating digital evidence under Michigan Rules of Evidence, with templates for distinctive characteristics analysis, reply doctrine, source evaluation, and admissibility assessment.

## MRE 901(b)(4): Distinctive Characteristics

**Gold standard for digital evidence authentication.**

```
DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS ANALYSIS
====================================
Evidence: [description]

DISTINCTIVE CHARACTERISTICS PRESENT:
[ ] Content references known facts only parties would know
[ ] Response pattern consistent with prior communications
[ ] Language/style matches known communications from party
[ ] Metadata (phone number, email, username) links to party
[ ] Timing correlates with known events
[ ] References to shared experiences/conversations

FOUNDATION REQUIREMENTS:
1. Witness testimony: [who can authenticate]
2. Exemplars needed: [known communications for comparison]
3. Corroborating evidence: [supporting documents]

AUTHENTICATION STRENGTH: [strong/moderate/weak]
```

```

=======================
Original Communication: [description]
Date/Time: [timestamp]
From: [sender]
To: [recipient]

Reply Communication: [description]
Date/Time: [timestamp]
From: [original recipient]
To: [original sender]

REPLY INDICATORS:
[ ] Temporal proximity (replied within [timeframe])
[ ] Subject matter continuity
[ ] Direct response to specific questions/statements
[ ] Acknowledgment of original message
[ ] Thread/conversation continuity

AUTHENTICATION: Reply authenticates original sender because
response demonstrates knowledge of original communication
and responds to its specific content.
```

## Multi-Stage Evidence Analysis Pipeline

### Stage 1: Source Evaluation
Assess authenticity, reliability, and chain of custody (use Source Evaluation Template above).

### Stage 2: Temporal Analysis
Build accurate timeline and identify temporal patterns. Look for:
- Frequency clusters (when activity peaks)
- Gaps (unexplained absences in communication)
- Sequence anomalies (out-of-order timestamps, retroactive edits)
- Correlation with external events (court dates, incidents)

### Stage 3: Causal Chain Analysis
Establish cause-and-effect relationships:
- Map Event A -> Event B -> Event C -> Outcome
- For each link: supporting evidence, alternative explanations, strength assessment
- Identify confounding factors
- Rate overall causal chain strength

### Stage 4: Bayesian Evidence Weighing
Quantify evidence strength using probabilistic reasoning:
- **Prior probability**: Baseline likelihood before evidence
- **Likelihood ratio**: P(E|H) / P(E|~H) for each piece of evidence
  - LR > 10: Strong support
  - LR 3-10: Moderate support
  - LR 1-3: Weak support
  - LR < 1: Evidence against hypothesis
- **Posterior probability**: Updated probability after all evidence

### Stage 5: Toulmin Argumentation
Structure evidence into persuasive legal argument:
- **Claim**: Conclusion for court to accept
- **Grounds**: Specific supporting evidence
- **Warrant**: Logical connection (legal principle or common sense)
- **Backing**: Case law, statutory authority, expert testimony
- **Qualifiers**: Strength, scope, confidence level
- **Rebuttals**: Anticipated counterarguments with responses

### In-camera review and sealing of sensitive evidence (MCL 722.1209) `[VERIFY current text]`

- A Michigan family court will seal identifying information and conduct an in-camera review when a party
  submits a sworn statement or pleading, under oath, alleging that disclosure would endanger the health,
  safety, or liberty of a party or child — the court must seal unless, after a hearing, it finds disclosure
  is in the interest of justice.
- Applies both to the general UCCJEA-style disclosure a party makes at the outset of a custody proceeding
  (present address, 5-year residential history, names/addresses of everyone the child lived with) and to
  sensitive evidence more broadly (medical/psychological records).
- **Drafting implication:** a motion for in-camera review/sealing should be a sworn statement making the
  specific safety allegation required by the statute — a general request for privacy or confidentiality
  without the sworn safety allegation is a weaker foundation.

### Unauthorized access to another party's digital accounts is a crime — no custody/DV exception (MCL 752.795) `[VERIFY current text]`

- Michigan law makes it a criminal offense to intentionally access a computer, computer system, or network
  without authorization (or beyond the scope of authorization granted). There is **no exception** for family
  law, custody, or domestic-violence matters, and no case law authority carving out a "best interests of the
  child" or "proving abuse" exception — courts have rejected that argument; any such exception would have to
  come from the legislature.
- The related Fiduciary Access to Digital Assets Act (MCL 700.1006 family) does not require a digital
  custodian to recover or disclose deleted content to a party in litigation; it governs fiduciary access
  only, not discovery.
- **Practical implication for evidence intake:** evidence obtained by a party accessing the other party's
  device or account without clear, provable authorization/consent carries real legal exposure for the party
  who obtained it, independent of whether the *content* of that evidence would otherwise be relevant — flag
  this explicitly during intake (see `evidence-templates`) and route the client to a **court-ordered**
  discovery mechanism (subpoena, request for production, MCR 2.310) instead of self-help access, even where
  the other party's own prior conduct (e.g., they accessed the client's device first) is offered as
  justification — that argument has no recognized legal exception either.
