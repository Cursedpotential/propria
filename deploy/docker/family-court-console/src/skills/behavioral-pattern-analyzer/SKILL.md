# Behavioral Pattern Analyzer - High-Conflict Custody Dynamics

When invoked, analyze the provided behavioral evidence (texts, emails, incident descriptions, witness accounts) for manipulation patterns and psychological abuse indicators. **Always analyze BOTH sides** to maintain credibility and identify reactive behavior defenses.

## Analysis Framework

### Phase 1: Pattern Identification

Scan the evidence for these pattern categories:

#### A. DARVO Detection (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender)
- **Deny**: Does the accused party deny behavior despite evidence?
- **Attack**: Does the accused attack the credibility/character of the person confronting them?
- **Reverse V&O**: Does the accused claim to be the victim when they are the aggressor?
- **Frequency**: How often does this cycle repeat?
- **Documentation quality**: Is the DARVO cycle captured in writing/recording?

#### B. Gaslighting Indicators
- Denying events that are documented
- Minimizing the other parent's experience ("You're overreacting")
- Rewriting history or contradicting prior statements
- Undermining the other parent's mental health credibility
- Creating confusion about what was agreed upon
- Using children as validators ("Even the kids think you're crazy")

#### C. Coercive Control Patterns (Power & Control Wheel)
- **Intimidation**: Threats, looks, actions, property destruction
- **Emotional abuse**: Put-downs, name-calling, making them feel crazy
- **Isolation**: Controlling who they see/talk to, limiting outside involvement
- **Minimizing/denying/blaming**: Making light of abuse, shifting responsibility
- **Using children**: Making them feel guilty through children, using visitation to harass
- **Economic abuse**: Controlling finances, preventing employment
- **Privilege**: Treating them as a servant, making all major decisions
- **Threats**: Threatening to take children, report to CPS, make false allegations

#### D. Parental Alienation Indicators
- Badmouthing the other parent to children
- Limiting contact/communication between child and other parent
- Interfering with parenting time
- Creating situations where child must choose sides
- Sharing inappropriate details of adult conflict with children
- Rewarding child for rejecting other parent
- Monitoring/restricting phone calls between child and other parent
- Making false abuse allegations to restrict contact

#### E. Triangulation & Flying Monkeys
- Using third parties to communicate or pressure
- Recruiting family/friends to take sides
- Using new partners to provoke or destabilize
- Involving children as messengers or spies
- Using professionals (therapists, teachers) as unwitting allies

#### F. Reactive Abuse Analysis (CRITICAL - DEFENSE TOOL)
This is where Parent A's reactive behaviors are reframed:

**Provocation Cycle Documentation**:
1. **Trigger**: What did Parent B do to provoke? (documented?)
2. **Escalation**: How did Parent B escalate before Parent A reacted?
3. **Reaction**: What was Parent A's reactive behavior?
4. **Exploitation**: How did Parent B use the reaction against Parent A?
5. **Pattern**: How many times has this cycle repeated?

**Reactive vs. Primary Aggression Distinguishers**:
| Factor | Reactive (Defense) | Primary (Concerning) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Initiation | Responds to provocation | Initiates conflict |
| Remorse | Feels guilty after | Minimizes or blames other |
| Pattern | Only occurs when provoked | Occurs unprovoked |
| Escalation | De-escalates when trigger removed | Continues escalating |
| Control | Loses control momentarily | Maintains control throughout |
| History | No pattern before this relationship | Pattern across relationships |

### Phase 2: Evidence Quality Assessment

For each identified pattern, rate:
- **Documentation strength**: Strong (written/recorded) / Moderate (witnessed) / Weak (self-report only)
- **Pattern consistency**: Isolated incident / Recurring / Pervasive
- **Corroboration**: Multiple sources / Single source / Contradicted
- **Admissibility likelihood**: Likely admissible / Questionable / Foundation issues

### Phase 3: Psychological Profile Mapping

Map identified patterns to DSM-5 personality disorder indicators (for MRE 702 purposes):

**NPD Indicators** (Narcissistic Personality Disorder):
- Grandiosity in communication
- Lack of empathy in response to child's needs
- Exploitation of the other parent
- Entitlement regarding custody/parenting decisions
- Need for admiration/control in the relationship dynamic

**BPD Indicators** (Borderline Personality Disorder):
- Emotional instability visible in communications (extreme shifts)
- Fear of abandonment driving alienation behaviors
- Splitting (idealizing/devaluing the other parent)
- Impulsive actions affecting children
- Unstable relationship patterns documented across interactions

**Psychopathy Indicators** (PCL-R adjacent):
- Callous disregard for child's emotional needs
- Superficial charm in court/professional settings vs. private behavior
- Pathological lying (documented contradictions)
- Manipulation without emotional investment
- Moral disengagement ("the rules don't apply to me")

**Managed Mental Health (Parent A Defense)**:
- Treatment compliance documentation
- Medication adherence records
- Therapeutic progress notes
- Stable functioning between episodes
- Self-awareness and accountability

### Phase 4: MCL 722.23 Factor Mapping

Map behavioral findings to specific best interest factors:
- Factor (a): How do patterns affect emotional ties?
- Factor (f): How do patterns bear on moral fitness?
- Factor (g): Mental health implications for both parties
- Factor (j): Which parent facilitates/obstructs the relationship?
- Factor (k): Domestic violence indicators
- Factor (l): Any other relevant behavioral concerns

### Phase 5: Strategic Recommendations

#### For Parent A Defense (Reactive Behavior):
1. **Reframing strategy**: How to present reactive behavior as trauma response
2. **Documentation needed**: What additional evidence would strengthen the defense
3. **Expert testimony**: Whether a psych expert on reactive abuse/trauma would help (MRE 702 analysis)
4. **Treatment narrative**: How to present treatment compliance as strength

#### For Parent B Offense (Pattern Exploitation):
1. **Motion strategy**: What motions would expose the patterns
2. **Discovery requests**: What documents/records to subpoena
3. **Cross-examination points**: Key contradictions to exploit
4. **Expert challenge**: Grounds to challenge opponent's experts (Daubert/Gilbert)

## CRITICAL RULES
- This is behavioral pattern analysis, NOT clinical diagnosis
- Always analyze BOTH sides - credibility depends on balanced assessment
- Flag when reactive behavior crosses from defensive to concerning
- Distinguish between legally relevant patterns and personality conflicts
- All recommendations must tie back to MCL 722.23 factors
- Note when expert testimony would be needed vs. when lay observation suffices
