1. Decisions that anchor the build

This is a separate backend product lane. It learns from the docstore lane’s failures, but it does not reuse its state, edit its files, or compete with its ownership.

System of durable analytical recordImmutable, versioned Parquet and Lance datasets, published to a Backblaze B2 S3-compatible lake.
Online retrievalWeaviate owns advanced vector, named-vector, hybrid, and future multi-vector search.
RelationshipsSurrealDB owns graph, temporal, provenance, atomic-unit, corroboration, and decision relationships.
PipelineCocoIndex drives incremental discovery and transformations; projections are rebuildable from the lake.
Critical evidence rule. “Duplicate” is not a deletion verdict. A matching message from two devices, the same fact in a screenshot and native export, or equivalent content in different formats may corroborate identity, timing, possession, or authenticity. The system retains every occurrence and models the relationship.

Chosen Lake-first publication with independent projections. Benchmark gate Multimodal provider, ColPali-class service, and production Docling execution profile. Human approval Any source relocation or copy-suppression decision.

2. Product and authority boundary

In scope

Read-only discovery; source manifests; content and structural fingerprinting; sidecar association; atomic/nested unit detection; typed extraction; summaries; embeddings; Parquet/Lance publication; Weaviate and Surreal projections; semantic/hybrid/session search; copy proposals and receipts.

Outside this build

TanStack/front-end design; custody approvals; changing original evidence; direct deletion; automatic identity claims from faces; silent movement of source bytes; treating the Workbench or this service as a canonical legal approval authority.

Promotion boundary

Imported values remain immutable. Machine-derived metadata is a versioned proposal. Human annotation is a separate overlay. Anything promoted into governed Platform/PostgreSQL workflows must cross a separately approved, receipt-producing boundary.

Non-negotiable invariants

  • Every output traces to source occurrence, acquisition/export unit, run, processor, model, prompt/schema, and hash assertions.
  • Every projection can be dropped and rebuilt from a published lake snapshot; no projection is the sole copy.
  • No removal action is inferred from similarity. Physical source operations require a reviewed plan, copy, destination checksum, receipt, and explicit retention decision.
  • One application instance cannot share runtime state, ports, collection names, graph namespaces, checkpoints, staging prefixes, or logs with another instance or with ccc.

6. Detector and router

Routing is evidence-bearing classification. It records how the decision was made, its confidence, and what fallback was used.

1 · Provider hints
MIME, native type, object metadata, Drive/Graph facets.
→
2 · Byte evidence
Magic/signature, encoding, container headers, bounded sample.
→
3 · Structural probes
Archive members, export manifests, repo markers, sidecar patterns.
4 · Policy
Size, sensitivity, parser safety, budget, allowed processors.
→
5 · Route
Primary processor plus zero or more augmenters.
→
6 · Receipt
Evidence, confidence, model/parser version, fallback, errors.

Route decision schema

route_decision_id, occurrence ID, detected media/container type, evidence list, selected route, augmenters, atomic-unit candidate, sidecar candidate, confidence, uncertainty codes, policy version, detector version, byte range inspected, timestamp, and terminal/fallback state.

Extension is a hint only. Unknown, malformed, encrypted, oversized, password-protected, and potentially hostile containers receive explicit routes; they do not silently disappear.

8. Identity, fingerprinting, and evidence-safe deduplication

Five identities remain separate:

  1. Occurrence identity: this provider/path/version observed at this acquisition point.
  2. Raw content identity: byte hash of the acquired object, normally SHA-256 plus size; optional fast hash is only a prefilter.
  3. Representation identity: normalized/textual/perceptual equivalence within a declared format family and algorithm version.
  4. Semantic assertion identity: a candidate claim/message/event that may be expressed by multiple independent artifacts.
  5. Atomic-unit identity: the export, conversation, album, repository, or package context that gives occurrences meaning.
RelationshipMeaningAutomatic action allowed?
byte_identical_copySame cryptographic hash and size. Storage may be content-addressed, but all occurrences survive.Logical byte reuse only; copy suppression requires lineage/policy review.
corroboratesIndependent device, export, account, or acquisition supports the same message/event/fact.No removal. Increase evidentiary context, never silently merge origins.
alternate_representationNative message versus screenshot, PDF versus DOCX, photo of a document, reformatted export.No removal. Link representations and retain modality-specific evidence.
derived_fromOCR, transcript, thumbnail, caption, normalized text, extraction, or generated summary.Derived object may be regenerated; source relationship is mandatory.
near_duplicate_candidatePerceptual/text similarity above a versioned threshold.Candidate clustering only; never an automatic fact or deletion decision.

Hash assertions are append-only: algorithm, implementation/version, byte range/full object, digest, observed size, time, worker, and verification result. Existing hashes and provenance sidecars are imported as assertions, not overwritten as truth; independently recomputed hashes can confirm or dispute them.

11. Parquet and Lance lake contract

Parquet is the interoperable tabular interchange. Lance is the versioned analytical table form optimized for later random access and ML/media workflows. Both are durable lake products; Weaviate is not the export of record.

Dataset families

source_occurrences, content_objects, hash_assertions, atomic_units, atomic_memberships, unit_edges, sidecars, sidecar_links, metadata_assertions, effective_metadata, documents, text_chunks, pages, images, faces, audio_segments, video_scenes, code_symbols, sessions, messages, decisions, entities, events, relationships, vectors, route_decisions, processing_runs, stage_attempts, errors, projection_checkpoints, copy_proposals, and copy_receipts.

B2 object layout

s3://<bucket>/casebible/lake/
  staging/<instance_id>/<run_id>/<dataset>/part-*.parquet
  published/<dataset>/schema=<version>/run_id=<run_id>/part-*.parquet
  lance/<dataset>/schema=<version>/table=<fresh-versioned-name>/...
  manifests/<run_id>.json
  receipts/<run_id>/publish.json
  dead-letter/<instance_id>/<run_id>/...

Publication is append-only: stage, validate counts/schema/checksums, write manifest, publish versioned objects/table, then atomically advance a small catalog pointer. Never overwrite an existing remote Lance table name. B2 lifecycle/object-lock decisions are infrastructure policy, not application assumptions.

DuckDB validates and queries Parquet locally or through its S3 extension. Partitioning follows common filters—dataset/schema/run and bounded source/date buckets—without creating millions of tiny files. Bulk writers use bounded record batches.

12. Weaviate, SurrealDB, and search behavior

Weaviate

Versioned collections such as ContentUnitV1, VisualPageV1, and FaceInstanceV1, all prefixed/scoped by instance and deployment. Named vectors preserve modality. Store lake IDs and filter metadata, not the only copy of extracted content.

SurrealDB

Nodes for occurrence, content, unit, artifact, representation, assertion, entity candidate, event candidate, person candidate, run, and review. Typed edges express containment, origin, derivation, corroboration, alternate representation, mention, timing, and review decisions.

Search service

Hybrid/lexical/vector retrieval with explicit target vectors, metadata/time/source filters, whole-session expansion, atomic-unit expansion, graph neighbors, reranking, and provenance-bearing citations to occurrence and lake version.

The search result contract includes score components, vector space/model, matched unit, surrounding unit/session context, source occurrences, representation and corroboration links, extraction/summary provenance, and permissions/policy redactions. It never presents a generated summary as source text.

13. Provider and Docling strategy

Text and summarization

Keep the existing NVIDIA NIM text embedding and structured enrichment path as the first working baseline. Before scale, probe model availability, dimension, token limits, rate limits, structured-output reliability, and cost. Store response metadata and model revision; do not infer success from an HTTP 200 with malformed output.

Multimodal benchmark—not a premature selection

Benchmark on a reviewed golden set spanning native text, scans, screenshots, photos of documents, visually rich PDFs, faces, and near-duplicate/corroborating artifacts. Score recall@k, nDCG, evidence-safe false merges, p50/p95 latency, bytes/tokens, storage amplification, and cost per 1,000 items.

Cheap Docling

  1. CPU baseline: local Docling standard pipeline, bounded concurrency, OCR only when the text probe requires it, document timeout enabled, results cached by content hash + pipeline version.
  2. Opportunistic GPU worker: separate queue/profile for high-value scanned/layout-heavy PDFs; tune OCR/layout/table batch sizes against actual memory.
  3. Remote worker spike: a disposable docling-serve execution profile only if measured local cost/throughput fails the gate.

Parse once and keep the Docling document/JSON, Markdown, page/layout records, error list, timing, and parser/model versions. A PARTIAL_SUCCESS is a recorded partial result, never silently promoted to complete. No bulk PDF run starts without a sampled cost/time estimate.

14. Multi-instance isolation and failure handling

Isolation key

CASEBIBLE_INSTANCE_ID is required and validated. It controls CocoIndex application/environment name and state database, working directory, lock names, ports, log/run paths, B2 staging prefix, Weaviate collection prefix/tenant, Surreal namespace/database, source checkpoints, cache keys, and metrics labels. The default must not overlap ~/.cocoindex_code or the ccc command.

Failure pointPolicyEvidence retained
Source unavailable / permission deniedBounded retry with jitter for transient failures; fail closed for auth/scope.Stage attempt, source ID, class, retry schedule, redacted provider response.
Malformed/encrypted/hostile inputSafe bounded probe, resource limits, explicit unsupported/quarantine route.Detection evidence and parser error; no silent skip.
NIM/provider timeout, 429, bad JSONConcurrency budget, timeout, retry-after, circuit breaker, schema validation.Request digest, provider/model, attempt, latency, cost if known, terminal state.
Partial parser successPersist partial output with status; prevent “complete” promotion.Per-page/component errors and completeness fields.
Lake write/publish failureWrite only to run staging; validate before immutable publish; resume by manifest.Part checksums, counts, manifest state, orphan-staging cleanup proposal.
Weaviate/Surreal projection failureCheckpoint per published lake version; idempotent upsert; replay from lake.Projection checkpoint, failed IDs, target response, reconciliation counts.
Process interruptionLease/heartbeat expires; next worker resumes incomplete stages, never assumes completion.Run heartbeat, worker identity, last committed batch.

Every run ends as complete, complete_with_errors, failed, or cancelled. “Processed” counts never include dead-letter rows. Concurrency limits exist at application, provider, parser, and target levels. Import/export connectors use documented CocoIndex target lifecycle and exception hooks where applicable.

19. Acceptance and release gates

  • Contract gate: schemas and relationship vocabulary pass fixtures for independent copies, alternate formats, derived OCR/transcripts, and nested exports.
  • Isolation gate: two simultaneous instances plus ccc demonstrate disjoint state, ports, locks, targets, and logs.
  • Recovery gate: forced interruption at every stage resumes without duplicate publication or missing occurrences.
  • Lake gate: DuckDB can query published Parquet; Lance can reopen the versioned tables; manifest counts and checksums reconcile.
  • Projection gate: Weaviate and Surreal rebuild from the same lake snapshot and reconcile IDs/counts/checkpoints.
  • Quality gate: NIM text and every multimodal candidate meet golden-set retrieval/extraction thresholds before corpus scale.
  • Cost gate: sampled throughput and cost forecasts are recorded before PDF, image, audio, or video bulk runs.
  • Evidence gate: search results expose original source occurrences and derivation; generated content is visibly separate.
  • Relocation gate: dry-run plan, human approval, copied bytes, destination checksum, receipt, and explicit source-retention outcome.
Definition of “done.” Done is not a successful demo query.
